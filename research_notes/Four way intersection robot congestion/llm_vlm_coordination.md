# LLM/VLM-Based Multi-Robot Coordination and Semantic Traffic Reasoning (2023–2026), and Composite/Sequenced Maneuver Commands

Research notes, 2026-09-29. Every cited system below was verified to exist via web search (arXiv/IEEE/ACM/venue pages). Claims marked "(abstract-level)" come from abstracts or search-result synthesis, not full-text reads — treat quantitative details there with slightly lower confidence. arXiv IDs beginning 25xx/26xx are 2025/2026 submissions.

## Q1: Which 2023–2026 systems use LLMs/VLMs for multi-robot conflict resolution or intersection management, and what did they find about reliability, latency, and grounding failures?

### Takeaway
A small but real literature now uses LLMs/VLMs as high-level arbiters for deadlock resolution, intersection right-of-way, and traffic signal control. The consistent architecture is: foundation model emits a symbolic/high-level decision → a deterministic or learned low-level executor converts it to motion; latency is universally treated as the binding constraint (solved via caching, anticipatory triggering, parallel pipelines, or fine-tuned small models), and nearly all results are simulation-only.

### Cited Findings
- **Garg et al., "Foundation Models to the Rescue: Deadlock Resolution in Connected Multi-Robot Systems"** (arXiv 2404.06413, 2024; earlier title "Large Language Models to the Rescue: Deadlock Resolution in Multi-Robot Systems"). Uses both text-only LLMs and image+text VLMs as high-level planners for deadlock resolution in connected multi-robot systems. The model's output is deliberately minimal: **assign a leader + a set of waypoints for the leader**; a GNN-based low-level distributed control policy then follows the waypoints to escape deadlock. Both LLM and VLM planners beat grid-based planners (A*) on goal-reaching rate and computational time in complex environments; extensive experiments across MRS environments (abstract does not confirm hardware; appears simulation) — [arXiv:2404.06413](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.06413); [Semantic Scholar entry for earlier title](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/09498b36bb66169f05e6be614a5493576123b6ed)
- **LLMDR: LLM-Driven Deadlock Detection and Resolution in Multi-Agent Pathfinding** (arXiv 2503.00717, March 2025). Integrates LLM inference with *learned* MAPF policies plus prioritized planning: the LLM detects deadlocks and provides "customized resolution strategies." Improves success rates of learned MAPF models specifically in deadlock-prone scenarios; evaluated on standard MAPF benchmark maps with varying agent numbers; **simulation only**, code at github.com/ssbacc/llmdr-dhc (abstract-level) — [arXiv:2503.00717](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.00717)
- **LIDSA: Cognitive Arbitration for Signal-Free Autonomous Intersection Management** (arXiv 2605.12321, 2026). An LLM acts as **explicit central arbitration agent** at a signal-free intersection, reasoning over vehicle intent and the conflict-zone situation, and assigns **symbolic right-of-way roles: CLEAR, YIELD, SHARE, FOLLOW**; each outcome is passed to a **deterministic kinematic executor** that converts it to vehicle-specific speed advisories. Latency is handled by (a) a **Memoized Arbitration Table** for recurring conflict signatures and (b) **anticipatory arbitration** triggered before vehicles reach the conflict zone. Reported results (simulation, abstract-level): −89.1% mean control delay vs baselines, maintains Level-of-Service C while all non-LLM baselines degrade to LOS F; under near-saturated demand −93% mean waiting time and −60.6% peak queue vs fixed-cycle; −48.8% fuel; 86.2% intent satisfaction vs 61.2% best non-LLM — [arXiv:2605.12321](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.12321)
- **LLMLight: Large Language Models as Traffic Signal Control Agents** (arXiv 2312.16044; KDD 2025). LLM as decision agent for traffic signal control from a "knowledgeable prompt" of real-time traffic conditions; motivated by RL methods' poor generalization and lack of interpretability. Introduces **LightGPT**, a fine-tuned specialized backbone, to make the approach cost-effective (i.e., latency/cost of frontier models was a problem worth fine-tuning away). Evaluated on ten real-world/synthetic datasets against nine baselines and ten LLMs, plus fifteen human expert evaluations — [arXiv:2312.16044](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.16044); [KDD version](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3690624.3709379)
- **CoLMDriver: LLM-based Negotiation Benefits Cooperative Autonomous Driving** (arXiv 2503.08683, 2025). Claimed first full-pipeline LLM-based cooperative (V2V) driving system: an **LLM negotiation module under an actor-critic framework** (feedback from previous vehicle decisions refines the negotiation policy) plus an **intention-guided waypoint generator** converting negotiation outcomes into executable driving commands; latency handled by a **parallel pipeline** (negotiation runs alongside driving rather than blocking it). Introduces **InterDrive**, a CARLA benchmark of 10 challenging interactive V2V scenarios; +11% success rate vs existing approaches; **simulation only** — [arXiv:2503.08683](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.08683)
- **Vehicle-Infrastructure Multi-layer Cooperative Decision-making Framework** (arXiv 2503.16552, 2025). LLM-based negotiation of **passing sequences** at unsignalized intersections, both within and between vehicle groups (V2V+V2I), i.e., the LLM's output is an ordered crossing sequence rather than a single action (abstract-level) — [arXiv:2503.16552](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.16552)
- **RoCo: Dialectic Multi-Robot Collaboration with Large Language Models** (arXiv 2307.04738; ICRA 2024). Each robot is an LLM agent; robots negotiate sub-task plans in natural-language dialog; the proposed joint plan is checked by **environment validations (IK failures, collision checks)** and textual descriptions of validation errors are fed back into the next dialog round until a valid plan emerges. On RoCoBench, full dialog-with-feedback reaches success up to 0.95; ablations without feedback or history underperform — [project page](https://project-roco.github.io/); [arXiv:2307.04738](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2307.04738)
- **Co-NavGPT** (arXiv 2310.07937, 2023; updated as "…Using Vision Language Models," IEEE journal version 2025). VLM as **global planner** for multi-robot cooperative visual target navigation: merges sub-maps from multiple robots into a global map, encodes robot states + frontier regions, and the VLM **assigns frontiers to robots**. Outperforms baselines on HM3D without task-specific training; **validated in real-world scenarios on quadrupedal robots** (one of the few hardware results) — [arXiv:2310.07937](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.07937)
- **SMART-LLM** (arXiv 2309.10062; IROS 2024). LLM converts a high-level instruction into multi-robot task plans via staged **task decomposition → coalition formation → task allocation**, prompted with Python scripts encapsulating robot skills and environment details — [arXiv:2309.10062](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.10062)
- **Large Language Models for Multi-Robot Systems: A Survey** (arXiv 2502.03814, 2025). Taxonomy: high-level task allocation, mid-level motion planning, low-level action generation, human intervention. Names the field's key obstacles as **mathematical reasoning limitations, hallucination, latency, and lack of robust benchmarks** — [arXiv:2502.03814](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.03814)
- **Amazon DeepFleet** (2025). Foundation model (robot-movement pretrained, not an off-the-shelf LLM) predicting interactions of mobile robots in fulfillment centers; used to assign tasks and **route around predicted congestion**, reported ~10% efficiency gain — industry precedent that warehouse congestion is being attacked with foundation models, though by prediction rather than semantic arbitration — [Amazon Science blog](https://www.amazon.science/blog/amazon-builds-first-foundation-model-for-multirobot-coordination)
- **Reliability threat finding:** LLM-powered multi-robot fleets are vulnerable to **semantic manipulation / behavioral cascades** — a single accepted false world-state claim can trigger fleet-wide replanning, congestion, or apparent mission infeasibility (arXiv 2609.30523, 2026) — [arXiv:2609.30523](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.30523v1)
- Adjacent verified systems (decentralized coordination, not conflict-focused): LLM-Flock (arXiv 2505.06513), SayCoNav (arXiv 2505.13729), Compositional Coordination for Multi-Robot Teams with LLMs (arXiv 2507.16068) — [survey context](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.03814)

### Inferences
- Decision granularity across these systems clusters into three levels: **(1) one symbolic role/strategy per agent per cycle** (LIDSA's CLEAR/YIELD/SHARE/FOLLOW — the closest published analog to our yield/reverse/reroute/wait enum), **(2) leader + waypoint set** (Garg et al.), and **(3) negotiated ordered sequences/plans** (CoLMDriver, 2503.16552, RoCo). Our current single-strategy-per-analysis design sits at level 1; published precedent exists at levels 2–3.
- No paper reports the foundation model commanding actuators directly in a conflict-resolution setting; every system inserts a deterministic executor, learned low-level policy, or validation loop between model output and motion. This matches our M3 grounder + M4 safety layer decomposition.
- Latency mitigation is never "make the model faster in the loop" alone: it is caching of recurring conflict signatures (LIDSA MAT), anticipatory triggering before the conflict matures (LIDSA), parallel/asynchronous pipelines (CoLMDriver), or fine-tuning a small dedicated model (LightGPT). All four are compatible with a 0.5–2 Hz Layer-2 loop.

### Gaps
- I found no published head-to-head **hardware** study of LLM/VLM conflict arbitration for ground mobile robots in a warehouse setting; hardware evidence is limited to Co-NavGPT's quadruped exploration demo and BTGenBot's single-robot tasks. Our closed-loop hardware/Isaac results appear ahead of the published literature here.
- Exact model names/versions used in Garg et al. 2404.06413 and LLMDR are not stated in the abstracts; full-text reads needed to pin model classes.
- LIDSA's numbers come from its own abstract (2026 preprint, likely not peer-reviewed yet); no independent replication found.

## Q2: How do they represent the scene to the model, and what is known about positional/ordering biases in such prompts?

### Takeaway
Scene representations split into structured text state (traffic/MAPF/negotiation systems), merged occupancy/semantic maps encoded into prompts (Co-NavGPT), and marked-up images (set-of-mark style). Positional/ordering bias in VLM prompts is now well documented: option-order and image-order effects can flip a large fraction of predictions, which independently corroborates our own presentation-order finding.

### Cited Findings
- **Set-of-Mark (SoM) prompting** (Yang et al., arXiv 2310.11441, 2023): overlaying segmentation-derived alphanumeric marks/boxes on images "unleashes" GPT-4V grounding; zero-shot GPT-4V+SoM beat the SOTA fully fine-tuned model on RefCOCOg referring-expression tasks. Baseline GPT-4V without marks "can hardly produce accurate coordinates" for objects — [arXiv:2310.11441](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.11441)
- **SoM does not transfer to open models:** SoM generally *decreases* performance of LLaVA-based models, plausibly because it leans on OCR ability the open models lack — [alphaXiv discussion of 2310.11441](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2310.11441v2)
- **Multi-image position bias** (CVPR 2025, "Identifying and Mitigating Position Bias of Multi-image Vision-Language Models"): merely **swapping input-image order alters ~30% of predictions and moves accuracy by up to 10%**; even GPT-4V favors content at the extreme ends of the sequence. Proposes training-free SoFt Attention (SoFA, causal↔bidirectional attention interpolation) as mitigation — [CVPR 2025 paper](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2025/papers/Tian_Identifying_and_Mitigating_Position_Bias_of_Multi-image_Vision-Language_Models_CVPR_2025_paper.pdf)
- **MCQA selection bias in LVLMs** (arXiv 2509.16805, 2025): selection bias is driven by **both option-token identity and option position**, and **intensifies with task difficulty** and fine-grained options; inference-time logit debiasing mitigates without retraining — [arXiv:2509.16805](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.16805v1)
- Scene-to-prompt choices in the systems above: LLMLight uses a structured text "knowledgeable prompt" of real-time lane/queue conditions ([arXiv:2312.16044](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.16044)); Co-NavGPT aggregates multi-robot sub-maps into a unified global map and encodes robot states + frontier regions into the prompt ([arXiv:2310.07937](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.07937)); LIDSA feeds vehicle *intents* plus conflict-zone traffic state as text ([arXiv:2605.12321](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.12321)); Garg et al. compare text-state-only LLM vs image+text VLM on the same task ([arXiv:2404.06413](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.06413)).

### Inferences
- The CVPR 2025 (~30% prediction flips from ordering) and MCQA-bias results generalize our project's presentation-order finding (gpt-4o first-slot/robot_1 prior under id_asc): this is a systematic property of current VLMs, not an artifact of our harness. A prereg that randomizes presentation order and conditions on the draw is aligned with, and citable against, this literature.
- The "bias intensifies with difficulty / fine-grained options" result predicts our composite-command extension will be *more* order-sensitive than the current 4-way strategy enum, because the option space grows (which robot does which stage). Order randomization and identity-symmetric prompts become more, not less, important under prereg-v2.
- SoM's LLaVA failure warns that any planned migration from frontier APIs to local open VLMs may lose annotated-frame grounding; a text-state fallback path (which Garg et al. show works for deadlocks) is a cheap hedge.

### Gaps
- No paper was found that measures ordering bias specifically in *BEV/overhead annotated robot scenes* (as opposed to multi-image or MCQ inputs); our experiments appear to be the closest data point.
- I did not find follow-up work quantifying SoM mark-collision failure modes (overlapping markers, occlusion) — relevant to our STALE-on-overlap gotcha, but unsourced.

## Q3: What command/plan representations let a slow reasoner drive multi-stage maneuvers safely, and how is partial failure of a stage handled?

### Takeaway
Four representations recur: meta-action sequences (driving), behavior trees (robotics), LLM-written code/policies, and PDDL/temporal-logic plans with symbolic verification. Multi-stage safety comes from syntactic+semantic validation before execution and from reactive structures (BT ticks, failure interpreters, replanning triggers) rather than from trusting the sequence; partial-failure handling is the least mature part everywhere.

### Cited Findings
- **DriveVLM / DriveVLM-Dual** (arXiv 2402.12289; CoRL 2024): the VLM's planning output is a **meta-action sequence** — an ordered chain drawn from **17 meta-action types** (speed up, slow down, turn left, change lane, wait, …) annotated to "encompass a complete driving strategy" consistent with the future trajectory; a downstream module refines it into waypoints. DriveVLM-Dual is the canonical **slow-fast dual system**: VLM as slow deliberative reasoner, classical pipeline as fast real-time planner; deployed reasoning latency on OrinX reported around 410 ms with speculative decoding (~2.7× decode speedup) (abstract/HTML-level) — [arXiv:2402.12289](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.12289v4)
- **Asynchronous fast-slow inference:** "Think at 5 Hz, Act at 20 Hz" (arXiv 2607.15621, 2026) formalizes running a slow VLA reasoner at ~5 Hz while a fast actor runs at 20 Hz for closed-loop driving; similarly Fast-in-Slow (arXiv 2506.01953) and ETA "Efficiency through Thinking Ahead" (arXiv 2506.07725) — [arXiv:2607.15621](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.15621)
- **Behavior trees from LLMs:** BTGenBot (arXiv 2403.12761, 2024) generates BTs with ~7B local LLMs and validates via **static syntactic analysis → validation system → simulation → real robot**; BT outputs "can be inspected, parsed, and rejected if [they violate] the allowed syntax or action set" — [arXiv:2403.12761](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.12761); [GitHub](https://github.com/AIRLab-POLIMI/BTGenBot)
- **CommandSwarm** (arXiv 2605.07764, 2026): natural language → BT for **robotic swarms** with explicit **command-level safety filtering** before execution, benchmarked across contemporary open LLMs — [arXiv:2605.07764](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.07764)
- **BETR-XP-LLM** (arXiv 2409.13356, 2024): **automatic BT expansion at runtime** — an LLM resolves errors outside the planner's capabilities both during planning and during execution, i.e., stage failure triggers targeted tree repair rather than full replan — [arXiv:2409.13356](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.13356)
- **Multi-machine synchronization via BT flags:** LLM-based BT generation for construction machinery keeps planning restricted to known database parameters for safety and coordinates multiple machines through **LLM-generated synchronization flags** in the trees — a concrete mechanism for "A does X, then B does Y" sequencing across agents (arXiv 2602.01041, 2026) — [arXiv:2602.01041](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.01041)
- **Code-as-Policies** (Liang et al., ICRA 2023): LLM writes policy code composing perception outputs and parameterized control primitives, including feedback loops — the "program of maneuvers" end of the granularity spectrum — [project page](https://code-as-policies.github.io/)
- **Symbolic verification of LLM plans:** "Robot Planning via LLM Proposals and Symbolic Verification" (MAKE 2026) and a hybrid Sense–Plan–Code–Act line where the LLM produces PDDL, a heuristic planner produces a provably valid plan, a second LLM converts it to code, and the code is validated **syntactically by compilation and semantically in simulation** before execution — [MAKE 8(1):22](https://doi.org/10.3390/make8010022); [UniDomain, arXiv:2507.21545](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.21545v1)
- **Pre-execution plan verification:** VerifyLLM (arXiv 2507.05118, 2025) checks LLM task plans before execution; "Plug in the Safety Chip" (Yang et al., ICRA 2024) enforces temporal-logic safety constraints on LLM-driven robot agents as a bolt-on module — [VerifyLLM](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.05118); [Safety Chip PDF](https://h2r.cs.brown.edu/wp-content/uploads/yang24.pdf)
- **Precedent for multi-stage *conflict* commands specifically:** the intersection negotiation frameworks output ordered crossing sequences (passing order within/between vehicle groups, arXiv 2503.16552) and CoLMDriver's negotiation consensus is executed as intention-guided waypoints with actor-critic re-evaluation as the interaction unfolds ([arXiv:2503.08683](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.08683)); RoCo's dialog produces a joint multi-robot sub-task plan re-validated every round ([arXiv:2307.04738](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2307.04738)). I found **no paper where a VLM emits a labeled composite maneuver of the exact form "A reverses, then B yields, then ordered crossing" over a BEV warehouse scene** — the ingredients exist separately (symbolic per-agent roles: LIDSA; ordered sequences: 2503.16552; staged meta-actions: DriveVLM), but not that combination.

### Inferences
- The literature's convergent recipe for a slow reasoner driving multi-stage maneuvers: (1) constrain the output to a **closed vocabulary of stages** (meta-actions / BT leaves / symbolic roles) rather than free text or code; (2) validate the whole sequence **before** execution (syntax, action-set membership, feasibility simulation); (3) execute reactively, with each stage's pre/post-conditions checked by the fast layer, so partial failure surfaces as a stage-level event; (4) on stage failure, either re-invoke the reasoner with the failure context (RoCo, BETR-XP-LLM) or fall back to a safe default (stop/hold).
- Partial-failure handling is the weakest documented area: RoCo re-validates per dialog round, BETR-XP-LLM repairs trees, but none of the traffic/deadlock papers report what happens when stage 2 of an issued sequence becomes infeasible mid-execution. This is a defensible novelty claim for prereg-v2 if we specify stage-failure semantics explicitly.
- A composite command as a short ordered list of (robot_id, primitive, trigger-condition) triples is the minimal representation consistent with all four precedent families, and it keeps the M3 grounder's job to per-stage schema validation plus feasibility gating — no need for full BT or code generation at our scale (2–4 robots, 4–6 primitives).

### Gaps
- No quantitative comparison found of single-action vs sequence-emitting foundation-model planners on the *same* conflict task (i.e., no paper isolates the value of compositeness itself). Prereg-v2 could be the first controlled test of this.
- DriveVLM's 410 ms / speculative-decoding figures come from HTML/derived summaries; verify against the CoRL paper before quoting in a prereg document.

## Q4: Any work explicitly on LLM-driven deadlock *recovery* (not avoidance) in embodied multi-agent settings?

### Takeaway
Yes — two directly on-point systems (Garg et al. 2404.06413; LLMDR 2503.00717) treat the deadlock as already occurred and use the foundation model to break it, both via minimal symbolic interventions (leader+waypoints; per-agent resolution strategies/priorities) executed by lower-level policies. Both are simulation-only.

### Cited Findings
- **Garg et al. 2404.06413** is recovery, not avoidance: robots "become stuck despite low-level control policies," and the LLM/VLM is invoked *after* deadlock to assign a leader and waypoints that the GNN controller then follows to "evade the deadlock"; compared against grid-based planners on goal-reaching rate and compute time — [arXiv:2404.06413](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.06413)
- **LLMDR (2503.00717)** is detection *and* recovery layered on learned MAPF policies: the LLM detects that agents are deadlocked and issues customized resolution strategies with prioritized planning; gains concentrate in deadlock-prone maps — [arXiv:2503.00717](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.00717)
- **Multi-Robot Navigation in Social Mini-Games** (arXiv 2508.13459, 2025) provides a definitions/taxonomy paper for deadlock-prone mini-games (doorways, corridors, intersections) and surveys resolution algorithms — useful for situating our head-on and four-way-intersection scenario families in an established taxonomy — [arXiv:2508.13459](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.13459)
- Classical baseline lineage for comparison framing: "Deadlock Analysis and Resolution in Multi-Robot Systems" (arXiv 1911.09146) — the pre-LLM formal treatment — [arXiv:1911.09146](https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.09146)

### Inferences
- Both recovery papers give the model *more* authority than our current design in one dimension (waypoints = spatial content) but *less* in another (no named maneuver semantics like reverse/yield). Neither issues time-ordered multi-robot maneuver sequences; our prereg-v2 composite command would be novel relative to both.
- Both position the classical comparator as a search-based planner (A*/prioritized planning), consistent with our planned VLM-vs-classical framing.

### Gaps
- Neither recovery paper reports hardware validation, failure-mode taxonomies (hallucinated robot IDs, infeasible waypoints), or retry/timeout policies in the abstracts; full-text reads needed if we want to cite their grounding-failure rates.
- Found nothing on VLM-driven recovery from *collision-adjacent* states (overlapping/contacting robots), which our STALE-on-overlap scenario touches.

## Q5: What evaluation designs do these papers use that a pre-registered VLM-vs-classical comparison should note?

### Takeaway
The field evaluates on fixed scenario suites with paired classical/learned baselines and standard domain metrics; distinctive elements worth copying are RoCo's ablations of the feedback loop, LLMLight's multi-LLM + human-expert evaluation, and CoLMDriver's purpose-built benchmark of named interactive scenarios. None of the surveyed papers pre-registered anything; ordering/positional confounds are unaddressed in all of them.

### Cited Findings
- **RoCoBench** (RoCo): standardized tabletop multi-robot scenarios; metrics = success rate, environment steps, number of replans; **ablations remove dialog history and validation feedback** to isolate the mechanism (feedback-full dialog: up to 0.95 success; ablations markedly worse) — [project page](https://project-roco.github.io/)
- **InterDrive** (CoLMDriver): 10 named, deliberately interactive V2V CARLA scenarios; success-rate delta vs prior methods (+11%) — [arXiv:2503.08683](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.08683)
- **LLMLight**: ten real-world + synthetic traffic datasets, nine non-LLM baselines **plus ten different LLM backbones**, plus **fifteen human experts rating interpretability** — the only paper found that treats "which LLM" as an experimental factor at scale — [arXiv:2312.16044](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.16044)
- **LIDSA**: traffic-engineering metrics (mean control delay, Level of Service, waiting time, queue length, fuel, intent satisfaction) vs fixed-cycle and non-LLM adaptive baselines, including a **near-saturation stress condition** — [arXiv:2605.12321](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.12321)
- **LLMDR**: standard MAPF benchmark maps with varying agent counts; success rate of the underlying learned policy with/without LLM intervention — [arXiv:2503.00717](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.00717)
- **Garg et al.**: goal-reaching rate + computational time vs grid-based (A*-family) planners across environments of varying complexity; also compares LLM vs VLM input modality on the same task — [arXiv:2404.06413](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.06413)
- **BTGenBot**: staged validation ladder as evaluation design — static syntax analysis → validator → simulation → real robot — [GitHub](https://github.com/AIRLab-POLIMI/BTGenBot)

### Inferences
- A prereg-v2 design that (a) fixes a named scenario suite (our head-on family + four-way intersection), (b) pairs the VLM against a classical resolver on identical initial states, (c) treats model identity and presentation order as registered factors, and (d) ablates the composite command back to single-strategy mode would exceed the methodological standard of every paper found — none randomize or even report prompt ordering, and none pre-register.
- The Garg et al. LLM-vs-VLM modality comparison and LLMLight's multi-backbone factor both suggest registering backbone (gpt-4o vs Gemini ER-2) as a factor rather than a nuisance, which matches our existing two-backend setup.
- BTGenBot's validation ladder maps directly onto our stack: schema validation (M3) → feasibility gate → Isaac sim run → hardware; reporting attrition at each rung is a citable, precedented reporting format for grounding failures.

### Gaps
- No surveyed paper reports refusal rates, hallucinated-agent-ID rates, or per-call latency distributions for the foundation model in the loop — the failure-mode accounting our prereg would add appears unprecedented in this specific literature (good for novelty, bad for citable baselines).
- No pre-registered study of LLM/VLM robot coordination was found at all (searched implicitly across all queries; absence of evidence, but consistent).

## Design implications for the prereg-v2 composite command (synthesis)

### Takeaway
Published precedent supports upgrading from one-strategy-per-analysis to a short, closed-vocabulary, per-robot staged sequence — provided each stage remains individually groundable and vetoable, the sequence is validated as a whole before dispatch, and stage-failure semantics are specified up front. The exact combination (VLM, BEV warehouse scene, labeled composite maneuver, rule-based grounder, hard safety layer) has no published instance, so prereg-v2 would be a genuine first.

### Inferences
- **Representation.** Emit the composite as an ordered list of stages, each `(robot_id, primitive ∈ {reverse, yield/hold, proceed, reroute, wait}, completion-condition)` — the DriveVLM meta-action-sequence pattern restricted to our primitive set, with per-agent roles in the LIDSA CLEAR/YIELD/SHARE/FOLLOW spirit. Keep it JSON-schema'd Pydantic, not free text or code: every safety-positive precedent (BTGenBot, CommandSwarm, LIDSA) uses a closed vocabulary the validator can reject on.
- **Grounding.** Validate the *sequence*, not just each stage: membership of every robot_id in the current SceneState, per-stage feasibility (our existing swap-gate generalizes to a stage-reorder gate), and a whole-plan check that no stage's precondition is destroyed by an earlier stage. This mirrors RoCo's validate-then-feedback loop; on rejection, either fall back to the current single-strategy path or re-prompt once with the validator's error text (RoCo shows textual error feedback measurably improves the next proposal).
- **Execution and partial failure.** Let M4 execute stages sequentially with completion-conditions checked at Layer-1 rate; specify in the prereg exactly three stage-failure outcomes: (i) timeout → abort remaining stages and revert to safe hold, (ii) safety-layer veto mid-stage → abort + re-invoke M2 with failure context (precedented by BETR-XP-LLM's runtime repair and by our own failure-context branch), (iii) scene resolved early → cancel remaining stages. No published system specifies this; writing it down is itself a contribution.
- **Loop-rate fit.** The 0.5–2 Hz analysis loop matches the published slow-layer regimes (DriveVLM-Dual; "think at 5 Hz act at 20 Hz"), and a composite command *reduces* required reasoner call frequency (one call covers several stages), which is the direction LIDSA's anticipatory arbitration and memoization also push. A memoized table of (conflict signature → composite plan) is a cheap, precedented latency/robustness add-on worth mentioning as future work.
- **Bias controls.** Because option-space growth amplifies positional/selection bias (CVPR 2025 ~30% flips; MCQA bias grows with difficulty), prereg-v2 must carry forward randomized robot presentation order conditioned on the draw, and should consider identity-symmetric prompts; this is both internally motivated (our presentation-order finding) and now externally citable.
- **Evaluation.** Register: paired identical initial states for VLM-composite vs VLM-single-strategy vs classical resolver; backbone as a factor; and a BTGenBot-style attrition ladder (schema-valid → feasibility-passed → sim-completed → deadlock-resolved) as the primary grounding-failure metric — none of which any surveyed paper does, and all of which their designs individually precedent.
