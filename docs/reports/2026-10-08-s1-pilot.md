### Deviations

1. **The default mock never reaches grounding.** This was recorded in NOTES.md before any run.
   - With `BACKEND_ID=mock` and no `VGU_MOCK_DECISION_FILE`, `MockBackend` returns `congestion_detected: false`.
   - `ReasoningPipeline._run_from_result` returns at the `congestion_detected=false` terminal. That happens before `resolve_grounding_view`, before stage 2 and before the grounder (`swarm_orchestrator/reasoning_pipeline.py`).
   - So a mock episode exercises the triggers, M2 and the Arm B shadow, but produces no grounded command, no `grounding=` tag, no escort and no `backup_line_hold`. Its dispatch is effectively Layer-2-off.
   - The brief assumed M3 defaults would resolve through the dynamic view. Under this code path they cannot.
   - A scripted mock was rejected: fixed robot ids cannot match emergent stalls, a frozen script livelocks (VM trap 3), and it would be an unregistered condition.
   - Consequence: grounded commands and tags, escort deviation and `backup_line_hold` were measurable **only** in the live ER-2 episode, which the stop rule then cancelled.
2. **The five-stack gate run was also episode 1.** Same seed, overlay and run line; this saves one full episode of VM time.
3. **Hold-backstop departure samples.** Departures after a dwell were mostly blocked by the jams. The gate therefore also uses release → first GT motion as departure-latency samples: same five-stack load, same goal-accept → first-motion quantity.
4. **robot_1's release sim-time** is extrapolated through the scene clock map, because its release preceded M5's first scene. Its Δ is reported both ways.

### Bring-up

| Attempt | Outcome | Evidence |

The failure mode is the known lifecycle bond timeout under load, the same class as the 4-stack 1-in-3 rate. One retry recovered it, so the bring-up result is not systematic.

### RTF

| Measure | Value |
|---|---|
| M1 scene stamp / M5 wall clock, 29.6–600.4 sim-s | **0.1639** |
| Isaac lifetime (06:37:45 → 07:38:06, 600 sim-s) | 0.1657 |
| 60-row segments | 0.146–0.214 (0.214 is the first segment, before the jams) |
| Release window (Δ_event / 24 wall-s) | 0.182–0.193, mean 0.188 |
| Dwell windows (5 sim-s / dwell wall) | 0.147 and 0.176 |

REC_SEC=4800 was more than enough. Option B ended the M5 tail 45 s after Isaac exited, 3560 s into the tail. At RTF 0.164, an episode costs about 62 wall-min end to end (driver 06:37 → 07:39). Isaac's 3621 s lifetime already includes the bring-up.

### Stagger re-derivation

| Basis | RTF | 4 / RTF (wall-s) |
|---|---|---|
| Episode | 0.164 | 24.4 |
| Release window, event-stamped Δ | 0.188 | **21.3** |
| Release window, departure-based Δ | ≈ 0.197 | ≈ 20.3 |

The release window is the quantity Δ depends on, and it ran faster than the episode average. The episode later slowed to about 0.155: 216 analyses plus DWB spinning on stuck robots. So 24 wall-s produced Δ ≈ 4.5 sim-s, above the 4.42 bound. **Recommended provisional value: about 21 wall-s.**

At 21.3 wall-s the window holds only while the release-window RTF stays below 4.42 / 21.3 = 0.207. That margin is about 10%, against a measured release-window spread of 0.182–0.193. This is Open Question S1-10 made concrete: a wall-clock stagger stays fragile, and counting the stagger in sim-s would remove the problem (an M5 change).

The margin is about 4.7 s over every sample (6.6 s without the extrapolated one). In RTF terms the gate breaks at 5 / (45 − 6.18) ≈ 0.129, or ≈ 0.123 with a 4.3 s departure. The evidence is thin: n = 2 dwells, 1 unblocked dwell departure.

### The rack wedge (stop rule)

- **What happened.** robot_3 (S → Wp, released at 39.8) cut diagonally north-west across the S arm and the junction instead of turning at the arm mouth. By 48 sim-s it was at (−0.58, −0.53) against the SW rack corner (−0.6, −0.6). It then settled at (−0.616, −0.52) and stayed there until about 325 sim-s.
- **Proxy wedge (pre-registered):** clearance under 0.105 m held for more than 10 sim-s with less than 3 cm of displacement, flagged at 57.8 sim-s.
  - The centre was 0.071–0.084 m from the corner.
  - The Nav2 footprint is 0.14 × 0.18 m: half-width 0.09, half-diagonal 0.114.
  - The GT position range over 75–324 sim-s was under 1.6 cm in x and y.
- **Physical (commanded vs moved): pinned.**
  - Sampled between about 200 and 244 sim-s, `/robot_3/cmd_vel` was a continuous 0.152–0.171 m/s with −1.5 rad/s.
  - GT translation stayed under 1.6 cm, and heading held at −2.51 to −2.57 rad.
  - `/robot_3/odom` moved about 1 mm over 4.4 sim-s.
  - The only rotation was 2 Nav2 spin recoveries. At about 129 s the spin turned it to −0.93 rad, and DWB drove it straight back into the corner.
- **Nav2, robot_3:**
  - 11 × "Failed to make progress", 11 × goal failed and 13 × "Begin navigating".
  - 11 × "Timed out while waiting for action server to acknowledge goal request" (`compute_path_to_pose` and `follow_path`).
  - 446 × "Control loop missed its desired rate".
  - No BackUp, no DriveOnHeading, no collision-monitor stop.
  - Other robots: 0 ack timeouts, 23–41 missed loops.
- **Costmap. The costmap during the pin was not captured.** Both captured maps are the startup full maps that `transient_local` delivered (stamps 16.8 / 17.4 sim-s). `costmap_updates` were never recorded.
  - Global costmap (static + obstacle + inflation layers), within 0.114 m of the GT pin pose: 1 lethal, 4 inscribed (99), 3 at 80. The static layer puts the pin pose on lethal and inscribed rack-corner cells.
  - Local costmap (rolling window, obstacle + inflation only, **no static layer**), at the published footprint: 13 cells, 1 lethal (100), 2 inscribed (99), 1 at 80. These are obstacle marks from the spawn-time scan, not the rack's static geometry.
  - The local costmap logs "inflation radius (0.070) is smaller than the computed inscribed radius (0.080)".
- **GT `robot_static_contacts`: 0 rows.** Per A8 this field is unreliable, so it is not evidence of no contact.
- **Release.** At about 325 sim-s a spin recovery turned it away from the corner (−2.4 → −0.92 → +0.28 rad) and it drove out. It reached Wp at 349.5 (M5 arrival) and completed its dwell at 354.5.
- **Class.** This is the A7a "corner wedge" (controller-side local minimum at an aisle mouth, fix-invariant), reproduced at the S-arm/W-arm junction corner. Here it involves **physical contact**. It is registration-relevant for S1: every S↔W* and N↔W* leg turns that kind of corner.
- **Decision.** The stop rule fired on the physical pin, not on how long it lasted. Self-clearance after 46% of the horizon is recorded, but it is not grounds to resume. Resuming is the owner's call.
