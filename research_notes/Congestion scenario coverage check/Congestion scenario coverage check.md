# One doorway family closes three contract holes

The pre-registration's battery does **not** yet cover the scenario classes recent literature evaluates on, but the gap is narrower and cheaper to close than the decision contract suggests. Measured against the field's canonical episodic suites — the social-mini-games (SMG) survey's 9-class taxonomy ([arXiv:2508.13459](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.13459v4)), SocialGym 2.0's five default scenes ([arXiv:2303.05584](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.05584)), and the 3-scene hardware-validated doorway/hallway/intersection suite ([arXiv:2308.10966](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.10966)) — the current head-on-corridor family occupies exactly one cell (hallway/corridor-symmetry deadlock), the anomaly scenes cover the single-robot BARN-style blockage cell, and the intersection cell is present but classification-only. Half the decision contract has no elicitor: bottleneck and crowding among the four types, convoy and priority_override among the six strategies. The literature-grounded fix is **one capacity-one aperture geometry run in three compositions** — a 3-robot same-side bottleneck, a 3-robot urgency-asymmetric priority scene scored by GameChat's %CP metric ([arXiv:2503.12333](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.12333)), and a 4-robot counter-flow convoy scene ([arXiv:2410.14406](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.14406)) — all buildable on the existing 0.55 m aisle_barrier corridor, three-robot launch, start-delay, and ordered-release machinery. Crowding, by contrast, is a throughput-defined regime (density sweeps, hundreds of agents) that an episodic ≤4-robot, 300 s battery fundamentally cannot measure and should be scoped out with documentation, not approximated ([arXiv:2404.16162](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.16162)). Resolution-scoring the four-way intersection stays in prereg-v2, where the composite-command design work already lives.

## The battery fills one cell of a nine-cell episodic canon

The field's episodic congestion evaluations have converged on small fixed suites whose class structure is stable across communities. The 2026 Autonomous Robots SMG survey fixes nine canonical scenes — doorway, intersection, hallway, L-corner, blind corner, crowded traffic, parallel traffic, perpendicular traffic, circular traffic — and notes SMGs typically involve **2–4 agents** ([arXiv:2508.13459](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.13459v4)); SocialGym 2.0 ships Open, Doorway, Hallway, Intersection, Roundabout ([arXiv:2303.05584](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.05584)); the Chandra hardware-validated line uses just doorway, hallway, and corridor intersection across F1/10, Jackal, and Spot platforms ([arXiv:2308.10966](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.10966)). MAPF operationalizes the same distinctions structurally: corridor symmetry (two agents swapping through a narrow passage) is the formally identified pairwise worst case ([ICAPS 2020](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/ICAPS/article/view/6661)), room-with-narrow-doors maps encode the doorway/bottleneck class, and warehouse maps at rising density encode crowding ([movingai MAPF benchmarks](https://movingai.com/benchmarks/mapf.html)).

Mapped onto that canon, the current battery is strong on exactly one class and thin everywhere else. The two-robot head-on family **is** the corridor-symmetry primitive — well covered, arguably over-sampled. The queued-third-robot scene adds flow ordering but remains a head-on variant, and with only two same-direction robots it cannot discriminate convoy from ordinary yield (a pair passing after a yield is "just following"; platooning becomes a distinct behavior at ≥3 same-direction agents, per the DARS 2024 platoon study and the SMG survey's inference thresholds — [arXiv:2410.14406](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.14406)). The blocked-lane anomaly scenes are well precedented, but only in the single-robot DynaBARN sense of robustness to un-modeled blockers ([IEEE 2023](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/10018758/)). The four-way intersection is present but not resolution-scored. In suite *count* the battery is respectable — the field publishes hardware results on 3-scene suites — but the gap is **class diversity: no same-direction-flow scene, no capacity-one aperture scene, no density scene, and no scene with asymmetric agents**. Reviewer expectation matters here too: GameChat pushes its doorway to 3–5 agents and Tensor-Coord's LLM-planning success collapses from 100% at 2 agents to 60% at 4 ([arXiv:2606.16478](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.16478)), so an LLM/VLM-coordination study whose only resolution-scored scenes are two-robot-plus-bystander invites the objection that it dodged the N-scaling cliff the field has already quantified. The consolation is real, though: no published LLM/VLM-coordination paper scores a battery of distinct congestion *phenomena* at all, and no 2024–2026 VLM paper consumes an overhead annotated scene for multi-robot congestion decisions — the closest analog is Oxford's non-VLM CCTV-network fleet controller running 4 robots in real aisles ([arXiv:2606.06762](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.06762)), which is worth citing as independent validation of the evaluation setting.

## Three aperture compositions close the bottleneck, priority, and convoy holes before the freeze

All three recommended additions instantiate the same eliciting structure — a capacity-one aperture, the SMG survey's "capacity-one resource/bottleneck contention" trait ([arXiv:2508.13459](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.13459v4)) — with different robot compositions, which is what keeps the build inside existing machinery. The aperture rule under the 0.45 m floor: width strictly between (robot diameter + navigation margin) and (robot diameter + 0.45 m), so one robot fits but two abreast necessarily violate the pairwise floor. GameChat's canonical 0.4 m opening sits *below* the project's floor and must be widened; per its own results the eliciting property is single-robot admittance, not the absolute width ([arXiv:2503.12333](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.12333v2)). The existing 0.55 m × 2.0 m aisle_barrier corridor already satisfies this for the project's robots, so the geometry is verified hardware, not new construction.

| Addition | Eliciting geometry | Robots | Scored metric | Build fit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **B1 Bottleneck** (type: bottleneck) | 0.55 m corridor at one arm of the plus; three robots converge from the **same side and the perpendicular arm** (no opposing headings, so contention is pure sequencing) | 3 | Flow rate through the constriction + per-robot stop/wait time + makespan, alongside type-classification correctness | **High** — reuses aisle_barrier, three-robot launch, per-robot start delays; new work is one scenario YAML + a flow-metric scorer |
| **B2 Priority override** (strategy: priority_override) | Same aperture; 2–3 robots from opposing/perpendicular arms; one robot's task tagged urgent in the M5 registry, with arrival order set so default right-of-way and urgency disagree | 3 | **%CP** — does the higher-priority robot cross the conflict point first (GameChat: 100% with negotiation vs 50% chance) — plus resolution success | **High-medium** — reuses corridor + ordered-crossing release as the grounding; new work is an urgency field in the task registry surfaced to the M2 prompt, and a %CP scorer (touches M5 schema and prompt builder, both small) |
| **B3 Convoy** (strategy: convoy) | Same corridor as counter-flow: **three same-direction robots meet one opposing robot**; correct resolution is the single robot yields per-arm and the three transit nose-to-tail | 4 (3+1) | Strategy-label correctness + total transit time / flow rate vs an alternation baseline | **Medium** — grounding composes existing primitives (per-arm yield + ordered same-direction release), but requires extending three-robot launch machinery to four, at the robot cap |

The composition choices carry literature-specific justifications. B1 deliberately excludes opposing headings because with only two converging robots a doorway degenerates into the head-on family; three robots is the threshold at which sequencing/throughput (bottleneck) separates from pairwise yield (deadlock), matching GameChat's 3–5-agent doorway extension as the established ≥3-robot bottleneck shape ([arXiv:2503.12333](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.12333v2)). B2's asymmetry is essential rather than decorative: in a symmetric two-robot scene, priority_override is observationally identical to yield — one robot yielding *is* the other receiving priority — so the scene must make the default order and the granted order disagree; the eliciting shape follows GamePlan's multi-arm merges with asymmetric agents ([arXiv:2109.01896](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.01896)), and fielded narrow-aisle practice already resolves corridor deadlocks by exactly this asymmetric grant ("one corridor side yields and replans while the opposing side holds," [arXiv:2609.10400](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.10400)). B3's counter-flow composition comes from the platooning literature's positive condition — platoons beat greedy navigation in passive and counter-flow traffic — and its negative condition supplies a free classification probe: convoy is the *wrong* answer under perpendicular flow ([arXiv:2410.14406](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.14406)), so the existing intersection scene doubles as a convoy-should-not-fire negative test at zero build cost. One honesty note belongs in the prereg text: no 2024–2026 warehouse/AMR paper names "convoy" as a discrete commanded, scored strategy — the precedents are emergent platooning and EFLUX-style formation split/merge ([arXiv:2607.12050](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.12050)) — so B3 is a novelty claim wearing a coverage-shaped geometry, and should be registered as such. If the four-robot launch extension threatens the freeze, B3 is the one addition that can slip to v2, at the documented cost of leaving convoy elicitor-less in v1.

These scenes also import a metrics obligation the current battery does not carry: the field scores bottleneck scenes by throughput/wait at the aperture and priority scenes by %CP, **not** by the deadlock-resolution rubric ([arXiv:2308.10966](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.10966); [arXiv:2503.12333](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.12333)). Flow rate through the constriction is the one bottleneck metric that transfers cleanly to a 300 s episode; makespan and per-robot wait are the honest stand-ins for throughput. Registering these per-class metrics before the freeze is as important as the geometries, because scoring a bottleneck scene on the deadlock rubric would reproduce the coverage gap one level down.

## Crowding and the throughput regime should be scoped out, not simulated in miniature

The single largest body of recent congestion work cannot fit this study by construction, and the prereg is stronger saying so explicitly than gesturing at it. Lifelong-MAPF and fleet-scale warehouse papers — traffic-flow-optimized planning ([arXiv:2308.11234](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.11234)), guidance-graph optimization at up to 3,000 agents ([arXiv:2402.01446](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.01446)), the League of Robot Runners at 10,000 agents and 97.7% density ([arXiv:2404.16162](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.16162)), Amazon-affiliated fulfillment-center tuning ([arXiv:2608.21533](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.21533)), and WareRover's coupled scheduling+MAPF simulator ([arXiv:2602.13999](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.13999)) — all score **steady-state throughput on continuous task streams**, a quantity undefined on a single-conflict 300 s episode with ≤4 robots. The scoping sentence the prereg can register verbatim: *no episodic ≤4-robot battery can measure the throughput-defined congestion regimes that dominate warehouse literature; the study's construct is conflict-resolution quality at constrictions, for which the mini-games metric set (success/deadlock rate, time-to-goal, stop time, flow rate, %CP) is the established instrument.*

Crowding as a decision-contract *type* falls under the same scoping. The canonical episodic operationalization — CrowdNav circle-crossing, scored by freezing/timeout rates — conventionally uses five-plus agents so that no single pairwise conflict explains the slowdown ([arXiv:2403.01537](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.01537)); at the study's 4-robot cap, an "open-area crowding" scene at the plus-junction would be geometrically indistinguishable from the intersection scene and could not claim to represent the density regime. The defensible v1 position is to **keep crowding in the classification enum as a documented distractor label with no elicitor**, citing the ≥5-agent convention and the throughput dependence, rather than build a token scene that invites the miniature-regime objection. Three further exclusions make the battery defensible against the full SMG 9-class checklist: **blind corner and L-corner** test occlusion and limited observability, which the overhead camera architecture removes by design — an exclusion that is architecturally principled, not a gap; **roundabout/circular traffic** has no counterpart geometry in a rack warehouse with a plus-shaped free floor; and **livelock and queue spillback** should not be added as named phenomena because no 2024–2026 AMR paper scores either separately (oscillatory non-progress is folded into deadlock in the mini-games line), so they would be unanchored labels. One candid caveat belongs alongside the exclusion list: the field never argues any small suite is *sufficient* — suites are presented as representative, and the SocRATES line explicitly argues fixed suites are incomplete ([arXiv:2412.19595](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.19595v1)) — so the prereg should frame the battery as covering the canonical episodic classes reachable under its constraints, not as exhaustive.

## Prereg-v2 inherits the intersection, the robot-blocker anomaly, and the token crowding option

Three items are genuinely v2 material. First and firmly: **resolution-scoring the four-way intersection**. This is where the composite-command design work already lives, the intersection design study's verdict stands, and the recovery literature that governs it (A-DRIVE's cost-ordered single retreat then inside-first crossing, [A-DRIVE](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2204.04910)) prescribes machinery — cost-ranked yielder selection, staggered release, stage-failure semantics — that does not exist yet and should not be rushed into a freeze. The classification-only intersection stays in v1, where it additionally serves as B3's negative probe. Notably, once v2 lands, the intersection becomes the natural second priority_override elicitor: the auction/right-of-way literature (GamePlan, right-hand-rule families) targets exactly that geometry ([arXiv:2109.01896](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.01896)).

Second: the **robot-blocker anomaly** — a faulted, stationary robot narrowing an aisle with a queue forming behind it, the MAPF target-symmetry analogue and the bridge case between the anomaly and bottleneck types. WareRover's argument that realistic congestion evaluation must include stochastic execution failures is the citable motivation ([arXiv:2602.13999](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.13999)), and no episodic benchmark scores it, making it a novelty claim rather than a coverage requirement — which is exactly why it can wait. It is also the cheapest v2 item on the list: the object/transient counterfactual machinery already exists, and the only new element is a scripted robot stall, so it is the natural first scene to pull forward if the freeze slips or B3 slips out. The discriminating detail worth preserving in its design: the blocker is a *tracked* robot rather than an untracked obstacle, which is precisely what should separate an anomaly classification from a deadlock one in the M2 output. Third, and only if reviewers demand a crowding elicitor v1 declined to build: a 4-robot antipodal crossing at the plus-junction as an explicit *token-scale* Open-cell scene, registered with the disclaimer that it samples the taxonomy cell without representing the density regime. The SEAN/HuNavSim overtaking class (same-direction speed mismatch) remains unbuilt in both versions; it elicits nothing in the decision contract that B3's counter-flow composition does not, and its pairwise form is below the study's conflict threshold.

## Conclusion

The coverage check reframes what the freeze decision actually is: not "add scenarios until the taxonomy is full," but "make every label in the decision contract falsifiable." Right now the contract offers four types and six strategies while the battery can only ever elicit two types and four strategies — meaning a VLM that never outputs bottleneck, crowding, convoy, or priority_override would be indistinguishable from one that uses them correctly. The three aperture compositions fix this for three of the four dead labels at the cost of roughly one geometry, two scorers, and one launch extension, because they exploit a structural fact the literature converged on independently: doorway-class contention is one *shape* with many *compositions*, and composition (same-side vs counter-flow vs asymmetric-urgency) is what selects the correct strategy. Crowding is the one label that cannot be made falsifiable under the study's constraints, and the honest move — an enum entry documented as unelicitable at episodic scale, with the throughput-regime scoping sentence registered — converts a coverage weakness into an epistemic strength reviewers can verify. The residual exposure after these changes is not coverage but novelty accounting: convoy-as-commanded-strategy, the robot-blocker anomaly, and the eventual composite intersection command each have no published scored instance, so v1's prereg should mark them as firsts, because there will be no external baseline to compare their failure rates against.
