# Swarm Robotics Congestion Foundations (2015–2026, with canonical older roots)

Researcher notes for positioning a VLM-coordinated warehouse congestion-resolution thesis (2–5 robots, discrete structured conflicts) against the swarm-scale congestion tradition.

Verification flags used below:
- **VERIFIED** = abstract/landing page fetched or full citation returned directly by search this session.
- **PARTIALLY VERIFIED** = existence + title confirmed by search snippets; some bibliographic detail (authors/volume/pages) not independently fetched.
- **UNVERIFIED** = from model background knowledge only; double-check before citing in the thesis.

## KQ1: Canonical swarm congestion work — common-target congestion, aggregation/dispersion, ant-inspired traffic rules and lane formation

### Takeaway
The swarm tradition treats congestion as an *emergent density/flow pathology* of many identical agents sharing space, mitigated by local rules (probabilistic state machines, potential/vector fields, queue formers, idleness/retreat rules, learned traffic rules) — never by identifying and resolving a discrete named conflict between specific robots. The canonical lineage is Marcolino & Chaimowicz (ICRA 2008 / IROS 2009) → Marcolino et al. 2017 (ORCA deadlock-at-target result) → dos Passos, Duquesne & Marcolino 2022/2023 (throughput theory + SQF/TRVF algorithms).

### Cited Findings
**Common-target congestion lineage (the core canon):**
- Marcolino, L. S. & Chaimowicz, L., "No Robot Left Behind: Coordination to Overcome Local Minima in Swarm Navigation," Proc. IEEE ICRA 2008 — coordination mechanism for swarms stuck in local minima during group navigation. PARTIALLY VERIFIED — [verlab UFMG publication page](https://www.verlab.dcc.ufmg.br/old/publicacoes/publi/marcolino08robot)
- Marcolino, L. S. & Chaimowicz, L., "Traffic Control for a Swarm of Robots: Avoiding Target Congestion," Proc. IEEE/RSJ IROS 2009 (St. Louis) — the field's classic framing: when many robots converge on the same target, local traffic-control behaviors (coordination fields, local sensing) reduce congestion at the target. PARTIALLY VERIFIED — [verlab UFMG publication page](https://www.verlab.dcc.ufmg.br/old/publicacoes/publi/marcolino09traffic1). (A companion IROS'09 paper on avoiding *group* conflicts between swarms exists per the verlab author page — UNVERIFIED details.)
- Soriano Marcolino et al. (2017): showed that the standard ORCA reciprocal collision-avoidance algorithm "reaches an equilibrium where robots could not arrive at the target despite avoiding collisions" — i.e., pure collision avoidance produces a congestion deadlock at a common target. Finding VERIFIED as quoted in dos Passos et al. ([arXiv:2201.09337](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.09337)); the 2017 paper itself (believed: "Avoiding target congestion on the navigation of robotic swarms," Autonomous Robots, 2017) is UNVERIFIED as to exact venue/volume — check before citing.
- dos Passos, Y. T., Duquesne, X. & Marcolino, L. S., "On the throughput of the common target area for robotic swarm strategies — extended version," [arXiv:2201.09335](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.09335) (2022). VERIFIED (abstract fetched). Introduces "common target area throughput" as the efficiency measure and proves the **asymptotic throughput is finite** while arrival-time-per-robot tends to infinity; analyzes three theoretical strategies: (1) parallel queues toward the target, (2) hexagonal packing through a corridor, (3) multiple curved trajectories touching the target boundary. (A version of this work appeared as "The throughput of the common target area..." in a journal — UNVERIFIED which; the companion algorithms paper's venue is confirmed below.)
- dos Passos, Y. T., Duquesne, X. & Marcolino, L. S., "Congestion control algorithms for robotic swarms with a common target based on the throughput of the target area," **Robotics and Autonomous Systems, vol. 159** (2023), [arXiv:2201.09337](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.09337). VERIFIED (venue via search result). Two algorithms, both built on artificial potential fields: **Single Queue Former (SQF)** — forms one queue into the target area — and **Touch and Run Vector Fields (TRVF)** — curved vector-field trajectories that touch the boundary of the circular target region. SQF "significantly outperforms all other algorithms for a large number of robots or when the circular target region radius is small." — [search result summary of arXiv:2201.09337](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.09337v2)

**Aggregation/dispersion congestion (Şahin lineage):**
- Soysal, O. & Şahin, E., "Probabilistic aggregation strategies in swarm robotic systems," Proc. IEEE Swarm Intelligence Symposium (SIS) 2005. VERIFIED existence — [METU AVESIS record](https://avesis.metu.edu.tr/yayin/fe72043b-02cb-410b-a8d9-74e849986738/probabilistic-aggregation-strategies-in-swarm-robotic-systems); [swarm-bots.org record](https://www.swarm-bots.org/index.php@main=2&sub=210&id=695.html). Aggregation behavior = combination of four basic behaviors (obstacle avoidance, approach, repel, wait) in a three-state finite state machine with two probabilistic transitions; performance measured by two metrics as a function of transition probabilities, simulation steps, and arena size.
- Follow-on METU work modeling self-organized aggregation macroscopically ("Modeling Self-Organized Aggregation in Swarm Robotic Systems", and a Turkish J. Electrical Eng. & Computer Sciences 15(2) 2007 paper comparing evolutionary vs probabilistic aggregation control). PARTIALLY VERIFIED — [TÜBİTAK journal vol 15 iss 2](https://journals.tubitak.gov.tr/elektrik/vol15/iss2/6/); [AVESIS record](https://avesis.metu.edu.tr/yayin/962b4e1f-a4d1-415f-8724-8299f67014d8/modeling-self-organized-aggregation-in-swarm-robotic-systems). Note: this lineage measures *aggregation performance* (cluster sizes/time), not congestion per se; it is the mechanism toolkit (probabilistic FSMs) the later congestion work reuses.

**Ant-inspired traffic rules and lane formation:**
- Couzin, I. D. & Franks, N. R., "Self-organized lane formation and optimized traffic flow in army ants," Proc. R. Soc. Lond. B 270 (2003) — the biological foundation: army ants form a three-lane structure (outbound outer lanes, returning center lane) from local interaction rules; the standard citation for lane formation in bidirectional swarm flow. VERIFIED title — [Konstanz repository record](https://kops.uni-konstanz.de/entities/publication/c505c71f-7270-438e-9858-fe0474b767b1/full); [PDF mirror](https://www.sccs.swarthmore.edu/users/08/bblonder/phys120/docs/couzin.pdf)
- Ant traffic strategies that transfer to robots: platoon formation, quasi-constant speed, and **no overtaking** avoid jams even at high density (studied for ant→CAV/robot-swarm transfer). PARTIALLY VERIFIED — [Transportation Research Interdisciplinary Perspectives, Jan 2025 (DOAJ)](https://doaj.org/article/3430bbb1212a4724b845e935cf340e98); [Pugno lab PDF](https://pugno.dicam.unitn.it/NP_PDF/654-JTRIP-ANTI-JAM_solution.pdf)
- "Emerging robot swarm traffic" (Sheffield Hallam repository): asks whether traffic of robots commuting to/from a base station should be explicitly organized or allowed to self-organize; contrasts army-ant three-lane patterns with human-pedestrian trail formation. PARTIALLY VERIFIED (title + content summary only; authors/venue not fetched) — [SHURA record](https://shura.shu.ac.uk/4189/)
- Aguilar, J. et al., "Collective clog control: Optimizing traffic flow in confined biological and robophysical excavation," **Science**, 17 Aug 2018 (vol. 361) — fire ants + robophysical diggers in narrow tunnels: digging throughput is optimized by **individual idleness and retreating** (unequal workload distribution); idleness reduces flow-stopping clog frequency, selective retreating reduces cluster dissolution time. The flagship "congestion as granular clogging" paper. VERIFIED — [Goldman lab PDF](https://crablab.gatech.edu/pages/publications/pdf/science-2018.pdf); [supplement](https://goldmanlab.gatech.edu/pages/publications/pdf/aan3891_Aguilar_SM.pdf)

### Inferences
- The mechanisms are uniformly **identity-free and local**: probabilistic FSM transitions, potential/vector fields, queue-forming fields, idleness/retreat probabilities, lane conventions. No mechanism in this canon reasons about *which* robot is in conflict with *which* — exactly the gap a 2–5-robot semantic resolver (VLM naming a conflict type and assigning roles) occupies.
- The ORCA-equilibrium result (Marcolino 2017) is a strong citable bridge: even the swarm literature concedes that reactive collision avoidance alone produces unresolvable congestion, motivating a higher-level mechanism — in their case a global vector-field redesign, in ours a semantic Layer-2.
- SQF (force everyone into one queue) is the swarm-scale analogue of our "convoy" strategy; TRVF/touch-and-run is an analogue of "reroute around the contested region." Useful for mapping our strategy enum onto swarm results.

### Gaps
- Exact bibliographic details of Marcolino et al. 2017 (Autonomous Robots?) and the second IROS'09 paper not fetched — verify via DBLP ([Marcolino DBLP](https://dblp.org/pid/312/5916) lists dos Passos; Marcolino has his own entry) before quoting in the thesis.
- Pheromone-robot implementations (e.g., physical stigmergy with light/phi-pheromones, Garnier-style) were not covered this session; if needed, a targeted pass on "artificial pheromone robot swarm traffic" is required.

## KQ2: Congestion METRICS the field uses

### Takeaway
The dominant, citable quantitative instruments are (i) **asymptotic throughput of a common target area** (robots/second through a bounded region as t→∞; dos Passos et al.), (ii) **task-level flow/throughput** for lifelong warehouse systems (tasks completed per unit time), and (iii) **clogging statistics** (clog frequency, cluster dissolution time) from the granular-flow tradition. These map cleanly onto oracle cost tables: per-conflict throughput, waiting/delay time, and a stall/clog indicator.

### Cited Findings
- **Common target area throughput**: proposed explicitly "for evaluating the access efficiency of a common target area as the number of robots in the swarm rises"; key theoretical property: throughput of a target region with a limited area as t→∞ (**asymptotic throughput**) is **finite**, in contrast to arrival-time-per-number-of-robots, which diverges — so throughput is the scale-stable metric. VERIFIED — [arXiv:2201.09335](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.09335), [arXiv:2201.09337](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.09337) (RAS vol. 159, 2023). The theory paper derives throughput for parallel-queue, hexagonal-packing-corridor, and curved-trajectory (touch-and-run) strategies; exact closed forms are in the paper body (not fetched — pull the PDF when building the cost table).
- **Lifelong-MAPF throughput**: "throughput" = number of tasks/goals completed per timestep in continuous-assignment warehouses; the standard objective optimized by guidance-graph work. VERIFIED usage — [Guidance Graph Optimization, arXiv:2402.01446](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.01446) (IJCAI 2024 per [proceedings PDF](https://ijcai.org/proceedings/2024/0035.pdf)); GRAND reports "+up to 10% throughput over existing schedulers" on congested warehouse benchmarks up to 500 agents — [arXiv:2512.03194 review](https://www.themoonlight.io/en/review/grand-guidance-rebalancing-and-assignment-for-networked-dispatch-in-multi-agent-path-finding)
- **Clogging metrics**: frequency of flow-stopping clogs and cluster dissolution time, borrowed from dense particulate/granular-ensemble physics; optimized via idleness and retreat probabilities. VERIFIED — [Aguilar et al., Science 2018](https://crablab.gatech.edu/pages/publications/pdf/science-2018.pdf)
- **Aggregation performance metrics** (precursor style): Soysal & Şahin measure aggregation quality via two metrics over transition probabilities/arena size (expected cluster size/ratio style metrics). PARTIALLY VERIFIED — [SIS 2005 record](https://avesis.metu.edu.tr/yayin/fe72043b-02cb-410b-a8d9-74e849986738/probabilistic-aggregation-strategies-in-swarm-robotic-systems)
- **Congestion as scalability loss**: Soma et al. 2023 quantify congestion indirectly as degradation of collective-decision/sampling performance with density (communication congestion + movement congestion), up to 150 robots. VERIFIED — [arXiv:2307.08568](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.08568)

### Inferences
- For the thesis's oracle cost tables, the defensible adoption is: **per-aperture/per-junction throughput** (robots/s through the contested region, the dos Passos object), **makespan/waiting-time delta vs. an uncontested baseline**, and a **binary stall/clog event** (Aguilar-style). Citing dos Passos et al. gives the throughput definition a swarm-literature pedigree; citing lifelong-MAPF gives the task-throughput pedigree at warehouse scale.
- No standard "congestion index" exists in swarm robotics; papers either use throughput/flow, time-based penalties, or domain proxies (decision time, sampling coverage). Stating this absence in the thesis is safe if phrased as "no single consensus congestion index."

### Gaps
- Exact closed-form throughput formulas (corridor hexagonal packing rate f(s, v, d), touch-and-run rate f(K)) were not extracted — fetch the [arXiv:2201.09335 PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2201.09335) when writing the metrics section.
- Waiting-time-at-intersection metrics for robot swarms (as opposed to AGV/intersection-management literature) were not found in this pass; the intersection-management literature (AIM, Dresner & Stone) is adjacent but not swarm — flagged as out of this note's scope.

## KQ3: Recent (2023–2026) swarm congestion work — learned policies, mean-field, central/semantic coordination

### Takeaway
Recent work moves in two directions: *learned environment-centric traffic rules* (GNN/RNN policies that modulate flows, up to a few hundred agents) and *mean-field/PDE density control* (congestion as a density field to be steered). Central coordination exists only in these aggregate, non-semantic forms — a learned guidance field or an optimal density controller — not as scene-level reasoning about discrete conflicts.

### Cited Findings
- Zhang, X., Gao, X., Wu, K., Pan, Z., "Learning Neural Traffic Rules," 2023, [arXiv:2312.01498](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.01498) (RA-L per abstract page). VERIFIED (fetched). Environment-centric Graph Recurrent Neural Network learns "a set of traffic rules to coordinate a vast group of unintelligent agents": the environment is segmented into blocks and the GRNN modulates agent velocities per region; agents only compute local collision-free velocities. Handles **up to 240 agents in real time**; trained via imitation and RL; "resolving agent congestion, closely resembling real-world traffic regulations."
- Soma, K., Vardharajan, V. S., Hamann, H., Beltrame, G., "Congestion and Scalability in Robot Swarms: a Study on Collective Decision Making," 2023, [arXiv:2307.08568](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.08568). VERIFIED (fetched). Two congestion types — **communication congestion and movement congestion** — evaluated across honey-bee-inspired, stigmergy-based, and division-of-labor coordination in a physics simulator with **up to 150 robots**; division of labor + versioned local communication scales best by minimizing congestion. Explicitly challenges the assumption that decentralized = scalable.
- Sinigaglia, C., Manzoni, A., Braghin, F., Berman, S., "Robust optimal density control of robotic swarms," **Automatica** (2025), [arXiv:2205.12592](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.12592). VERIFIED venue via ASU lab PDF — [Automatica 2025 PDF](https://faculty.engineering.asu.edu/acs/wp-content/uploads/sites/33/2025/01/Sinigaglia-Automatica-2025-Robust-optimal-density-control-of-robotic-swarms.pdf). Mean-field model: swarm as a continuum density governed by an advection–diffusion PDE with bilinear velocity-field control; computes a robot velocity field driving the swarm to a target equilibrium density with proven stability under perturbations. Congestion here = undesired density concentration, controlled globally but without any per-robot or per-conflict semantics.
- Communication-free DRL collective navigation for UAV swarms (LiDAR-only implicit leader-follower, 5-UAV real-world transfer), 2026 — [arXiv:2601.13657](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.13657). PARTIALLY VERIFIED (search snippet only). Representative of the learned-local-policy direction; congestion is not its explicit objective.
- RL + collision-control hybrids that let robots learn to adjust preferred speeds under agent congestion exist in this space. UNVERIFIED specifics (no single paper pinned this session).

### Inferences
- Even the newest "centralized" swarm congestion mechanisms (GRNN traffic rules, mean-field density controllers, guidance graphs) operate on **aggregate flow representations** — region velocities, edge weights, density fields. None performs scene understanding, conflict classification, or strategy selection per conflict. This is the cleanest 2023–2026 contrast for the thesis's Layer-2 claim.
- The Soma et al. communication-vs-movement congestion split is a useful taxonomy to cite when arguing that our setting isolates *movement* congestion in structured layouts.

### Gaps
- No dedicated 2023–2026 survey of "swarm congestion" specifically was found (swarm navigation surveys exist but were not fetched); if the thesis needs a survey citation, a targeted pass on Swarm Intelligence journal / ANTS proceedings tables of contents is still needed.
- ANTS conference (ANTS 2022/2024) proceedings were not individually swept; coverage here is arXiv/ICRA/IROS/journal-weighted.

## KQ4: Warehouse/AGV swarm-scale congestion (100+ robots) framed as emergent flow

### Takeaway
The 100+-robot warehouse literature (lifelong MAPF) treats congestion as an emergent flow property of the whole fleet and mitigates it with global, aggregate instruments — optimized guidance graphs, learned rebalancing distributions — evaluated purely by fleet throughput. Conflicts are prevented wholesale by the planner, not detected and semantically resolved.

### Cited Findings
- Zhang, Y. et al., "Guidance Graph Optimization for Lifelong Multi-Agent Path Finding," **IJCAI 2024**, [arXiv:2402.01446](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.01446), [IJCAI proceedings PDF](https://ijcai.org/proceedings/2024/0035.pdf). VERIFIED. Optimizes edge weights of a "guidance graph" (a versatile representation of global movement guidance) to raise lifelong-MAPF throughput; scales to maps of 93×91 with **up to 3,000 agents**; motivated by "automated warehouses in which hundreds of robots are continually assigned new tasks."
- GRAND: "Guidance, Rebalancing, and Assignment for Networked Dispatch in Multi-Agent Path Finding" (2025), [arXiv:2512.03194](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.03194v2). PARTIALLY VERIFIED (review-site summary). GNN policy trained by RL outputs a desired **distribution of free agents** over an aggregated warehouse graph, converted to region-to-region rebalancing via min-cost flow; up to **500 agents**, **+10% throughput** on congested warehouse benchmarks, real-time.
- Lifelong LaCAM with local guidance for lifelong MAPF (2026), [alphaxiv record for arXiv:2605.16855](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2605.16855.md). PARTIALLY VERIFIED (title only) — further evidence the frontier is guidance-field-style congestion shaping.
- Wurman, P., D'Andrea, R., Mountz, M., "Coordinating Hundreds of Cooperative, Autonomous Vehicles in Warehouses," AI Magazine 29(1), 2008 (the Kiva Systems paper) — the founding industrial citation for the 100+-robot warehouse regime. UNVERIFIED this session (background knowledge; low risk, but confirm volume/pages).
- Li, J. et al., "Lifelong Multi-Agent Path Finding in Large-Scale Warehouses" (RHCR), AAAI 2021, arXiv:2005.07371 — the standard windowed-replanning throughput baseline that guidance-graph papers improve upon. UNVERIFIED this session (background knowledge; confirm before citing).

### Inferences
- The warehouse-swarm literature and our thesis sit at opposite ends of a scale axis: lifelong MAPF *prevents* congestion with global plans over hundreds of robots and measures aggregate throughput; our setting *resolves* an already-manifest, localized structured conflict (2–5 robots) with semantic reasoning. Positioning sentence: swarm/MAPF treats congestion as a flow to be shaped; we treat it as an event to be understood.
- Guidance graphs and GRAND's desired-agent-distribution are the closest the field gets to "central semantic coordination" — but the semantics are purely spatial/statistical (edge weights, densities), with no scene understanding, conflict typing, or natural-language-grounded strategy selection.

### Gaps
- No swarm-robotics (strictly decentralized, 100+ physical robots) study was found that frames *warehouse* congestion as emergent flow outside the MAPF/guidance tradition; physical-testbed swarm traffic at that scale appears absent from this pass (Kilobot-scale testbeds address aggregation, not warehouse flow).

## KQ5: Does ANY swarm-congestion work use vision-language or semantic scene reasoning?

### Takeaway
No. Across the common-target, clogging, learned-traffic-rule, mean-field, and lifelong-MAPF congestion literatures found this session, none uses vision-language models or semantic scene reasoning for congestion. The nearest neighbors are VLM-guided *swarm navigation* systems (SwarmVLM, ImpedanceGPT) that tune control parameters from scene semantics — but they do not define, measure, or resolve congestion, and they do not engage the swarm congestion metrics tradition. The absence claim holds, with a mandatory SwarmVLM carve-out.

### Cited Findings
- "SwarmVLM: VLM-Guided Impedance Control for Autonomous Navigation of Heterogeneous Robots in Dynamic Warehousing," 2025, [arXiv:2508.07814](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.07814v1). VERIFIED existence. VLM + RAG select impedance-control parameters for a UAV-leader / ground-robot-follower pair in dynamic warehouse navigation; 92% success over 12 real-world trials; 80% accuracy in object detection and impedance-parameter selection under optimal lighting. **Navigation/obstacle semantics, not congestion**: no throughput, flow, waiting-time, or conflict-resolution objective, and only a leader–follower pair rather than inter-robot conflict.
- "ImpedanceGPT: VLM-driven Impedance Control of Swarm of Mini-drones for Intelligent Navigation in Dynamic Environment," 2025, [arXiv:2503.02723](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.02723v1). VERIFIED existence. Same group/pattern: VLM adjusts impedance parameters for a mini-drone swarm around obstacles — again no congestion framing.
- "Multimodal Large Language Models-Enabled UAV Swarm: Towards Efficient and Intelligent Autonomous Aerial Systems," 2025, [arXiv:2506.12710](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.12710). PARTIALLY VERIFIED (title/abstract-level). Vision/MLLM-enabled UAV swarm architecture proposal (missions like wildfire response); architectural survey, not a congestion study.
- Negative evidence from the congestion canon itself: the mechanisms in every congestion paper found (SQF/TRVF potential fields — [arXiv:2201.09337](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.09337); idleness/retreat — [Science 2018](https://crablab.gatech.edu/pages/publications/pdf/science-2018.pdf); GRNN regional traffic rules — [arXiv:2312.01498](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.01498); PDE density control — [arXiv:2205.12592](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.12592); guidance-graph edge weights — [arXiv:2402.01446](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.01446)) are all geometric/statistical; none ingests images or language, and none performs semantic conflict classification.

### Inferences
- The thesis's absence claim should be phrased precisely: "to our knowledge, no work in the swarm-robotics congestion tradition (common-target throughput, clogging, traffic-rule learning, mean-field density control, lifelong-MAPF guidance) employs vision-language or semantic scene reasoning to detect or resolve congestion." The SwarmVLM/ImpedanceGPT line must be carved out explicitly as VLM-for-navigation-parameters, not VLM-for-congestion-resolution — consistent with the existing claim-1 carve-out already in the project's paper-folder review.
- Conversely, the swarm literature gives us the *measurement* vocabulary (asymptotic throughput, clog frequency, task throughput) that the VLM-coordination literature lacks — adopting dos Passos-style throughput for our oracle cost tables lets the thesis bridge the two traditions rather than merely contrast them.

Recommend a final pre-submission check on "LLM swarm congestion," "foundation model swarm traffic," and the ANTS 2024 proceedings.
