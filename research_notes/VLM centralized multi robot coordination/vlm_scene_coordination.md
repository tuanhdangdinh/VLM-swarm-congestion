# Vision-Language Models (Image-in-the-Loop) for Multi-Robot and Traffic Coordination from Camera Scenes (2022–2026)

Scope: systems where a VLM consumes an actual scene image (overhead/top-view, roadside, or annotated frame) and makes coordination, congestion, deadlock, or traffic-control decisions for multiple agents. Text-only LLM planners excluded (covered by another researcher). Prepared 2026-10-01.

## Which published systems feed real scene images to a VLM for multi-agent / traffic decisions?

### Takeaway
A small but fast-growing cluster exists. The closest precedent to a VLM-as-centralized-congestion-planner is Garg et al.'s hierarchical framework (MIT-REALM/ASU), where a VLM is queried **reactively on deadlock detection** with a **top-view image** of the observed environment and outputs a leader assignment plus waypoints for 5–50 robots. In traffic, VLMLight and VLALight put intersection camera images directly into the signal-control loop. Notably, the only *field-deployed* warehouse multi-robot-from-cameras system (Oxford, 2026) deliberately uses classical perception, not a VLM — leaving the VLM-overhead-warehouse niche open.

### Cited Findings

**Multi-robot deadlock/coordination (closest precedents):**
- "Foundation Models to the Rescue: Deadlock Resolution in Connected Multi-Robot Systems" — Garg, Zhang, Arkin, Fan (MIT), arXiv:2404.06413, 2024. Hierarchical framework: foundation-model high-level planner resolves deadlocks by assigning a leader + waypoints to a connected MRS; GNN-based low-level control executes. VLMs "perform better in terms of the goal-reaching rate and computational time for complex environments" vs grid-based planners — [arXiv:2404.06413](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.06413)
- Extended/final version: "Online Motion Planning for Connected Multi-Robot Systems using Vision Language Models as High-level Planners" — Garg, Nair, Zhang, Arkin, Fan; Proceedings of Machine Learning Research (vol TBD), 2026 (PMLR header "vol vvv:1–21, 2026"). Key mechanisms read directly from the PDF: (a) VLM used **reactively, not proactively** — triggered only when a deadlock is detected, so "the VLM does not lead to a violation of safety as it is taken care of by the provably safe low-level control policy"; (b) deadlock detection is velocity-based: average MRS speed below threshold δ_v while average distance-to-goal exceeds δ_d; (c) "When a deadlock is detected, a VLM is prompted to generate a set of deadlock-resolving navigation waypoints for the leader, conditioned on a top-view image-based description of the so far observed environment"; (d) experiments vary 5–50 agents; VLM planners beat LLM-only planners and A* on goal-reaching rate within a time budget; (e) they explicitly pre-evaluate the "spatial reasoning capability of various vision- and text-based foundation models by evaluating their ability in locating the obstacles correctly and generating viable waypoints in the free space"; (f) leader-follower hierarchy for large teams; (g) contrasts itself with prior work that converts images to text for LLMs: "in contrast, our work directly uses the output of a VLM for planning" — [PDF, mit-realm.github.io](https://mit-realm.github.io/VLM-gcbfplus-website/files/Online_planning_using_VLMs.pdf); project site [VLM-gcbfplus-website](https://mit-realm.github.io/VLM-gcbfplus-website/). Environment state passed as base64 JPEG top-view with agent locations, goals, and LiDAR-observed obstacles — [OpenReview copy](https://openreview.net/pdf?id=vz1p8TRRAb)
- "Safe Multi-Robot Coordination via VLM-LLM Reasoning and Reachability Analysis" — arXiv:2609.27816, 2026. Centralized safety-aware framework for a heterogeneous team (vision-capable quadruped + camera-less vehicle); a VLM interprets the visual stream on a central server (MQTT), and extracted semantics are mapped into conservative metric constraints for reachability-based planning — [arXiv:2609.27816](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.27816)
- "COMRES-VLM: Coordinated Multi-Robot Exploration and Search using Vision Language Models" — arXiv:2509.26324, 2025. VLM-coordinated multi-robot exploration/object search in unknown indoor environments — [arXiv:2509.26324](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.26324)
- "Multi-Agent Robotic Control with Onboard Vision-Language Models" — Rachwał et al., arXiv:2607.07403, accepted PAAMS 2026. Compact onboard VLMs (3–20B) in a warehouse sim; a "Megamind" orchestration agent manages decision-making across specialized agents (safety inspection, maintenance, search, package QC); fully-onboard MAS shown viable vs cloud — [arXiv:2607.07403](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.07403). Note: multi-*agent software* orchestration per robot more than fleet-level spatial coordination.

**Traffic signal control / traffic scenes (image in the loop):**
- "VLMLight: Traffic Signal Control via Vision-Language Meta-Control and Dual-Branch Reasoning" — Wang, Chen, Pang, Cai, Chen, Kan, Pun; arXiv:2505.19486, 2025. First image-based TSC simulator with multi-view visual perception at intersections; safety-prioritized meta-controller picks between fast RL policy (routine traffic) and a multi-agent LLM reasoning branch (critical cases); −65% emergency-vehicle waiting time vs RL-only with <1% routine-performance degradation — [arXiv:2505.19486](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.19486)
- "VLALight: A Vision-Language-Action Model for Traffic Signal Control" — arXiv:2609.36934, 2026. Claimed first end-to-end VLA for TSC from multi-view roadside videos; outperforms transportation-based, RL-based, and LLM/VLM-based baselines on seven real-world traffic-flow datasets across three urban networks — [arXiv:2609.36934](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36934)
- "VLALight: Lightweight Vision-Language-Action Models for Emergency-Aware Traffic Signal Control" — arXiv:2609.30709, 2026. Lightweight end-to-end mapping from combined directional camera views + signal-phase text to discrete signal actions — [arXiv:2609.30709](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30709)
- "GPT-4V as Traffic Assistant: An In-depth Look at Vision Language Model on Complex Traffic Events" — arXiv:2402.02205, 2024. GPT-4V on real traffic incident videos; strong cognition/reasoning on classic events, but notable failure cases in understanding — [arXiv:2402.02205](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.02205)
- "CoT-VLM4Tar: Chain-of-Thought Guided Vision-Language Models for Traffic Anomaly Resolution" — arXiv:2503.01632, 2025. VLM looks at traffic scenes and issues anomaly-resolution actions via CoT — [arXiv:2503.01632](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.01632)
- "On the Road with GPT-4V(ision): Early Explorations of Visual-Language Model on Autonomous Driving" — arXiv:2311.05332, 2023. Foundational capability study: GPT-4V strong at scene understanding, causal reasoning, intention recognition in driving scenes, but documented weaknesses in "direction discernment, traffic light recognition, vision grounding, and spatial reasoning" — [arXiv:2311.05332](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.05332v2)

**Overhead/external-camera robot control (contrast points):**
- "Multi-Robot Planning and Control from CCTV Camera Networks in a Real Warehouse" — Robinson, Ramtoula, Izaaryene, Newman, De Martini (Oxford); arXiv:2606.06762, 2026. **Classical perception, no VLM**: operates entirely in image space over an uncalibrated pixel-wise topological camera graph; 4 robots, 30 cameras, 6 × 27 m aisles; shared-resource management in overlapping views prevents collisions and deadlocks; claims "first field demonstration of multi-robot planning and coordination using only an external camera network" — [arXiv:2606.06762](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.06762)
- "ODG-NoMaD: Overhead-Camera Direction-Guided NoMaD" — arXiv:2608.21395, 2026. Overhead camera guides a diffusion navigation policy; single-robot guidance, VLM involvement unconfirmed from the snippet — [arXiv:2608.21395](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.21395)
- "Embodied Hazard Mitigation using Vision-Language Models for Autonomous Mobile Robots" — arXiv:2509.06768, 2025. VLM inspects camera feeds for warehouse anomalies (misplaced boxes, floor obstructions, spills) and triggers responses — monitoring/safety rather than multi-robot coordination — [arXiv:2509.06768](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.06768)
- Survey context: "Advancing Multi-Robot Networks via MLLM-Driven Sensing, Communication, and Computation: A Comprehensive Survey" — arXiv:2604.00061, 2026 — [arXiv:2604.00061](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.00061)

### Inferences
- The Garg et al. line is the single strongest positioning anchor: it validates the exact pattern of this thesis (velocity-threshold deadlock trigger → top-view image → VLM semantic decision → safe low-level executor) but in simulation with LiDAR-constructed synthetic top-views, not a *real overhead camera* in a *warehouse* with *ArUco-tracked physical robots*. A thesis doing the latter, with an explicit congestion-strategy taxonomy (yield/reroute/convoy) and RAG grounding, occupies a demonstrably open slot.
- The Oxford CCTV warehouse paper proves the deployment context is real and current, while its deliberate avoidance of VLMs sharpens the thesis contrast: semantic congestion-type reasoning (why robots are stuck, which resolution strategy fits) is exactly what their geometric resource-locking approach does not attempt.
- Traffic signal control is the most mature image-in-the-loop multi-agent domain; its architecture lesson (VLMLight) matches the thesis's two-layer design: fast policy for routine flow, slow VLM reasoning branch only for critical/congested cases.

### Gaps
- No published system found that feeds a *real physical overhead warehouse camera* frame to a VLM for *fleet-level congestion resolution* — the Garg work is simulation; the real-warehouse camera work is non-VLM. This is a genuine literature gap, not a search failure (multiple query phrasings tried).
- Specific VLM model names used in 2404.06413/PMLR-2026 experiments were not extractable from abstract/first pages (paper says "best available pre-trained VLMs"); read the full PDF (saved locally at the scratchpad tool-results path, 21 pp.) for the model table.

## Set-of-mark / visual prompting for selecting among spatial options — who uses it for action selection in robotics?

### Takeaway
SoM (Microsoft, 2023) established that overlaying alphanumeric marks turns VLM grounding into a multiple-choice problem; PIVOT and MOKA carried it into robot action selection. The core published rationale — "VLMs are better at multiple-choice problems than directly producing continuous-valued locations" — is the exact justification for annotated-frame + discrete-strategy prompting in a congestion planner.

### Cited Findings
- "Set-of-Mark Prompting Unleashes Extraordinary Visual Grounding in GPT-4V" — Yang et al. (Microsoft), arXiv:2310.11441, Oct 2023. SAM/SEEM segments the image; regions overlaid with alphanumerics/boxes/masks; zero-shot GPT-4V+SoM beats fully-finetuned SOTA on RefCOCOg referring segmentation — [arXiv:2310.11441](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.11441); code [microsoft/SoM](https://github.com/microsoft/SoM)
- "MOKA: Open-World Robotic Manipulation through Mark-Based Visual Prompting" — Fang (Liu), Levine et al.; arXiv:2403.03174, RSS 2024. Overlays 2D candidate keypoints on RGB images, turning affordance reasoning into VQA; explicit design rationale: "VLMs are better at multiple-choice problems than directly producing continuous-valued locations," so candidate points are plotted and the VLM selects the optimal set — [arXiv:2403.03174](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.03174); [RSS 2024 proceedings p062](https://www.roboticsproceedings.org/rss20/p062.pdf)
- "PIVOT: Iterative Visual Prompting Elicits Actionable Knowledge for VLMs" — Nasiriany et al. (Google DeepMind), arXiv:2402.07872, ICML 2024. Casts robot action/navigation selection as iterative visual question answering: candidate actions drawn as annotated arrows/points on the image, VLM picks among them, distribution refined over iterations. (arXiv id from assistant's prior knowledge; title/year confirmed in search results referencing "PIVOT: Iterative Visual Prompting Elicits Actionable Knowledge for VLMs (2024)" — [MOKA related-work context](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.03174v3))
- "Maestro: Orchestrating Robotics Modules with Vision-Language Models for Zero-Shot Generalist Robots" — arXiv:2511.00917, 2025/26: VLM as orchestrator over robotics modules, a recent continuation of the VLM-chooses-among-options pattern — [arXiv:2511.00917](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.00917)
- Survey: "Visual Prompting in Multimodal Large Language Models: A Survey" — arXiv:2409.15310, 2024 — catalogs marks, boxes, arrows as prompting devices — [arXiv:2409.15310](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2409.15310)

### Inferences
- M1's annotated frame (status-colored boxes, IDs, velocity arrows, goals) is a set-of-mark prompt in the SoM/MOKA sense; citing this line reframes the thesis's annotation module as *visual prompt engineering for action selection*, not just visualization.
- No published work was found applying SoM-style marking to *multi-robot congestion scenes* (marks = robot IDs, choices = which robot yields / which strategy applies) — PIVOT/MOKA are single-robot manipulation/navigation. This extension is claimable as novel.

### Gaps
- PIVOT's exact arXiv id (2402.07872) is from prior knowledge, not re-verified digit-for-digit this session; confirm before camera-ready citation.
- No quantitative robotics study found comparing marked-image action selection vs raw-coordinate output for *mobile-robot fleet* decisions specifically.

## Evidence on VLM spatial-reasoning limits relevant to coordination

### Takeaway
The failure modes a congestion planner must engineer around are well documented: near-chance left/right discrimination, strong positional priors (upper-left/upper-right biases), vertical-position-as-depth confusion, unreliable counting, near-random performance on relative movement across image sequences, and inability to integrate information over time. These directly motivate discrete strategy menus, symmetry-breaking gates, and state-vector side-channels alongside the image.

### Cited Findings
- "LRR-Bench: Left, Right or Rotate? Vision-Language Models Still Struggle With Spatial Understanding Tasks" — arXiv:2507.20174, 2025. Most pure VLMs hover around chance on left/right; best model only 55.33% — [arXiv:2507.20174](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.20174v1)
- Same benchmark family: VLMs handle simple absolute positions but on image *sequences* and relative movement perform "close to random guessing in most cases" while humans reach ~90% — [arXiv:2507.20174](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.20174v1)
- "Why Is Spatial Reasoning Hard for VLMs? An Attention Mechanism Perspective on Focus Areas" — arXiv:2503.01773, 2025. Attention-level account of spatial-reasoning failure — [arXiv:2503.01773](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.01773v2)
- "Why Far Looks Up: Probing Spatial Representation in Vision-Language Models" — arXiv:2605.30161, 2026. Documents "vertical-distance entanglement": models use an object's vertical image position as a proxy for distance, causing systematic spatial errors — [arXiv:2605.30161](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.30161)
- Positional priors: ChartAB benchmark finds strong pretrained position biases — Phi-3.5 biased toward upper-left; 7–8B VLMs (LLaVA-1.6 etc.) toward upper-right — [arXiv:2510.26781](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.26781)
- "OmniSpatial: Towards Comprehensive Spatial Reasoning Benchmark for Vision Language Models" — arXiv:2506.03135, 2025. Broad spatial-reasoning benchmark incl. perspective-taking; VLMs fail to adopt others' viewpoints ("empathetic spatial reasoning") — [arXiv:2506.03135](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.03135v2); see also perspective-taking failures in [APC-VLM preprint](https://apc-vlm.github.io/static/Perspective_Aware_Reasoning_Preprint.pdf)
- Multi-image failure modes: MIMIC controlled diagnostic — counting/listing/odd-one-out across multiple images are critical LVLM failure modes — "More Images, More Problems? A Controlled Analysis of VLM Failure Modes," [arXiv:2601.07812](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2601.07812)
- Temporal integration: Continuous Perception Benchmark — counting identical cubes as a camera pans; "none [of current multimodal systems] able to reliably solve the benchmark" — [arXiv:2408.07867](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.07867); [Stanford project page](https://ai.stanford.edu/~zywang/projects/ContinuousPerceptionBenchmark/)
- "CompareBench" — even the strongest VLMs "consistently fail at temporal ordering and spatial relations, and often make mistakes in basic counting that are trivial for humans" — [arXiv:2509.22737](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.22737)
- "Chronological Blindness: Benchmarking Temporal Reasoning in VLMs with CHRONOSIGHT" — arXiv:2606.16334, 2026; decomposes the human-model temporal gap into perceptual, format, and reasoning failure — [arXiv:2606.16334](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.16334)
- Driving-domain confirmation: GPT-4V weaknesses in "direction discernment … vision grounding, and spatial reasoning" in real driving scenes — [arXiv:2311.05332](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.05332v2)
- Geometric viability of VLM-proposed actions in multi-robot settings: Garg et al. explicitly pre-test whether foundation models can "locat[e] the obstacles correctly and generat[e] viable waypoints in the free space" before using them as planners — i.e., waypoint infeasibility is a recognized enough risk to merit a dedicated evaluation — [PMLR 2026 PDF](https://mit-realm.github.io/VLM-gcbfplus-website/files/Online_planning_using_VLMs.pdf)

**Project-internal (unpublished) corroboration — flag as own-work evidence, not literature:** this project's pre-registered experiments found gpt-4o's robot choice in head-on scenes is driven by a first-slot presentation-order prior plus a robot_1 token prior, with obstacle text only used under id_desc ordering (0.96 feasible vs chance), necessitating an M3 `swap` gate; and Gemini ER-2 systematically opens with `reverse` on asymmetric scenes while leaving geometry unused. These replicate the published position-bias/feasibility findings in the thesis's exact task (see project memory: presentation-order finding, Gemini ER-2 integration).

### Inferences
- The documented failure profile (left/right ≈ chance; sequences ≈ random; position priors) justifies three thesis design choices as literature-grounded rather than ad hoc: (1) single annotated keyframe + numeric state vector instead of raw multi-frame video; (2) discrete strategy enumeration instead of free-form geometric commands; (3) a downstream grounding/feasibility layer (M3 + M4 safety) that assumes VLM spatial output may be geometrically infeasible.
- Position-bias literature plus the project's own presentation-order finding together support randomized/order-controlled prompt construction as a methodological requirement, citable from both sides.

### Gaps
- No published paper found that measures *geometric infeasibility rates* of VLM-suggested multi-robot maneuvers specifically (e.g., % of suggested yield/reroute actions violating clearance); Garg et al.'s viability pre-test is the nearest published analogue, and the project's own data may be the first task-specific measurement.

## Embodied-reasoning model families (Gemini Robotics-ER, Cosmos Reason, SpatialVLM, RoboPoint): multi-robot / scene-level coordination claims

### Takeaway
These families claim strong *scene-level spatial* primitives — pointing, 2D/3D localization, trajectory prediction, temporal/physical reasoning — but none claims multi-robot *coordination*. They are positioned as perception/reasoning substrates; fleet-level congestion arbitration is explicitly absent from their reported capabilities, which is the thesis's opening.

### Cited Findings
- "Gemini Robotics: Bringing AI into the Physical World" — arXiv:2503.20020, 2025. Gemini Robotics-ER extends Gemini to embodied reasoning: object detection, pointing, trajectory and grasp prediction, multi-view correspondence, 3D bounding boxes — [arXiv:2503.20020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.20020)
- "Gemini Robotics 1.5: Pushing the Frontier of Generalist Robots with Advanced Embodied Reasoning, Thinking, and Motion Transfer" — arXiv:2510.03342, 2025. ER 1.5 reported at 52.6% on diverse pointing benchmarks (+15 pts over GPT-5) and SOTA multiview success detection (0.79–0.80) — [arXiv:2510.03342](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.03342); capability framing (pointing for spatial reasoning, relational logic "move X to Y", motion reasoning, constraint compliance) per [EmergentMind topic summary](https://www.emergentmind.com/topics/gemini-robotics-er-1-5) (secondary source)
- "Gemini Robotics-ER 1.6" — Google DeepMind blog, April 2026: improves pointing, counting, success detection over ER 1.5 and Gemini 3.0 Flash; adds instrument reading — [DeepMind blog](https://deepmind.google/blog/gemini-robotics-er-1-6/); [MarkTechPost coverage](https://www.marktechpost.com/2026/04/15/google-deepmind-releases-gemini-robotics-er-1-6-bringing-enhanced-embodied-reasoning-and-instrument-reading-to-physical-ai/). No multi-robot coordination claim in either source.
- "Cosmos-Reason1: From Physical Common Sense To Embodied Reasoning" — NVIDIA, arXiv:2503.15558, 2025. 8B and 56B reasoning VLMs; hierarchical ontology of space/time/physics; 4-stage training ending in Physical-AI RL; 56B slightly outperforms OpenAI o1 on their physical-common-sense benchmark; 7B/56B give >10% embodied-reasoning gains over backbone VLMs — [arXiv:2503.15558](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.15558)
- Cosmos-Reason2 (8B): adds improved spatiotemporal understanding, timestamp precision, 2D/3D point localization and bounding boxes with reasoning explanations; positioned as "planning model to reason what steps an embodied agent might take next" (single-agent framing) — [GitHub nvidia-cosmos/cosmos-reason2](https://github.com/nvidia-cosmos/cosmos-reason2); [HF nvidia/Cosmos-Reason2-8B](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/Cosmos-Reason2-8B); [NVIDIA Cosmos docs](https://docs.nvidia.com/cosmos/latest/reason2/index.html). - "SpatialVLM: Endowing Vision-Language Models with Spatial Reasoning Capabilities" — CVPR 2024 (arXiv:2401.12168, id from prior knowledge). Internet-scale 3D spatial VQA data injects metric spatial reasoning into VLMs — CVPR 2024 venue confirmed via [RoboPoint related work](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.10721)
- "RoboPoint: A Vision-Language Model for Spatial Affordance Prediction for Robotics" — Yuan, Duan, Blukis, Pumacay, Krishna, Murali, Mousavian, Fox; arXiv:2406.10721, CoRL 2024. Predicts image keypoint affordances from language; motivated by VLMs' inability to "precisely articulate robot actions using language"; synthetic instruction-tuning pipeline — [arXiv:2406.10721](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.10721); [GitHub](https://github.com/wentaoyuan/RoboPoint)
- "RoboSpatial: Teaching Spatial Understanding to 2D and 3D Vision-Language Models for Robotics" — arXiv:2411.16537 (CVPR 2025) — spatial-understanding training for robotics VLMs — [arXiv:2411.16537](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.16537v4)
- "RoboRefer: Towards Spatial Referring with Reasoning in Vision-Language Models for Robotics" — arXiv:2506.04308, 2025 — [arXiv:2506.04308](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.04308)

### Inferences
- All coordination-relevant claims in these reports are *primitives* (point at X, count Y, detect success, localize in 3D) evaluated on single-robot or static-scene benchmarks. None of the primary sources reviewed claims congestion, deadlock, right-of-way, or fleet arbitration capability. A thesis using one of these (Cosmos Reason2 / Gemini ER) as the backbone for centralized swarm congestion resolution is applying them one level above their evaluated scope — defensible as a novel application study.
- Cosmos Reason's open weights + physical-AI RL post-training make it the natural open-model backend for the project's `VLMBackend` interface (already anticipated in CLAUDE.md's external-library verification list).

## Anything that detects and resolves CONGESTION/DEADLOCK among mobile robots from images specifically?

### Takeaway
Exactly one published line does both from images: Garg et al. (arXiv:2404.06413 → PMLR 2026), which detects deadlock *kinematically* (velocity threshold) and resolves it *visually* (VLM on a top-view image). Classical image-space deadlock *prevention* exists (Oxford CCTV warehouse), and non-visual deadlock resolution is a mature control-theory literature — but VLM-from-image congestion-type classification (the thesis's M2 role) has no direct published occupant.

### Cited Findings
- Garg et al.: deadlock detected when average MRS speed < δ_v while mean distance-to-goal > δ_d; VLM then prompted with top-view image to emit leader + waypoints; evaluated 5–50 agents vs LLM and A* baselines; VLM variant wins on goal-reaching rate — [PMLR 2026 PDF](https://mit-realm.github.io/VLM-gcbfplus-website/files/Online_planning_using_VLMs.pdf); [arXiv:2404.06413](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.06413); [OpenReview](https://openreview.net/pdf?id=vz1p8TRRAb)
- Related-work note inside that paper: Sinha et al. 2024 use an LLM for high-level intervention but "only a single agent and … a small set of control strategies"; several prior works "convert images to text descriptions for prompting LLMs with state information" (Sinha et al. 2024; Xie et al. 2024; Kwon et al. 2023; Shah et al. 2023a; Dorbala et al. 2024) — useful boundary between this image-in-loop survey and the text-only survey — [PMLR 2026 PDF](https://mit-realm.github.io/VLM-gcbfplus-website/files/Online_planning_using_VLMs.pdf)
- Non-VLM deadlock resolution baselines (for the related-work section's control-theoretic shelf): "Deadlock Analysis and Resolution in Multi-Robot Systems" — [arXiv:1911.09146](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1911.09146); "LIVEPOINT: Fully Decentralized, Safe, Deadlock-Free Multi-Robot Control in Cluttered Environments with High-Dimensional Inputs" — [arXiv:2503.13098](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.13098); "DS-MPEPC: Safe and Deadlock-Avoiding Robot Navigation in Cluttered Dynamic Scenes" — [arXiv:2303.10133](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.10133); classic decentralized detection/resolution: Jäger & Nebel, IROS 2001 — [PDF](https://gki.informatik.uni-freiburg.de/papers/jaeger-nebel-iros01.pdf)
- Image-space (non-VLM) deadlock prevention in a real warehouse via shared-resource locking across overlapping CCTV views — [arXiv:2606.06762](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.06762)
- VLM warehouse-scene anomaly monitoring exists (misplaced boxes, obstructions, spills) but stops at flagging, not fleet re-coordination — [arXiv:2509.06768](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.06768); surveillance-VLM evaluation: [arXiv:2510.23190](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.23190v1)

### Inferences
- Positioning sentence the thesis can defend: *prior image-in-the-loop work either resolves deadlock geometrically (waypoints for a leader, Garg et al.) or prevents it with image-space resource locks (Robinson et al.); none classifies the congestion type from a real overhead camera and selects among semantic resolution strategies (yield/reroute/convoy) for execution through a safety layer.* The thesis's M2 (congestion-type + strategy) → M3 (RAG grounding) decomposition has no published equivalent found.
- Garg et al.'s reactive-trigger design (VLM only on deadlock, provably-safe low level always in charge) is the same safety argument as the thesis's Layer-1/Layer-2 split and should be cited as convergent architecture.

### Gaps
- Could not determine whether any 2026 follow-up to Garg et al. adds congestion-type classification or real-camera input (searches found none; their "future directions" section in the PMLR paper, pp. 15+, was not read this session — worth reading the saved PDF in full).
- No image-in-the-loop work found for *intersection-style right-of-way arbitration between robots* (as opposed to traffic signals for cars) — appears genuinely unpublished.
