# Classical (Non-Learning, Non-LLM) Algorithms for Multi-Robot Congestion: Capability Map and Documented Failures

Researched 2026-10-06. Scope: four classical families — (1) reactive collision avoidance, (2) complete/optimal MAPF, (3) rule-based traffic regulation, (4) deadlock detection & recovery — their measured capabilities and documented failure modes across congestion classes (head-on corridor, intersection contention, bottleneck/funnel, circular wait, fault-seeded blockage).

## Key Question 1: For each classical family, where is it sufficient and where is failure documented with numbers?

### Takeaway
Every classical family has a crisply documented failure regime: reactive methods (ORCA/CBF/DWA) provably deadlock in symmetric and confined geometries (ORCA success as low as 15% in tight passages); MAPF solvers are complete only under assumptions real execution violates; rule-based regulation (AIM, zone control) is efficient but brittle to non-conforming participants (strong degradation at 5–10% non-compliant vehicles); and timeout-based recovery is heuristic with no completeness guarantee.

### Cited Findings

**Family 1 — Reactive collision avoidance (ORCA/RVO, DWA/TEB, potential fields, CBFs)**

- ORCA-driven agents in confined spaces (corridors, doors, tight passages) "are likely to fail, causing agents to get stuck in deadlock with their velocities lowered to zero"; combining ORCA with locally confined MAPF raised success rate "from 15% to 99% in certain cases" — i.e., plain ORCA succeeded in only ~15% of trials in those tight-passage scenarios (Dergachev & Yakovlev, 2021) — [arXiv:2107.00246](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.00246)
- In corridor swaps between two agent groups, reactive ORCA agents back up, then return to the desired direction, producing a standing deadlock/traffic jam; ORCA is "myopic to future deadlocks that result from current decisions" (Dergachev & Yakovlev line of work combining Theta*, ORCA, Push-and-Rotate, 2020) — [arXiv:2008.01227](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.01227)
- "In scenes where robots distribute symmetrically, ORCA usually fails to plan reasonable collision avoidance velocities, resulting in robots getting stuck" — Robotica paper explicitly titled on the ORCA deadlock problem — [Cambridge Robotica](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/robotica/article/addressing-the-deadlock-problem-of-the-optimal-reciprocal-collision-avoidance-algorithm-in-distributed-multirobot-navigation/70CEA27CFAE13D30DD3ABCD189F9019D)
- CBF controllers: Grover et al. formally characterize deadlock as a force equilibrium on the boundary of the safe set — robot has zero control output and zero velocity while the reference command is nonzero and the goal unreached; "deadlock occurs to ensure safety when safety is on the brink of being violated"; the deadlock set is non-empty and bounded (2019–2021) — [arXiv:1911.09146](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1911.09146); [CMU RI "Why Does Symmetry Cause Deadlocks?"](https://www.ri.cmu.edu/publications/why-does-symmetry-cause-deadlocks)
- Grover et al.: for two-robot CBF systems, robot heterogeneity does NOT prevent deadlock; geometric symmetry of starts/goals renders deadlock stable; the number of candidate deadlock configurations grows ~factorially (bound 2^(N choose 2); {1, 1, 4, 18} valid configurations for N = 1–4), so their exact analysis stops at three robots; their rotation-based resolution gives provable deadlock-free goal convergence only for the analyzed 2–3 robot cases (validated on Khepera-IV robots) — [arXiv:1911.09146](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1911.09146)
- Frozen robot problem (conservative planners under crowd uncertainty): "once the environment surpasses a certain level of complexity, the planner decides that all forward paths are unsafe, and the robot freezes in place"; Trautman's 488-run Caltech dining-hall study measured a state-of-the-art non-cooperative planner behaving unsafely >3x as often as the cooperative (IGP) model at crowd densities near 0.8 humans/m² (2010–2015) — [Trautman Caltech thesis](https://thesis.library.caltech.edu/7724/7/caltech_thesis_pete_trautman.pdf)

**Family 2 — Complete/optimal MAPF (CBS, PIBT, LaCAM, priority-based, windowed lifelong)**

- PIBT has documented livelock: "since PIBT's sequential agent assignment prioritizes agents being not on their goals, livelock situations might be triggered where two agents reach their goal vertex periodically but PIBT never reaches the goal configuration" (PIBT guarantees only reachability — each agent eventually reaches its goal at some time — not simultaneous arrival; LaCAM adds constraints on configuration generation to restore completeness) — [Anytime PIBT, arXiv:2504.07841](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.07841); [Improving LaCAM, arXiv:2305.03632](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.03632)
- Windowed/real-time MAPF: "all prior real-time / windowed approaches are theoretically incomplete and mention deadlock/livelock as a problem"; only recent frameworks (WinC-MAPF, Real-Time LaCAM, 2024–2025) restore completeness to windowed planning — [Real-Time LaCAM, arXiv:2504.06091](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.06091)
- RHCR (Li et al., windowed lifelong MAPF for warehouses, 2020/2021): resolves collisions only within a bounded horizon w and ignores them beyond it; scales to 1,000 agents (38.9% of empty cells) in simulated warehouses, but the window deliberately sacrifices long-horizon collision/deadlock guarantees, and RHCR "struggles with scalability when handling thousands of agents" — [arXiv:2005.07371](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.07371); [APF-enhanced lifelong MAPF, arXiv:2505.22753](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.22753)
- Priority-based planning (classical prioritized schemes) is incomplete: corridor/swap instances are unsolvable under any fixed priority ordering; this is the standard motivation in the LaCAM/PIBT literature — [Improving LaCAM, arXiv:2305.03632](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.03632)

**Family 3 — Rule-based traffic regulation (right-of-way, lanes, AIM reservations, AGV zone control)**

- Dresner & Stone AIM (2004–2008): reservation-based intersection management performs "two to three hundred times better than traffic lights" in delay and "closely approximat[es] an overpass" under full autonomy and reliable V2I — [JAIR 2008](https://cs.utexas.edu/~aim/papers/JAIR08-dresner.pdf); [AAMAS 2005](https://www.cs.utexas.edu/~aim/papers/AAMAS05-intersection.pdf)
- AIM brittleness: performance "strongly degrades if even a few vehicles are human-driven"; FCFS-based AIM degraded significantly with just 5–10% human-operated vehicles because accommodating them forces fallback to traffic-light-like phases that block autonomous flow (Dresner & Stone 2007; survey confirmation) — [Dresner & Stone 2007](https://www.cs.utexas.edu/%7Epstone/Papers/bib2html-links/2007alag-dresner.pdf); [CAV intersection survey, arXiv:1811.03074](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1811.03074)
- Bidirectional AGV systems have two canonical deadlock types: head-on deadlock (two facing AGVs with interlocked paths) and loop-cycle deadlock (routes of ≥4 AGVs forming a cycle); prevention is "more complicated than in unidirectional systems" — unidirectional lane assignment is the classical fix, trading throughput for safety — [IJARS 2016 via DOAJ](https://doaj.org/article/7729c87dcbf84f3c9f4b166ff9b2757b)
- Zone locking gives deterministic mutual exclusion but reintroduces circular wait at the resource level (AGV holds zone A, requests B; another holds B, requests A); correct designs need total reservation orders, full conflict-set reservation before entry, or wait-cycle detection + reroute; "a single unowned Boolean can block the warehouse permanently after a communications interruption" because the controller cannot determine which vehicle is entitled to release the lock — [industrial AGV control note](https://industrialmonitordirect.com/blogs/knowledgebase/panasonic-fp7-designing-multi-agv-warehouse-control); [hybrid supervisory bidirectional AGV control, HAL 2022](https://hal.archives-ouvertes.fr/hal-03826887)
- Petri-net deadlock prevention for AGV/manufacturing systems (surveys 2004–2012, NJIT/Zhou/Li): policies are compared on structural complexity vs behavioral permissiveness vs computational complexity — the documented trade-off is that maximally permissive supervisors are computationally hard, while tractable ones over-restrict the system (throughput loss) — [NJIT survey](https://digitalcommons.njit.edu/fac_pubs/12869); [deadlock control literature review](https://digitalcommons.njit.edu/fac_pubs/18195)

**Family 4 — Deadlock detection & recovery**

- Scheduled resource allocation (Banker-style avoidance) appears in the AGV/manufacturing literature as "deadlock avoidance" within the Petri-net/automata framework, with the same permissiveness-vs-tractability trade-off — [NJIT deadlock control methods](https://digitalcommons.njit.edu/fac_pubs/20657)
- Timeout-based recovery (Nav2 spin/backup/wait/clear-costmap) is single-robot and heuristic; field reports show the stack can hang after a planning failure and stop responding to new goals, and robots in corridors ~10–20 cm wider than the body oscillate and get stuck between walls — [NVIDIA forum: Nav2 hangs after planning failure](https://forums.developer.nvidia.com/t/nav2-navigation-hangs-after-path-planning-failure-on-jetson-orin-nx-isaac-ros-3-2-nvblox/355433); [ROS Answers: narrow corridors](https://answers.ros.org/question/328267/navigation-in-narrow-corridors)
- MAPF-POST (Hönig et al., ICAPS 2016) is the classical execution-robustness bridge: post-processes a MAPF plan into a Temporal Plan Graph / Simple Temporal Network schedule in polynomial time, handles non-holonomic robots and velocity limits, guarantees a safety distance, and "exploits slack to absorb imperfect plan executions and avoid time-intensive replanning in many cases" — note the hedge "in many cases": it absorbs bounded timing error, not faults — [ICAPS 2016](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/ICAPS/article/view/13796); [hierarchical plan-generation/execution framework, arXiv:1804.00038](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1804.00038)

### Inferences (capability map across congestion classes)

| Congestion class | Reactive (ORCA/DWA/CBF) | MAPF (CBS/PIBT/LaCAM) | Rule-based (AIM/zones/lanes) | Detect & recover (timeout/WFG) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Head-on corridor | FAILS — canonical deadlock case; ~15% success documented | Solved in plan space (complete solvers); fails open-loop under delays | Solved by unidirectional lanes at throughput cost | Timeout + backup can clear 2-robot case but is unguaranteed; who-backs-up is unresolved by symmetric local rules |
| Intersection contention | Degrades with density; symmetric arrivals → ORCA/CBF equilibria | Solved (conflict resolution is MAPF's core case) | Best case for reservations/AIM (2–300x vs lights), brittle to non-compliance | Usually unneeded if regulated |
| Bottleneck/funnel | Frozen-robot behavior at high density (Trautman numbers) | Solvable but cost explodes; windowed solvers push congestion outside horizon → livelock | Zone control serializes = handles it at throughput cost | Clear-costmap/wait helps transient cases only |
| Circular wait (≥3 robots) | FAILS — exactly the stable CBF deadlock sets; exact analysis intractable beyond N=3 | Complete solvers avoid it in plan space; windowed/prioritized planners re-create it | Zone locking re-creates it at resource level unless total ordering enforced | Wait-for-graph detection works IF the wait relation is observable; recovery choice (who yields) is unspecified by classical theory |
| Fault-seeded blockage (dead/stalled robot) | Treated as static obstacle only after mapped as one; until then robots wait indefinitely | Violates MAPF's model — no solver handles an unmodeled permanently faulted agent without an out-of-band replan trigger | Zone held by dead AGV blocks permanently ("single unowned Boolean blocks the warehouse") | The only family that addresses it, and only via timeouts whose threshold encodes a guess about the blocker's nature |

- Project-internal corroboration of the reactive/recovery rows (internal evidence, not externally citable): natural head-on deadlocks in a 0.55 m x 2.0 m aisle were not resolved by stock Nav2 behaviors and required an orchestrated backup + hold + yield sequence (see `docs/implementation_notes.md`; memory notes `project_phase3_backup_not_moving_fix`, `project_m4_backup_closed_loop_verified`).

### Gaps
- No single paper reports deadlock rates for DWA/DWB specifically in multi-robot corridors; the corridor-failure numbers found are for the ORCA family and CBF controllers. DWB evidence is anecdotal (forums/issues), not benchmarked.
- The Robotica ORCA-deadlock paper's own failure percentages are paywalled; only the qualitative symmetric-failure claim was accessible.

## Key Question 2: What do MAPF solvers assume that real warehouse execution violates, and what is the measured degradation?

### Takeaway
Classical MAPF completeness/optimality is conditional on a known static graph, synchronized discrete time, perfect execution, and central knowledge of all goals; under real delays, plans either risk collision or must be re-serialized (one robot's delay propagates through the whole dependency chain), and the robust fixes are documented as "computationally expensive, overly-conservative, and robust only in expectation."

### Cited Findings
- Classical MAPF assumes agents move synchronously on a known graph in discrete timesteps with perfect action execution; "the execution of MAPF plans may present unexpected delays that hinder the system's ability to follow the prescribed plan" — [Introducing Delays in MAPF, arXiv:2307.11252](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2307.11252)
- Enforcing the plan's visit ordering to stay safe under one agent's delay makes "all agents scheduled to visit a vertex after the agent that is delayed... wait, accumulating the same delay" — a single delayed robot propagates its delay through the dependency graph — [arXiv:2307.11252](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2307.11252)
- k-robust MAPF (Atzmon et al., JAIR 2020): a plan is k-robust if it stays collision-free when each agent suffers up to k delays; construction requires each vacated vertex to stay unoccupied for k steps (direct throughput cost); the paper also contributes robust execution policies including under communication delays — [JAIR 11734](https://jair.org/index.php/jair/article/view/11734)
- Later assessment of the robust-MAPF line: "these robust planning techniques suffer from being computationally expensive, overly-conservative, and are robust only in expectation (probabilistically)" — [ICAPS PlanRob 2023, paper 14](https://icaps23.icaps-conference.org/program/workshops/planrob/PlanRob-23_paper_14.pdf)
- MAPF-POST converts a discrete plan into a continuous, kinematics-aware STN schedule with guaranteed inter-robot safety distance, absorbing execution noise via slack "in many cases" without replanning — the classical answer to asynchronous execution, but it preserves the plan's passing order, so it cannot absorb a fault that invalidates that order — [Hönig et al., ICAPS 2016](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/ICAPS/article/view/13796)
- Windowed lifelong replanning (RHCR) trades completeness for tractability by ignoring collisions beyond horizon w; the incompleteness and deadlock/livelock of all prior windowed approaches is stated explicitly in the 2025 Real-Time LaCAM paper — [arXiv:2005.07371](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.07371); [arXiv:2504.06091](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.06091)

### Inferences
- Violated-assumption → consequence map: (a) synchronized time → violated by per-robot controller timing → k-step vertex conflicts; (b) perfect execution → violated by wheel slip/localization noise → collision risk or chain-wide delay propagation through the TPG; (c) known static map → violated by dropped items and stopped robots → plan invalid, full replan; (d) central knowledge of all goals → violated by online task streams → handled by lifelong MAPF, but only by giving up long-horizon guarantees (windowing).
- Degradation is structural, not graceful: a MAPF plan under an unmodeled persistent fault stalls the whole dependency chain (every follower waits) until an out-of-band mechanism (timeout, replan trigger) intervenes. That out-of-band slot is exactly where a supervisory semantic layer (M2/M5) sits.

### Gaps
- Concrete collision-probability-vs-delay-probability curves (e.g., failure % at delay probability p) exist in Atzmon et al. 2020 and the delay-MAPF literature but could not be transcribed: the JAIR page exposes only the abstract and the arXiv:2307.11252 PDF exceeded the fetch size limit. Flag as "documented but numbers not transcribed here."
- No public measured degradation data from a production warehouse (proprietary); the closest public signal is Amazon's framing under Key Question 5.

## Key Question 3: Is there a congestion class classical methods cannot resolve in principle, motivating semantic/reasoning layers?

### Takeaway
Two documented in-principle limits: (1) symmetric-geometry deadlock is a *stable equilibrium* of purely reactive safety-filtered controllers (Grover's duality analysis), so no local tuning removes it — resolution requires an external symmetry-breaking decision; (2) classical methods have no mechanism to distinguish a permanently faulted blocker from a temporarily stopped robot — every classical recovery compresses this semantic question into a timeout constant, and the frozen-robot literature shows classical planners under uncertainty fail safe into immobility rather than reason about other agents' intent.

### Cited Findings
- Grover et al. prove deadlock for CBF-controlled robots is a stable equilibrium induced by geometric symmetry of starts/goals; heterogeneity does not help; their provable resolution exists only for 2–3 robots and requires a *separate, non-reactive* maneuver phase (rotation protocol) — i.e., the fix is itself a small deliberative layer bolted onto the reactive one — [arXiv:1911.09146](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1911.09146)
- "Agents need to exhibit a form of coordination as some must yield to others" — reciprocal rules alone cannot decide *who* yields in symmetric corridor encounters; this is the stated motivation for hybrid ORCA+MAPF methods — [arXiv:2107.00246](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.00246)
- The freezing robot problem is framed as "a fundamental impediment to classical motion planning algorithms": above a complexity threshold the planner finds all forward paths unsafe and freezes; Trautman argues it "can be avoided if the robot anticipates human cooperation," i.e., only by modeling other agents' intent — a semantic, not geometric, capability — [Trautman thesis](https://thesis.library.caltech.edu/7724/7/caltech_thesis_pete_trautman.pdf)
- AGV practice documents the fault-ambiguity failure concretely: after a communications interruption, a zone lock with no determinable owner "can block the warehouse permanently" — the system state does not encode *why* a resource is held or whether the holder will ever release it — [industrial AGV control note](https://industrialmonitordirect.com/blogs/knowledgebase/panasonic-fp7-designing-multi-agv-warehouse-control)
- Windowed MAPF incompleteness (deadlock/livelock of all prior real-time approaches) shows that once classical planners bound their horizon for tractability, long-horizon congestion reasoning must live somewhere else — [arXiv:2504.06091](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.06091)
- That 2025 research addresses AMR fleet deadlock with non-classical additions (MARL deadlock handling; opinion-dynamics corridor negotiation) is itself evidence the classical stack's gap is recognized as open — [arXiv:2511.07071](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.07071); [arXiv:2504.20947](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.20947)

### Inferences
- The common structure across all four families' failures is missing information of a *semantic* kind: who should yield (symmetric deadlock), whether a blocker will move (fault vs pause), whether congestion is transient or structural (window vs horizon). Classical methods compress each into a scalar chosen offline (priority index, timeout, window length); when the scalar's implicit assumption is wrong, failure is total (stall), not graceful.
- This is the precise motivation slot for the M2 VLM layer: classify the congestion type (head-on vs circular wait vs fault-seeded) and select a strategy (yield/reroute/convoy) — supplying the symmetry-breaking and blocker-semantics decisions classical layers provably (symmetric case) or demonstrably (fault case) cannot derive from local state. Our internal M1 finding that overlapping stopped robots read as STALE rather than STOPPED (memory: `project_m1_stale_vs_stopped`) is a live instance of the fault-ambiguity class.
- Epistemic honesty: "cannot in principle" is *proven* only for the symmetric-deadlock case (Grover's stability result, analyzed up to N=3). The fault-ambiguity limit is documented practice plus an information-theoretic argument, not a theorem.

### Gaps
- No paper was found that poses exactly the "permanent blocker vs temporarily stopped robot" discrimination problem and measures classical-method failure on it; the claim rests on the AGV lock-ownership evidence and the structure of MAPF fault assumptions. This may be a gap our experiments can claim to fill.

## Key Question 4: Nav2-specific documented behavior in narrow-aisle multi-robot conflicts?

### Takeaway
Nav2's DWB/costmap/recovery stack is designed and documented as single-robot; it has no peer-robot coordination primitive, its recovery behaviors are open-loop timeout heuristics, and community reports document stalls in narrow corridors and post-failure hangs — but no formal multi-robot Nav2 congestion benchmark with failure rates was found in the literature.

### Cited Findings
- Narrow corridors (~10–20 cm clearance over robot width) are a documented pain point even single-robot: global/local costmap inflation tuning conflicts, and robots "turn slightly... and continue turning, resulting in the robot getting stuck between two walls" — [ROS Answers: navigation in narrow corridors](https://answers.ros.org/question/328267/navigation-in-narrow-corridors); [ROS Answers: diff-drive through doorways](https://answers.ros.org/question/414264/helping-diff-drive-robot-get-through-doorways/?sort=votes)
- Field report: Nav2 "becomes unresponsive to new goal poses after an initial path planning failure occurs" — failure handling itself can wedge the stack — [NVIDIA developer forum](https://forums.developer.nvidia.com/t/nav2-navigation-hangs-after-path-planning-failure-on-jetson-orin-nx-isaac-ros-3-2-nvblox/355433)
- 2025 papers adding deadlock layers on top of Nav2-class stacks (MARL deadlock handling for AMRs; nonlinear-opinion-dynamics corridor negotiation) explicitly motivate themselves by deadlocks the stock stack cannot resolve — [arXiv:2511.07071](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.07071); [arXiv:2504.20947](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.20947)

### Inferences
- Nav2's recovery set (spin, backup, wait, clear-costmap) assumes the blockage is a sensor artifact (clear-costmap), transient (wait), or escapable by ego-motion (spin/backup). None encodes the existence of another decision-making robot; in a two-robot head-on, both robots running identical recovery policies reproduce the Q1 symmetry problem at the recovery level.
- Project-internal verified evidence (for our own report, internal citations): natural head-on deadlock in the 0.55 m x 2.0 m aisle was not resolved by stock Nav2 behaviors and required an orchestrated odometry-based backup + hold + yield sequence (verified end-to-end 2026-07-02, commit 05ab38f; `docs/implementation_notes.md`); brittleness we hit — cancel-all race zeroing cmd_vel, collision_monitor `stop_pub_timeout` 2.0 s, DWB goal-tolerance stalls (commit 09778fa), bt_navigator silent crash on float-typed integer params — matches the class of failures the external reports describe.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed benchmark quantifying Nav2 multi-robot narrow-aisle failure rates was found; evidence is forum reports, issues, and motivation sections of 2025 papers. Our own measurements may be among the few quantified data points.

## Key Question 5: What hybrid classical architectures run in deployed fleets, and what residual congestion do they report?

### Takeaway
State of practice is layered: central (windowed) route planning + reservation/zone execution + local safety stops, on engineered unidirectional grids. Even at Amazon's maturity (1M+ robots, 300+ sites), congestion remained significant enough that their 2025 DeepFleet model was justified by a ~10% travel-efficiency gain from congestion reduction — the measurable residual the classical stack left.

### Cited Findings
- Amazon operates 1M+ robots across 300+ fulfillment centers; a typical FC floor runs 4,000+ drive robots; Amazon states they recognized from the start of large-scale deployment that "they needed to solve the congestion problem" — [Amazon Science: how Amazon robots navigate congestion](https://www.amazon.science/latest-news/how-amazon-robots-navigate-congestion); [Supply Chain 24/7](https://www.supplychain247.com/article/amazon-1-million-robots-ai/e_commerce)
- DeepFleet (June 2025), described by Amazon Robotics VP Scott Dresser as "an intelligent traffic management system for a city filled with cars moving through congested streets," is credited with ~10% improvement in robot travel efficiency via congestion reduction — the size of the residual the pre-2025 (classical + heuristic) stack left — [Digital Commerce 360](https://www.digitalcommerce360.com/2025/07/03/amazon-deepfleet-ai-model-for-robotics/); [eWeek](https://www.eweek.com/fr/news/amazon-robots-automation-jobs/)
- Amazon Science publishes on congestion *prediction* for large mobile-robot fleets — congestion is an explicit, persistent operational metric, not a solved problem — [Amazon Science publication](https://amazon.science/publications/congestion-prediction-for-large-fleets-of-mobile-robots)
- Research-side hybrid state of the art mirrors deployment: RHCR-style central windowed MAPF for routing (1,000 agents) + MAPF-POST-style robust execution + local safety; the 2021 hybrid ORCA+MAPF result (15%→99%) is the same architecture in miniature — [arXiv:2005.07371](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.07371); [ICAPS 2016](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/ICAPS/article/view/13796); [arXiv:2107.00246](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.00246)
- AGV industry practice for bidirectional systems is hybrid supervisory control: an offline-verified zone/supervisor layer plus online routing, documented as necessary because pure local control cannot prevent head-on and loop-cycle deadlocks — [HAL hal-03826887](https://hal.archives-ouvertes.fr/hal-03826887); [IJARS 2016](https://doaj.org/article/7729c87dcbf84f3c9f4b166ff9b2757b)

### Inferences
- Deployed fleets largely avoid the reactive-deadlock failure class by *environment design* (reserved grids, unidirectional lanes, human-exclusion zones) rather than by solving it — Kiva/Amazon drive units run on a reserved grid. A mixed-traffic aisle setting (ours) deliberately removes that crutch, which is why the Q1 failure modes are live for us.
- Amazon answering the residual with a learned foundation model (DeepFleet) suggests the classical toolbox plateaued on fleet-level congestion; caveat for our related-work framing: DeepFleet targets fleet-level flow optimization, not per-incident semantic deadlock resolution, which remains the niche for per-scene VLM reasoning.

### Gaps
- OTTO Motors and Locus Robotics publish no quantitative residual-congestion or deadlock-rate figures (marketing material only); no reliable public numbers found.
- Amazon's 10% figure is a company-reported estimate relayed by press, not an independently audited measurement.
