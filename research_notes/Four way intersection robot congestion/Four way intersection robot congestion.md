# Literature backs one yielder, not two

Across four bodies of literature — reservation-based autonomous intersection management (AIM), warehouse MAPF and deployed-fleet practice, decentralized reactive control, and LLM/VLM coordination — the strongest and most consistent finding is that a formed four-way jam is resolved by **selecting the single cheapest-to-move robot to recede, then releasing the survivors in an explicit priority order**, never by simultaneous multi-robot retreat. The proposed prereg-v2 composite command ("cross-pair recedes, then the axis pair yields with ordered crossing") therefore needs one structural correction: the recede stage should default to *one* robot chosen by retreat cost (distance to a free pocket plus robots stacked behind it), with the two-robot recede as a staggered, sequential fallback — A-DRIVE, the flagship recovery protocol, explicitly rejects multi-vehicle backing as "difficult and time- and fuel-consuming" ([A-DRIVE](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2204.04910)). Beyond that correction the composite command is well precedented in shape: A-DRIVE's recede-then-cross two-phase logic, Tachet et al.'s finding that batching compatible crossings roughly doubles service capacity ([PLOS ONE 2016](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0149607)), and the LLM-robotics literature's convergent recipe of closed-vocabulary staged sequences validated before dispatch all point the same way. The apparatus constraints are also well matched: Grover et al. prove deadlocked robots sit *exactly at* the safety margin ([IJRR/arXiv:2206.01781](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.01781)), so the 0.45 m floor guarantees the first legal motion is a retreat and the safety layer must whitelist distance-increasing reverse motion; and the 0.5–2 Hz reasoner matches published slow-fast regimes ([DriveVLM-Dual](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.12289v4)). The exact combination — a centralized VLM issuing a labeled composite recovery maneuver over a BEV warehouse scene above a rule-based grounder — has no published instance, which makes prereg-v2 a defensible first and simultaneously means there is no citable baseline for its failure rates.

## Prevention is the doctrine everywhere; commanded recovery is an open niche

The two mature engineering traditions — AIM and warehouse MAPF — both make the formed scrum *unreachable by construction*, and therefore have almost nothing to say about resolving one. Classic AIM (Dresner & Stone) grants each vehicle an exclusive space-time reservation over an n×n tile grid, simulated internally before CONFIRM; a vehicle "may not pilot the vehicle into the intersection without a reservation," so under compliant behavior no mutual block can form ([Dresner & Stone, JAIR 2008](https://jair.org/index.php/jair/article/download/10542/25241/19590)). The one deadlock D&S encountered — vehicles decelerating inside the box and never recovering — was **designed out** by letting the manager choose acceleration schedules, not recovered from at runtime; the only in-box fault handling is an EMERGENCY-STOP broadcast ([Dresner & Stone 2008, §3.4.2, §3.2](https://jair.org/index.php/jair/article/download/10542/25241/19590)). The MAPF side is identical in spirit: a valid plan has no vertex or swap conflicts ([Stern et al., SoCS 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.08291)), RHCR "assume[s] that the drive units can execute any MAPF plan perfectly" ([RHCR, AAAI 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.07371)), and execution robustness means preserving the *planned passing order* under delays via Action Dependency Graphs ([Hönig et al., RA-L 2019](https://whoenig.github.io/publications/2019_RA-L_Hoenig.pdf)) or online reordering of those dependencies (SADG, [arXiv:2010.05254](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2010.05254)) — never physical retreat. Deployed fleets follow the same doctrine at industrial scale: Kiva/Amazon runs 500+ vehicles under central path reservation ([Wurman et al., AI Magazine 2008](https://www.dcsc.tudelft.nl/~sc4081/2018/assign/pap/robert_kiva_paper.pdf)), AGV practice partitions floors into mutually exclusive zones, and container terminals reserve grid-blocks in advance explicitly "to make room between AGVs and to prevent deadlocks" ([OR Spectrum 2006](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-540-49550-5_12)).

Two consequences follow for prereg-v2. First, the study's start state — a physically formed jam — is, in the prevention literature's terms, evidence that a reservation layer has already *failed*; A-DRIVE makes this explicit, attributing formed deadlocks to system failure (control, communication, localization, time-sync) or geometry exceeding sensing range even "when the centralized and decentralized cooperation protocols work properly" ([A-DRIVE](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2204.04910)). That framing is worth adopting: the VLM layer is a *fault-regime* coordinator, complementary to (not competing with) reservation-style prevention, and its grounding should tolerate one non-compliant robot rather than assume all four execute. Second, the prevention canon still supplies the post-recovery discipline. RHCR's own admission that intersection starvation is an accepted cost of throughput ("if we always let the horizontal agents move and the vertical agent wait, we maximize the throughput but lose completeness," [RHCR §4.4](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.07371)) and the Levin–Boyles paradox — FCFS reservations can be *worse than signals* in networks because low-conflict vehicles jump high-priority flows ([Transportation Research Part A 2016](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0965856416303822)) — jointly say that crossing order after recovery should be computed per episode from conflict footprint, with an aging/timeout escalation as the out-of-band fairness guard. Both of D&S's proven anti-starvation devices (per-lane head-of-line exclusivity via the reservation-distance rule, and retry timeouts that effectively raise a waiting robot's priority) are single-scalar bookkeeping ideally suited to the deterministic M3 grounder rather than per-cycle VLM judgment ([Dresner & Stone 2008, §3.4.3–3.4.4](https://jair.org/index.php/jair/article/download/10542/25241/19590)).

## The recovery canon prescribes cost-ordered single retreat, then inside-first crossing

The only literature that gives a complete withdrawal-ordering rule for a formed junction jam is A-DRIVE (Aoki & Rajkumar, IEEE IV 2022), and its structure is almost exactly the proposed composite command — with three load-bearing details. Each vehicle computes a **yielding cost χ = s·D_SPACE + t·N_f** (distance to the nearest evacuation space plus number of followers); the priority rules are (1) vehicles *inside* the intersection outrank those outside, (2) among insiders, larger yielding cost wins the right to stay, (3) outsider ties break randomly; lower-priority vehicles **recede along their own recorded arrival path**; and retreats are staggered by cost-proportional wait timers Δ_i = a·χ + R_i rather than executed simultaneously ([A-DRIVE §III](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2204.04910)). A-DRIVE explicitly prefers a single yielder ("it is preferable that the single vehicle yields and recedes, because the backing operation of multiple vehicles might be difficult and time-consuming") and rejects Ethernet-style exponential backoff because backing maneuvers are expensive ([A-DRIVE](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2204.04910)). Its evaluation carries a directly relevant negative result: a *static* lane-priority rule forces low-priority vehicles to "recede multiple times," with recovery time growing steeply with intersection size — evidence that yielder selection must be situational, not fixed per arm or per axis ([A-DRIVE §IV](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2204.04910)). The older ancestors agree on sequencing: Jäger & Nebel's IROS 2001 protocol resolves detected deadlocks by asking involved robots **one at a time** for an alternative trajectory "until the deadlock is resolved" ([IROS 2001](https://gki.informatik.uni-freiburg.de/papers/jaeger-nebel-iros01.pdf)), and warehouse practice retreats a conflicting AGV to a pre-designated *spare point* — a surveyed refuge cell, not an ad-hoc position ([IEEE 7985596](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/7985596)).

For the composite command this yields a concrete prescription. **Stage 1 should select the recede set by A-DRIVE-style cost, defaulting to one robot**, not by axis identity: "cross-pair recedes" is only the right answer when both members of that pair genuinely have the cheapest clear reverse paths, and when two retreats are truly necessary they should be sequenced (cost-staggered), never simultaneous — no surveyed source supports coordinated simultaneous backing, and A-DRIVE's future-work note defers exactly the case where evacuation space is contested. **Stage 2's ordered crossing should be inside-first, then per-episode priority, with the yielder re-entering last** — the consensus ordering across all four un-jamming sources. The single-yield primitive already in the apparatus (own-arm retreat, ordered release) is not a simplification of this literature; it *is* the literature's primitive, and the per-arm retreat guarantee in the scenario design actually simplifies A-DRIVE's general problem, because when every robot has a clear arrival arm behind it, yielder selection degenerates from feasibility gating to pure cost ranking. One genuine open problem should be stated honestly in the prereg: no source provides a verified procedure for the worst case where *every* candidate yielder's reverse path is blocked by followers (cascaded backup), and no formal optimality result exists for yielder selection at all — every published rule is a heuristic cost. Equally, no published system performs **centralized commanded recovery** from a formed scrum; A-DRIVE and Jäger–Nebel are decentralized, so the M2→M3 commanded-recovery pipeline is both a citation gap and a defensible novelty claim.

## Reactive-control theory explains why the jam is stable and what the 0.45 m floor implies

The decentralized line (ORCA, buffered Voronoi cells, CBF-QP) supplies the theory of why the jam formed and why forward-only maneuvering cannot break it. All three families deadlock on (near-)symmetric configurations because a symmetric reactive rule maps a symmetric input to a symmetric response, and bottleneck geometry keeps regenerating the symmetric input; ORCA "usually fails to plan reasonable collision avoidance velocities" in symmetric scenes ([Topology-Guided ORCA](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.16771)), BVC's own authors offer only heuristics that "perform well in practice to alleviate deadlock" ([Zhou et al., RA-L 2017](https://msl.stanford.edu/papers/zhou_fast_2017.pdf)), and Grover, Liu & Sycara characterize CBF deadlock as a **force equilibrium on the boundary of the safe set**, with symmetric initial conditions *provably* producing it ([arXiv:2206.01781](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.01781)). Three of their results bear directly on the apparatus. First, **Theorem 3: two robots in deadlock sit exactly the safety distance D_s apart** — mapped onto the hard 0.45 m pairwise floor, a genuinely formed jam presents with gaps at or near 0.45 m, zero spatial slack, so any forward or rotational first move violates the floor and **at least one robot must move away**. This is the theoretical justification for the own-arm-retreat primitive, and equally the warning that the M4 safety layer must classify reverse-along-arrival-arm as distance-increasing and exempt it from the min-distance stop — otherwise the safety filter re-creates precisely the stall it was meant to prevent (consistent with the project's existing backup-phase exemption). Second, the deadlock set is measure-zero but *attracting* near bottlenecks, so the practical trigger condition is the neighborhood — sustained low progress plus tight pairwise gaps — not exact zero velocity. Third, admissible deadlock configurations explode combinatorially ({1, 1, 4, 18} for N = 1..4 robots, [arXiv:2206.01781](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.01781)), which argues for a generic recovery procedure over per-configuration analysis at exactly N = 4.

The same literature converges on the claim that at a bottleneck the resolution *is* a total ordering. Continuous methods either stumble into an order through instability — Jankovic et al. show passing order in CBF intersections is "negotiated through exponentially unstable equilibria — no agent ever makes a discrete go/yield decision," and host-only decentralized CBF policies gridlock ([arXiv:2207.04915](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.04915)) — or compute one explicitly: Chandra et al.'s social mini-games line resolves doorway/intersection deadlock by perturbing onto "liveness sets," which operationally means picking a queue position ([arXiv:2308.10966](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.10966)), and GamePlan proves collision- and deadlock-freeness of an auction-derived turn order, a proof that requires only *a* strict order, not its particular key ([arXiv:2109.01896](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.01896)). Two further transfers matter for the design. The unlabeled-disc separation bounds — a solution always exists when like endpoints are ≥ 4 radii apart and start-to-goal ≥ 3 radii, constants proven tight ([SoCG 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.07777)), with PSPACE-hardness below that regime ([arXiv:1408.2260](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1408.2260)) — translate, with 2r ≈ 0.45 m, to **retreat pockets pairwise ≥ ~0.9 m apart and ≥ ~0.68 m from another robot's crossing path**; this is a heuristic transfer (the theorems concern unlabeled discs in polygons), but it is the only principled clearance number available, and the hardness result is the formal argument for why a slow deliberative sequencer is justified at all. And the RMTRACK principle — provable safety and deadlock-freeness under arbitrary delays when the executive only *re-times* motion along fixed paths ([arXiv:1603.08582](https://arxiv.org/abs/1603.08582)) — says the composite command should stay on the existing roadmap (named pockets, holds, ordered entries, never freehand geometry), which is what makes a 0.5–2 Hz reasoner that cannot re-decide quickly survivable mid-resolution. Finally, reversing is essentially absent as a first-class maneuver in the entire VO/BVC/CBF line — retreat appears only incidentally (pseudo-goal perturbation, [arXiv:2407.17766](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.17766)) or in dedicated recovery work — so a sequencer that owns the full retreat contract (target pocket, rear-clearance check, hold condition, release condition) is beyond, not behind, the decentralized state of the art; the corollary cost is that a reversing robot temporarily increases occupancy behind it, so clearing the retreat lane must be part of the same discrete decision.

## LLM/VLM precedent: closed-vocabulary stages, whole-sequence validation, and order bias that grows with the option space

The 2023–2026 foundation-model coordination literature establishes the architecture prereg-v2 already uses and sharpens what the composite command must look like. Every published conflict-resolution system inserts a deterministic executor, learned policy, or validation loop between model output and motion — none commands actuators directly. Decision granularity clusters into three levels: one symbolic role per agent per cycle (LIDSA's CLEAR/YIELD/SHARE/FOLLOW passed to a deterministic kinematic executor, [arXiv:2605.12321](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.12321)); leader-plus-waypoints (Garg et al.'s foundation-model deadlock *recovery*, where the LLM/VLM is invoked after robots are stuck, assigns one leader and a waypoint set, and a GNN controller executes — "move exactly one, chosen by semantic reasoning," [arXiv:2404.06413](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.06413)); and negotiated ordered sequences (LLM-negotiated passing sequences at unsignalized intersections, [arXiv:2503.16552](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.16552); CoLMDriver's V2V negotiation with intention-guided waypoints, [arXiv:2503.08683](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.08683)). The staged-maneuver representation has a direct precedent in DriveVLM's meta-action sequences — ordered chains from 17 closed action types, refined downstream into waypoints, run as a slow VLM over a fast classical planner ([arXiv:2402.12289](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.12289v4)) — and the safety recipe recurs across BTGenBot's validation ladder (static syntax → validator → simulation → real robot, [arXiv:2403.12761](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.12761)), CommandSwarm's command-level safety filtering ([arXiv:2605.07764](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.07764)), and RoCo's environment-validation feedback loop, where feeding validator error text back into the next proposal round measurably improves it, up to 0.95 success on RoCoBench ([arXiv:2307.04738](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2307.04738)). Critically, **no paper was found where a VLM emits a labeled composite maneuver of the form "A reverses, then B yields, then ordered crossing" over a BEV warehouse scene**; the ingredients exist separately but not the combination, and no paper isolates the value of compositeness itself against a single-action baseline on the same conflict task — prereg-v2 can be the first controlled test of exactly that.

The bias literature converts the project's own presentation-order finding from a harness artifact into a citable systematic property. Swapping input-image order alters roughly **30% of multi-image VLM predictions** and moves accuracy by up to 10 points, with content at sequence extremes favored ([CVPR 2025](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2025/papers/Tian_Identifying_and_Mitigating_Position_Bias_of_Multi-image_Vision-Language_Models_CVPR_2025_paper.pdf)); selection bias in vision-language MCQA is driven by both option identity and position and **intensifies with task difficulty and finer-grained options** ([arXiv:2509.16805](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.16805v1)). The second result carries a specific warning: the composite command *enlarges* the option space (which robot plays which stage), so order sensitivity should be expected to worsen, not stay flat, relative to the current strategy enum — randomized presentation order conditioned on the draw, plus identity-symmetric prompts, become more important under prereg-v2, and the deterministic M3 swap/ordering gate remains necessary as the enforcement point for symmetry breaking (the social mini-games result that symmetric inputs produce "identical, conflicting responses" applies to a VLM with a first-slot prior just as much as to ORCA). On evaluation design, no surveyed paper pre-registers, randomizes prompt ordering, or reports refusal rates, hallucinated-ID rates, or per-call latency distributions; LLMLight's treatment of backbone identity as an experimental factor across ten LLMs ([arXiv:2312.16044](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.16044)) and RoCo's mechanism ablations are the best elements worth copying. A BTGenBot-style attrition ladder (schema-valid → feasibility-passed → sim-completed → deadlock-resolved) is the precedented reporting format for grounding failures, and registering backbone (gpt-4o vs Gemini ER-2) as a factor matches both the literature and the existing two-backend setup.

## What prereg-v2 should register for the composite command

Synthesizing across all four literatures, the supported design is a **two-stage-atomic composite with a cost-selected, default-single recede stage**: Stage 1, the robot with minimum retreat cost (shortest clear reverse to a named pocket, fewest followers — A-DRIVE's χ) backs out along its own arrival arm; Stage 2, the remaining robots cross in a per-episode priority order (inside-first, conflict-footprint-aware, never static per-arm), with the yielder re-entering last. Where the cross-pair-recedes variant is retained, it should be grounded as two cost-ranked sequential retreats with staggered release, and the prereg should note that simultaneous multi-robot backing has no support anywhere in the surveyed literature. The command representation should be an ordered list of `(robot_id, primitive, completion-condition)` stages in a closed, JSON-schema'd vocabulary — the DriveVLM/LIDSA pattern restricted to the existing primitive set — with M3 validating the *sequence* as a whole (robot-ID membership, per-stage feasibility, no stage destroying a later stage's precondition) and on rejection either falling back to the single-strategy path or re-prompting once with the validator's error text per the RoCo result. Stage-failure semantics must be specified up front, because no published system does: (i) stage timeout → abort remainder, revert to safe hold; (ii) safety-layer veto mid-stage → abort and re-invoke M2 with failure context; (iii) early scene resolution → cancel remaining stages. Two deterministic-layer guards come straight from the canon: M5 should treat "commanded but stationary for N cycles" as a first-class layer-inconsistency signal (the Jankovic gridlock class, and the project's own collision_monitor cmd_vel-zeroing incident, [arXiv:2207.04915](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.04915)), and the release stage should gate on the previous crosser being clear of the *exit* lane by a time margin, not merely outside the box polygon — D&S's edge-tile lesson, and the point where residual collision risk concentrates under a hard 0.45 m floor ([Dresner & Stone 2008, §3.4.6](https://jair.org/index.php/jair/article/download/10542/25241/19590)). Finally, Tachet's FAIR-vs-BATCH result licenses one cheap throughput upgrade: one command per cycle need not mean one crossing per cycle — the VLM picks the batch axis and order, and the grounder expands the release to all mutually compatible crossings checkable against the pairwise floor ([Tachet et al. 2016](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0149607)).

## Conclusion

The deepest pattern across the four literatures is a division of labor the field arrived at independently, repeatedly: a semantic or economic layer chooses *who and in what order*, while a deterministic layer owns *whether it is safe* — and every documented failure of hybrid stacks is one layer silently vetoing or starving the other. That places the real design risk for prereg-v2 not in the VLM's choice quality but in the seams: the safety floor that must permit distance-increasing retreat, the exit-lane margin, the commanded-but-stationary detector, and the deterministic liveness rules (head-of-line exclusivity, aging) that must never be delegated to per-cycle model judgment. The literature also hands the study an unusually clean novelty position: centralized commanded recovery from a formed scrum, a labeled composite recovery maneuver from a VLM, an isolated test of compositeness, pre-registration, and failure-mode accounting each individually have no published instance — but the same absences mean prereg-v2's grounding-failure and refusal rates will be the first numbers of their kind, with nothing to benchmark against except its own single-strategy ablation. That ablation is therefore not optional methodology; it is the only baseline the field can offer.
