# Scenario Taxonomies and Benchmark Scene Classes for Multi-Robot Congestion Evaluation (2019–2026)

## Key Question 1: Scenario classes in the social-navigation / social mini-games line

### Takeaway
The social mini-game (SMG) line has converged on a small canonical set — doorway, hallway, intersection, corner variants, and circulating/crossing traffic — with a recent Autonomous Robots survey (Chandra et al. 2026) fixing a 9-class taxonomy; simulator benchmarks (SocialGym 2.0, SEAN 2.0) ship 5-class scene sets that overlap heavily with it.

### Cited Findings
- Chandra et al., "Multi-Robot Navigation in Social Mini-Games: Definitions, Taxonomy, and Algorithms" (Autonomous Robots, Springer 2026; arXiv Aug 2025) defines SMGs as "constrained and cluttered environments that have high agency (e.g., doorways, hallways, corridor intersections)" and catalogs solvers under a unified taxonomy — [arXiv:2508.13459](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.13459); [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10514-026-10251-w)
- The survey's Section 2.4 identifies **9 canonical SMG scenarios**: (1) Doorway — bottleneck testing prioritization and quick decisions; (2) Intersection — agents from different directions meeting; (3) Hallway — two-way corridor traffic, overtaking and yielding; (4) L-Corner — sharp turn with same start/goal positions; (5) Blind Corner — limited visibility, agents moving toward each other; (6) Crowded Traffic — many dynamic agents moving randomly; (7) Parallel Traffic — side-by-side deadlock avoidance; (8) Perpendicular Traffic — crossing paths with right-of-way management; (9) Circular Traffic — circular flow with merging — [arXiv HTML v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.13459v4)
- The same survey states "it is typical for SMGs to include around 2-4 agents in the most commonly occurring SMGs," and lists five distinguishing traits of an SMG: mutual occupancy contention, capacity-one resource/bottleneck contention, crossing flows inducing turn-taking, occlusion/limited observability, and conflicting objectives with heterogeneous costs — [arXiv HTML v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.13459v4)
- Formal SMG definition (Definition 1 of the survey): an SMG occurs when "for some δ>0 and integers a,b∈(0,T) with b−a>δ, there exists at least one pair i,j where collision occurs across preferred trajectories for all t∈[a,b]" — i.e., the conflict is defined on *preferred* trajectories persisting over an interval, not on instantaneous proximity — [arXiv HTML v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.13459v4)
- Chandra et al., "Deadlock-free, Safe, and Decentralized Multi-Robot Navigation in Social Mini-Games via Discrete-Time Control Barrier Functions" evaluates in simulation and on physical robots (F1/10, Clearpath Jackal, Boston Dynamics Spot) using exactly three scenes: **doorway, hallway, corridor intersection** — [arXiv:2308.10966](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.10966); companion workshop paper [IROS 2023 MAD-Games](https://iros2023-madgames.f1tenth.org/papers/chandra.pdf)
- SocialGym 2.0 (AAAI 2024; arXiv 2023) ships **five default social environments: Open, Doorway, Hallway, Intersection, Roundabout**, plus UT Austin campus building maps inherited from SocialGym 1.0; it is a MARL benchmark (PettingZoo/Stable-Baselines3, ROS-integrated) for multi-robot policies — [arXiv:2303.05584](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.05584); [AAAI](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/30562); [GitHub](https://github.com/ut-amrl/SocialGym2)
- SEAN 2.0 (IEEE RA-L 2022, Tsoi et al.) formalizes navigation context as *social situations* (robot task + environmental factors) and provides logic-based classifiers for **five common situations**, generated via scripted pedestrians, a Behavior Graph method, and handcrafted scenarios — [IEEE Xplore](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/9851501/); [author PDF](https://www.nathantsoi.com/papers/SEAN_2.0_Formalizing_and_Generating_Social_Situations_for_Robot_Navigation.pdf)
- The pairwise human-robot scenario vocabulary used across social-nav benchmarking (HuNavSim ROS 2 benchmark and related metric papers): **Passing** (pedestrian starts opposite the robot, they move toward and pass each other), **Overtaking** (robot starts behind a slower pedestrian going the same direction), **Crossing** (human moves perpendicular to robot's direction), plus Approaching and Following/Accompanying — [HuNavSim, arXiv:2305.01303](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.01303)
- DynaBARN (IEEE 2023) extends BARN with **60 Gazebo environments** of dynamic obstacles whose motion profiles (speed, trajectory smoothness via polynomial order, accel/decel, count) are systematically varied by difficulty metrics — explicitly motivated by prior benchmarks using only one obstacle-motion model (constant velocity, social force, or ORCA) — [IEEE Xplore](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/10018758/); [project page](https://cs.gmu.edu/~xiao/Research/DynaBARN/DynaBARN.html)
- A cross-community guideline document exists: "Principles and Guidelines for Evaluating Social Robot Navigation Algorithms" (Francis et al., arXiv 2023) — [arXiv:2306.16740](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.16740) (appeared in search results; scenario-level content not extracted in this pass)

### Inferences
- The SMG 9-class list is a superset of every simulator's default scene set; a battery covering doorway, hallway (head-on + same-direction), intersection, one corner variant, and one open/crowded scene would match the field's de facto canon.
- Against the project's battery: hallway head-on is well covered; **doorway (capacity-one aperture with converging flow), L-corner/blind corner (occlusion), roundabout/circular traffic, and crowded open traffic are absent** from resolution-scored scenes. Blind corner is notable for a VLM-based system because it isolates *observability*, which an overhead camera partially removes — arguably out of scope, worth stating explicitly.
- SEAN/HuNavSim's "overtaking" class (same-direction speed mismatch in a corridor) has no analogue in the battery; it is the minimal same-direction-flow scene and a natural low-cost addition.

### Gaps
- The exact names of SEAN 2.0's five formalized social situations were not extracted from the primary PDF (search snippets attribute the passing/crossing/overtaking list partly to secondary sources such as HuNavSim); verify against the RA-L paper before citing the five by name.
- The SMG survey excerpt gave no per-scenario robot counts beyond the general "2–4 agents" statement.

## Key Question 2: Scene classes in MAPF and lifelong-MAPF benchmarks

### Takeaway
The Stern et al. 2019 suite fixes seven grid classes (game, city, maze, room, empty, random, warehouse) as the field standard, with warehouse and 1-cell-corridor maze maps added specifically for congestion stress; the League of Robot Runners adds industrial fulfilment and sortation maps at up to 10,000 agents, and pairwise "corridor symmetry" (two agents swapping through a narrow passage) is the formally identified worst-case primitive.

### Cited Findings
- The Stern et al. 2019 MAPF benchmark (SoCS 2019) contains **33 grids in seven types**: game grids, city maps, maze-like grids, room grids (rooms with narrow doors), empty open grids, random-obstacle grids, and warehouse-inspired grids; a maze map with **1x1 corridors** was added Aug 2019 and **4 warehouse maps** in Nov 2019 — [movingai MAPF benchmarks](https://movingai.com/benchmarks/mapf.html); map-type figure reproduced at [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/figure/An-example-of-a-map-from-each-type-of-maps-available-in-the-grid-MAPF-benchmark_fig3_333915713)
- League of Robot Runners (Harabor et al., ICAPS 2024 demo) draws maps from classical MAPF benchmarks (game, city, random) plus two industrial-inspired domains: **fulfilment** (warehouse order-picking) and **sortation** (package sorting); the sortation map is a 140x500 grid run with 10,000 agents, and a 32x32 random map is run with 800 agents at 97.7% density; problem sizes range 100–10,000 agents — [LoRR ICAPS'24 paper](https://harabor.net/data/papers/lorr-demo-icaps24.pdf); [leagueofrobotrunners.org](https://www.leagueofrobotrunners.org/); [GitHub org](https://github.com/MAPF-Competition)
- Li, Gange, Harabor, Stuckey, Ma, Koenig, "New Techniques for Pairwise Symmetry Breaking in Multi-Agent Path Finding" (ICAPS 2020) formalizes **corridor symmetry**: "corridor symmetry arises when two agents attempt to pass through the same narrow passage in opposite directions," and **target symmetry** (one agent's shortest path crosses another agent's already-occupied goal cell); each causes exponential blow-up in CBS conflict resolution — [ICAPS 2020](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/ICAPS/article/view/6661); journal version [AIJ 2021](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0004370221001259)
- Lifelong-MAPF scaling work identifies congestion and stop-and-go as the emergent failure mode at high agent density in warehouse maps, motivating congestion-avoiding guidance — "Scaling Lifelong Multi-Agent Path Finding to More Realistic Settings" — [arXiv:2404.16162](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.16162)
- Warehouse/AGV traffic-management practice papers use narrow-aisle corridor networks and treat corridor deadlocks asymmetrically: "corridor deadlocks are handled asymmetrically by selecting one corridor side to yield and replan while the opposing side holds position," combined with congestion-aware global planning using directional edge heat maps to bias search away from narrow, frequently used corridors — [arXiv:2609.10400](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.10400) (traffic management for large heterogeneous vehicles in narrow industrial environments)

### Inferences
- MAPF's canonical congestion primitives map onto the project's types: corridor symmetry = the two-robot head-on family (well covered); target symmetry = an agent parked on another's goal (close to the project's "blocked-lane anomaly" but with a robot, not an object, as blocker — an uncovered variant); room-with-narrow-doors = doorway/bottleneck (uncovered); high-density random/sortation = crowding (uncovered).
- MAPF operationalizes "bottleneck" structurally (a map feature: door, 1-wide corridor, aisle) rather than behaviorally; throughput on warehouse maps at rising agent counts is the standard crowding/congestion measurement — this supports scoring bottleneck scenes by throughput/delay rather than by resolution success alone.

### Gaps
- The primary Stern et al. 2019 SoCS paper PDF was not fetched directly; the seven-type inventory comes from the maintained movingai benchmark page and a figure derived from the paper — reliable but the per-type map counts (beyond 33 total) were not extracted.
- No MAPF source found that names a *minimal* scenario subset; MAPF evaluation convention is all 33 maps × scaling agent counts.

## Key Question 3: Operationalizing deadlock vs bottleneck vs crowding vs anomaly; which classes need ≥3 robots

### Takeaway
The field operationalizes deadlock as persistent mutual blocking on preferred/required paths (a liveness violation), bottleneck as contention for a capacity-one map feature measured by throughput/wait, and crowding as density-driven slowdown or freezing without a discrete blocking cycle (the "freezing robot problem"); anomaly-style unexpected blockers live in a separate benchmark line (BARN/DynaBARN-style obstacle courses). Classes that are only meaningful at ≥3 robots: crowding, convoy, circular/roundabout traffic, and multi-arm intersections.

### Cited Findings
- Deadlock (SMG framing): the SMG Definition 1 requires a persistent interval where preferred trajectories collide; a companion "liveness function" measures geometric symmetry ("the lower the liveness function value, the closer the scenario is to an SMG") — deadlock is thus a liveness violation from symmetric configurations, not a proximity event — [arXiv:2508.13459 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.13459v4)
- Deadlock (AGV/warehouse framing): deadlocks are classified by the structure of precedence/wait-for relationships among AGVs — **cyclic** (circular dependency) vs **acyclic** — and are distinguished from congestion, which is recoverable slowdown; "wrong decisions during planning can lead to congestion or in the worst case deadlocks, requiring human intervention" — [MARL deadlock handling among AMRs, arXiv:2511.07071](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.07071); [BDD-based deadlock avoidance for AGVs, Springer 2025](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-032-26204-2_16)
- Bottleneck: the SMG survey names "capacity-one resource/bottleneck contention" as a defining SMG trait separate from crossing flows; the doorway scenario is described as "a bottleneck that tests how well planners manage congestion" — bottleneck = contention at a capacity-one aperture, resolvable by sequencing, measured by flow smoothness — [arXiv:2508.13459 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.13459v4)
- Crowding: the canonical operationalization is the CrowdNav **circle-crossing** scene — agents start on a 4 m-radius circle (positions perturbed ±0.5 m, agent radius 0.3 m) and cross to antipodal goals, typically 5 ORCA-controlled humans + 1 robot; failure manifests as the **freezing robot problem**, "indicated by high timeout rates" when humans are treated as obstacles — [Mixed Strategy Nash Equilibrium for Crowd Navigation, arXiv:2403.01537](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.01537); [Frozone, arXiv:2003.05395](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2003.05395); multi-robot extension in [HeR-DRL, arXiv:2403.10083](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.10083)
- Crowding as fleet-level phenomenon: warehouse fleets at 30/50/100 units show "congestion and stop-and-go behavior" under live demand — a throughput degradation, not a discrete conflict — [arXiv:2404.16162](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.16162)
- Anomaly / unexpected blocker: DynaBARN evaluates navigation against obstacles with systematically varied motion profiles precisely because "a single method... restricts the variety of scenarios," i.e., the anomaly line tests robustness to un-modeled blockers rather than multi-agent conflict — [DynaBARN, IEEE 2023](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/10018758/)
- Robot counts: SMGs typically involve 2–4 agents ([arXiv:2508.13459 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.13459v4)); circle-crossing uses 5+ agents ([arXiv:2403.01537](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.01537)); LoRR crowding regimes use hundreds to 10,000 ([LoRR ICAPS'24](https://harabor.net/data/papers/lorr-demo-icaps24.pdf)); corridor-symmetry deadlock is defined pairwise, on exactly 2 agents ([ICAPS 2020](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/ICAPS/article/view/6661))

### Inferences
- Minimum-robot thresholds by class: head-on corridor deadlock — 2 suffices (matches ICAPS corridor symmetry). Doorway bottleneck — 2 robots can conflict but ≥3 converging robots is what separates *sequencing/throughput* (bottleneck) from *pairwise yield* (deadlock); with only 2 it degenerates into the head-on family. Crowding — needs ≥4–5 agents (circle-crossing convention) so that no single pairwise conflict explains the slowdown. Convoy — needs ≥3 (with 2 it is just following). Cyclic deadlock at an intersection — needs ≥3 (a 3- or 4-cycle of wait-for edges); the project's 2-robot scenes can only produce acyclic/pairwise deadlocks.
- Operational scoring split implied by the literature: deadlock scenes scored by resolution success/liveness restoration; bottleneck scenes by throughput/total wait at the aperture; crowding scenes by slowdown/timeout (freezing) rates; anomaly scenes by detection + reroute success. The project currently scores everything on the deadlock rubric — adding bottleneck/crowding scenes will also require their metrics.

### Gaps
- No single paper found that defines all four of the project's decision types {deadlock, bottleneck, crowding, anomaly} in one taxonomy; the mapping above is assembled across communities (SMG, AGV control, crowd-nav, BARN). The closest single-source discriminator is the SMG survey's five traits.
- The arXiv:2511.07071 full text (grid geometries, robot counts per deadlock scenario) could not be fetched (PDF over fetch size limit; abstract lacks detail).

## Key Question 4: Scenario shapes that elicit convoy/platooning and priority-override as the correct resolution

### Takeaway
Convoy/platooning is elicited by same-direction or counter-flow travel of ≥3 robots through dense or constrained space — the DARS 2024 platooning study shows formation beats independent (greedy) navigation exactly in passive and counter-flow crowds, and loses in perpendicular flow; priority-override is elicited by multi-arm merges (unsignaled intersections, roundabouts, merges, doorways) with asymmetric agents, where auction/priority-ordering methods (GamePlan) are the game-theoretically optimal resolution.

### Cited Findings
- "On the Benefits of Robot Platooning for Navigating Crowded Environments" (DARS 2024) tests three crowd configurations — **passive, counter-flow, and perpendicular-flow crowds**, up to 200 robots — and finds platooning "less disruptive and more effective in dense crowds than the greedy strategy" for passive and counter-flow cases, while greedy wins under perpendicular flow; an adaptive switcher combines both — [arXiv:2410.14406](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.14406)
- Platooning structurally: "a platoon refers to a group of agents that move together in leader-follower pairs while maintaining visibility"; it simplifies communication topology and decentralized control since each agent interacts with at most two others — [arXiv:2410.14406](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.14406)
- Formation-adaptation work tests teams traversing a narrow corridor while maintaining/adapting formations (circle, wedge, **line**) — narrow-corridor traversal by a team is the canonical trigger for collapsing to single-file/line (convoy) formation — [Coordinated Multi-Robot Navigation with Formation Adaptation, arXiv:2404.01618](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2404.01618)
- GamePlan (IEEE RA-L 2022, Chandra & Manocha) targets **unsignaled intersections, roundabouts, and merging**: a game-theoretic auction in which each agent's priority (turn order) is derived from observed driving style, assigning higher priority to more aggressive/impatient agents; "game-theoretically optimal and prevents collisions and deadlocks," reducing deadlocks up to 30% at four-way intersections — [arXiv:2109.01896](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.01896); [UMD ISR summary](https://isr.umd.edu/news/story/algorithm-helps-autonomous-vehicles-navigate-common-tricky-traffic-situations)
- The SMG survey catalogs deadlock-resolution strategies including **Right-Hand-Rule and auctions** (reactive priority assignment) as a distinct family from proactive deadlock prevention (IMPC-DR etc.) — priority-override is thus a recognized resolution class tied to turn-taking scenes — [arXiv:2508.13459 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.13459v4)
- The SMG survey's "Perpendicular Traffic" scenario is explicitly about "crossing paths with right-of-way management," and its "Doorway" scenario "tests prioritization" — [arXiv:2508.13459 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.13459v4)
- In industrial narrow-aisle deadlocks, the fielded resolution is itself a priority assignment: "selecting one corridor side to yield and replan while the opposing side holds position" — [arXiv:2609.10400](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.10400)

### Inferences
- Convoy-eliciting shape for the project: a corridor/aisle with **≥3 same-direction robots meeting opposing traffic** (counter-flow), or a team of ≥3 needing to transit a narrow aisle — convoy is correct when robots share direction and the constraint is width, so ordering them nose-to-tail preserves throughput. The existing queued-third-robot variant is geometrically closest but is framed as a deadlock scene; a same-direction 3-robot aisle transit (no head-on component) would isolate convoy cleanly.
- Priority-override-eliciting shape: a **multi-arm merge with asymmetric agents** — asymmetry in urgency/task priority (GamePlan's driving style), payload, or arrival time is what makes uniform yielding wrong and precedence-granting right. Pure symmetric two-robot head-on scenes cannot distinguish priority_override from yield (one robot yielding *is* the other receiving priority). The project's classification-only 4-way intersection is exactly the geometry the priority literature targets: with sequenced turn-taking commands (rather than the single-yield recovery it was proven unresolvable under), it becomes the natural priority_override elicitor.
- The platooning paper's negative result matters for the decision contract: convoy is the *wrong* answer under perpendicular flow — an intersection scene should never be labeled convoy, which gives a useful negative test for the VLM.

### Gaps
- No warehouse-robot paper found that names "convoy" as a discrete commanded strategy (as opposed to emergent platooning or formation control); the project's convoy strategy as an M3-groundable command appears to have no direct literature template, only the formation/platooning analogues above.
- GamePlan is human-driver-centric (driving-style bids); no found paper auctions priority among homogeneous warehouse AMRs using task urgency — the closest is FIFO arrival-order precedence mentioned as the baseline auction — [UMD ISR](https://isr.umd.edu/news/story/algorithm-helps-autonomous-vehicles-navigate-common-tricky-traffic-situations).

## Key Question 5: Published minimal scenario suites (3–8 scenes) treated as sufficient coverage

### Takeaway
Yes — the field repeatedly accepts small fixed suites: 3 scenes (doorway/hallway/intersection) in the Chandra hardware-validated line, 5 scenes in SocialGym 2.0 (adds Open and Roundabout), 5 formalized situations in SEAN 2.0, and 9 in the SMG survey's diagnostic taxonomy; MAPF instead uses 33 maps of 7 classes with scaling agent counts.

### Cited Findings
- 3-scene suite: doorway, hallway, corridor intersection — used for both simulation and three physical platforms in the DT-CBF deadlock-free navigation paper — [arXiv:2308.10966](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.10966)
- 5-scene suite: Open, Doorway, Hallway, Intersection, Roundabout — SocialGym 2.0 default environments (AAAI 2024) — [arXiv:2303.05584](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.05584)
- 5 formalized social situations with logic-based classifiers — SEAN 2.0 (RA-L 2022) — [IEEE Xplore](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/9851501/)
- 9-scene diagnostic taxonomy: doorway, intersection, hallway, L-corner, blind corner, crowded traffic, parallel traffic, perpendicular traffic, circular traffic — SMG survey, with open-source scenario library **SMGLib** (https://socialminigames.github.io/) — [arXiv:2508.13459 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.13459v4)
- 33-map / 7-class suite (game, city, maze, room, empty, random, warehouse) — Stern et al. 2019, maintained at movingai — [movingai](https://movingai.com/benchmarks/mapf.html)
- 60-environment generated suite (dynamic-obstacle difficulty sweep) — DynaBARN — [project page](https://cs.gmu.edu/~xiao/Research/DynaBARN/DynaBARN.html)
- Scenario-generation-as-coverage alternative: SocRATES (arXiv 2024) automates scenario-based testing of social navigation from context descriptions, implying fixed suites are seen as incomplete — [arXiv:2412.19595](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.19595v1)

### Inferences
- **Coverage-gap verdict for the project's battery** (resolution-scored: two-robot head-on corridor family + queued-third-robot variant + single-robot blocked-lane anomaly; classification-only 4-way intersection):
  1. **Bottleneck type — uncovered.** Canonical elicitor: doorway/narrow-gap (aisle end, charging-bay entrance) with 2–3 robots converging from the *same side or perpendicular* directions, so contention is over sequencing through a capacity-one aperture rather than opposing headings. Precedents: SMG Doorway, SocialGym Doorway, MAPF room-with-narrow-doors.
  2. **Crowding type — uncovered.** Canonical elicitor: ≥4–5 robots in an open zone or wide junction with crossing goals (circle-crossing analogue) or a warehouse block at high robot density; scored by slowdown/timeout, not resolution. Precedents: CrowdNav circle-crossing, SMG Crowded Traffic, LoRR high-density instances.
  3. **Convoy strategy — no elicitor.** Add a ≥3-robot same-direction aisle transit (optionally with counter-flow), where line/platoon ordering is the correct output. Precedent: DARS 2024 platooning (counter-flow condition), formation-adaptation narrow-corridor work.
  4. **Priority_override strategy — no elicitor.** Add asymmetry (task urgency, load, arrival order) to a merge or doorway scene; or upgrade the existing 4-way intersection from classification-only by allowing sequenced turn-taking commands, which is the resolution family (auctions/right-hand-rule) the literature applies to exactly that geometry. Precedent: GamePlan.
  5. Classes present in canonical suites but absent and plausibly optional for an overhead-camera warehouse: blind corner and L-corner (occlusion is mitigated by the overhead view), roundabout/circular traffic (no such geometry in a rack warehouse). Documenting these as deliberate exclusions would make the battery defensible against the SMG 9-class checklist.
  6. The battery's anomaly class (blocked lane) is well precedented but only in the *single-robot* BARN/DynaBARN sense; a multi-robot anomaly (robot queue forming behind a failed robot — target-symmetry analogue) is the missing bridge case between anomaly and bottleneck.
- At 3 scenes with hardware validation (Chandra et al.), the project's current head-on + anomaly battery is not far below published minimal suites in *count* — the gap is class diversity, specifically the absence of any same-direction-flow or density scene.

### Gaps
- No paper found that explicitly argues a given small suite is *sufficient* coverage; suites are presented as representative, and the SocRATES line explicitly argues against fixed-suite sufficiency.
- League of Robot Runners per-map congestion phenomenology (which map class elicits which failure) is not documented in the sources fetched; only aggregate throughput framing.
