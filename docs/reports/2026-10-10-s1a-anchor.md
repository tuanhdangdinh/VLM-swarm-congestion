## Summary

The S1a clogged-regime mini-program staged by the S1b first-light wave ran to completion: **3 episodes (seeds 3, 4, 5), echo mock, suppression + `refusal_no_action` + `escort_turn_cap` on, H = 300 sim-s, $0 API**. One Nav2 bring-up abort (seed 5, attempt 1) was retried cleanly. Everything ran under the pre-registered rule 5 of the VM `NOTES.md` (written 2026-10-09, before any seed-3/4/5 data).

**Every episode ends in permanent latch clusters — the clogged regime is confirmed on all three fresh seeds.** But the seeds differ sharply in how long the floor stays alive:

- **A1 (seed 3) is the first S1 episode in which Layer 2 executed a resolving yield closed-loop in task-stream traffic.** robot_3 yielded, robot_2 passed (`gate_admit` sim 78.2 → `gate_release` sim 123.2, reason `hold_timer`), the pair's latch interval ended, and **2/25 tasks completed** before the floor gridlocked at 147.1 rel-s.
- **A2 (seed 4) and A3 (seed 5) are E2-like fast freezes** (gridlock 37.7 and 24.6 rel-s, 0 tasks each).

Two Layer-2 mechanisms were observed live for the first time:

1. **The yield-infeasible fallback dispatches, and it loops.** When the echo's yield grounds as `yield=infeasible` (A3, ×11) or `retreat=no-valid-candidate` (A3 ×1, A1 ×1), M3 falls back to a `reroute` + waits command that **`refusal_no_action` does not withhold** (it is not a `yield refused:` fan-out) and that the suppression reference never arms on (the outcome is a dispatch). In A3 this re-dispatched a near-identical command to **all five robots every ~30 sim-s to the horizon**: the yielder's empty reroute FAILED each cycle, the four waits COMPLETED. This is the E2 what-if "open risk" (empty-`reroute` fallback) observed live, at full cadence — on the deployed pre-fix tree; the `feat/v3-resolvability` item 3 (`empty_reroute_fallback` withhold; merged as `31c8ce0`, **not deployed to the VM**) targets exactly this class ([§4](#4-surprises-vs-e2s-pattern-step-6--owner-material)).
2. **A dispatched yield is futile when the yielder is latched to a third robot.** A2's one dispatch named robot_1 yielder / robot_5 passer, so the exempt pair was r1+r5 — but robot_1 was latched to **robot_2**. The min-distance hold kept zeroing the backup, no motion occurred, and the bus FAILED the yielder ~15 sim-s later. (This dispatch also carried `grounding=escort_turn_cap:robot_5:2.485->1.558` — the **first live exercise of the escort cap**, albeit on a command that never admitted its passer.)

### A1 narrative (the one resolution)

Three robots drew N first. The W arm clogged immediately (r1/r4/r5 triple latched by 18.6 and never released), while r2 and r3 queued at N. The third analysis (27.4 rel-s) dispatched the echo yield: robot_3 backed off 2.35 m, robot_2's N arrival was credited when the gate admitted it (it was already at the node's doorstep), robot_3 then reached N itself. Both next legs ran **west into the frozen W-arm triple**: r2 latched to r1 at 113.6, r3 latched to r2 at 147.2, completing a single 5-robot component. The gate released on the 45 sim-s `hold_timer`, not clearance. One stale re-arrival log line (robot_3 "arrived" at its previous node) fired inside the empty-reroute window — an M5 arrival-accounting quirk, not a task event.

### Why A2's dispatched yield did nothing

The dispatch came 0.6 sim-s after the r1+r2 latch formed. The exemption is pair-scoped (`exempt pairs = robot_1+robot_5`), so the r1+r2 min-distance violation kept zeroing robot_1's backup; the command bus FAILED the yielder after ~15 sim-s of no motion and the passer's wait COMPLETED. A yield dispatched to a yielder already latched outside the yielder–passer pair cannot execute.

## 4. Surprises vs E2's Pattern (Step-6 / Owner Material)

1. **Seed variance is large.** E2 suggested S1a freezes in ~25 sim-s with Layer 2 irrelevant. Seed 3 shows the other tail: a resolving yield executed closed-loop, 2 tasks, and a floor that stayed partly alive to 147 rel-s. The anchor's time-to-gridlock spans an order of magnitude over 6 episodes, so single-episode comparisons on this axis are uninformative; outcome_class + the gridlock time together are the usable row.
2. **The dispatched-fallback loop, live-confirmed — and already addressed offline.** `refusal_no_action` closed the *withheld* loop; A3 shows the *dispatched* loop on the deployed (pre-fix) tree: `yield=infeasible` / `retreat=no-valid-candidate` fall back to `reroute`+waits, which dispatch, fail or no-op, cool down, and re-dispatch every ~30 sim-s to the horizon (12 commands, 60 robot-commands, in one episode). Each cycle cancels and re-issues held Nav2 goals on all five robots — the E1 harm pattern in a dispatched guise. The `feat/v3-resolvability` work (its item 3, `empty_reroute_fallback`, implementation note 2026-10-10; merged into `feat/v3-integration` as `31c8ce0` during this session, **not deployed to the VM**) withholds exactly this class on the same `retreat=no-valid-candidate` / `yield=infeasible:` tags; A3 is the live evidence of what it prevents and a ready-made regression scenario for it. The VM ran the staged pre-fix tree deliberately, to stay comparable with E2.
3. **Exemption is pair-scoped but latches are not.** A2's yielder was latched to a third robot, so the exempt clearing manoeuvre could not move — a second mechanism (beyond refusal) by which latch clusters defeat Layer 2. Any latch-aware release design must exempt the yielder against *all* its latch partners, not only the passer.
4. **`escort_turn_cap` exercised live for the first time** (A2: 2.485 → 1.558 m), on a command that then failed for the unrelated reason above. Still unexercised on a successful pass (A1's yield had `escort_m=None`).
5. **The `hold_timer` released A1's gate, not clearance** — the passer completed, the yielder held to the 45 sim-s backstop, then both resumed into the frozen W arm. Consistent with the units ruling (sim-s backstop); no violation.
