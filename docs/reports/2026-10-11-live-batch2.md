**The verdict is sensitive to the rule.** By the pre-A24 rule, all three opportunity episodes would read ARCH, giving LIVELOCK, 3 of 3. **The A24 conjunction decides it.** The facts the conjunction is built on are these:

- **The pattern is escalation, not oscillation.** Across 12 live episodes and 23 clearing dispatches, not one A-B-A alternation occurred. The live "intermittent" episodes are escalation sequences: a different strategy each time, on a growing cluster (yield → reverse → multi-robot reverse). E3's livelock was period-2 oscillation (alternation 6).
- **The grounder shaped many dispatches.** 6 of the 10 clearing dispatches in E13, E16 and E17 were reshaped by M3 (repaired, or vb-mismatched), and one more (E17 `6745a136`) was M3's own default grounding. This includes the one productive E17 event.
- **The executor shaped the outcomes.** Three of the unproductive yields (E13 `984e3bdf`, E17 `6745a136` and `6d37a549`) FAILED in execution without admit (§5.3).

**These three episodes fall in a gap the rules do not cover.**

- Under A24 none of them can be ARCH, because each has 0 alternations.
- Rule 11's APPARATUS clause requires "class not intermittent", so they do not fit that either.
- Labelling them APPARATUS is this scorer's residual choice (`attribution_verdict`: opportunity without ARCH is APPARATUS). It is not a registered rule.

The pooled verdict holds by the letter of A24's pooled rule: 0 ARCH and at least 1 opportunity episode give NO LIVELOCK. The two readings, side by side:

| Reading | E13, E16, E17 | Pooled |
|---|---|---|
| A24 conjunction | not ARCH; episode class unregistered (scored APPARATUS) | **NO LIVELOCK** (3 opportunity, 0 ARCH) |
| Rule 11 (pre-A24) | ARCH (`intermittent`) | **LIVELOCK**, 3 of 3 |

**OWNER RULING NEEDED:** is non-oscillating escalation churn (`intermittent`, opportunity met, 0 alternations) APPARATUS, or a third class? The data says "no oscillation under a real model". Whether escalation without progress counts as architecture churn is a definitional choice.

### 5.2 (b) The Productive-resolution Distribution (the Headline)

**Bar and derivation, stated in rule 14 before any batch-2 data and before this computation.**

- **Bar:** at least 8 classified S1/S1b episodes (echo + live, the rule-11 population) with a nonzero productive count.
- **If the bar is met:**
  - `budget_k` = the nearest-rank p95 of per-event dispatches-into-group;
  - `window_sim_sec` = max(p95 dispatch-to-progress latency, the longest inter-dispatch gap in a productive group), rounded up to 10 s;
  - `alternation_max` = the maximum alternations in a productive group;
  - the triple is accepted only if a ChurnLedger replay withholds no productive event and does withhold inside E3.
- **If the bar is not met:** report n_more = ceil((8 − k_nonzero) / r_live).

|---|---|---|---|---|
| All classified S1/S1b (12 echo/pilot + 12 live) | 24 | 19 × 0, 4 × 1 (A1, E10, E11, E17), 1 × 2 (E6) | **5** | **1** |
| Live only | 12 | 2, 0, 0, 1*, 0, 1, 0, 0, 0, 0, 0, 1* | 4 | 2 |

| Alternations inside groups with a productive event | 6 groups | all 0 | — | max 0 |

**Verdict: the bar is NOT met (5 < 8), so no triple is recommended.**

- **The more-data number.** The live nonzero rate pooled over E6-E17 is r_live = 4/12. That gives **n_more = ceil((8 − 5) / (4/12)) = 9 more live episodes**. At the observed median of $0.030 and about 56 min each, that is roughly $0.27-0.45 and 9 VM-hours.
- **The rate is falling.** It went from 3/6 (E6-E11) to 1/6 in batch 2. If batch 2's 1/6 is the better estimate, the number is ceil(3 / (1/6)) = 18.
- **The 9 is an expected value.** With p = 1/3, nine episodes give at least 3 more nonzero episodes only about 62 % of the time; about 15 are needed for 90 %.
- **It also assumes yield execution stays as it is.** 6 of 9 dispatched live yields FAILED without admit (§5.3), and three of them are among the unproductive events of E13, E14 and E17. A yield-execution fix could thicken the distribution faster than more episodes would.
- **Indicative only, not a recommendation.** On the current six productive events, the derivation would give (budget_k 3, window 130 s, alternation_max 0). The window comes from the longest inter-dispatch gap in a productive group (E6, 122.6 s), which exceeds the 70.4 s latency p95. That triple passes the acceptance replay: it withholds no productive event, withholds E3 twice (alternation, from its first A-B-A), and withholds 6 of A3's 12 identical reroutes (`batch2/triple_indicative_3_130_0.json`). The bar exists precisely because n = 6 events cannot defend p95 = 3: one more 3-dispatch event moves it.
