# VLM Coordinators Referee Fleets Classical Planners Still Execute

**Prepared:** 2026-10-01. Synthesized from four research-note files in `research_notes/VLM centralized multi robot coordination/` (centralized LLM/VLM planners; image-in-the-loop VLM coordination; verify-repair layers; classical baselines).

The literature has converged on exactly the division of labor this thesis proposes — a generative model supplies semantic intent while classical machinery owns executability — but no published system occupies the thesis's specific cell: a VLM reading a **real overhead warehouse camera** and emitting **scene-wide ordered recovery plans** that a **classical module verifies and minimally repairs with logged provenance**, compared head-to-head against a **literature-grounded rule-based coordinator** on identical Nav2 scenarios. The nearest neighbor, Garg et al.'s foundation-model deadlock resolver (MIT-REALM), validates the exact architectural pattern — velocity-triggered deadlock detection, top-view image to a VLM, safe low-level executor — but in simulation with synthetic top-views, emitting only leader-plus-waypoints, with no congestion-type taxonomy, no repair provenance, and no rule-based comparison arm ([arXiv:2404.06413](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.06413); [PMLR 2026 PDF](https://mit-realm.github.io/VLM-gcbfplus-website/files/Online_planning_using_VLMs.pdf)). Prompted LLMs demonstrably lose to classical solvers at path-level multi-agent planning ([arXiv:2401.03630](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.03630)), which is why the field's winning niche — and the thesis's — is the **event-triggered semantic referee over a classical execution layer**. The thesis's sharpest genuinely novel contributions are (a) the three-way per-leg provenance metric (verbatim/repaired/defaulted), which no embodied-planning paper reports despite its formal ancestor existing since 2006 as "plan stability," and (b) the matched two-arm VLM-vs-rule-based comparison on identical warehouse deadlock scenarios, which no paper in the sweep attempts. What it cannot claim as novel: centralized deadlock recovery per se, the fast/slow two-rate split, or LLM-vs-classical comparison in general.

## Centralized LLM topologies: CMAS wins where global state is cheap

The standard vocabulary comes from Chen et al. (ICRA 2024), who name three topologies: **CMAS** (one central LLM emits actions for all robots), **DMAS** (per-robot LLM agents in turn-taking dialog), and hybrids **HMAS-1/2** (central proposer plus local LLM checkers) ([arXiv:2309.15943](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.15943)). The thesis architecture — one central VLM over per-robot Nav2 clients — is CMAS at the semantic layer and should cite this framing directly. Landmark centralized systems are SMART-LLM (staged decomposition → coalition → allocation under few-shot prompting, [arXiv:2309.10062](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.10062)), LaMMA-P (LLM allocation feeding a Fast Downward PDDL planner, [arXiv:2409.20560](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.20560)), COHERENT (central assigner in a Proposal-Execution-Feedback-Adjustment loop, [arXiv:2409.15146](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.15146)), DART-LLM (central DAG decomposition, [arXiv:2411.09022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.09022)), and Co-NavGPT — notable as a **single VLM acting as global planner**, merging multi-robot sub-maps and assigning frontiers ([arXiv:2310.07937](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.07937)). Dialog systems (RoCo [arXiv:2307.04738](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.04738), CoELA [arXiv:2307.02485](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.02485), GameChat [arXiv:2503.12333](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.12333v1)) dominate tabletop and household settings; centralized single-model systems dominate wherever a global state summary is cheap to construct — maps, frontier lists, warehouse grids — which is precisely the overhead-camera warehouse case. The evidence now favors the centralized side outright: "centralized methods, where a single LLM handles planning, have been proven superior in terms of success rate and token efficiency," while decentralized dialog suffers prompt-length blowup as robots and rounds grow ([arXiv:2603.02669](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.02669); survey [arXiv:2502.03814](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.03814v1)).

Critically, **almost no credible system lets the central model emit motion-level commands**. The consensus output is allocation plus ordered subgoals, waypoints, or priority designations, refined by classical machinery: LaMMA-P generates PDDL problems that Fast Downward sequences; TwoStep's LLM only splits goals into subgoals ([arXiv:2403.17246](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.17246)); LLMDR's LLM classifies which MAPF agents are deadlocked and suggests priority strategies that classical PIBT executes ([arXiv:2503.00717](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.00717)); Garg et al.'s foundation model outputs only a leader assignment and waypoints for a GNN low-level policy. The counter-example proves the rule: a central LLM made to output MAPF move sequences directly "fail[s] to plan on the harder room map and maze map of the standard MAPF benchmark," succeeding only on obstacle-free rooms ([arXiv:2401.03630](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2401.03630)). Documented failure modes for central-model sequencing — **degradation beyond ~6 agents in centralized architectures, GPT-4 latency of 15–30 s per planning step, faithfulness hallucinations, up to 65% accuracy drops as problem clauses accumulate** ([arXiv:2502.03814](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.03814v1)) — jointly justify keeping the central model at the semantic/arbitration level on a slow, event-triggered loop. Together, LLMDR and Garg et al. establish "LLM/VLM as deadlock arbiter over a non-LLM execution layer" as a recognized niche; the thesis's M2 congestion planner is a VLM-grounded, scene-wide instance of it. One articulation gap worth noting: no surveyed system maintains a persistent central plan that the model incrementally edits against a task registry — closed-loop systems either re-propose wholesale each round (COHERENT's PEFA) or intervene only on events (Garg, LLMDR).

## Image-in-the-loop coordination is thin, and VLM spatial limits dictate the design

Systems that feed an actual scene image to a VLM for multi-agent decisions form a small cluster, and exactly one published line both detects and resolves deadlock from images: Garg et al., extended to the PMLR 2026 paper. Its mechanisms map one-to-one onto the thesis: the VLM is used **reactively, not proactively** — triggered only when average fleet speed falls below a threshold while distance-to-goal remains high — so "the VLM does not lead to a violation of safety as it is taken care of by the provably safe low-level control policy"; the prompt is a base64 top-view image with agent locations, goals, and LiDAR-observed obstacles; experiments span **5–50 agents**, with VLM planners beating LLM-only planners and A* on goal-reaching rate within a time budget ([PMLR 2026 PDF](https://mit-realm.github.io/VLM-gcbfplus-website/files/Online_planning_using_VLMs.pdf); [OpenReview](https://openreview.net/pdf?id=vz1p8TRRAb)). This is convergent architecture and must be cited as such. The sharpest contrast point is the only field-deployed warehouse multi-robot-from-cameras system: Oxford's CCTV work (4 robots, 30 cameras, 6 × 27 m aisles) **deliberately uses classical perception, not a VLM**, preventing deadlocks via image-space shared-resource locking across overlapping views ([arXiv:2606.06762](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.06762)). It proves the deployment context is real while leaving semantic congestion-type reasoning — why robots are stuck, which resolution strategy fits — unattempted. Traffic signal control supplies the most mature image-in-the-loop precedent and the same two-layer lesson: VLMLight routes routine traffic to a fast RL policy and critical cases to slow VLM deliberation, cutting emergency-vehicle waiting time 65% with under 1% routine degradation ([arXiv:2505.19486](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.19486)). The embodied-reasoning model families (Gemini Robotics-ER [arXiv:2503.20020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.20020), [arXiv:2510.03342](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.03342); Cosmos-Reason1 [arXiv:2503.15558](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.15558) and Reason2) claim spatial *primitives* — pointing, localization, success detection — but **none claims multi-robot coordination, congestion, or right-of-way arbitration**; using one as a swarm-coordination backbone is applying it one level above its evaluated scope, defensible as a novel application study.

The documented spatial-reasoning failure profile converts three thesis design choices from ad hoc to literature-grounded. VLMs hover near chance on left/right discrimination (best model **55.33%** on LRR-Bench) and perform "close to random guessing" on relative movement across image sequences where humans reach ~90% ([arXiv:2507.20174](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.20174v1)); they carry strong positional priors (upper-left/upper-right biases by model family, [arXiv:2510.26781](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.26781)), confuse vertical image position with distance ([arXiv:2605.30161](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.30161)), fail temporal integration ([arXiv:2408.07867](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.07867)), and fail counting and temporal ordering "trivial for humans" ([arXiv:2509.22737](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.22737)). This motivates: a single annotated keyframe plus numeric state vector rather than video; a discrete strategy menu rather than free-form geometry; and a downstream grounding layer that assumes VLM spatial output may be infeasible — Garg et al. themselves pre-test whether models can locate obstacles and generate viable waypoints before trusting them. The set-of-mark line supplies the prompting rationale: MOKA's explicit design principle that "VLMs are better at multiple-choice problems than directly producing continuous-valued locations" ([arXiv:2403.03174](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.03174); SoM [arXiv:2310.11441](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.11441); PIVOT [arXiv:2402.07872](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.07872), ID to re-verify) reframes M1's annotated frame — status-colored boxes, IDs, velocity arrows — as visual prompt engineering for action selection. No published work applies SoM-style marking to multi-robot congestion scenes; that extension is claimable. The project's own pre-registered findings (gpt-4o first-slot presentation-order prior and robot_1 token prior; Gemini ER-2's systematic `reverse` opener with geometry unused) replicate the published position-bias results in the thesis's exact task, but must be flagged as own-work evidence, not literature.

## Propose-verify-repair exists everywhere; minimal classical repair and provenance reporting do not

The 2022–2026 verification canon splits into four families: LLM-as-translator into a sound symbolic planner (LLM+P [arXiv:2304.11477](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.11477), ViLaIn [arXiv:2311.00967](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2311.00967), LaMMA-P); feasibility gating *during* generation (SayCan [PMLR v205](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v205/ichter23a/ichter23a.pdf), Grounded Decoding [arXiv:2303.00855](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.00855)); LLM/TAMP hybrids with formal checkers (AutoTAMP [arXiv:2306.06531](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.06531), SayPlan [arXiv:2307.06135](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.06135)); and the generate-test-critique position crystallized as LLM-Modulo ([arXiv:2402.01817](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.01817)). The thesis's M3 — post-hoc, leg-by-leg audit of a complete VLM plan for multi-robot navigation — sits in a cell these families bracket but none occupies; VLM-side verification (ViLaIn, ReplanVLM, VeriGraph [arXiv:2411.10446](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.10446)) is single-robot and manipulation-centric. On the repair question, the field's taxonomy has an empty third cell: nearly every LLM-robotics system chooses **re-prompt repair** — the LLM fixes its own plan guided by validator error messages (ISR-LLM [arXiv:2308.13724](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2308.13724), CoPAL [arXiv:2310.07263](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.07263v3), AutoTAMP, LLM-Modulo, ReplanVLM [arXiv:2407.21762](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.21762)) — or **reject-and-escalate** (SayCan gating; KnowNo's conformal-prediction ask-for-help [PMLR v229](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v229/ren23a.html)). **Deterministic minimal repair by a non-LLM module is essentially absent from the LLM era**; it lives instead in the classical "plan stability" tradition, where Fox et al. (ICAPS 2006) defined stability as the difference between original and repaired plan and showed repair beats replanning on it ([Strathprints PDF](https://strathprints.strath.ac.uk/2776/1/strathprints002776.pdf)). A classical M3 performing bounded, auditable edits — swap, clamp, default — against RoCo's expensive, unbounded re-prompting loop is the clean differentiation, and RoCo is the single most important related work to differentiate against since it already closes a multi-robot propose-verify loop with collision checking ([arXiv:2307.04738](https://arxiv.org/html/2307.04738)).

On provenance as a first-class metric, the answer after a targeted sweep is **no published system logs per-leg provenance (verbatim/repaired/defaulted) of a model-proposed plan and reports it as an experimental quantity**. Four partial precedents each capture one slice: ViLaIn reports stage-wise survival (syntactic correctness >99% vs valid plans >58%, [ICRA 2024 PDF](https://www.lsta.media.kyoto-u.ac.jp/publications/shirai-ICRA2024.pdf)); ReplanVLM reports error detection 95.7% and correction 80% — repair success, not preservation ([arXiv:2407.21762](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.21762)); RoboGuard reports unsafe-plan execution falling from **92% to below 3%** under its temporal-logic shield — binary per plan, no breakdown ([arXiv:2503.07885](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.07885)); and classical plan stability predates LLMs entirely. The field itself notes the measurement gap: whole-plan validity rate is criticized as "inadequate for differentiating between the performances of various strategies" ([arXiv:2412.10675](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2412.10675)). The winning framing: **plan stability (Fox et al. 2006) transplanted to the VLM-proposer setting and refined to a three-way provenance label** — citing precedent while sharpening novelty against the obvious reviewer objection. Two hardening steps remain: check whether RoCo's tables report replan counts as a headline number, and sweep "intervention/override rate" vocabulary in AV-safety literature, where an equivalent statistic could hide under different words. On the shield side, M4's safety invariants map to the CBF/safety-filter layer ([arXiv:2311.13714](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.13714)) and M3 to the plan-level logic-shield layer (Safety Chip [arXiv:2309.09919](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2309.09919), RoboGuard); published systems rarely have both layers *and* report what each rejected.

## Classical baselines prevent; the published recovery playbook is thin

The dominant classical pattern is a central coordinator allocating space-time resources while robots execute locally: Kiva/Amazon's central software assigns pods, stations, and paths across **500+ vehicles** while "the Kiva robots are essentially independent" ([AI Magazine 2008](https://www.dcsc.tudelft.nl/~sc4081/2018/assign/pap/robert_kiva_paper.pdf)); RHCR decomposes lifelong MAPF into windowed instances, replanning every h timesteps and scaling to **1,000 agents** ([arXiv:2005.07371](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.07371)); the competitive central-planner cadence for 500-agent warehouses is ~1 Hz under a 1-second compute budget ([arXiv:2512.03194](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2512.03194)). Two consequences follow. First, the thesis's 0.5–2 Hz slow layer is **not novel as a rate** — RHCR and the three-layer AGV traffic-management architecture ([IEEE T-ASE 2023](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/10132864/)) already operate there, and fast/slow VLM stacks are standard in adjacent domains (VLM-UDMC's 0.3–1.5 s VLM over a 20 Hz controller, [arXiv:2507.15266](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.15266); VLMLight) — novelty must rest on *what* the slow layer decides. Second, the classical coordinator decides allocations and priorities, **never semantic maneuver commands**; yield/reroute/convoy outcomes emerge implicitly from reservation denial. The deadlock literature is overwhelmingly avoidance/prevention — banker's-algorithm variants ([Springer 1998](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1023/A:1007969601583)), zone-control circular-wait blocking, time-expanded graphs — while published *recovery* of formed deadlocks is sparse: Jäger & Nebel's replan-alternatives (IROS 2001, [PDF](https://gki.informatik.uni-freiburg.de/papers/jaeger-nebel-iros01.pdf)), Grover et al.'s provably correct but decentralized resolution for point robots (IJRR 2023, [arXiv:2206.01781](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.01781)), and A-DRIVE's detect-and-back-up at structured intersections ([IV 2022](https://dl.acm.org/doi/abs/10.1109/IV51971.2022.9827436)). None is a centralized, perception-driven, commanded recovery over a Nav2-class stack. Industrial recovery procedures (timeout rollback, dispatcher override) exist only as vendor anecdote — do not cite.

This thinness cuts both ways: it supports the thesis's novelty but also means the rule-based comparison arm must be assembled, not adopted. The defensible assembly is fully citable component-by-component: conflict detection from zone-control circular-wait tests, yielder selection by **FCFS per Dresner & Stone's AIM** — where FCFS reservation granting alone beats optimized signals ([JAIR 2008](https://www.cs.utexas.edu/~aim/papers/JAIR08-dresner.pdf)) — and commanded retreat per A-DRIVE. Framing the classical arm as "AIM-style FCFS yielding + A-DRIVE-style backup" makes it a literature-grounded representative of its family, not a strawman. On head-to-heads, the record shows LLMs losing to classical solvers at path-level MAPF (Chen/Koenig/Dilkina), foundation models *helping* otherwise-strong low-level policies escape deadlocks (LLMDR over PIBT; Garg et al. over GCBF+), and general LLM-vs-classical-planner benchmarks ([arXiv:2507.23589](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.23589)) — but **no two-arm comparison of a central VLM congestion resolver against a rule-based classical reasoner on the same embodied warehouse scenarios**. Nearest neighbors compare LLM-aided vs un-aided learned policies or prompt variants. DeepFleet marks the industrial ceiling from the other direction: Amazon trains fleet foundation models (not LLMs/VLMs) on 700K–5M robot-hours for congestion forecasting ([arXiv:2508.08574](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.08574)), confirming industry itself is moving from hand-built allocators toward learned central coordination.

## Positioning: four claims to make, three to refuse

The thesis should position itself in one sentence pattern the notes support verbatim: *prior image-in-the-loop work either resolves deadlock geometrically (leader + waypoints, Garg et al.) or prevents it with image-space resource locks (Robinson et al.); none classifies congestion type from a real overhead camera and selects among semantic resolution strategies executed through a verification layer.* The defensible novelty claims, each with its guard phrasing:

| # | Claim | Basis | Nearest prior to cite and differentiate |
|---|-------|-------|------------------------------------------|
| 1 | First VLM coordinator consuming a **real physical overhead warehouse camera** frame (set-of-mark annotated) for fleet-level congestion resolution with an explicit strategy taxonomy | Garg et al. is simulation with LiDAR-synthetic top-views; Oxford CCTV is non-VLM | [arXiv:2404.06413](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.06413); [arXiv:2606.06762](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.06762) |
| 2 | First **deterministic, non-LLM minimal repair** of a VLM-proposed multi-robot plan, with **three-way per-leg provenance (verbatim/repaired/defaulted) as a first-class reported metric** — plan stability transplanted to the VLM-proposer era | Empty cell in the repair taxonomy; no embodied paper reports preservation statistics | Fox et al. 2006 ([PDF](https://strathprints.strath.ac.uk/2776/1/strathprints002776.pdf)); RoboGuard; ReplanVLM |
| 3 | First **matched two-arm comparison** of a central VLM congestion resolver vs a literature-grounded rule-based coordinator (AIM-FCFS + A-DRIVE backup) on identical Nav2 warehouse deadlock scenarios | No such head-to-head found after multiple query variants | [arXiv:2401.03630](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.03630); LLMDR; Garg et al. |
| 4 | Scene-wide **ordered plan** output (vs single leader+waypoints or single-robot priority hints) from the VLM, audited leg-by-leg | Garg emits leader+waypoints; LLMDR emits priority strategies | Same |

Claims to refuse, because reviewers will find the prior art immediately: centralized or commanded deadlock recovery per se (A-DRIVE, LLMDR, Garg et al.); the fast/slow two-rate architecture or the 0.5–2 Hz cadence (RHCR, VLM-UDMC, VLMLight, [arXiv:2606.20458](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.20458v1)); LLM-vs-classical comparison in general; and propose-verify loops over multi-robot motion (RoCo already does this — the differentiator is *who repairs and whether it is logged*, not that verification exists). Three epistemic caveats belong in the thesis text: absence-of-evidence claims rest on 2026-10-01 searches and an equivalent provenance statistic could hide under "intervention rate" vocabulary; several load-bearing papers still need full-PDF metric extraction before the related-work chapter is final (Garg et al.'s model table and metrics, LLMDR's metrics, Grover et al., RoCo's replan-count tables); and a handful of IDs are known only via the survey or prior knowledge and need verification (GameChat's venue, PIVOT 2402.07872, SpatialVLM 2401.12168, VADER, SELP). Terminology to adopt for discoverability: "CMAS," "fast–slow (dual-system) architecture," "event-triggered high-level replanning," "plan stability."

## Conclusion

The literature's trajectory makes the thesis's architecture look less like a bet and more like the field's converged design applied to an unoccupied domain cell: every strand — topology comparisons favoring centralized single-model coordination, documented VLM spatial failures forcing discrete menus and side-channel state, the universal proposer/verifier split, and classical coordinators already running at ~1 Hz — independently arrives at "semantic referee above, sound machinery below." What the field has conspicuously not done is *measure the seam between the layers*. Verification is everywhere as a mechanism and nowhere as a reported quantity; that inversion — treating provenance as the experiment rather than the plumbing — is the thesis's most defensible and least crowded contribution, more so than the warehouse deployment itself, because it is a metric other groups can adopt regardless of domain.

The two-arm design also carries an underappreciated hedge: because the rule-based arm is assembled from the strongest citable classical components rather than a strawman, *either* outcome is publishable — a VLM win demonstrates semantic scene understanding earning its latency cost, while a classical win would be the first controlled evidence that the foundation-model-referee niche (LLMDR, Garg et al.) does not survive contact with literature-grade rule-based baselines on embodied warehouse scenarios. The provenance log is what makes the null result interpretable: it distinguishes "the VLM proposes well but repair erases its contribution" from "the VLM's surviving decisions genuinely outperform FCFS," a decomposition no prior comparison can make.
