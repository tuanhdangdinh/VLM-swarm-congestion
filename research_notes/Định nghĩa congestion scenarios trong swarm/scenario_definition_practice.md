# How Papers Formally Define and Construct Congestion/Deadlock Scenarios (Multi-Robot & Swarm)

Scope: catalog of scenario-definition practice across MAPF/MAPD benchmarks, swarm clogging physics, multi-robot deadlock papers, and navigation benchmarks. Years noted per paper. Researched 2026-10-06.

A quick taxonomy of "what formal object a scenario is" that emerged from this research:

1. **Benchmark instance** (MAPF): a tuple (graph/grid map, agent start/goal list), distributed as map + `.scen` files; difficulty is an emergent property measured post-hoc by solver success rate vs agent count — Stern et al. 2019.
2. **Generator + difficulty ordering** (BARN, MRPB): procedurally generated or hand-designed static worlds, ranked by geometric difficulty metrics; congestion is encoded as obstacle clearance, not as multi-agent interaction.
3. **Physical control-parameter sweep** (swarm clogging physics): a fixed geometry (ring track, aperture, tunnel) with density or aperture/body-size ratio as the controlled parameter; congestion is defined by a measured phase transition on the fundamental diagram or clog statistics — Zuriguel-style, Aguilar, single-file robot swarms.
4. **Canonical staged conflict geometry** (deadlock papers): hand-crafted doorway/hallway/intersection "mini-games" or symmetric initial conditions that provably or empirically force deadlock — Grover et al., Chandra et al.
5. **Task-stream regime** (MAPD/lifelong MAPF): scenario = map + online task arrival process + agent count; congestion emerges from throughput saturation rather than being staged; solvability is guaranteed by structural "well-formedness" conditions — Ma et al. 2017.

## KQ1: What are the standard canonical congestion scenarios recurring across papers (head-on corridor, doorway/aperture, intersection, ring exchange, bottleneck funnel), and their typical dimensions relative to robot size?

### Takeaway
A small set of geometries recurs across communities: doorway/aperture, hallway (head-on corridor), corridor intersection, circular/ring track, and bottleneck funnel into a common target. Where dimensions are stated, the critical regime is consistently ~1–2 robot/body widths of passage (tunnel = 2 ant body widths in Aguilar; "corridors of width 1" grid cells in MAPF; stop-distance 15 cm between single-file robots).

### Cited Findings
- **Doorway, hallway, corridor intersection** are formalized as the three "social mini-games" — "non-cooperative, non-communicative, partially observable multi-robot navigation problem[s] in constrained spaces with multiple conflicting agents" — evaluated on F1/10 robots, Clearpath Jackal, and Boston Dynamics Spot (Chandra et al., 2023–2024) — [arXiv 2308.10966](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.10966)
- MAPF literature identifies deadlocks as occurring "most commonly in narrow corridors, where there is not enough room for vehicles to move past each other"; experience-based MAPF explicitly treats "narrow corridors of width 1 (also known as doorways)" as the canonical hard structure (RSS 2024) — [Experience-based MAPF with narrow corridors](https://roboticsconference.org/program/papers/87); [RSS proceedings p087](https://roboticsproceedings.org/rss20/p087.html)
- **Four-robot intersection deadlock**: industrial multi-robot traffic control documents describe the canonical case of four robots on bidirectional edges at an intersection all instructed to turn left, producing deadlock/collision — [USPTO: Roadmap annotation for deadlock-free multi-agent navigation](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/10642282)
- **Bottleneck/tunnel (aperture) with explicit width-to-body ratio**: Aguilar et al. (Science, 2018) used excavation tunnels of width **2 ant body widths (BW)**; lab ants excavating their own tunnels selected a similar width/density, and robophysical analogs reproduced the clogging regime — [Aguilar et al., Science 361:672 (2018), DOI 10.1126/science.aan3891](https://crablab.gatech.edu/pages/publications/pdf/science-2018.pdf)
- **Circular/ring single-file track**: Zuriguel-group robot-swarm experiment uses Alphabot2 (Raspberry Pi) robots on a circular track, line-following in single file, each robot programmed to **stop when it detects another robot within 15 cm**; density is varied by robot count on the fixed track (2024) — [Single file motion of robot swarms, arXiv 2403.08683](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.08683)
- **Funnel into a common target area**: dos Passos et al. formalize congestion around a shared circular target region and derive three canonical access geometries — parallel queues toward the target, hexagonal packing through a corridor, and multiple curved ("touch and run") trajectories to the target boundary (2022) — [On the throughput of the common target area for robotic swarm strategies, arXiv 2201.09335](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2201.09335); [Congestion control algorithms..., arXiv 2201.09337](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2201.09337)
- **Grid benchmark topologies** (MAPF): the Stern et al. 2019 benchmark spans 24 maps in six families — city maps (Berlin/Boston/Paris, 256×256), Dragon Age Origins game maps (81×65 to 656×1491), Dragon Age 2 maps, open grids (8×8 to 48×48), open grids + random obstacles (32×32, 64×64), and maze/room grids (32×32 to 128×128); mazes and rooms supply width-1 corridor topology — [Stern et al., Multi-Agent Pathfinding: Definitions, Variants, and Benchmarks (SoCS 2019), ar5iv 1906.08291](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1906.08291)
- Lifelong-MAPF experimental sections use named large instances "Random 800, Game 4000, Warehouse 8000, and Sortation 10000" (map type + agent count), i.e., warehouse/sortation-center grids at thousands of agents (2026) — [Lifelong LaCAM with Local Guidance, arXiv 2605.16855](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.16855)
- **Symmetric circle/antipodal exchange** as the canonical reciprocal-avoidance deadlock scenario: ORCA "usually fails to plan reasonable collision avoidance velocities" when "robots distribute symmetrically," motivating a dedicated literature on the ORCA deadlock problem — [Addressing the deadlock problem of ORCA, Robotica (Cambridge)](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/robotica/article/abs/addressing-the-deadlock-problem-of-the-optimal-reciprocal-collision-avoidance-algorithm-in-distributed-multirobot-navigation/70CEA27CFAE13D30DD3ABCD189F9019D)
- One search-surfaced analysis reports deadlocks occur "systematically across all symmetric scenarios for n ∈ [2, 30] agents when exact rotational symmetry is preserved," especially with small time steps or slow agents (deterministic simulators) — surfaced in search results alongside [arXiv 2507.20293](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.20293); attribution to a specific paper could not be confirmed from the fetched material (see Gaps).

### Inferences
- The communities converge on the same ~5 geometries, but parameterize them differently: physics papers parameterize by aperture-to-body-size ratio and density; MAPF papers by graph topology (corridor width in cells) and agent count; deadlock-control papers by symmetric initial/goal placement.
- "Width ≈ 1–2 robot widths" is the implicit shared definition of a *critical* passage: width 1 forces strict ordering (pure deadlock structure), width ~2 permits clogging-arch formation (stochastic clogging), width >2 is free-flow-capable. This matches the project's existing 0.55 m aisle for ~0.25–0.3 m robots (≈2 robot widths — clogging regime, not pure width-1 ordering).

### Gaps
- Exact doorway/corridor widths in meters for the Chandra et al. social mini-games were not in the abstract; the full paper/appendix would need to be read for geometry tables.
- The n ∈ [2,30] systematic-symmetric-deadlock claim ("RORCAL") could not be attributed to a verified primary source URL.
- RoboCup Logistics League and Open-RMF scenario geometries were not covered within the tool budget (no primary sources fetched).

## KQ2: Which papers give an EXPLICIT formal definition or operational criterion of congestion/deadlock, rather than just depicting it?

### Takeaway
Explicit formalizations exist in four distinct styles: (a) deadlock as a dynamical-systems equilibrium set (Grover et al.); (b) congestion as a measurable throughput quantity (dos Passos); (c) congestion as a phase identified on the fundamental diagram with divergence-based transition criteria (robot-swarm clogging physics); (d) structural solvability conditions whose violation permits deadlock (well-formed MAPD). MAPF benchmarks formalize *conflicts* (vertex/edge/etc.) but not congestion itself.

### Cited Findings
- **Deadlock as equilibrium / force balance (Grover, Liu, Sycara, CMU, 2019–2022)**: deadlock is "an equilibrium of system dynamics that causes robots to stall before reaching their goals"; via duality theory (KKT mechanics interpretation) they prove (1) "system deadlock is characterized by a force-equilibrium on robots" and (2) deadlock occurs exactly when safety is on the brink of violation, allowing deadlock to be "interpret[ed] as a subset of the state space... non-empty and located on the boundary of the safe set" — ["The Before, During, and After of Multi-Robot Deadlock", arXiv 1911.09146](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1911.09146.pdf); [CMU RI overview](https://www.ri.cmu.edu/publications/deadlock-analysis-and-resolution-for-multi-robot-systems); see also ["Why Does Symmetry Cause Deadlocks?"](https://ri.cmu.edu/?p=127222)
- **Congestion as throughput of the common target area (dos Passos et al., 2022)**: throughput is formally defined as "the inverse of the average time between arrivals at the target area" and proposed as *the* evaluation metric for target-area congestion as swarm size grows; strategies are then derived to maximize it under constant max speed and fixed minimum inter-robot distance — [arXiv 2201.09335](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2201.09335); [arXiv 2201.09337](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2201.09337)
- **Congestion as fundamental-diagram phase (robot swarm single-file, 2024)**: the experiment "replicate[s] the fundamental diagram... with a transition from free flow to congested traffic as the density of the system increases," observes stop-and-go waves, and identifies a *second* transition — "above a certain density... a divergence of average jam duration and average number of robots involved" marks the change from congested intermittent flow to a totally congested state — [arXiv 2403.08683](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.08683)
- **Clogging as intermittent flow interrupted by clogs (Zuriguel et al.)**: "when a large set of discrete bodies passes through a bottleneck, the flow may become intermittent due to clogs that obstruct the constriction," a framework unified across granular matter, colloids, and crowds ("Clogging transition of many-particle systems flowing through bottlenecks," Scientific Reports 2014) — [record at Univ. de Navarra portal](https://portalcientifico.unav.edu/documentos/5fa07ea5299952440aae6142)
- **Well-formedness as a formal no-deadlock condition (Ma et al., AAMAS 2017)**: a MAPD instance is **well-formed** iff (a) the number of tasks is finite, (b) there are no fewer non-task endpoints than agents, and (c) between any two endpoints there exists a path traversing no other endpoints. Endpoints = agent start locations ∪ task pickup/delivery locations ∪ parking locations; the intuition is agents may only rest where they "cannot block other agents" — [Lifelong Multi-Agent Path Finding for Online Pickup and Delivery Tasks, arXiv 1705.10868](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1705.10868); [AAMAS'17 PDF](https://www2.cs.sfu.ca/~hangma/pub/aamas17.pdf)
- **MAPF conflict taxonomy (Stern et al., 2019)**: five formally defined conflict types — vertex, edge, following, cycle, and swapping conflicts — standardize what counts as an agent-agent violation, but the paper defines no congestion measure; difficulty is operationalized via solver success only — [arXiv 1906.08291](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1906.08291)
- **Deadlock-freedom via liveness sets (Chandra et al.)**: deadlock avoidance is formalized as keeping the system state within "liveness sets" via barrier certificates ("preemptively perturb their state in a minimally-invasive fashion onto liveness sets"); evaluation metrics include success rate, collision rate, **stop time**, velocity change, path deviation, time-to-goal, and **flow rate** — [arXiv 2308.10966](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.10966)
- Lifelong-MAPF papers treat congestion operationally as a throughput deficit attributable to algorithm myopia: "most LMAPF algorithms split the problem into a sequence of MAPF instances and solve each one with a limited horizon by ignoring future collisions and congestion," and congestion is reported as the mechanism behind observed throughput losses (e.g., WPPL vs PIBT comparisons) — [Scaling Lifelong MAPF to More Realistic Settings, arXiv 2404.16162](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2404.16162); [arXiv 2605.16855](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.16855)

### Inferences
- There is no single cross-community operational criterion of "congested." The closest things to thresholds are: (i) divergence of jam duration/size at a critical density (physics), (ii) throughput below the theoretical maximum of the target area (dos Passos), (iii) deadlock-set membership (Grover: zero velocity at force equilibrium on the safe-set boundary). Papers that merely *depict* congestion (most warehouse AGV and MAPF papers) rely on reader intuition plus throughput/makespan numbers.
- For a VLM congestion detector, the physics-style operational criterion (velocity ≈ 0 for N robots within a constriction, sustained for T seconds — i.e., a jam with measurable duration and size) is the most directly transferable; Grover's definition is the most rigorous but requires knowing controller internals.

### Gaps
- No fetched source gave a "velocity below X m/s for T seconds" style threshold verbatim; deadlock-detection thresholds in experimental sections (e.g., how Chandra et al. detect a run as deadlocked) were not in the abstracts fetched.
- Wait-for-graph / blocking-relation formalisms (classic in AGV deadlock literature, e.g., Petri-net / banker's-algorithm AGV papers from the 1990s–2000s) were not surfaced by searches within budget; this body of work (e.g., Reveliotis, Wu & Zhou) almost certainly contains the most explicit blocking-relation definitions and should be covered by a follow-up search.

## KQ3: How do papers justify scenario difficulty (density per free cell, critical density from fundamental diagrams, solver runtime)?

### Takeaway
Three justification styles: MAPF benchmarks define difficulty *behaviorally* (max agents solvable within a 30 s timeout, incremented until failure); navigation benchmarks define it *geometrically a priori* (BARN's five metrics, MRPB's narrow/dynamic scenario design); physics experiments define it via *measured critical densities* on the fundamental diagram.

### Cited Findings
- **Stern et al. 2019 (MAPF)**: each of the 24 maps has **25 scenarios** of ~**1,000 randomly paired source-target pairs**; evaluation starts at 2 agents and "continues iteratively until the algorithm... cannot solve the created MAPF problem in reasonable time"; difficulty is reported as problems solved within a **30-second timeout** and min/max agent counts solved per map (e.g., brc202d: min 2, max 22 agents) — [ar5iv 1906.08291](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1906.08291)
- **BARN (2020)**: **300 procedurally generated** navigation environments "ordered by a set of difficulty metrics"; the five metrics are **distance to closest obstacle, average visibility, dispersion, characteristic dimension, and tortuosity**; low-numbered worlds are open/easy, high-numbered worlds cramped/difficult — [Benchmarking Metric Ground Navigation, arXiv 2008.13315](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2008.13315); [BARN dataset page (UT Austin)](https://www.cs.utexas.edu/~xiao/Research/BARN/BARN.html); the ICRA BARN Challenge runs on these ranked worlds — [BARN Challenge](https://www.cs.utexas.edu/~xiao/Research/BARN_Challenge/BARN_Challenge.html)
- **MRPB 1.0 (2020)**: difficulty is designed-in via "large-scale, partially unknown, and dynamic complex environments," including a **23.7 × 25.5 m² maze** with deliberately narrow passages; performance is quantified by three metric families (safety, efficiency, smoothness) — [arXiv 2011.00491](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2011.00491)
- **Critical density from the fundamental diagram**: the single-file robot swarm experiment locates two empirical critical densities — free-flow→congested (fundamental-diagram knee) and intermittent→total congestion (divergence of mean jam duration and jam size) — with repeatability enabling "comprehensive statistical analysis of the global dynamics" — [arXiv 2403.08683](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.08683)
- **Tunnel density optimality**: Aguilar et al. justify the 2-BW tunnel as the regime where clogging is a real risk and show ants/robots *select* a density that optimizes tunnel flux ("the ants optimize the tunnel flux and thus excavation efficiency by implementing workload inequality and retreats") — [Science 2018 PDF](https://crablab.gatech.edu/pages/publications/pdf/science-2018.pdf)
- **Lifelong MAPF**: difficulty is justified by density of agents ("dense environments where agents are packed tightly together") and by the computational trade-off — "high-quality solvers often become computationally overwhelmed while fast, local solvers often suffer from severe congestion" — [arXiv 2605.16855 overview](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2605.16855.md); scaling challenges (thousands of agents, realistic warehouses) catalogued in [arXiv 2404.16162](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2404.16162)

### Inferences
- MAPF's "hardness" is solver-relative (an instance is hard iff contemporary solvers fail on it), while BARN/MRPB hardness is solver-independent geometry, and physics hardness is a measured phase boundary. A congestion-scenario suite for a VLM study can borrow all three: fix geometry metrics a priori (BARN-style), sweep agent density (physics-style), and report an operational outcome (throughput/solve-rate, MAPF-style).
- Agents-per-free-cell density is implicitly the MAPF difficulty knob (agent count on a fixed map), but no fetched MAPF source normalizes by free-cell count explicitly.

### Gaps
- BARN's generation mechanism (cellular automata, per background knowledge) was not confirmed in fetched text — the sources fetched only say "procedurally generated."
- No source fetched gives an explicit "agents per free cell" density threshold used to label a MAPF instance congested.

## KQ4: How do task-driven setups (MAPD, lifelong MAPF) characterize where/when congestion emerges, vs staged setups that place robots into conflict directly?

### Takeaway
Task-driven setups never *place* congestion: they define a map + an online task stream + agent count, guarantee structural solvability via well-formedness, and let congestion emerge at hotspots (endpoints, narrow passages) as an observed throughput loss; difficulty is tuned via task-arrival frequency and agent count. Staged setups (deadlock papers, mini-games) directly construct initial conditions — symmetric placements, opposed goals through a shared constriction — that provably or reliably force the conflict.

### Cited Findings
- **MAPD (task-driven)**: "agents have to attend to a stream of delivery tasks in an online setting," each assigned agent moving pickup→delivery while avoiding collisions; solvability is guaranteed not by limiting load but by the **well-formed** structural condition (finite tasks; ≥ as many non-task endpoints as agents; endpoint-to-endpoint paths avoiding other endpoints) — [arXiv 1705.10868](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1705.10868); [AAMAS'17 PDF](https://www2.cs.sfu.ca/~hangma/pub/aamas17.pdf)
- **Lifelong MAPF (task-driven)**: congestion is characterized as an emergent consequence of algorithm myopia — limited-horizon replanning "ignoring future collisions and congestion" — and is diagnosed post-hoc by throughput comparisons across algorithms on warehouse/sortation maps at fixed agent counts (Random 800 ... Sortation 10000); "WPPL causes more congestion than PIBT and has lower throughput" in those instances — [arXiv 2605.16855](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.16855); research-challenge framing in [arXiv 2404.16162](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2404.16162)
- Layout, not only load, determines where congestion emerges: optimized warehouse layouts "greatly reduc[e] traffic congestion and improv[e] throughput... compared to commonly used human-designed layouts" — reported within the lifelong-MAPF scaling literature — [arXiv 2404.16162](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2404.16162)
- **Staged (deadlock-control)**: Grover et al. characterize exactly which "controller parameters, initial conditions, and goal locations lead to deadlock" for CBF-controlled robots — i.e., the scenario is constructed analytically from the conditions that put the system on the deadlock set; symmetry of configuration is identified as the causal mechanism — [arXiv 1911.09146](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1911.09146.pdf); ["Why Does Symmetry Cause Deadlocks?" (CMU RI)](https://ri.cmu.edu/?p=127222)
- **Staged (mini-games)**: social mini-games are defined directly as constrained-space encounters "with multiple conflicting agents" in doorway/hallway/intersection geometries — the conflict is the scenario, evaluated per-encounter with stop-time and flow-rate metrics — [arXiv 2308.10966](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.10966)
- **Staged (physics)**: the ring-track experiment stages congestion purely by the density control parameter (robot count on fixed track, 15 cm stopping headway), with no tasks at all; the Aguilar excavation setup is a hybrid — robots have an ongoing task stream (dig/deposit cycles) but the tunnel geometry (2 BW) guarantees recurring conflict — [arXiv 2403.08683](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.08683); [Science 2018](https://crablab.gatech.edu/pages/publications/pdf/science-2018.pdf)
- **Staged (industrial)**: the canonical four-robot left-turn intersection deadlock is specified as a concrete reproducible configuration in AGV traffic-control patents — [USPTO 10642282](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/10642282)

### Inferences
- The two paradigms answer different questions: task-driven setups measure *how often and where* congestion arises under realistic load (good for system-level claims); staged setups guarantee the phenomenon occurs on every trial (good for mechanism-level claims about detection/resolution). Deadlock-resolution papers overwhelmingly stage; warehouse-throughput papers overwhelmingly let it emerge.
- Well-formedness is the field's only widely cited *formal* bridge between the two: it tells you which task-driven instances can never hard-deadlock, so any observed stall in a well-formed instance is algorithmic congestion, not structural impossibility. This is a useful negative-control concept (see KQ5).

### Gaps
- Exact task-frequency parameter values used in Ma et al. 2017 experiments (tasks per timestep sweeps) were not captured from the fetched abstract; the paper's experimental section would need direct reading.
- Where congestion spatially concentrates in lifelong MAPF (heatmap analyses) was not found in the fetched material, though the layout-optimization result implies hotspot structure.

## KQ5: What negative controls / free-flow baselines do experimental papers use, if any?

### Takeaway
Explicit "negative control" framing is rare. Physics experiments have it structurally — the free-flow branch of the fundamental diagram below critical density is the built-in baseline — and BARN/MAPF benchmarks have it implicitly (low-numbered open worlds; 2-agent instances). Deadlock papers typically contrast deadlock-prone symmetric configurations against perturbed/asymmetric ones rather than against free flow.

### Cited Findings
- **Fundamental-diagram free-flow branch as baseline**: the single-file robot swarm experiment explicitly measures the free-flow regime at low density before the congestion transition, and the intermediate intermittent regime before total congestion — the low-density runs are the control against which the two transitions are identified — [arXiv 2403.08683](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.08683)
- **Protocol-contrast controls (Aguilar 2018)**: clogging-mitigation claims are established by comparing behavioral protocols within the same 2-BW tunnel — "idleness reduces the frequency of flow-stopping clogs and selective retreating reduces cluster dissolution time," i.e., the all-active (eager) collective serves as the congested control condition against which lazy/reversal protocols are measured — [Science 2018](https://crablab.gatech.edu/pages/publications/pdf/science-2018.pdf)
- **Difficulty-ordered world sets**: BARN's 300 worlds span from "more open and thus easier to navigate" (low-numbered) to "cramped and difficult" — the easy end functions as the free-flow baseline for any planner evaluated across the full ordered set — [BARN dataset page](https://www.cs.utexas.edu/~xiao/Research/BARN/BARN.html)
- **Incremental agent protocol (MAPF)**: evaluation "start[s] by creating a MAPF problem of two agents" and grows until failure — so every solver's curve includes its own near-trivial baseline instances — [ar5iv 1906.08291](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1906.08291)
- **Symmetric vs perturbed configurations**: the ORCA-deadlock literature treats exact symmetry as the pathological condition — deadlocks arise "in scenes where robots distribute symmetrically" — implying asymmetric placements as the implicit non-deadlocking control — [Robotica ORCA-deadlock paper](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/robotica/article/abs/addressing-the-deadlock-problem-of-the-optimal-reciprocal-collision-avoidance-algorithm-in-distributed-multirobot-navigation/70CEA27CFAE13D30DD3ABCD189F9019D); Grover et al. characterize precisely which initial conditions do vs do not lead to the deadlock set — [arXiv 1911.09146](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1911.09146.pdf)
- **Well-formedness as structural control**: because well-formed MAPD instances are guaranteed solvable, they certify that any observed stall is algorithmic rather than an unsolvable scenario — [arXiv 1705.10868](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1705.10868)

### Inferences
- For a congestion-detection study (e.g., VLM classification), the literature's practice suggests two kinds of negative control: (i) same geometry, sub-critical density (physics style — same corridor, fewer/offset robots, free flow); (ii) same density, non-critical geometry (BARN style — wide aisle). Few robotics papers run both; adopting both would exceed current field practice.
- No paper fetched reports a deliberate "sham congestion" control (robots stopped for reasons other than conflict — e.g., charging) to test detector specificity; this appears to be an open methodological niche.

### Gaps
- Warehouse AGV/AMR simulator papers (2020–2026) with explicit free-flow baseline runs were not reached within the tool budget; Open-RMF, Flatland (rail), and RoboCup Logistics League scenario-definition practice remains uncovered — a follow-up pass should target the Flatland NeurIPS competition papers (malfunction-induced congestion via stochastic agent breakdowns) and Open-RMF traffic-negotiation docs.
- No source was found that defines a quantitative free-flow reference (e.g., "velocity ≥ 90% of nominal") used as an explicit acceptance criterion for a non-congested trial.
