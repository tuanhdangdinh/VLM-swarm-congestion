# Newest Planner Scan Spares All Five Claims

**Prepared:** 2026-10-04. Synthesized from three research-note files in `research_notes/VLM planner coordinator recent work/` (newest coordinator scan; plan-generation quality; plan-feasibility grounding), building on `reports/VLM coordination recent advances.md` (2026-10-01) and its four note files.

All five registered claims survive the 2026-10-04 freshness pass — including the five named threats that gated the `prereg-v1` follow-up: GAVEL's now-read evaluation section, both MDPI papers (finally identified and scoped via abstracts), arXiv:2609.27816, DuoMind, and Fresh Memory Stale Plans. The one forced change is to claim 2's wording: GAVEL operationalizes a two-way repair-vs-replan split and quantifies it as an ablation (repair-only arm 76.2% success vs. full system 91.8%, [arXiv:2609.19315](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.19315v1)), so claim 2 can no longer read as "first to distinguish repaired from replanned plan content" — it must carry four explicit qualifiers: **three-way** (verbatim/repaired/defaulted), **logged per leg as an experimental quantity**, with **deterministic minimal repair**, for **multi-robot congestion plans**. On the measurement side, the plan-generation literature now directly underwrites the thesis design: stating the action grammar in-prompt roughly tripled semantic accuracy in a controlled 2025 study while grammar-constrained decoding achieved 0% syntax errors but collapsed semantics to ~2% ([arXiv:2505.14763](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.14763v1)); Levenshtein edit distance over action sequences is a standard secondary metric, but nobody scores distance-to-the-minimally-repaired-plan (repair cost); "missing-step" is a named first-class error category (the published analog of our no-recede-legs failure) while **axis-mirror errors appear in no published taxonomy** — a citable novelty. On feasibility grounding, candidate-maneuver overlays, constraints-in-prompt, and deterministic symmetry designation are adoptable published recipes, while composing an ordered multi-robot plan *from* marked candidates has no prior art and is itself claimable; grid overlays carry a measured per-backend regression risk that mandates an A/B pilot before freezing.

## Prompted grammar triples validity; constrained decoding destroys it

The field's validity numbers for full plan emission are bimodal and bleak at the bottom: non-reasoning LLMs achieve roughly **3–14% valid plans on Blocksworld** while o4-mini reaches 94%, and on failed plans only **2.65 of 8.4 ground-truth actions on average are executable** before the first constraint violation, with more than half of weak-model plans failing at the very first action ([arXiv:2508.01300](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.01300v1)). The multi-agent version is worse exactly where it matters for us: LLMs emitting full joint move sequences for MAPF manage ~62.5% success at 10 agents only on *empty* 20×20 maps and suffer near-total failure on room and maze maps — precisely the regime where geometry forces ordered yields, back-outs, and detours, i.e., the congestion regime ([arXiv:2401.03630](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.03630), TMLR 2025 version; numbers from corroborated snippets, quote-check before thesis citation). VLM-input embodied benchmarks top out below ~45% even on single-next-action choice (GPT-4V 37.63% direct, 43.04% with multimodal CoT + self-consistency on EgoPlan-Bench2, [arXiv:2412.04447](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.04447)). Two gaps are worth stating in the thesis: **no 2025–2026 benchmark scores multi-robot joint action-sequence emission as a first-class artifact**, and no benchmark names *emission rate* (did a parseable plan come out at all) as a metric — both of which the thesis's metric set supplies. The MAPF-LLM failure on constrained maps is the correct external baseline to cite for why a verifier-backed central VLM plan is not a solved problem.

The grammar question now has a quantified answer, and it lands squarely on the thesis's side. A controlled 2025 comparison of eight PDDL-generation pipelines found that putting grammar/syntax knowledge in-prompt lifted accuracy from **~7% to ~23%**, while *enforcing* the same grammar via constrained decoding yielded **0% syntax errors but ~98% semantically wrong outputs (~2% accuracy)** — the token masking "suppressed the semantic tokens" the model needed; solver-plus-validator feedback revision beat everything, more than doubling performance ([arXiv:2505.14763](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.14763v1)). The mechanism is independently confirmed: constrained decoding "alters the model's distribution at every token," degrading reasoning-intensive outputs despite guaranteed validity ([arXiv:2603.03305](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.03305v1)), consistent with the earlier strictness ordering from Let Me Speak Freely ([arXiv:2408.02442](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2408.02442)), and 2026's Meta-Ctrl explicitly decouples syntax (guaranteed mechanically) from semantics (left to the model) — the same architecture as our classical layer ([arXiv:2608.22149](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.22149)). The thesis pipeline — grammar stated in-prompt, no hard-constrained decoding, deterministic external repair downstream — is therefore the literature's best-performing shape, with one differentiation to state plainly: the published best pipeline repairs by *re-prompting with validator feedback*, ours repairs *deterministically without an LLM call*. One caveat belongs in a footnote: no paper isolates the schema-statement ablation on an embodied or multi-robot task (2505.14763 is text-domain PDDL), so our per-model "consistency-with-stated-grammar rate" has no exact published counterpart — reporting it is itself a contribution, not a replication.

## Edit distance is mainstream; repair cost and axis-mirror are not

Plan-scoring conventions have converged enough to cite and extend. Levenshtein edit distance over action sequences, usually normalized by max sequence length, is now a standard secondary metric in LLM-planning evaluations; the richest published scheme is 2508.01300's trio of **Steps-to-Validity** (Levenshtein distance to the *optimal* plan), **Last Executable Action** (index of the final valid action), and **Action-Quality Mapping** with per-action labels {correct, misplaced, same_act, diff_act, redundant} ([arXiv:2508.01300](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.01300v1)). Two of its findings shape our metric design directly: 52.3% of failed plans needed effectively complete replanning — edit distance saturates at plan length for half the failures, so report a **longest-valid-prefix metric alongside edit distance** — and failures are structural rather than length-driven (no monotonic decay along the plan). The thesis's differentiators remain intact after this survey: every found paper measures distance to a *gold reference*, none measures distance to the *minimally repaired feasible plan* (repair cost), and none defines an agent-permutation-invariant distance for interleaved multi-agent plans where swapping agent order is semantically free — the latter we must define ourselves, with no precedent to borrow. AQM's per-action labels are the published precedent to cite when introducing per-leg provenance: a recognized per-action-labeling practice, extended from quality labels to origin labels.

On failure taxonomies, the thesis's two signature failure modes split cleanly into "published, cite and instantiate" and "unpublished, name and claim." **Missing-step** — necessary prior actions omitted — is a first-class named error category in Embodied Agent Interface's taxonomy (alongside additional-step, wrong-temporal-order, and affordance errors, detected via per-action precondition verification, [arXiv:2410.07166](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.07166)), and 2025–26 agent-failure taxonomies carry a "Logic Error: missing prerequisites" category (attribution between [arXiv:2609.02371](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.02371) and [arXiv:2606.04874](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.04874) still unresolved — pin before citing). Our observed no-recede-legs failure should be framed as the *multi-agent geometric instance of the missing-step class*: a known single-agent error shown to persist and interact with geometry in congestion, with the MAPF collapse on constrained maps as the population-level evidence ([arXiv:2401.03630](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.03630)). By contrast, **no found taxonomy names symmetry or axis-mirror errors** — choosing the mirrored but infeasible side of a symmetric resolution. After targeted searches across the deadlock and warehouse angles, this failure mode appears unpublished; naming it, connecting it to the project's presentation-order findings (first-slot/robot_1 priors as positional symmetry-breaking), and measuring it is a citable contribution, with the honest hedge that this is absence-after-targeted-search, not proof.

## GAVEL tightens claim 2's wording; the other four threats clear outright

The five named reading gates are now resolved, and the registered claim set survives whole. **GAVEL's evaluation section** (full HTML read) settles the question the 2026-10-01 report left open: it *does* route failures two ways — deterministic graph-derived repair versus LLM replanning reserved for semantic errors — but quantifies the split **only as an ablation**: gavel-basic (repair only) 76.2% success at 1.00 LLM attempts versus full gavel 91.8% at 1.5 attempts, with a failure-breakdown figure but **no per-action provenance logging, no repaired-vs-replanned count table, no plan-preservation metric**, single-agent, at BEHAVIOR-1K symbolic level ([arXiv:2609.19315](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.19315v1)). The forced tightening of claim 2 is exact: drop any reading of "first to distinguish repaired from replanned plan content" and register instead — *first **three-way per-leg provenance** (verbatim / repaired / defaulted), **logged per leg as an experimental quantity**, produced by **deterministic minimal repair**, for **multi-robot congestion plans*** — four differentiators GAVEL demonstrably lacks. Two phrasing safeguards: write "GAVEL does not *report* a per-leg or per-failure repaired-vs-replanned tabulation" (its logs might permit reconstructing one if code is released), and note that the "defaulted" class — verifier substitutes a safe fallback when repair fails — has no published analog anywhere in the survey.

The remaining four threats clear with useful positioning language attached. **arXiv:2609.27816** (Safe Multi-Robot Coordination via VLM-LLM Reasoning and Reachability Analysis) is the closest new neighbor — a VLM+LLM proposer with a zonotope-reachability formal gate — but it is ego-camera, two robots, has **no congestion or deadlock scenarios, no quantitative baseline, no persistent plan** (reactive 30 Hz, candidates regenerated each cycle), and is pure **audit-and-reject**: failed predicates mean the motion is "rejected outright," with only aggregate audit fractions reported (39.8% of commands approved, 60.2% withheld over 1,953 decision cycles) and "no incremental repair" ([arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.27816)). It is the paper to cite as verification-gate convergence for claim 4 — which stays differentiated as audit-**and-repair with logged provenance** against both 2609.27816's reject-only gate and GAVEL's unlogged repair. **The two MDPI gates are cleared for threat purposes but not for quoting numbers**: MAKE 8(1):22 is Pesjak & Žabkar's single-robot Sense–Plan–Code–Act pipeline (LLM→PDDL→symbolic planner→code→compile/simulation validation, ~71–75% task completion, [S2 record](https://api.semanticscholar.org/graph/v1/paper/DOI:10.3390/make8010022)), and Robotics 15(4):80 turns out to contribute repair *timing* — an LLM heuristic deciding *when* to initiate PDDL plan repair — not preservation accounting as earlier snippets suggested ([S2 record](https://api.semanticscholar.org/graph/v1/paper/DOI:10.3390/robotics15040080)); both are single-robot, neither touches multi-robot plans, congestion, or provenance, and full texts remain 403-blocked after exhausting every access route, so any sentence quoting their internal metrics must wait for institutional access or an author request. **DuoMind** is distributed per-robot VLM orchestrators over VLA executors for long-horizon *manipulation* (plus the RoboPoly benchmark) — no central plan, no congestion, no navigation ([arXiv:2610.02161](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02161)). **Fresh Memory, Stale Plans** is claim 5's one real conceptual neighbor: it maintains inherited persistent plans across distributed LLM agents, links plan steps to their recorded inputs, and checks "derivation currency" before protected actions — but it is **non-embodied** (software-agent workflows, 143–237 ms stall overheads), so claim 5's "embodied" qualifier is exactly what keeps it clear, and the paper usefully legitimizes plan staleness as a measurable quantity from the distributed-systems side ([arXiv:2609.03340](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03340)). Claims 1 and 3 face no new pressure at all: two broad date-sorted arXiv sweeps through 2026-10-01 surfaced no overhead-camera VLM congestion coordinator and no VLM-vs-rule-based Nav2 comparison, and 2609.27816's own admission that no comparable baseline exists reinforces claim 3's open ground. Residual debts, honestly: EvoPlan ([arXiv:2607.06724](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.06724)) was never read beyond a snippet and should get a one-paper check before any "no prior repair statistics" sentence freezes; CoRL 2026 camera-readies and ICRA 2027 OpenReview were not inspectable; Semantic Scholar's Aug–Sep 2026 indexing lag (plus two rate-limited re-checks) means "zero citers" claims for LLMDR and Oxford CCTV are soft.

## Composition from marked candidates is unclaimed; selection, constraints, and designation are recipes

The feasibility-grounding survey sorts into one claimable gap and three adoptable recipes. The gap first: the PIVOT lineage has matured from marking candidate *actions* to rendering whole candidate *trajectories* — numbered, colored polylines with index labels at endpoints that a VLM picks among ([arXiv:2602.09002](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.09002v1)), including an asynchronous slow-VLM/fast-planner variant architecturally parallel to our Layer-2/Layer-1 split ([arXiv:2606.20458](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.20458)) — but in **every published instance the VLM selects one pre-rendered trajectory per agent per query**. No paper marks candidate maneuvers for multiple robots on one image and asks the model to **compose an ordered joint plan over them**; the closest analogs compose per-arm sub-instruction sequences from a predefined atomic prompt set (MA-VLA, [arXiv:2608.25864](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.25864)) or emit leader-plus-waypoints from one overhead plot (VLM-GCBF+, [PMLR PDF](https://mit-realm.github.io/VLM-gcbfplus-website/files/Online_planning_using_VLMs.pdf)). Doing composition-from-marked-candidates is therefore novel; the safe published-pattern decomposition — and the recommended adoption — is per-leg *selection* (one labeled choice per robot, drawn from the verifier's own candidate set) plus model-stated ordering, with the grammar enforcing enabling actions. This conversion is strategically important because it transforms our hardest observed failure, the axis-mirror of the modeled recede set, into a discrete choice over only modeled options.

Constraints-in-prompt is published-supported and cheap. The MAPF recipe — list each agent's currently feasible actions in the prompt ("single-step observation") and re-prompt on checker errors ([arXiv:2401.03630](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.03630)) — maps directly onto our verifier's candidate sets ("robot_k may: reverse-south, yield-slot-B, wait") and attacks mirror-set and mis-assigned-yielder failures by construction, since infeasible options are never offered; survey-level evidence has validator-feedback loops "nearly doubling" performance ([ACL 2025 survey](https://aclanthology.org/2025.acl-long.958.pdf)), supporting a bounded one-to-two-retry structured-error re-prompt before classical fallback at our 0.5–2 Hz cadence. VLM-GCBF+ — the verified nearest-neighbor system — states constraints textually, demands strict JSON, provides the overhead image *plus* textual coordinates, and found image tokens stay flat (~1k per intervention) as scene complexity grows while text-only descriptions balloon; its feasibility stance is ours: the VLM is advisory, a provably-safe layer owns feasibility, and **no 2026 system was found that trusts raw VLM plan geometry** ([PMLR PDF](https://mit-realm.github.io/VLM-gcbfplus-website/files/Online_planning_using_VLMs.pdf)). The RLVR result — constraint-trained 3–4B models beating large prompted ones on multi-robot validity ([arXiv:2505.20573](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.20573)) — is the deferred path if prompting plateaus.

Grid overlays come with a measured per-backend landmine. The gains are real — Mark-Grid scaffolds (predict a labeled cell ID, not raw coordinates) lift GUI click accuracy from ~5% to ~72% on ScreenSpot-v2 ([arXiv:2509.11548](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.11548)), consistent with the earlier 9×9-grid IoU doubling (0.27→0.56, [arXiv:2411.18270](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.18270)) — but VLM2-Bench reports **significant performance drops for Qwen2.5-VL-7B** under dot-grid prompting ([arXiv:2502.12084](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.12084)), so the grid must be A/B-piloted per backend (gpt-4o, Gemini ER-2) against the current SoM-only frame before freezing, and used for **discrete cell-ID reference** ("robot_2 reverse into C4," verifier maps label→metric), not coordinate regression. All measured grid gains are point-grounding tasks; transfer to plan-level outputs is our inference and should be labeled as such. Our overhead frame is already on the favorable side of the documented ego→allo gap — benchmark performance rises as viewpoint approaches bird's-eye ([arXiv:2511.18200](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.18200)) — so no ego-to-allo machinery is needed. Finally, symmetry: published systems break symmetric deadlocks by **designation, never by scoring both modes** — VLM-GCBF+ removes the choice from the model entirely at N≥10 (leader = minimum distance to goal, deterministic), GameChat breaks ties via urgency-grounded negotiation ([arXiv:2503.12333](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.12333)) — which endorses having the classical layer designate the yielder before the VLM call. **No published canonicalization or accept-either-mirror scoring protocol for VLM plan evaluation exists**; outcome-based evaluations implicitly accept either mirror. Our prereg must therefore document its own choice (canonicalize by reflection, or score both mirrors and take the max) as a defined convention, and the in-house random-order-plus-condition-on-draw protocol is ahead of the literature rather than behind it.

## Conclusion

The newest scan converts the thesis's position from "defensible" to "confirmed on the field's trajectory with the measurement layer still unoccupied": three independent 2026 systems (GAVEL, 2609.27816, the MDPI pair) converge on verify-gate architectures, yet across all of them validity is enforced, rejected, or iterated — never *attributed*, leg by leg, to verbatim, repaired, or defaulted origins. The literature has also, in effect, pre-validated the thesis's prompt design (told grammar, soft contract, external deterministic repair) with numbers strong enough to cite as design justification rather than mere precedent. Two asymmetries are worth exploiting while they last: the concurrent-work framing for GAVEL and 2609.27816 is only credible while those papers are months old, which argues for tagging the tightened claim 2 wording promptly; and two of the thesis's observed phenomena — the axis-mirror failure class and mirror-equivalence-aware plan scoring — are currently nameable, not just measurable, since nobody else has named them.

Outstanding before any "no prior work reports X" sentence goes to print: read EvoPlan (2607.06724) once; obtain the two MDPI full texts through institutional access before quoting any of their internal metrics; re-check GAVEL's released artifacts (if any) so the claim stays "does not report a per-leg tabulation," not "cannot produce one"; and re-verify the snippet-sourced numbers flagged UNVERIFIED in the notes (MAPF 62.5%, Mark-Grid 5→72%, EgoPlan 43.04%, the six-category taxonomy attribution) against primary texts before they appear in the thesis.
