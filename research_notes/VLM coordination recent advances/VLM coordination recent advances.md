# Newest Papers Narrow Two Claims, Spare Three

**Prepared:** 2026-10-01. Synthesized from four research-note files in `research_notes/VLM coordination recent advances/` (novelty-threat scan; verification-debt deep reads; adoptable inference-time techniques; evaluation methodology), building on `reports/VLM centralized multi robot coordination.md`.

The 2025–2026 wave leaves the thesis's two strongest registered claims — the overhead-camera VLM congestion-strategy taxonomy (claim 1) and the matched two-arm VLM-vs-rule-based comparison (claim 3) — **unoccupied**, but forces re-wording of the other three: the deterministic verify-and-repair harness is no longer novel on its own (GAVEL repairs LLM plans from a graph world model, [arXiv:2609.19315](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19315); Physical Agentic AI gates a multi-robot crew per-dispatch with reported step statistics, [arXiv:2608.22657](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.22657)), and the persistent centrally-edited plan mechanism now exists in game environments (SyncPlan, [arXiv:2608.01652](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.01652)). What survives intact after full-PDF deep reads of the load-bearing prior art is the thesis's sharpest metric claim: **no paper audits a model-proposed plan leg-by-leg and reports the verbatim/repaired/defaulted fraction** — Garg et al.'s PMLR 2026 paper executes VLM output with no verification step at all, LLMDR delegates validity to PIBT by construction, RoCo's Table 2 reports re-plan *iteration counts* (the nearest precedent, which must be differentiated rather than denied), and a six-query vocabulary sweep found no "intervention/override rate" anywhere that means fraction-of-plan-preserved. On the engineering side, the technique literature hands the coordinator three near-free upgrades (labeled metric grid, reasoning-field-first JSON, permutation voting) and two strong prohibitions (no free-text spatial CoT, no VLM self-verifier); on methodology, paired clustered statistics, Kaplan-Meier/RMST for censored episodes, pinned-snapshot nondeterminism caveats, and a benchmark-situating table would put the evaluation above the current venue median. Three verification debts gate the `prereg-v1` tag: GAVEL's evaluation section, two paywalled MDPI full texts, and a DeepFleet skim.

## Claims 1 and 3 stay open; claims 2, 4, 5 must name four concurrent papers

The scan (2026-10-01, arXiv API + Semantic Scholar, post-2025-07 window) found **no paper in which a VLM classifies congestion or deadlock type from a scene image and selects among discrete resolution strategies**. The nearest neighbors bracket claim 1 without touching it: EFLUX has an LLM choose among discrete formation actions (split/merge/scale) to reduce deadlock, but over a structured text scene representation, not an image, and with no congestion taxonomy ([arXiv:2607.12050](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.12050)); a highway mixture-of-experts system does VLM congestion *detection* from fixed cameras with no robot actuation ([arXiv:2508.17205](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.17205)). An arXiv full-text sweep for cs.RO/cs.MA abstracts containing congestion plus any language-model term returned only nine papers through 2026-09, none a warehouse multi-robot congestion resolver. The residual risk to claim 1 is compositional, not direct: a reviewer can assemble it from Oxford CCTV (overhead-camera fleet control, classical) plus Garg/GameChat (foundation-model deadlock strategies), so the registration should position the **taxonomy itself** as the contribution, not the camera-plus-VLM pairing. Claim 3 likewise has no occupant — VLM-vs-baseline comparisons are routine but the baselines are greedy heuristics or RL policies (D-VLC beats a "geometric greedy baseline" by 55.8% on completion time, [arXiv:2607.29009](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.29009)), never a literature-grounded rule-based coordinator on identical Nav2 scenarios. Usefully, two new non-LLM systems validate that the rule-based arm is a live research baseline rather than a strawman: Virtual Traffic Lights, a centralized stop-command conflict resolver validated on real quadrupeds and Duckiebots ([arXiv:2511.07811](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.07811)), and a hybrid RL-MAPF deadlock-recovery layer ([arXiv:2511.22685](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.22685)); both belong in the Arm-B design citations.

Claim 2 is the most crowded. **GAVEL** (Duke, 2026-09) builds a graph world model that "can predict the consequences of LLM-generated actions before execution, detect violations, and repair those whose corrections follow directly from the world model," reserving LLM replanning for semantic errors — success 41.2%→91.8% with Qwen3-8B on single-robot BEHAVIOR-1K household tasks ([arXiv:2609.19315](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19315)). **Physical Agentic AI** (2026-08) puts a deterministic Robot Orchestrator between an LLM Mission Planner and a heterogeneous robot crew, authorizing one skill at a time, and reports per-dispatch gate statistics: retrieval raises skill grounding from 51% to 96% yet planners still dispatch 23–29% faulted steps; per-dispatch enforcement cuts false dispatch to 0% ([arXiv:2608.22657](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.22657)). Neither is congestion, neither repairs minimally (PAAI refuses rather than repairs), and neither reports a three-way per-leg provenance fraction — but both are under four months old and must be cited as **concurrent work**, with claim 2 narrowed to the provenance *reporting* plus the minimal-repair-for-multi-robot-congestion combination, dropping "first deterministic non-LLM repair" as a standalone clause. Claim 4's defensible core shrinks the same way: PAAI already audits a scene-wide ordered plan step-by-step with refuse-only semantics, so the claim becomes audit **with repair and provenance**. Claim 5 is partially occupied by **SyncPlan**, where a centralized LLM emits per-agent action chains in one call, a Plan Staleness Detector monitors the persistent plan, and execution feedback triggers correction — but in Overcooked/Honor-of-Kings, with wholesale replanning rather than minimal in-place edits and no leg-level audit ([arXiv:2608.01652](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.01652)); the cascade-containment paper's "Verify-Adapt-Hold" selective plan preservation is adjacent in spirit and worth a citation ([arXiv:2609.30523](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30523)). Claim 5's surviving core: incremental minimal edits to a persistent plan, in an embodied Nav2 warehouse, integrated with claim 2's provenance audit. The citation graph is otherwise quiet: Garg et al. has only five indexed citers — among them **Seeing-Saying-Solving** (decentralized natural-language conflict recovery among heterogeneous warehouse robots with temporal-logic verification, [arXiv:2505.13376](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.13376)), which belongs in claims 1/3 related work as the decentralized-warehouse counterpoint — while LLMDR and Oxford CCTV show **zero** Semantic Scholar citations as of the scan, and DeepFleet remains a predictive fleet world model, not a semantic resolver ([arXiv:2508.08574](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.08574)). The field's drift toward decentralized dialogue (GameChat, Seeing-Saying-Solving) and hierarchical leader assignment leaves the centralized-perception angle differentiated.

## Deep reads settle it: the provenance metric has no prior occupant

Every page of the four load-bearing papers was read, and the pattern is uniform: **validity is either delegated or iterated, never attributed.** Garg et al.'s PMLR 2026 version (VLM-GCBF+) queries the VLM reactively on deadlock detection (average fleet speed below δv=0.2 while mean goal distance exceeds δd=0.4) and executes the returned leader-plus-waypoints JSON directly — **no repair, feasibility check, or output verification appears anywhere in the 21 pages**; the stance is explicit: safety "is taken care of by the provably safe low-level control policy" ([PMLR PDF](https://mit-realm.github.io/VLM-gcbfplus-website/files/Online_planning_using_VLMs.pdf)). Its four metrics are reach rate, intervention count, planner latency, and tokens; the appendix even uses intervention count as an *indirect proxy* for plan quality ("the number of high-level planner interventions is generally inversely proportional to the quality of the plan"), confirming no direct plan-validity statistic exists. Two citation cautions: per-model reach rates exist only as bar/box plots (Figs. 5, 12), so quote the qualitative text (low-level alone reaches 0; random planner <0.5; A* beats VLMs on small rooms but loses on large mazes), never invented numbers. LLMDR's LLM emits only a JSON classification — deadlocked-agent groups plus a "leader"/"radiation" strategy label — converted into priorities for PIBT, which generates the collision-free actions, so **plan validity is guaranteed by construction and no per-call LLM accuracy, parse-failure, or override statistic is reported**; the paper's tables carry only end-task success rate and episode length (e.g., DHC 1%→83% on warehouse/64 agents) ([arXiv:2503.00717](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.00717)).

RoCo is the precedent that must be engaged head-on, because the earlier report's open question is now answered: **yes, RoCo's Table 2 reports "average number of re-plan attempts" as a headline metric** for every method and task over 20 runs (Dialog variant: 3.5 attempts on Pack Grocery, 1.0 on Sweep Floor; Central Plan oracle: up to 3.9), fed by a five-stage validation suite — parsing, task constraints, IK, collision, waypoints — that bounces failed plans back for re-prompting ([arXiv:2307.04738](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2307.04738)). The differentiation is precise, not defensive: RoCo counts **whole-plan rejection iterations until a plan passes**, never the fraction of a plan preserved versus overridden, reports no per-check breakdown, and no first-attempt validity rate. Grover et al. anchors the opposite pole: their "provably-correct decentralized algorithm for deadlock resolution" is a three-phase switching controller (CBF-QP → feedback-linearized rotation at exactly the safety distance → go-to-goal) with no-relapse and convergence proofs, but only for two and three robots and one deadlock geometry ([arXiv:2206.01781](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.01781)) — the clean anchor for a "guaranteed control-theoretic exit maneuver vs. semantic heuristic resolution" axis in related work. Finally, the vocabulary sweep closes the last escape hatch: AV disengagement rates are per-distance human-override statistics ([arXiv:1912.03618](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1912.03618)) and are themselves criticized as non-standardized ([EE Times](https://www.eetimes.com/disengagements-wrong-metric-for-av-testing/)); RL shield-intervention and failsafe-intervention rates are per-action, per-timestep ([Fear Field, EAAI 2025](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0952197625000557); [arXiv:2303.03339](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.03339)); CBF safety-filter activation is per-control-cycle ([arXiv:2507.07805](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.07805)). None audits a discrete multi-step plan. The positioning sentence writes itself: *shield-intervention rates are the per-action analogue, disengagement rates the per-mission-time analogue, and RoCo's re-plan count the plan-rejection-iteration analogue of per-leg plan provenance; none reports what fraction of a proposed plan survived the checker.* The term can be introduced as new with those three as nearest kin, with the standing caveat that absence after six targeted queries is evidence, not proof.

## Three near-free upgrades to adopt, four A/B arms to register, two hard prohibitions

The technique notes converge on a short adoption list filtered by the pipeline's constraints (single annotated frame, temperature 0, discrete menu, classical repair layer downstream). **Adopt outright, roughly 1× cost:** first, a labeled metric grid on the annotated frame — a trivial 9×9 grid overlay roughly doubled localization IoU (0.27→0.56) as pure preprocessing ([arXiv:2411.18270](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.18270)), and axis/mark-grid scaffolds are confirmed helpers in GUI grounding ([arXiv:2509.11548](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.11548)); drawn in world-frame meters matching the numeric state block, it attacks both the near-chance left/right problem and the project's observed ignored-geometry-text failure by letting cross-modal agreement do the grounding. Second, **reasoning-field-first JSON** for the plan output: strict constrained decoding measurably hurts reasoning while helping pure classification ([Let Me Speak Freely, EMNLP 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.02442)), and 2026 work pins the mechanism on premature serialization, with "performance recovering whenever unconstrained reasoning precedes structured submission" ([Capacity, Not Format](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.09410)) — so the congestion-type classification stays strict-JSON while the multi-robot plan schema puts a bounded `analysis` field as its first key. Third, **permutation voting/averaging**: marginalizing over option orders cancels token and position bias *per decision*, strictly stronger than per-call randomization ([PriDe, ICLR 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.03882)), and visual context "does not act as a shield against selection bias." Three cyclic shifts of robot listing and menu letters (~3× cost) converts the pre-registered first-slot/robot_1 prior from confound into noise, and doubles as the field's best test-time-scaling move — majority voting consistently beats verification-centric best-of-N for VLMs (VLAA-Thinker GeoQA 52.1% MV vs 44.3% self-verified BoN, [Aha Moment Revisited](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.17417)) — crucial on the Gemini ER-2 backend, where no temperature control closes the sampling axis. A mirror-flip consistency gate (left/right answers that fail to invert under a mirrored, ID-remapped frame are prior-driven and escalate to the classical fallback) is cheap own-inference test-time-augmentation logic, best housed in the verify layer.

**Register as A/B arms, not defaults:** 2–3 timestamped keyframes — the best decomposition evidence shows direction questions gain +17–19 pp from multi-frame input with only weak order dependency ([arXiv:2607.12304](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.12304)), though LRR-Bench warns sequences alone leave relative-motion judgment near chance ([arXiv:2507.20174](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.20174)); a motion-trail overlay arm in the same A/B (no literature either way — a genuine gap); a schematic re-render arm, where literature support is only concept-level ([Thinking with Blueprints](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.01984)) but project data is strong — 0/240 refusals on synthetic frames versus ~22–25% on realistic ones makes schematization the only identified prompt-level refusal mitigation; and a pre-cropped congestion-zone zoom tile, which captures the Visual Sketchpad mechanism (+9.1% spatial for the agentic version, [arXiv:2406.09403](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.09403)) at ~1.5× cost with no tool loop. **Prohibit:** long free-text spatial chain-of-thought — "CoT prompting consistently degrades performance in visual spatial reasoning" across 17 models and 13 benchmarks, with models hallucinating visual details from textual priors ([arXiv:2604.16060](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.16060)) — and any VLM self-verifier or pairwise tournament selection stage, since VLMs cannot distinguish image-grounded answers from confident language-prior guesses at selection time, and apparent perturbation-selection gains of up to +31.8 pp vanish against format-matched controls ([It's the Decoding Format](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.01207)). The classical verify/repair layer is the correct verifier precisely because it is external and symbolic. Agentic crop/zoom loops and world-model imagination (AVIC's ~230× token cost, [arXiv:2602.08236](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.08236)) are excluded on infrastructure and cost grounds. Note the deliberate tension resolution: the bounded reasoning field is kept short and structured (forced per-robot one-liners) so the reasoning-first pattern does not reintroduce the spatial-CoT failure.

## Paired statistics, survival curves, and honest nondeterminism put the evaluation above venue median

Four upgrades are decision-ready. **First, paired design with clustered and bootstrap statistics.** Kress-Gazit et al. is the anchor citation for the field's weakness — papers reporting success rates with "little to no statistical analysis" ([arXiv:2409.09491](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.09491)) — and Miller supplies the recipes: paired comparisons on the same items for variance reduction, clustered standard errors for episodes grouped by scenario, resampling, and power analysis ([arXiv:2411.00640](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.00640)). Because both arms run the same frozen battery, pairing on (scenario, seed) with McNemar's exact test on binary outcomes, Wilson intervals on proportions, and scenario-clustered SEs is the single highest-value choice; no venue-mandated checklist exists at CoRL/ICRA/RA-L, so adopting this emerging best-practices literature already exceeds the modal 10–20-runs-no-CIs norm. **Second, Kaplan-Meier/RMST for censored episodes.** Treat each episode's resolution as right-censored time-to-event at horizon H, plot per-arm KM "fraction unresolved" curves, and report the RMST difference up to H with bootstrap CI as the primary time metric — valid without proportional hazards ([Royston & Parmar 2013](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3922847/)), which matters because yield-vs-reroute plausibly changes the *shape* of resolution, not just its rate. PhAIL gives the robotics precedent — right-censored time-to-success pairs resolving comparisons at N≤30 rollouts where binary success rates could not ([arXiv:2605.29710](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.29710)) — and the motivating bias number is stark: in one agent corpus, 47% of trajectories hit administrative max-step stops and were ~3× longer than completed ones ([arXiv:2605.24756](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.24756)). No robotics paper applies RMST by name, so the adoption is itself near-novel. **Third, the pre-registration novelty phrasing.** Pre-registration exists in robotics only at HRI's margins — 3.38% of 296 child-robot-interaction manuscripts ([arXiv:2309.01822](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2309.01822)) — plus the OSF Simulation Studies Preregistration Template ([COS](https://www.cos.io/blog/introducing-the-simulation-studies-preregistration-template)); the survivable wording is "we are not aware of any pre-registered evaluation of an LLM/VLM-based robot coordination system," never "first pre-registered robotics experiment." The git-tag freeze is functionally an OSF registration; mirroring a timestamped pointer to OSF converts internal discipline into an external credential at near-zero cost, and Pawel et al.'s demonstration that questionable simulation practices "allow for spurious claims of superiority of any method" ([arXiv:2203.13076](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.13076)) is the exact argument for the freeze. **Fourth, nondeterminism and cost caveats.** Report T=0 but do not claim determinism: batch-size variation, not floating point, is the primary nondeterminism source ([arXiv:2506.09501](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.09501v2)), and one 120B model achieved only 12.5% output consistency at T=0 with fixed seeds ([arXiv:2511.07585](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.07585v1)); pin dated snapshots, state the run-date window as a drift bound ([arXiv:2510.25506](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.25506)), and quantify within-cell variance from repeats. Cost reporting follows the Princeton line — dollar-cost Pareto frontiers, not proxies ([AI Agents That Matter](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.01502); [HAL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.11977)) — with the twist that at 0.5–2 Hz, decision latency is a *system* variable coupled to time-to-resolution, not merely a cost. Finally, situate the custom battery against the new benchmark ecosystem rather than replacing it: PARTNR's finding that SoTA LLMs show "poor coordination and failures in task tracking and recovery from errors" ([ICLR 2025](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/file/a3cf318fbeec1126da21e9185ae9908c-Paper-Conference.pdf)) is the motivation citation, and a related-work table contrasting the thesis metric set (resolution rate, time-to-resolution, regret, provenance, churn) against the benchmarks' success-rate-plus-steps norm (LLM-Coordination [NAACL 2025](https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-naacl.448/); CoCoBench [arXiv:2608.28266](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.28266); Tool-RoCo [arXiv:2511.21510](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.21510)) is a cheap, defensible contribution since none covers Nav2-driven warehouse congestion.

## Conclusion

The net effect of the 2025–2026 crop is clarifying rather than threatening: GAVEL, Physical Agentic AI, and SyncPlan independently converging on verify-gate-correct architectures within a four-month window confirms the thesis sits on the field's trajectory, while the deep reads prove the specific measurement it proposes — per-leg provenance of a model-proposed plan — still has no occupant under any vocabulary. The registry's center of gravity should shift accordingly: the taxonomy (claim 1), the matched two-arm design (claim 3), and the provenance metric (the hardened core of claims 2/4/5) are the three load-bearing contributions; deterministic repair, scene-wide planning, and persistent plans are now framing, cited as concurrent work. That re-weighting is also time-sensitive — concurrent-work framing is only credible while the overlap papers are months old, which argues for tagging soon rather than letting the window close.

Three debts gate the tag: read GAVEL's evaluation section (it may report a two-way repaired-vs-replanned statistic that would force a further wording tweak to "three-way, per-leg, multi-robot"), fetch the two 403-blocked MDPI texts (MAKE 8(1):22 and Robotics 15(4):80, the latter studying plan preservation as a property), and skim DeepFleet before the related-work chapter characterizes it. Two smaller debts from the methodology notes ride along: confirm PhAIL's estimator is actually Kaplan-Meier before citing it as such, and re-check the exact format-restriction numbers in the EMNLP paper's tables. Everything else in this report is adoptable as written.
