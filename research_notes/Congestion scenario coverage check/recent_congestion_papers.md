# Recent (2024–2026) Congestion Papers in Multi-Robot / Warehouse-AMR Systems

Citation-verified sweep for the pre-registration coverage check. Every paper below was located via arXiv API or web search on 2026-09-29; verification depth is marked per paper: **[abs]** = abstract-only read, **[full]** = full-text (HTML) partially read, **[snip]** = search-snippet only. Study constraints for the FIT assessment: episodic scenes, ≤3–4 robots, hard 0.45 m pairwise floor, 300 s horizon.

## Q1. What 2024–2026 papers explicitly target warehouse/AMR congestion, and what scenes do they measure on?

### Takeaway
Warehouse congestion work splits into two clean camps: (a) lifelong-MAPF / fleet-scale papers measuring *throughput* on continuous task streams with hundreds–thousands of agents (fundamentally flow-based), and (b) constrained-space navigation papers measuring *deadlock/success* on episodic 2–5-robot doorway/corridor/intersection scenes. Almost nothing sits in between.

### Cited Findings

**Fleet-scale / flow-based (throughput camp):**
- RL-guided Rolling-Horizon Prioritized Planning for lifelong MAPF in warehouse automation (Zheng, Ma, Araki, Chen, Wu; arXiv 2026-03): congestion phenomenon = traffic congestion degrading system throughput; scenes = "realistic warehouse simulations" varied over agent densities, planning horizons, layouts; the learned policy "proactively prioritizes congested agents and strategically redirects agents from congestion." Flow-based, continuous task streams. **[abs]** — [arXiv:2603.23838](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.23838)
- Traffic Flow Optimisation for Lifelong MAPF (Chen et al., AAAI 2024): congestion from "free-flow optimal planning" concentrating agents; guides agents via congestion-avoiding paths; standard MAPF benchmark maps, lifelong task streams. **[snip]** — [arXiv:2308.11234](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.11234); [AAAI DOI](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1609/aaai.v38i18.30054)
- Guidance Graph Optimization for lifelong MAPF (IJCAI 2024): optimizes edge weights of a guidance graph to maximize throughput; "a well-optimized guidance graph reduces traffic congestion by encouraging agents to move in the same direction on an edge"; eight benchmark maps, up to 93×91 grids and 3,000 agents. **[snip]** — [arXiv:2402.01446](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.01446); follow-ups: [Online GGO, arXiv:2411.16506](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.16506); [Mixed guidance graphs (edge directions + weights), arXiv:2602.23468](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.23468)
- Congestion Mitigation Path Planning (Kato, Okumura, Sasaki, Yokomachi): phenomenon = "congestion intensifies where many agents enter the same area from different directions"; embeds edge-usage costs; evaluated in both discrete- and continuous-space scenarios at varying densities; metrics = local congestion + system throughput. **[abs]** — [arXiv:2508.05253](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.05253)
- Online task assignment + lifelong path finding at Meituan warehouses (Zhu, Xu, Guo, Lin; 2025): rule-based lifelong planner that "works well even in environments with severe local congestion"; scenes = real Meituan warehouse layouts in simulation; metrics = execution time (83.77% of deployed system) and agents-for-equal-throughput (60%). **[abs]** — [arXiv:2502.07332](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.07332)
- Model-Free Adaptive Parameter Tuning for warehouse fulfillment centers (Tokekar, Benosman, Chandan, Barbosa, Caldara, Durham — Amazon Robotics-affiliated authors; 2026): phenomenon = floor/lane congestion caused by pod "digout" strategies; extremum-seeking control tunes planner cost parameters against measured throughput; simulation over map and fleet-size variations, +5.0% / +8.4% throughput. **[abs]** — [arXiv:2608.21533](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.21533)
- Scaling Lifelong MAPF to More Realistic Settings (SoCS 2024, from the League of Robot Runners 2023 winners): names the two central research challenges as planning for up to 10,000 agents / up to 97.7% agent density, and "alleviating congestion and the effect of myopic behaviors," proposing moving guidance and traffic rules. Amazon-Robotics-sponsored competition; PIBT is the default planner. **[snip]** — [arXiv:2404.16162](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.16162); [SoCS page](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/SOCS/article/view/31565); [winning-entry repo](https://github.com/DiligentPanda/MAPF-LRR2023)
- NAHACO congestion-aware 3D AGV planning (2025): congestion-aware loss (CARL) in ACO; warehouse tests report congestion reduced up to 56.1%. **[abs]** — [arXiv:2504.01985](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.01985)
- Fragment Planner for corridor-dominant agricultural (polytunnel) environments (Heselden & Das; 2026): phenomenon = spatially dense navigation bottlenecks under contention; resource-centric (not agent-centric) conflict resolution; lifelong simulation on a 3.6 km topological map, varying fleet sizes; metric = task throughput (95% of optimal). **[abs]** — [arXiv:2603.12994](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.12994)
- Prioritized planning for puzzle-based storage at maximum density (Zhang, Geft, Yu, Bekris; 2026): deadlock-free parallel execution at full storage density; rectangular 2D grids; metric = makespan, near-linear improvement with robot count. **[abs]** — [arXiv:2608.07734](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.07734)

**Episodic / constrained-space (deadlock camp):**
- LiveNet (Gouru, Lakkoju, Chandra; 2024): deadlocks and collisions in "narrow doorways, hallways, and corridor intersections"; decentralized NN controller with unified safety+liveness CBF; evaluated in simulation on those scene types; baselines "fail to even reach the goal" or are 10–20× slower. Episodic, small robot counts. **[abs]** — [arXiv:2412.04659](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.04659)
- LIVEPOINT (Chen & Chandra; 2025): point-cloud CBFs; "zero collisions or deadlocks and a 100% success rate" in doorway and intersection scenarios vs MPC/ORCA/MPNet failures. Episodic. **[abs]** — [arXiv:2503.13098](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.13098)
- Opinion-driven (Nonlinear Opinion Dynamics) narrow-corridor traversal (Alghamdi & Park; 2025): narrow-corridor passage formulated as a multi-robot navigation game; deadlock likelihood is the phenomenon; a game-reduction technique scales to larger groups; simulation only. **[abs]** — [arXiv:2504.20947](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.20947)
- Deadlock-free social mini-games via discrete-time CBFs (Chandra et al., final version online 2025-04): doorway, hallway, corridor-intersection scenes; hardware = F1/10, Clearpath Jackal, Boston Dynamics Spot; metrics = success rate, collision rate, stop time, Δvelocity, path deviation, time-to-goal, flow rate. **[snip]** — [arXiv:2308.10966](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.10966); [survey/taxonomy version, Autonomous Robots 2026](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10514-026-10251-w)
- Multi-robot planning from CCTV camera networks in a real warehouse (Robinson, Ramtoula, Izaaryene, Newman, De Martini; Oxford, 2026): closest published analog to this project's M1 — off-board control from a distributed camera network; overlapping camera regions treated as shared resources "held by one robot at a time to prevent collisions and deadlocks"; field demo with **4 robots, 30 cameras, six 27 m aisles**; metrics = mission times + coordination statistics. **[abs]** — [arXiv:2606.06762](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.06762)
- MARL for deadlock handling among AMRs in intralogistics (Müller; dissertation, 2025): explicitly "deadlock-capable MAPF" reference models; grid-based environments + external logistics simulator; finding: MARL (CTDE, PPO/IMPALA) beats rule-based deadlock handling in "complex, congested environments," while rule-based stays competitive in sparse ones. **[abs]** — [arXiv:2511.07071](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.07071)

### Inferences
- The warehouse-congestion literature's dominant scored quantity is *throughput on continuous streams*, which an episodic 300 s battery cannot reproduce; the episodic camp's scored quantities (deadlock rate, success rate, time-to-goal, stop time, flow rate through the constriction) are the ones the study can adopt directly.
- The Oxford CCTV paper (2606.06762) is worth citing in the pre-registration as independent evidence that camera-network-based fleet coordination at ~4 robots in real aisles is a recognized evaluation setting.

### Gaps
- Robot counts for LiveNet/LIVEPOINT per-scene were not visible from abstracts (their scenario families are 2–4 agents per the social mini-games line, but exact counts unverified).
- I found no 2024–2026 paper that scores *queue spillback* (queue backing up past an upstream junction) as a named phenomenon in AMR settings; it appears only implicitly inside lifelong-MAPF congestion.

## Q2. Do recent papers treat BOTTLENECK (≥3 robots at a narrowing) and CROWDING (open-area density) as distinct testable regimes, and with what metrics?

### Takeaway
Yes, but the distinction is carried by scenario taxonomies rather than by named regimes: the social mini-games line explicitly separates "Open" (density/crowding) from "Doorway/Hallway/Intersection/Roundabout" (bottleneck) scenes, while the lifelong-MAPF line treats crowding as an *agent-density sweep* on a fixed map with throughput as the metric.

### Cited Findings
- SocialGym 2.0 benchmarks exactly five mini-game scenarios — **Open, Hallway, Doorway, Roundabout, Intersection** — as distinct evaluation regimes for multi-agent social navigation. **[snip]** — [arXiv:2303.05584](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.05584)
- The 2026 Autonomous Robots survey "Multi-robot navigation in social mini-games: definitions, taxonomy, and algorithms" formalizes constrained-space conflict scenes (doorways, hallways, intersections) as a scenario taxonomy, i.e., treats them as canonical, distinct testable geometries; it notes that without explicit scheduling these scenes produce "deadlocks, collisions, or non-smooth trajectories." **[snip]** — [Springer, Auton. Robots 2026](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10514-026-10251-w); base paper [arXiv:2308.10966](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.10966)
- Lifelong-MAPF papers operationalize crowding as density sweeps: RL-RH-PP "generalizes effectively across agent densities" (metric: total throughput) **[abs]** — [arXiv:2603.23838](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.23838); the SoCS 2024 challenges paper names "extremely high agent density (e.g., 97.7%)" as a regime of its own. **[snip]** — [arXiv:2404.16162](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.16162)
- CMPP distinguishes *local* congestion ("many agents enter the same area from different directions") from system-level throughput, scoring both. **[abs]** — [arXiv:2508.05253](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.05253)
- Metrics observed across the sweep: throughput (GGO, RL-RH-PP, CMPP, Fragment Planner), makespan / sum-of-costs (Tensor-Coord, cascade-containment paper, puzzle-based storage), time-to-goal + stop time + **flow rate** (social mini-games CBF paper), deadlock/collision counts and success rate (LIVEPOINT, LiveNet, GameChat), % correct priority (GameChat). Wait-time *distributions* were not reported as a headline metric in any abstract I read. **[abs/snip]** — sources as above.
- GameChat's extended tests push the doorway scene from 2 up to **3, 4, and 5 agents** — i.e., a ≥3-robot bottleneck through a single 0.4 m opening is an established episodic evaluation shape. **[full]** — [arXiv:2503.12333 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.12333v2)

### Inferences
- A pre-registered battery that adds (a) a ≥3-robot bottleneck (same-direction or mixed flows through one narrowing) and (b) one open-area crowding scene at 3–4 robots would mirror the field's own regime split at episodic scale; the literature's crowding regime proper (density sweeps, 100s of agents) is out of reach and can be honestly scoped out by citing the throughput-metric dependence.
- Flow rate through the constriction (mini-games CBF paper) is the one bottleneck metric that transfers to a 300 s episodic scene; makespan and per-robot wait time are the practical stand-ins for throughput.

### Gaps
- No 2024–2026 paper found that scores wait-time *distributions* (as opposed to means) for robot congestion; that metric seems to live in pedestrian-dynamics literature, not AMR papers.
- No paper found that explicitly *contrasts* bottleneck vs crowding as named regimes within one evaluation; the contrast must be assembled from scenario taxonomies.

## Q3. Recent work where CONVOY/platooning through a contested passage, or PRIORITY-OVERRIDE, is the tested resolution — what scenario shape elicits it?

### Takeaway
Priority-override is directly and quantitatively tested in GameChat (task-urgency precedence at a 0.4 m doorway and a 0.4 m × 0.4 m intersection, scored as "% correct priority"); convoy-through-contested-passage as a *scored resolution strategy* is essentially absent from 2024–2026 AMR literature — the nearest analogs are formation split/merge (EFLUX) and resource-centric partial-progression in corridors (Fragment Planner).

### Cited Findings
- **GameChat** (Chandra group, IEEE conf. 2025): agents with three task-urgency tiers (hospital > airport > grocery) negotiate leader/blocker roles via LLM dialogue; scenario shapes = **doorway: 0.4 m opening, agents start 2 m away on opposite sides; intersection: 0.4 m × 0.4 m crossing, agents 2 m from center**; 2 agents primary, 3–5 agents extended (doorway); simulation only; metric "% CP" = higher-priority agent reaches the conflict point first — 100% for GameChat vs 50% (chance) for non-communicative baselines; also makespan, collision/deadlock counts, min velocity, path deviation; ≥35% time reduction vs naive baseline in intersection. **[full]** — [arXiv:2503.12333](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.12333); [IEEE Xplore](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/11357267/)
- **EFLUX** (Zhang, Wu, Shi, Diller, Sukhatme, Kumar; 2026): LLM agentic framework reasoning jointly over formation *deformation* (scale/shear) and *reconfiguration* (split/merge) for multi-robot teams traversing confined/cluttered passages; simulation + hardware; claims reduced deadlock vs baselines. Split-then-merge through a constriction is the closest thing to elicited convoy behavior. **[abs]** — [arXiv:2607.12050](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.12050)
- Fragment Planner: in corridor-dominant environments, "partial route progression" through contended resources (i.e., letting a queue advance fragment-by-fragment through a corridor) is the mechanism; elicited by long single-lane polytunnel corridors under lifelong load. **[abs]** — [arXiv:2603.12994](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.12994)
- Ordering-flexible multi-robot convoying exists in 2024 (T-RO, guiding vector fields) but the convoy is around a *moving target*, not through a contested passage. **[snip]** — [arXiv:2401.06439](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.06439)
- Nonlinear Opinion Dynamics corridor paper: the narrow corridor with opposing groups is the shape that elicits commitment (who proceeds vs yields) — functionally a decentralized precedence mechanism, though not framed as explicit priority grant. **[abs]** — [arXiv:2504.20947](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.20947)

### Inferences
- For the study's convoy strategy arm, the literature-grounded eliciting shape is: **≥3 same-side robots + opposing traffic at a single narrowing**, where sequential same-direction passage (platoon) beats alternation — GameChat's 3–5-agent doorway and EFLUX's split/merge corridors are the citable precedents, but no prior work scores "convoy" as a discrete strategy label, so the study would be novel there (worth stating in the prereg).
- For priority-override, GameChat's %CP is a directly reusable scored outcome: give one robot an explicit high-priority task and score whether the grant is honored at the constriction. Its 0.4 m doorway is *below* the study's 0.45 m pairwise floor — the geometry must be widened; what matters per GameChat is that the opening admits only one robot at a time.

### Gaps
- No 2024–2026 warehouse/AMR paper found where "convoy/platooning through a contested passage" is a named, scored resolution strategy. This is a genuine literature hole the prereg can claim.
- No AMR paper found testing an externally *granted* precedence (a coordinator overriding local right-of-way); GameChat's priority is negotiated peer-to-peer, and prioritized-planning priorities are planner-internal, not scored behaviors.

## Q4. What do recent LLM/VLM-coordination papers evaluate on beyond pairwise conflicts — any multi-robot congestion suites?

### Takeaway
LLM/VLM-coordination evaluations remain overwhelmingly pairwise or small-N and episodic: the best beyond-pairwise congestion evidence is GameChat's 3–5-agent doorway and Tensor-Coord's 2–4-agent grids (where success degrades sharply with N); no LLM/VLM paper evaluates on a flow-based multi-robot congestion suite.

### Cited Findings
- GameChat extends to 3, 4, 5 agents in the doorway scenario (see Q3); still episodic, simulation-only. **[full]** — [arXiv:2503.12333](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.12333)
- "Why Solving Multi-agent Path Finding with LLMs has not Succeeded Yet" (Chen et al., 2024): evaluates LLMs on empty, room, and maze maps from the standard MAPF benchmark; LLMs solve easy scenarios but "fail to generate valid solutions when scenarios become harder" — i.e., LLM planning breaks precisely as congestion rises. **[snip]** — [arXiv:2401.03630](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.03630); related benchmark framing: [Solving MAPF as an LLM Benchmark, OpenReview](https://openreview.net/forum?id=8hAxEFRVQT)
- Tensor-Coord (2026): LLM multi-agent delivery planning on 5×5 grids; conflict-free convergence = 100% at 2 agents, 80% at 3, 60% at 4 — quantifying the same N-scaling cliff. **[abs]** — [arXiv:2606.16478](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.16478)
- EFLUX: LLM strategy selection (split/merge/deform) for formations in confined passages, sim + hardware (see Q3). **[abs]** — [arXiv:2607.12050](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.12050)
- Cascade containment in LLM-powered multi-robot systems (Khalid & Min; 2026-09): congestion appears as a *consequence* of manipulated claims propagating through an LLM fleet; evaluation = multi-robot transportation environment, injected false obstacle claims, varying team sizes; metrics = cascade containment, sum-of-costs, makespan, coverage. **[abs]** — [arXiv:2609.30523](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30523)
- VLM-coordination papers evaluate on exploration/search, not congestion: COMRES-VLM (up to 6 robots, indoor exploration) **[abs]** — [arXiv:2509.26324](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.26324); CRAFT (multi-quadruped navigation, VLM reward refinement) **[abs]** — [arXiv:2509.14380](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.14380); SimWorld-Robotics (urban, 2-robot cooperative search; SOTA VLMs "struggle") **[abs]** — [arXiv:2512.10046](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.10046)
- MemTransfer (2026): 100 navigation cases in a simulated warehouse under a frozen VLM policy — a warehouse VLM *navigation* benchmark (route blocking, memory), but single-agent, not congestion. **[abs]** — [arXiv:2609.32313](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.32313)
- Survey context: LLMs for Multi-Robot Systems survey (2025) catalogs the field; no congestion suite listed. **[snip]** — [arXiv:2502.03814](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.03814)

### Inferences
- No published LLM/VLM-coordination paper scores a battery of distinct congestion *phenomena* (deadlock vs bottleneck vs crowding vs blockage); the field's evaluations are per-scenario success/makespan. A phenomenon-typed episodic battery would exceed current LLM/VLM-coordination evaluation practice, not lag it — but reviewers will expect at least one >2-robot scene, given GameChat and Tensor-Coord both go to 4–5.

### Gaps
- Could not find any 2024–2026 VLM paper that consumes an *overhead/BEV annotated scene image* for multi-robot congestion decisions (the closest are single-robot VLM navigation and the non-VLM Oxford CCTV system); the M2-style setup appears unpublished.

## Q5. New benchmark releases 2024–2026 for multi-robot congestion/traffic in structured environments?

### Takeaway
Several new platforms landed in 2024–2026 — WareRover (coupled scheduling+MAPF warehouse sim), GRACE (grid/roadmap/continuous unified benchmark), the League of Robot Runners competition infrastructure, and the social mini-games taxonomy — but all congestion-specific ones are flow-based; the only episodic suite is the mini-games family.

### Cited Findings
- **WareRover** (2026): holistic RMFS simulator tightly coupling order scheduling with MAPF; integrates dynamic order streams, physics-aware motion constraints, heterogeneous kinematics, and stochastic execution failures; authors argue decoupling scheduling from "low-level congestion" is "a fundamental bottleneck" and that SOTA algorithms "falter under these realistic coupled constraints" — an explicit essential-scenario claim: congestion evaluation must couple dispatching with execution and include non-nominal failures. **[abs]** — [arXiv:2602.13999](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.13999); [project page](https://hhh-x.github.io/WareRover/)
- **GRACE** (Zang, Mannucci, Barz, Schillinger, Lier, Hönig; 2026): unified 2D MRPP simulator/benchmark instantiating the same task at grid, roadmap, and continuous abstraction levels with a common evaluation protocol; general-purpose, not congestion-targeted. **[abs]** — [arXiv:2603.10858](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.10858)
- **League of Robot Runners** (Amazon-Robotics-sponsored lifelong-MAPF competition): benchmark infrastructure with 1 s/step planning budgets, up to ~10,000 agents; the SoCS 2024 retrospective names congestion alleviation as a headline open challenge. Flow-based by construction. **[snip]** — [arXiv:2404.16162](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.16162)
- **Social mini-games** (SocialGym 2.0 five-scenario suite; 2026 Autonomous Robots taxonomy survey): the standing episodic constrained-space benchmark family (Open/Hallway/Doorway/Roundabout/Intersection). **[snip]** — [arXiv:2303.05584](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.05584); [Springer 2026 survey](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10514-026-10251-w)
- **MemTransfer** (2026): 100-case simulated-warehouse VLM navigation benchmark (single-agent; route blocking as a condition). **[abs]** — [arXiv:2609.32313](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.32313)
- Industry signal (low-reliability marketing/blog sources, use cautiously): trade coverage describes single-robot failure cascades reducing adjacent-robot throughput 12–18% until cleared, and an MIT/Symbotic congestion-prediction collaboration claiming +25% throughput — directionally consistent with the peer-reviewed RL-RH-PP paper (Wu group), which is the citable artifact. **[snip]** — [CXTMS blog](https://cxtms.com/blog/mit-symbotic-ai-warehouse-robot-congestion-prevention-throughput-2026); [Promwad](https://promwad.com/news/warehouse-amrs-in-2026-what-changes-when-perception-routing-and-fleet-logic-move-to-the-edge); peer-reviewed anchor: [arXiv:2603.23838](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.23838). There is also a granted US patent "Robot congestion management" ([USPTO 12083681](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/12083681)) — existence verified, content unread.

### Inferences
- WareRover's stochastic-execution-failure argument is the nearest published cousin to the study's "anomaly blockage" phenomenon (a stalled/faulted robot creating congestion); no episodic benchmark scores it, so a 3-robot blocked-aisle scene with one faulted robot would be novel and can cite WareRover's essentiality argument plus the industry cascade anecdotes as motivation.

### Gaps
- No dedicated *episodic* multi-robot congestion benchmark release found in 2024–2026; the mini-games suite (2023 origin, 2026 survey codification) remains the only episodic option.
- Livelock as a distinct phenomenon: no 2024–2026 AMR paper found that names and scores livelock separately from deadlock (oscillatory non-progress is folded into "deadlock" or "non-smooth trajectories" in the mini-games line).

## FIT assessment against the study's constraints (episodic, ≤3–4 robots, 0.45 m pairwise floor, 300 s)

### Takeaway
The mini-games family (doorway, hallway, corridor intersection, roundabout, open) at 2–4 robots is the literature-sanctioned scenario set that fits the constraints outright; every throughput-defined congestion regime (lifelong MAPF, GGO, LoRR, WareRover, fulfillment-center tuning) fundamentally cannot fit and should be scoped out explicitly rather than approximated.

### Cited Findings (fit-by-fit)
- **FIT — directly usable:** doorway (2–5 robots, GameChat [arXiv:2503.12333](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.12333)); corridor intersection (2+ robots; GameChat, LiveNet [arXiv:2412.04659](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.04659), LIVEPOINT [arXiv:2503.13098](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.13098), mini-games CBF [arXiv:2308.10966](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.10966)); narrow corridor with opposing groups (NOD [arXiv:2504.20947](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.20947)); hallway and roundabout (SocialGym 2.0 [arXiv:2303.05584](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.05584)); small-N episodic grids (Tensor-Coord, 2–4 agents [arXiv:2606.16478](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.16478)); few-robot real-aisle coordination (Oxford CCTV, 4 robots [arXiv:2606.06762](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.06762)).
- **FIT with adaptation:** GameChat geometry (0.4 m opening, 2 m approach) is below the 0.45 m pairwise floor — scale the opening to admit exactly one robot at the study's robot diameter + floor; the eliciting property is single-robot admittance, not the absolute 0.4 m. **[full]** — [arXiv:2503.12333](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.12333v2). EFLUX-style split/merge corridors also adapt to 3–4 robots. **[abs]** — [arXiv:2607.12050](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.12050)
- **CANNOT FIT (flow-based / large-N by construction):** lifelong-MAPF throughput suites and density sweeps (RL-RH-PP [arXiv:2603.23838](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.23838); traffic-flow AAAI 2024 [arXiv:2308.11234](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.11234); GGO family [arXiv:2402.01446](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.01446), [arXiv:2411.16506](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.16506), [arXiv:2602.23468](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.23468)); League of Robot Runners (10k agents, 97.7% density [arXiv:2404.16162](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.16162)); CMPP dense flows [arXiv:2508.05253](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.05253); WareRover dynamic order streams [arXiv:2602.13999](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.13999); fulfillment-center digout tuning [arXiv:2608.21533](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.21533); Meituan lifelong TAPF [arXiv:2502.07332](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.07332); Fragment Planner lifelong corridors [arXiv:2603.12994](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.12994). Their scored quantity (steady-state throughput) is undefined on a 300 s single-conflict episode with ≤4 robots.

### Inferences (coverage-check verdict for the prereg)
- The current head-on-corridor-centric battery covers only one cell of the field's episodic scenario taxonomy. Literature-grounded additions, in priority order: (1) **corridor intersection** (crossing flows — the single most-used non-head-on scene: mini-games CBF, LiveNet, LIVEPOINT, GameChat); (2) **≥3-robot doorway/bottleneck** with mixed same-side/opposing composition (GameChat 3–5-agent extension — also the shape that can elicit convoy vs alternation); (3) **priority-override scene** scoring a %CP-style correct-precedence outcome (GameChat); (4) **anomaly-blockage scene** (one faulted robot narrowing an aisle — motivated by WareRover's stochastic-failure essentiality claim; unscored anywhere episodically, so it is a novelty claim, not a coverage claim); (5) an **open-area 3–4-robot crowding** scene covers the taxonomy's "Open" cell at token scale, but cannot claim to represent the density-sweep crowding regime.
- Honest scoping sentence available for the prereg: no episodic ≤4-robot battery can measure the throughput-defined congestion regimes that dominate warehouse literature; the study's construct is conflict-resolution quality at constrictions, for which the mini-games metric set (success/deadlock rate, time-to-goal, stop time, flow rate, %CP) is the established instrument.

### Gaps
- Exact per-scene robot counts for LiveNet/LIVEPOINT/mini-games-CBF hardware runs unverified (abstract/snippet-level only).
- Livelock and queue-spillback have no 2024–2026 AMR scoring precedent found; if the battery includes them, they are novel phenomenon labels without direct literature anchors.
