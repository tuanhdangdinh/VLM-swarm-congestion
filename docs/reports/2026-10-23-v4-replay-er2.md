**Cost method.** Every new cache file is one billed call. Dollars are an
estimate from token counts:

- ER-2 uses the project's cost_watch formula (`$1/M` in, `$5/M` out plus
  thought, from `scripts/s1b_live_batch_stats.py`), which reproduces the
  recorded $1.25.
- Sonnet 5.5 uses `$2/M` in and `$10/M` out (`claude-api` skill price table,
  cached 2026-10-06).

These figures are not the provider bill. Calls refused with 429 bill nothing.
The total stays under the $12 cap, and the R4 gate held: cumulative spend was
$2.65 after R1-R3, below $6.

**Sampling.**

- ER-2: the Interactions API has no temperature field, so the `temperature
  0.0` request cannot be honoured. The configured values are dropped and
  recorded per call as `unsupported_sampling`. `thinking_level` is low.
- Sonnet 5.5: the pinned `anthropic` section configures 0.0, but the backend
  never sends it, because the model returns HTTP 400 on non-default sampling.
  It is recorded as `unsupported_sampling`. `thinking: between_tools` was in
  effect, with `thinking_tokens` 0 on all 330 calls.
- R4 reasoner: `max_attempts` 4 and grey fallback after 2. Every item answered
  on attempt 1.
- ER-2 reasoner: the shared block, 2 attempts.

**Latency.**

- ER-2 calls took about 8 s each, measured from cache-file write times. The
  partial runs were rescored from the cache, so their run files show no
  latency.
- Sonnet 5.5 calls took about 4.2 s each (`latency_ms` in R4).

**How the partial ER-2 runs were recovered.**

- The bench writes its output only at the end of a run. A 429 also puts the

  killed, and nothing was billed for the items they had not reached.
- A cache-only rescorer (`data/bench/v4/analysis_20261023/cached_only.py`, untracked, beside the other analysis scripts and their outputs) then re-ran `replay_items` per
  item, with a backend that raises on any cache miss. It kept only items

- The dropped items are a contiguous tail in every run, so the kept items are
  item-order prefixes.
- The **common prefix of R1-R3 is 110 items**: churn_wave 5, live_batch 48

  That is 72 recorded-valid and 66 recorded-positive items, almost all on
  `s1b_dynamic.yaml`. **No shield, escort or tranche-1 scenes are included.**
- The common-prefix tables below restrict **every** column, recorded included,
  to the same ids.

**Resume after the cap is raised.** Re-run the three original commands

## Option Choices and First-Slot Bias (Common 110)

Options are ordered yields → reverses → reroutes → waits, closest pair first.
Original option 1 is therefore almost always the closest executable yield. When
R2 chose it, it was a yield 61 times, a reverse 2 and a wait 1.

| Chosen | R2 (generator order) | R3 (shuffled), presented slot | R3 (shuffled), original index |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **64 (0.582)** | **7 (0.064)** | **66 (0.600)** |
| 2 | 6 | 3 | 7 |
| 3 | 2 | 6 | 5 |
| 4 | 2 | 9 | 3 |
| 5+ | 21 | 70 | 14 |
| none | 15 (0.136) | 15 (0.136) | 15 |
| parse failure | 0 | 0 | 0 |

- The mean is 19.9 options per item, so chance for any one option is about
  0.05.
- **There is no first-slot bias.** R2 and R3 choose the same underlying option
  on 83/110 items. After shuffling, original option 1 is still chosen on 66/110
  (vs 64/110 when shown first). Presented slot 1 drops to 7/110, and original
  option 1 landed in slot 1 on only 4 items. The concentration on option 1 is a
  content preference for the closest executable yield, not a position effect.
- **"none".** It is 15/110 in both runs, and 10 of those 15 are on recorded
  positives. A "none" scores as a missed positive, which explains part of the
  GF coverage loss.
- **R4b (Sonnet, unshuffled, all 187).** It chooses option 1 on 94 (0.503) and
  "none" on 14 (0.075), with 0 parse failures. On the common 110 it chooses
  option 1 on 60 and "none" on 8. R4b was not shuffled, so its option-1 share
  is not separated from position.

## Interpretation (Conservative)

1. Exploratory only: 31 pilot and tranche-1 episodes, no frames, one replay per item. The ER-2 comparison covers 66 positives from early `s1b_dynamic` batches only.
2. Text-only ER-2 roughly reproduces its live answers on detection (0.93) and involved sets (0.79), but strategy only 0.64. One run gives no variance estimate.
3. On the common positives, R1 matches recorded feasibility (0.52 vs 0.53) and coverage is a little lower (0.59 vs 0.65); one unseeded run cannot separate image from sampling effects.
4. Grounding-first makes every positive execute as asked (by construction) but costs coverage: about 0.38-0.42 vs 0.59-0.65 for ER-2, and 0.58 vs 0.66 for Sonnet over 137.
5. The loss is consistent with a preference for the closest-pair yield plus "none" on 10/66 positives, not with listing order: shuffling leaves the original-option-1 share unchanged (0.58 vs 0.60).
6. GF raises yielder-vs-MC agreement (ER-2 5/8 vs 2/4; Sonnet 21/25 vs 9/23). The MC denominators are tiny.
7. On the same grey-image 66, Sonnet 5.5 baseline has higher strict feasibility than ER-2 (0.52 vs 0.40, R4a vs R1) at similar coverage and F1, with fewer `junction_clear` answers, on a prompt tuned for ER-2.
8. Next lever, if pursued: options that span the whole active cluster (multi-robot), or cluster-conditioned option lists. Pair-only options cap coverage.
9. (Rev 2) On the full 137 recorded positives, text-only ER-2 baseline matches its live answers on strict feasibility (0.374 vs 0.372) and is a little lower on coverage (0.642 vs 0.686). The prefix result in conclusion 3 extends to shield, escort and tranche-1 scenes.
10. (Rev 2) Grounding-first's trade holds on the full set for ER-2: strict feasibility 0.37 → 1.0 and MC yielder agreement 3/11 → 22/25, but coverage 0.64 → 0.47 and matched F1 0.90 → 0.84. Sonnet GF keeps more coverage than ER-2 GF (0.577 vs 0.474), partly because ER-2 answers "none" more often (23-26 vs 7 of 137).
11. (Rev 2) Shuffling still changes nothing material (R2 vs R3 within 0.01 on every headline metric; original option 1 chosen 95 vs 90 of 187).
12. (Rev 2) Sonnet 5.5 baseline remains ahead of ER-2 baseline on strict feasibility over the 137 (0.523 vs 0.374) at similar coverage and F1. Under GF the two models tie on feasibility by construction, and Sonnet is ahead on coverage.
