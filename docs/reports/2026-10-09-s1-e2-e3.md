## Summary

The offline diagnosis splits E1's "0.00 m rear room on 44/46 analyses" in two:

- **The number is a code artifact.** The 44 refusals are one frozen scene. Its node-handoff arriver, robot_4, faces 57°, 3° outside the 30° axis snap. So the dynamic view falls back to the declared x-axis junction box, measured across the full aisle band. Every rack face sits on the declared mouth at x = ±0.6, so every robot reads 1e-16 m on both sides. This is the A13(ii) mouth-straddle defect, on the one branch (`dynamic_arms:ambiguous`) that the footprint fix never covered. All 46 E1 artifacts re-ground byte-identically offline.
- **The true room is mixed.** robot_1 and robot_3 are genuinely boxed in at 0 m: the gate jaw and the SW rack. The other three robots have 0.5–1.9 m, but sit inside or near the safety latch radius.
- **The refusal outcome is invariant, so no geometry fix shipped.** Declared geometry reads 0 for every robot in S1, so these scenes would be refused anyway. A heading-axis snap would back robot_4 33° off its lane.

Two fixes shipped, both default off and flag-scoped. Each is merged into `feat/v3-integration`, with suite delta zero against a fresh `bedd6a3` baseline.

- **`refusal_no_action`** (M5, task stream only). A refused yield is withheld instead of fanning out waits.
- **`escort_turn_cap`** (M3, dynamic mode). The escort and clearance distance are capped at the passer's distance to its turn.

- **Fix 1 works mechanically.** E2 withheld every refusal (5/5), the suppression armed, and Layer 2 sent M4 nothing all episode.
- **The scene froze before Layer 2 could act.** By about 47 sim-s all five robots were held by the M4 min-distance latch: an S-strip triple and a W-arm pair. A latched pair has no release except an exempt clearing manoeuvre. Every analysis was refused: one single-robot refusal, plus four on the second `ambiguous` sub-class (matching lanes, bearing across them).
- **Even a lane view finds no candidate.** An offline what-if that grounds the S-strip pair on its lane axis finds no valid retreat candidate, because the yielder is sandwiched.
- **Result:** 0/25 tasks, no executed yield, and 0 Nav2 recoveries, since every goal was cancelled by the latch.
- **What E2 cannot settle.** The E1 harm claim (waits pre-empt Nav2 recoveries) stays untested closed-loop: no Nav2-free wedge lasted long enough. The escort cap was never exercised.

### Verdict and split

- **Code defect: the reported number.** 44/44 refusals print 0.00 m for every robot because of the declared-geometry fallback. It is the same mouth-straddle class as A13(ii), on the `ambiguous` branch.
  - It is **outcome-invariant in S1**. Declared S1 geometry gives every robot 0 room at any pose, because the rack quadrants start at the mouth and every ±0.6 m band reaches one. So `ambiguous` always means refusal.
  - **No geometry fix shipped.** Snapping robot_4 to the y arm would ground a y-lane, but M4 backs along the start heading, 33° off that axis. That is about 0.54 m of drift per metre, roughly 0.7 m over a 1.3 m backup, which defeats the 0.05 m box-sweep margin. That would be the lane-vs-machinery defect again.
  - **Step-6 recommendation:** an explicit, honest refusal on the ambiguous branch ("heading between axes"), with no fake 0.00 m.
- **Genuine geometry: 2/5 robots.** robot_1 (jaw) and robot_3 (SW rack) truly have 0 m.
- **Latch-bound: the other 3.** They have 0.5–1.9 m of static room, but they sit in permanent min-distance latch pairs. From the M1 scene log:
  - robot_2/robot_4 from 51.3 sim-s, minimum 0.411 m;
  - robot_3/robot_5 from 104.5 sim-s, minimum 0.383 m.
- **Why latch pairs cannot recover.** The safety monitor cancels a latched robot's Nav2 goal, and `/safety/holds` stops the client's retry. A pair separates only through an exempt clearing manoeuvre, which the view refuses here.
- **Where the problem sits.** Downstream, in what happens after the refusal. robot_1 was **never latched** after the yield (r1/r5 windows end at 99.1 sim-s), so it is the one E1 pin held only by Layer-2 waits.

### What happened

- **The jam (37–47 sim-s).** Three robots drew S as their first task: robot_1 from E, robot_2 from Wm and robot_3 from N. Meanwhile robot_5 was released at 37.4 sim-s, leaving S northbound for E.
  - The two occupied-node approaches (r1 → S at 37.5, r3 → S at 42.8) both met robot_5 travelling out of S.
  - robot_1 and robot_5 met side by side in the 1.2 m S strip and latched at 0.305 m.
  - robot_3 closed behind robot_1 and robot_5 and latched to both.
  - robot_2, heading Wm → S, reached the **SW rack corner at 38.1 sim-s**. That is the same corner class as E1's robot_3, here with a 0.040 m centre clearance. robot_4 (Wp → S) then latched to robot_2.
- **The freeze.** By **46.9 sim-s all five robots were latch-held** and static to the 600 sim-s horizon (`live_watch` 20 s ranges 0.000 m).
- **Layer 2's part.** Its first analysis came at 39.6 sim-s, after robot_2 was already at the corner. No command ever reached M4. **The gridlock formed and persisted independently of Layer 2.**

**Why every analysis refused (the second `ambiguous` sub-class).**

- **The geometry.** The handoff pair robot_1/robot_5 are both on the y axis (robot_1 at −101°, robot_5 at 81°). Side by side, their bearing is x (|dx| 0.28 > |dy| 0.13), so rule 5 ("matching lanes across the bearing") returns the declared geometry, and the gate reads 0.00 m for everyone.
- **The what-if.** An offline what-if re-grounded both E2 ambiguous snapshots (`40e873aa`, `68b6f27f`) with a lane view on the shared heading axis (y). It passes the gate, but the retreat sweep returns `retreat=no-valid-candidate`. robot_1 is sandwiched: robot_3 sits 0.42 m behind it, inside the backup corridor. The yield then falls back to a `reroute` with empty parameters, and what M4 does with that was not checked.
- **Conclusion.** E2's refusals are **genuine geometry** at the yielder, not only the reporting artifact.

**robot_2's pin class.**

- **Latch-held.** `wedge_capture` (`evidence/E2_r2_swcorner_011541`, 60 s, costmaps + updates) shows `/robot_2/cmd_vel` = 0.
- **Its Nav2-free window was too short.** It ran from 38.1 sim-s (at the corner) to 44.9 (latched): about 7 sim-s, shorter than the pre-registered 20 sim-s commanded-vs-moved window and shorter than Nav2's progress-checker cycle. So it is **not** a physical pin under the registered test, and the stop rule did not fire.
- **No self-release.** Nothing released by the horizon. The latch has no release path.

### New residual: latch-cluster gridlock

The brief asked for "A13(iv) + A14" verdicts. **No A14 ruling exists** in prereg-v3 (`feat/v3-design`) or in any v3 branch's docs as of this report. The residual below is this report's own finding, offered as A14 material.

- **The pattern.** Both episodes end in **permanent min-distance latch clusters**:
  - E1: 2 pairs, from 51.3 and 104.5 sim-s.
  - E2: all 5 robots by 46.9 sim-s.
- **How they form.** Nav2 brings robots to 0.30–0.42 m in the 1.2 m aisles: head-on and side-by-side in a strip, or queued at a node. The safety monitor then cancels both goals and holds them. Its only release path is an exempt clearing manoeuvre (yield/reverse).
- **Why Layer 2 cannot help.** The dynamic view refuses exactly the configurations latches create:
  - off-axis headings (E1);
  - side-by-side lane pairs (E2);
  - a sandwiched yielder (E2 what-if).
- **The consequence.** E1 and E2 both ended in a tail gridlock that no Layer-2 decision cleared. E1's one executable yield (cb78) did run, but before the latch pairs formed.
- **Hypothesis for Step 6, not a finding.** If this pattern holds, S1's task-completion outcome says little about reasoning.
- **Open risk before any resume: the empty-`reroute` fallback.** A production lane view that reaches `retreat=no-valid-candidate` falls back to a `reroute` with no waypoints (the E2 what-if). `refusal_no_action` does not withhold it, so a live episode could dispatch it. M4's handling of it is unchecked.
- **Step-6 / owner options (none built here):**
  - a latch-aware release, for example a timed, exempt mutual back-off for a latched pair with free rear room;
  - multi-step clearing, back the rear robot first;
  - S1 traffic de-confliction, for example staggering or limiting simultaneous same-node draws (E2's three S draws in the first leg set up the jam);
  - accepting latch gridlock as a registered outcome class.
