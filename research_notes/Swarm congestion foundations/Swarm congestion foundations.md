# Two congestion traditions license small-N semantic resolution

The swarm-robotics and flow-physics congestion literatures hand the thesis everything it needs except its own contribution: a canonical anchor set of roughly a dozen citations (Marcolino/dos Passos on common-target congestion; Lighthill–Whitham, Nagel–Schreckenberg, Helbing, Liu–Nagel, Zuriguel on flow and jamming), a ready-made metric suite (asymptotic throughput, clog frequency, cluster-dissolution time, and a clogged-vs-intermittent order parameter that principled-ly separates "resolved slowly" from "never resolves"), a formalized deadlock-vs-congestion distinction assembled from two independent traditions, and — critically — a verified absence of any vision-language or semantic scene reasoning across the entire congestion canon, with only the SwarmVLM/ImpedanceGPT navigation-parameter line requiring an explicit carve-out. The most strategically valuable finding is the scale-bridging chain: the physics literature's own causal unit of congestion is the few-body structure — arches of a handful of grains ([Zuriguel et al. 2014](https://diposit.ub.edu/dspace/handle/2445/126027)), "small groups of pedestrians temporarily block the way out" ([Helbing et al. 2000 retrospective](https://collective-dynamics.eu/index.php/cod/article/view/A168)) — so a thesis resolving 2–5-robot structured conflicts is intervening at congestion's generative unit, not studying a toy reduction. The posture that follows: **adopt** the metrics, **cite** the anchors, and **position against** the uniform identity-free, aggregate-flow character of every existing mechanism — none of which names which robot is in conflict with which, or why.

## Twelve anchors and five named phenomena cover the backdrop

The swarm-robotics congestion canon runs through a single lineage the thesis should cite as its spine. Marcolino & Chaimowicz established the field's classic framing — many robots converging on one target, mitigated by local traffic-control behaviors — at ICRA 2008 and IROS 2009 ([verlab record](https://www.verlab.dcc.ufmg.br/old/publicacoes/publi/marcolino09traffic1)). The pivotal 2017 result, quoted in the successor papers, is that **standard ORCA reciprocal collision avoidance "reaches an equilibrium where robots could not arrive at the target despite avoiding collisions"** ([dos Passos et al., arXiv:2201.09337](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.09337)) — the swarm literature's own concession that reactive avoidance alone produces unresolvable congestion, which is precisely the motivation slot a semantic Layer-2 occupies. The lineage culminates in dos Passos, Duquesne & Marcolino's throughput theory ([arXiv:2201.09335](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.09335)) and the SQF/TRVF algorithms in **Robotics and Autonomous Systems vol. 159 (2023)** ([arXiv:2201.09337](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.09337)). Flanking cites: Soysal & Şahin's probabilistic aggregation FSMs ([SIS 2005](https://avesis.metu.edu.tr/yayin/fe72043b-02cb-410b-a8d9-74e849986738/probabilistic-aggregation-strategies-in-swarm-robotic-systems)) for the mechanism toolkit, Couzin & Franks' army-ant three-lane formation ([Proc. R. Soc. B 2003](https://kops.uni-konstanz.de/entities/publication/c505c71f-7270-438e-9858-fe0474b767b1/full)) for bidirectional-flow conventions, and Aguilar et al.'s fire-ant/robophysical tunnel study in **Science 361 (2018)** ([PDF](https://crablab.gatech.edu/pages/publications/pdf/science-2018.pdf)) as the flagship congestion-as-granular-clogging paper. Note the two verification caveats from the research pass: the exact venue/volume of Marcolino et al. 2017 (believed Autonomous Robots) and the second IROS'09 companion paper need a DBLP check before camera-ready.

The flow-physics backdrop compresses into six to eight citations carrying five named phenomena. The **fundamental diagram** (flow rises with density to a capacity maximum, then falls to jam density) traces to Greenshields 1935, with the macroscopic theory and **kinematic shock waves** from Lighthill & Whitham 1955 plus Richards 1956 — the LWR framing restated throughout the modern CA-traffic literature ([arXiv:cond-mat/9808152](https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/9808152)). **Phantom jams** — spontaneous stop-and-go waves above a critical density with no external cause — belong to Nagel–Schreckenberg 1992. Pedestrian dynamics contributes Helbing–Molnár's 1995 social-force model and Helbing–Farkas–Vicsek's Nature 2000 **faster-is-slower effect** with intermittent **arching/clogging** at exits, confirmed in the retrospective and follow-up literature ([Collective Dynamics](https://collective-dynamics.eu/index.php/cod/article/view/A168); [arXiv:1706.06140](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1706.06140)); Seyfried et al. supply the benchmark empirical pedestrian fundamental diagram ([arXiv:physics/0506170](https://arxiv.org/abs/physics/0506170)). Granular physics contributes **jamming** as a phase transition (Liu–Nagel 1998's jamming phase diagram; O'Hern et al. 2003's point J) and the **clogging transition** at bottlenecks, unified across sheep, pedestrians, grains and colloids by [Zuriguel et al. 2014](https://diposit.ub.edu/dspace/handle/2445/126027). The terminological discipline matters: *clogging* (constriction, few-body arches) is the physics term closest to warehouse bottleneck congestion; *jamming* is bulk density-driven rigidification; *phantom jam* is flow instability without an obstacle. The single best bridge cite between the two traditions is Alonso-Llanes, Garcimartín & Zuriguel's single-file robot experiment ([arXiv:2403.08683](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.08683)): the Pamplona granular-clogging group **reproducing the fundamental diagram on actual wheeled robots**, with a free-flow → intermittent → totally-congested two-stage transition. One sentence through that paper connects the thesis's warehouse robots to the entire clogging literature.

## Four metrics transfer directly into small-N scored cells

The field offers no consensus "congestion index" — stating that absence is safe if phrased as "no single consensus congestion index" — but four measurable quantities port cleanly into per-cell oracle cost tables, each with literature pedigree. First, **asymptotic throughput**: dos Passos et al. prove that the throughput of a bounded common target area as t→∞ is **finite, while arrival-time-per-robot diverges**, making throughput the scale-stable efficiency measure ([arXiv:2201.09335](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.09335)); lifelong MAPF independently standardizes task throughput (goals per timestep) as the warehouse objective ([GGO, IJCAI 2024](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.01446); [Chen et al., AAAI 2024](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/30054)). Citing both gives per-junction throughput a double pedigree: swarm theory and warehouse practice. Second and third, the Aguilar pair: Science 2018 scores excavation strategies by **(a) frequency of flow-stopping clogs and (b) dissolution time of clusters that do form** ([PDF](https://crablab.gatech.edu/pages/publications/pdf/science-2018.pdf)) — an incidence × time-to-resolution decomposition directly adoptable as cost-table columns, with clog-dissolution time being literally time-to-resolution for a 3–5-agent clog. Fourth, the **clogged-vs-intermittent order parameter**: Zuriguel et al. define the clogging transition by the **divergence of the mean time lapse between consecutive passages (power-law tail exponent ≤ 2)** ([UB record](https://diposit.ub.edu/dspace/handle/2445/126027); [arXiv:2004.04413](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.04413)). This is the citable rationale for treating "unresolved after timeout" as a distinct outcome class rather than a large time value — a methodological decision the thesis would otherwise have to defend ad hoc.

Two supporting measures round out the suite. The robot single-file study adds **jam duration and jam size (robots per jam)**, whose divergence marks the transition to total congestion ([arXiv:2403.08683](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.08683)) — small-N analogs: episode duration and number of robots drawn in. And **delay versus free-flow baseline** is the oldest traffic metric of all: free-flow speed is the fundamental diagram's zero-density intercept (Greenshields/LWR), and in the thesis's setting the baseline is cheap — run each cell with one robot — which makes per-robot delay *exactly* the flow-theoretic quantity rather than an analogy. The faster-is-slower curve (egress time has a minimum at an optimal desired speed, [retrospective](https://collective-dynamics.eu/index.php/cod/article/view/A168)) additionally justifies that "push harder" strategies can legitimately score worse in the oracle tables. One caveat: "time-to-resolution" as a named metric does not appear verbatim anywhere in the flow literature — the honest citation is to its nearest named kin, clog-dissolution time and jam duration — and the closed-form throughput expressions in dos Passos should be pulled from the arXiv PDF before the metrics section is written.

## Deadlock and congestion are formally distinct — in two separate vocabularies the thesis must bridge

The literature does formalize deadlock as qualitatively distinct from congestion, but in two traditions that never cite each other, and the thesis can present their correspondence as original related-work structure. The discrete tradition — AGV/manufacturing resource theory — defines **deadlock as circular wait** on shared traffic resources (each vehicle blocked by another, none can proceed), adds **livelock** (moving but never arriving) as a third category, and frames "traffic liveness" as the property deadlock destroys ([Reveliotis group](https://www2.isye.gatech.edu/~spyros/publications/Med-19.pdf); [A-DRIVE, arXiv:2204.04910](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2204.04910)). Crucially, this literature treats deadlock as a *cause* of congestion, not a point on the same continuum. The continuous tradition — Grover, Liu & Sycara's WAFR analysis — defines deadlock as a **force-equilibrium of the closed-loop dynamics**: robots stand still at a stable equilibrium short of their goals, with the deadlock set non-empty, bounded, and on the boundary of the safety set ([arXiv:1911.09146](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1911.09146)). The flow-physics vocabulary supplies the quantitative version of the same dichotomy: **congested flow is intermittent (power-law bursts, finite mean passage time) while the clogged state is permanent arrest (divergent mean)** ([Zuriguel via UB](https://diposit.ub.edu/dspace/handle/2445/126027); [arXiv:2004.04413](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.04413)), with the robot single-file experiment exhibiting all three regimes explicitly ([arXiv:2403.08683](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.08683)).

The mapping onto a deadlock/bottleneck/anomaly taxonomy is therefore assembled, not quoted, and the thesis should say so. **Deadlock** ↔ circular wait / force-equilibrium / clogged-absorbing state: structural stoppage requiring external or rule-based intervention (Grover et al. + AGV liveness + Zuriguel's divergent-mean criterion). **Bottleneck** ↔ intermittent clogging at a constriction: finite but heavy-tailed resolution times (Zuriguel 2014; Helbing 2000 arching). **Anomaly** has no flow-physics counterpart — it is a fault/outlier category, and conceding that honestly is stronger than forcing a citation. A second citable correspondence falls out for free: the two deadlock formalisms map onto the system's own two layers — the resource/circular-wait view is M5's task-and-zone registry perspective, the dynamical-equilibrium view is M4's Nav2/velocity perspective. No published paper unifies the two definitions in one framework; the nearest is the AGV literature's informal "deadlock causes congestion," which leaves the unification as legitimate thesis contribution rather than borrowed structure.

## The scale-bridging chain defeats "your N is too small"

No single paper states the thesis's scale argument, but five verified links assemble into a chain that pre-empts the predictable reviewer objection. Link one: macro congestion transitions demonstrably exist in robot collectives — the measured robot fundamental diagram with diverging jam duration ([arXiv:2403.08683](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.08683)), Kuckling et al.'s ICRA 2024 finding that **performance separates into two phases, nearly-optimal and minimal, at critical system sizes** ([arXiv:2310.11843](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.11843)), and RMFS queueing models showing blocking effects growing strongly with fleet size ([TUM](https://mediatum.ub.tum.de/doc/1453020/document.pdf)). Link two: the generative events of those macro transitions are few-body structures — clogs form when **a few particles arrive simultaneously and form an arch** whose stability decides continuous flow versus arrest ([arXiv:2004.04413](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.04413); [Zuriguel 2014](https://diposit.ub.edu/dspace/handle/2445/126027)), and pedestrian egress time is controlled by the statistics of "small groups of pedestrians temporarily block the way out" ([Helbing retrospective](https://collective-dynamics.eu/index.php/cod/article/view/A168)). Link three: few-body conflicts are exactly where formal analysis is tractable — Grover et al.'s deadlock theory is exact at N = 2, 3 with hardware demonstrations at precisely that scale, and complexity grows roughly exponentially beyond it ([arXiv:1911.09146](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1911.09146)). Link four: micro-conflict policy provably determines macro flow — Aguilar et al. optimize system throughput by changing individual behavior *inside* the few-agent clog (idleness, selective retreat) ([Science 2018](https://crablab.gatech.edu/pages/publications/pdf/science-2018.pdf)). Link five, the converse: ant systems show the jammed branch of the fundamental diagram can be entirely *absent* when individuals adopt conflict-level conventions — no overtaking, priority rules ([arXiv:0903.2717](https://arxiv.org/abs/0903.2717); [Poissonnier et al. 2019, eLife](https://cdn.elifesciences.org/articles/48945/elife-48945-v1.pdf)).

The chain's conclusion: studying structured 2–5-robot conflict cells is studying congestion at its unit of formation. The physics literature's own causal unit is small-N; the macro quantities (throughput collapse, jam divergence) are statistics *over* these micro events. The thesis must present this as a synthesis with the citation chain attached — no paper quantitatively decomposes a macro fundamental diagram into a census of identified small-N conflict motifs, and that gap is itself defensible related-work content. Two housekeeping items before print: the exact robot count in Aguilar's robophysical trials (memory says ~4; check the Science PDF) and the journal of record for arXiv:2403.08683 remain unverified.

## No congestion work uses semantic reasoning — and the carve-out is navigation, not congestion

The absence claim holds across every branch of the congestion literature surveyed, and it holds by mechanism inspection, not just by failed keyword search. The instruments in every congestion paper found are geometric or statistical: SQF/TRVF potential and vector fields ([arXiv:2201.09337](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.09337)), idleness/retreat probabilities ([Science 2018](https://crablab.gatech.edu/pages/publications/pdf/science-2018.pdf)), GRNN regional traffic rules modulating velocities for up to 240 agents ([arXiv:2312.01498](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.01498)), advection–diffusion PDE density control ([Sinigaglia et al., Automatica 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.12592)), guidance-graph edge weights scaling to 3,000 agents ([arXiv:2402.01446](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.01446)), and GRAND's learned agent-distribution rebalancing at 500 agents ([arXiv:2512.03194 review](https://www.themoonlight.io/en/review/grand-guidance-rebalancing-and-assignment-for-networked-dispatch-in-multi-agent-path-finding)). None ingests images or language; none classifies conflicts; none selects a strategy per conflict. Even the field's most "centralized" mechanisms operate on aggregate flow representations — region velocities, edge weights, density fields — and even DeepFleet, Amazon's fleet foundation model trained on hundreds of thousands of warehouse robots with congestion forecasting as a headline application, forecasts the flow field rather than understanding any scene ([arXiv:2508.08574](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.08574v2); [Amazon Science](https://www.amazon.science/blog/amazon-builds-first-foundation-model-for-multirobot-coordination)).

The mandatory carve-out is the Skolkovo line: **SwarmVLM** ([arXiv:2508.07814](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.07814v1)) uses a VLM plus RAG to select impedance-control parameters for a UAV-leader/ground-follower pair in dynamic warehouse navigation (92% success over 12 real-world trials), and **ImpedanceGPT** ([arXiv:2503.02723](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.02723v1)) applies the same pattern to a mini-drone swarm. Both are VLM-for-navigation-parameters: no throughput, flow, waiting-time, or conflict-resolution objective, no engagement with any congestion metric, and no inter-robot conflict (a leader–follower pair is not a conflict dyad). The precise phrasing the notes recommend, consistent with the claim-1 carve-out already in the project's paper-folder review: "to our knowledge, no work in the swarm-robotics congestion tradition — common-target throughput, clogging, traffic-rule learning, mean-field density control, lifelong-MAPF guidance — employs vision-language or semantic scene reasoning to detect or resolve congestion." Three residual risks before asserting this in print: absence was established by targeted search, not an exhaustive ANTS 2024 / Swarm Intelligence journal sweep; the MLLM-UAV-swarm survey ([arXiv:2506.12710](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.12710)) may contain forward-looking sections *proposing* semantic congestion handling and should be skimmed in full; and a final pre-submission pass on "LLM swarm congestion" and "foundation model swarm traffic" is cheap insurance against a workshop paper.

## Conclusion

The two traditions turn out to be complementary in a way that strengthens the thesis beyond mere gap-claiming: the swarm/flow literature has rigorous measurement and no semantics, while the VLM-coordination literature has semantics and no measurement — so adopting dos Passos-style throughput, Aguilar's clog-frequency/dissolution-time pair, and Zuriguel's divergent-mean order parameter lets the thesis *bridge* the traditions rather than only contrast them, grounding VLM strategy selection in the field's own quantitative instruments. The positioning sentence that falls out of the whole corpus: swarm and MAPF work treats congestion as a flow to be shaped; this thesis treats it as an event to be understood. Even the strategy enum inherits pedigree — SQF (force one queue) is the swarm-scale ancestor of "convoy," TRVF (touch-and-run around the boundary) of "reroute" — so the VLM is selecting among strategies the identity-free literature already validated at scale, but doing so per named conflict, per robot, per cause.

Three verification debts gate camera-ready: the Marcolino 2017 venue (DBLP check), the canonical-cite page ranges (Liu–Nagel, O'Hern, Helbing–Molnár, NaSch, Wurman/Kiva, Li/RHCR), and the specific numbers inside the RMFS/OR PDFs (the ~85% throughput-collapse figure came from search synthesis and must be pulled from the source documents before quoting).
