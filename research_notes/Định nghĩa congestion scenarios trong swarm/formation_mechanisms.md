# Mechanisms of Congestion Formation in Multi-Robot Systems and Swarms

Scope: mechanism-level account of why/how congestion forms — (1) density-driven jamming and clogging, (2) deadlock structure in reactive navigation, (3) network/flow effects, (4) fault-seeded cascades, (5) scaling with N. Each mechanism is tied to defining papers and quantitative signatures, with notes on transfer to our setting (0.55 m aisle, 0.178 m-radius ≈ 0.356 m-diameter robots).

Research date: 2026-10-06. All quantitative claims carry inline sources; anything I could not source is in Gaps.

## Key Question 1 — What quantitative signatures distinguish a transient slowdown from a developing jam?

### Takeaway
The literature converges on two families of order parameters: (a) the shape of the fundamental diagram (flow vs. density) with a critical density ρc beyond which flow decreases, and (b) the tail of the distribution of time lapses between consecutive passages through a constriction — a transient slowdown has a bounded-mean lapse distribution, while a developing clog shows a power-law tail whose mean diverges (the clogging transition is *defined* by that divergence).

### Cited Findings
- **Clogging transition = divergence of the mean time lapse.** Zuriguel et al. (Sci. Rep. 4:7324, 2014, "Clogging transition of many particle systems flowing through bottlenecks") showed that across sheep herds, pedestrian crowds, granular assemblies, and colloids, the distribution of time lapses between passages of consecutive bodies through a bottleneck exhibits a **power-law tail with an exponent α that depends on system condition**, and "the transition to clogging is identified in terms of the divergence of the average time lapse"; the unified state diagram's key control feature is a **length scale set by the finite orifice size** — [UTN-RIA record of Zuriguel et al. 2014](https://ria.utn.edu.ar/items/aabd0661-46f3-43c6-810e-4608824409ff/full); [UB repository record](https://diposit.ub.edu/dspace/handle/2445/126027).
- **Faster-is-slower (FIS) verified experimentally in three systems.** Pastor, Garcimartín, Zuriguel et al. (PRE 92, 062817, 2015) experimentally demonstrated FIS — increased driving/eagerness reduces flow rate — in (a) humans evacuating a room, (b) sheep entering a barn, (c) grains in a 2D hopper on a vibrated incline; FIS is attributed to **transient arches that intermittently clog the outlet via frictional contacts**, and the time-lapse distributions again show power-law tails — [arXiv:1507.05110](https://arxiv.org/abs/1507.05110v1); [UNav PDF of PRE 92, 062817 (2015)](https://en.unav.edu/documents/14434042/14571109/2015_PRE92_062817.pdf/7b8bb198-ae9c-52c5-82c2-05a4af07d87a); FIS was first predicted in computer simulations of pedestrian egress through a narrow exit — [Experimental evidence of the faster-is-slower effect (UNav)](https://en.unav.edu/documents/15083165/15065268/experimental-evidence-of-the-faster-is-slower-effect.pdf).
- **Fundamental diagram critical density.** Across pedestrian studies and guidelines, the density at maximum flow ρc ranges from **1.75 m⁻² to 7 m⁻²**; the SFPE Handbook and Weidmann's guideline (25 collected data sets) put maximum flow at **ρ ≈ 1.8 persons/m²**, with flow decreasing beyond it — [arXiv:1908.07208](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1908.07208); the shape of the speed–density relation approaching jam density is still debated and lab data near jam density are scarce for safety reasons — [arXiv:1908.07208](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1908.07208); the classic revisit of the pedestrian fundamental diagram is Seyfried et al. 2005 — [arXiv:physics/0506170](https://arxiv.org/pdf/physics/0506170).
- **Jams can form below nominal capacity.** Seyfried et al. (2007), laboratory bottleneck experiments: "a jam occurs even if the incoming flow does not overstep the capacity defined by the maximum of the flow according to the fundamental diagram" — i.e., jam onset is stochastic, not a deterministic capacity threshold — [arXiv:physics/0702004](https://arxiv.org/pdf/physics/0702004).
- **Industrial operationalization: congestion = excess delay vs. free flow.** Amazon Robotics defines congestion as "the additional time a robot needs when traversing a planned path compared to the free-flow scenario" (single robot, no interference), predicts per-location delay over ~60 s horizons in six 10-second windows with a ConvLSTM, and reports a **4.4% throughput improvement** in sortation centers from congestion-aware dynamic path planning and **30–40% MAPE improvement** in travel-time estimation — [Amazon Science blog: Predicting congestion in fleets of robots](https://amazon.science/blog/predicting-congestion-in-fleets-of-robots).

### Inferences
- A power-law lapse-time tail t^(−α) has a divergent mean for α ≤ 2; hence a practical jam-onset detector for our system is: the log-survival plot of inter-passage (or inter-progress) intervals at a choke point bends from exponential to power-law and the fitted α drifts toward 2. (Mathematical property applied to the Zuriguel criterion; the records fetched report condition-dependent exponents but do not state the α = 2 threshold explicitly — see Gaps.)
- For M1/M5 telemetry: a transient slowdown preserves a stationary mean clearance time; a developing jam shows (i) clearance-time mean growing with observation window, (ii) a rising fraction of robots in STOPPED/SLOW states that does not relax, (iii) intermittent (bursty) flow through the aisle rather than merely slower flow. Burst–arrest intermittency is the signature of arch/clog formation rather than mere density increase.
- The FIS effect maps directly to robot policy: raising speed limits or aggressiveness of recovery behaviors in a contested aperture should *increase* clearance time once contact-like interactions (deadlock negotiations, safety stops) dominate — relevant to M4 safety-limit tuning.

### Gaps
- The exact numeric exponent values α and the formal phase-boundary condition (e.g., "clogged phase ⇔ α ≤ 2") from Zuriguel et al. 2014 could not be verified from the accessible records (the Nature page redirected to a login wall); only "divergence of the average time lapse" is directly sourced.
- No published fundamental diagram measured specifically for differential-drive warehouse robots (as opposed to pedestrians/grains/MAPF grid agents) was found in this search.

## Key Question 2 — Under what formal conditions do reactive avoidance algorithms provably deadlock?

### Takeaway
Deadlock in reactive navigation is now formally characterized: for optimization-based controllers (CBF/ORCA-family), deadlock is a **force/constraint equilibrium on the boundary of the safety set**, made stable by **geometric symmetry of initial positions and goals**; empirically ORCA deadlocks systematically in rotationally symmetric configurations for n = 2…30 and in tight passages, and the standard cure is injecting asymmetry (bias/noise) or escalating to a local MAPF solver.

### Cited Findings
- **Duality-theory characterization (Grover, Liu, Sycara).** "Why Does Symmetry Cause Deadlocks?" formally analyzes 2- and 3-robot CBF-controlled systems: using KKT/duality, system deadlock is characterized as a **force equilibrium on robots**; the deadlock set is **non-empty, bounded, and lies on the boundary of the safety set**; geometric symmetry of initial positions and goals constrains the controller to generate velocities that render deadlock stable, and "conferring skewness to the system" (breaking symmetry) evades deadlock; validated on Khepera-IV robots — [arXiv:1911.09146](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1911.09146.pdf); [CMU RI page](https://www.ri.cmu.edu/publications/why-does-symmetry-cause-deadlocks); companion work "Deadlock Analysis and Resolution for Multi-Robot Systems" — [CMU RI](https://www.ri.cmu.edu/publications/deadlock-analysis-and-resolution-for-multi-robot-systems); see also "The Before, During, and After of Multi-Robot Deadlock" — [DeepAI record](https://deepai.org/publication/the-before-during-and-after-of-multi-robot-deadlock).
- **ORCA systematic deadlock in symmetric scenes.** The Robotica paper "Addressing the deadlock problem of the optimal reciprocal collision avoidance algorithm in distributed multi-robot navigation" reports that in symmetric robot distributions ORCA "usually fails to plan reasonable collision avoidance velocities, resulting in the robot getting stuck," with **deadlocks occurring systematically across all symmetric scenarios for n ∈ [2, 30]** when exact rotational symmetry is preserved, especially with small integration time steps or slow agents; in highly deterministic settings (small time steps, no noise) the deadlocks are systemic, whereas real-world stochasticity often breaks them — [Cambridge Robotica article](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/robotica/article/addressing-the-deadlock-problem-of-the-optimal-reciprocal-collision-avoidance-algorithm-in-distributed-multirobot-navigation/70CEA27CFAE13D30DD3ABCD189F9019D).
- **ORCA in tight passages; local MAPF escalation (Dergachev & Yakovlev, CASE 2021).** In confined areas (narrow passages, doors) "deadlocks are likely to occur due to the egoistic behaviour of the agents"; coupling Theta* + ORCA\* with locally confined MAPF solvers (Push and Rotate, ECBS) for subgroups detected at deadlock risk raised **success rate from 15% to 99%** in certain cases — [arXiv:2107.00246](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2107.00246); the group's ORCA implementation documents the same deadlock property — [PathPlanning/ORCA-algorithm (GitHub)](https://github.com/PathPlanning/ORCA-algorithm).
- **Deadlock vs. livelock taxonomy; reciprocal dance.** The 2025 survey "Multi-Robot Navigation in Social Mini-Games: Definitions, Taxonomy, and Algorithms" formalizes doorway / corridor / intersection mini-games; defines deadlock (complete motion cessation: reactive methods in symmetric configurations where agents face each other with opposing velocities and environmental constraints — corridor width relative to robot diameter — prevent deviation, so no unique avoidance solution exists) vs. livelock (indefinite oscillation without collision or progress), and describes the **"reciprocal dance"** — robots alternately switching passing-side preferences, unable to settle a convention — [arXiv:2508.13459](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.13459).
- **Potential-field local minima as a swarm-navigation deadlock mechanism.** Marcolino & Chaimowicz, "No Robot Left Behind: Coordination to Overcome Local Minima in Swarm Navigation" (ICRA 2008): in potential-field-style swarm navigation, robots get trapped in local minima created by obstacles and by other robots, requiring explicit coordination to escape — [VerLab publication page](https://www.verlab.dcc.ufmg.br/old/_export/raw/publicacoes/publi/marcolino08robot).

### Inferences
- Coffman-condition instantiation in navigation: the "resource" is space (a cell/corridor segment); mutual exclusion = collision constraint; hold-and-wait = a robot occupies its footprint while waiting for the next segment; no preemption = safety layers forbid displacing another robot; circular wait = a cycle in the wait-for graph of blocked robots. Grover's force-equilibrium result is the continuous-space analog of circular wait: each robot's goal-attraction is exactly cancelled by active safety constraints from neighbors. (Framing inference; no retrieved paper explicitly instantiates all four Coffman conditions for navigation — see Gaps.)
- For our Isaac head-on scenarios: exact symmetry (identical robots, mirrored poses, deterministic control loops) is precisely the regime shown to produce systemic deadlock; injecting per-robot asymmetry (e.g., M3-grounded `swap`/priority/yield commands) is the literature-sanctioned resolution class — this is the formal justification for the M2→M3 pipeline's existence.
- Formal detection hooks computable from M1 `RobotState` histories: deadlock = all involved robots' speeds ≈ 0 while goal distance > ε and avoidance constraints active; livelock/reciprocal dance = bounded-position oscillation with nonzero velocity and no net goal progress.

### Gaps
- No paper found in this search proving a *closed-form corridor-width bound* (e.g., "ORCA deadlocks iff corridor width < k × robot diameter"); the width-relative-to-diameter condition appears qualitatively in the mini-games survey, and the fetched excerpt lacked numeric per-scenario deadlock rates.
- Koren & Borenstein's classic 1991 analysis of potential-field limitations (oscillation in corridors, local minima, goals non-reachable near obstacles) is the canonical potential-field-pathology citation but was not independently retrieved here; Marcolino 2008 is sourced instead.

## Key Question 3 — What is known about the critical density / capacity of narrow corridors and intersections relative to robot diameter?

### Takeaway
Bottleneck capacity grows **linearly with aperture width** (pedestrian lab data), jams at bottlenecks arise stochastically even below nominal capacity, and clogging physics is governed by the **ratio of aperture size to body diameter**; our 0.55 m aisle with 0.356 m-diameter robots has width/diameter ≈ 1.54 — strictly single-lane and deep in the intermittent/clogging-prone regime for any bidirectional or merging use.

### Cited Findings
- **Linear capacity–width law.** Seyfried et al. 2007 bottleneck lab experiments: "the data show a linear growth of the flow with the width," contradicting earlier stepwise (lane-based) capacity models; and jams occur below the fundamental-diagram capacity maximum — [arXiv:physics/0702004](https://arxiv.org/pdf/physics/0702004); enhanced empirical bottleneck data in Seyfried et al. 2009 — [arXiv:0810.1945](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0810.1945).
- **Bottleneck flows can exceed fundamental-diagram maxima.** Maximal flow values measured at bottlenecks can significantly exceed the maxima of empirical fundamental diagrams (short-lived, before jam formation) — [arXiv:1908.07208](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1908.07208).
- **Orifice-size length scale controls clogging.** In the unified Zuriguel et al. clogging state diagram, the "most conspicuous feature is the presence of a length scale qualitatively related to the presence of a finite size orifice" — aperture size in units of body size is the key control parameter separating free flow from intermittent/clogged regimes — [UTN-RIA record of Zuriguel et al. 2014](https://ria.utn.edu.ar/items/aabd0661-46f3-43c6-810e-4608824409ff/full).
- **Critical pedestrian density for flow maximum ≈ 1.8 m⁻²** (Weidmann/SFPE; ρc range 1.75–7 m⁻² across studies) — [arXiv:1908.07208](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1908.07208).
- **Confined-space failure rates for robot algorithms.** For ORCA-driven agents in environments with tight passages, uncoordinated success rate was as low as **15%**, versus **99%** with locally confined MAPF coordination in the same geometry — a direct measurement of how narrow passages collapse reactive-navigation reliability — [arXiv:2107.00246](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2107.00246).

### Inferences
- Our geometry: aisle width 0.55 m / robot diameter 0.356 m ≈ **1.54 diameters**. Consequences: (a) two robots cannot pass abreast (needs ≥ 2 diameters plus margins, i.e., ≳ 0.75–0.8 m for our robots; residual clearance beside a robot in the aisle is 0.55 − 0.356 = 0.194 m < 0.356 m); (b) any bidirectional assignment through the aisle guarantees a head-on conflict that reactive avoidance cannot resolve inside the aisle (no lateral escape dimension), matching the mini-games "corridor" deadlock condition; (c) in clogging-physics terms, an aperture of ~1.5 body diameters is deep in the intermittent/clogging regime — apertures need several body diameters before clogging becomes negligible.
- One-way capacity of such an aisle is set by (robot length + safety headway)/speed — a headway law, not a width law — so the binding capacity resource in our warehouse is *aisle direction scheduling*, not width; this supports guidance-graph/direction-assignment interventions (see Q5).

### Gaps
- The widely quoted granular rule that clogging becomes negligible only when outlet/particle diameter ratio exceeds ≈ 5 could not be verified from sources fetched here (the Zuriguel record only states the orifice length scale exists); treat "~5 diameters" as unconfirmed in these notes.
- The numeric coefficient of the pedestrian linear capacity law (≈ 1.9 persons·m⁻¹·s⁻¹ is commonly quoted) was not visible in the fetched abstract; only linearity is directly sourced.
- No study found giving a critical density specifically for circular robots in corridors as a function of robot diameter.

## Key Question 4 — How does one faulted/stopped robot propagate into system-level congestion?

### Takeaway
The propagation mechanism is well described in industrial sources — one robot's stop forces path adjustments that force further adjustments (cascading re-planning and queueing), and "disabled robots" are an explicit trigger condition in deployed congestion-management systems — but controlled academic cascade measurements from a *single* seeded fault are scarce; the best quantitative handles are delay-propagation prediction (Amazon) and swarm-scale congestion forensics (DARPA OFFSET).

### Cited Findings
- **Cascading-adjustment mechanism (deployed systems).** Amazon's "Robot congestion management" patent (US 12,083,681): adjustments made by one robot create a cascading effect in which conflicts created by one robot force adjustments from other robots, decreasing fleet efficiency and task completion; "a number of manually disabled robots" is listed among the conditions identifying congested states — [US12083681 (Google Patents)](https://patents.google.com/patent/US12083681); related congestion-avoidance/common-resource-access patent — [US11150647](https://patents.google.com/patent/US11150647).
- **Delay propagation is predictable from occupancy fields.** Amazon Science: congestion (excess delay vs. free flow) at each grid location is forecast from fleet occupancy (robots represented as Gaussian occupancy distributions) with a ConvLSTM over six 10-second windows; the floor is a grid with exclusive cell occupancy — the formal substrate in which one stopped robot blocks a cell and a queue forms behind it; congestion-aware planning yielded **+4.4% sortation throughput** — [Amazon Science blog](https://amazon.science/blog/predicting-congestion-in-fleets-of-robots).
- **Swarm-scale congestion forensics.** "Congestion Analysis for the DARPA OFFSET CCAST Swarm" analyzes congestion in a fielded 100+-vehicle heterogeneous swarm, premised on vehicles increasingly interfering with each other as swarm size grows — [arXiv:2307.16788](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2307.16788).
- **Stopped robots become obstacles for the swarm.** Marcolino & Chaimowicz's swarm navigation work treats robots trapped in local minima (stopped) as obstacles the rest of the swarm must coordinate around — the stopped robot converts from agent to obstacle — [No Robot Left Behind (VerLab)](https://www.verlab.dcc.ufmg.br/old/_export/raw/publicacoes/publi/marcolino08robot); their traffic-control work targets congestion when many robots converge on a common region — [Traffic Control for a Swarm of Robots (VerLab)](https://www.verlab.dcc.ufmg.br/old/publicacoes/publi/marcolino09traffic1).
- **Jams form below capacity → small perturbations suffice.** Seyfried et al.: bottleneck jams occur even when inflow is below nominal capacity — a stopped body at a constriction is exactly such a perturbation, nucleating a jam that need not otherwise have formed — [arXiv:physics/0702004](https://arxiv.org/pdf/physics/0702004).

### Inferences
- Mechanistic chain for our warehouse: faulted robot occupies one diameter of a 1.54-diameter aisle → aisle capacity drops to zero (passing geometrically impossible: 0.194 m residual < 0.356 m needed) → followers queue (spillback) → the queue tail reaches the upstream intersection → intersection capacity collapses for *crossing* flows too → network-level cascade. In our geometry the first cascade link is deterministic, not probabilistic.
- Cascade onset is measurable in M1 telemetry as the spatial growth rate of the contiguous STOPPED cluster upstream of the fault (queue-spillback front speed — the traffic-theory shockwave speed). The M1 STALE-vs-STOPPED distinction matters: the faulted robot reads STOPPED/STALE persistently while followers oscillate SLOW↔STOPPED.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed controlled experiment was found that seeds exactly one faulted robot in a multi-robot warehouse and measures the resulting cascade (cluster growth rate, time-to-system-degradation). Patents and the Amazon blog establish the mechanism and operational handles but publish no open cascade statistics; the OFFSET paper analyzes operational congestion, not a single seeded fault.
- The phantom-jam / stop-and-go wave literature (Nagel–Schreckenberg cellular automata; Sugiyama's circular-road experiment) is the classic road-traffic analog for perturbation-seeded jams but was not retrieved in this search, so it is uncited here.

## Key Question 5 — How does congestion scale with robot count N in confined environments?

### Takeaway
Group throughput grows **sublinearly** with N (interference), passes a **density optimum**, then *declines* — formalized by Lerman & Galstyan's interference model, Hamann's swarm-performance scaling and the Universal Scalability Law (contention + coherency terms), and reproduced at warehouse scale in lifelong-MAPF studies where *removing* 1,500 of 4,000 agents maximized throughput; guidance-graph (directed-flow) interventions recover ~5× throughput, a Braess-flavored result where constraining individual route choice improves system flow.

### Cited Findings
- **Sublinear scaling from interference (defining paper).** Lerman & Galstyan, "Mathematical model of foraging in a group of robots: effect of interference" (Autonomous Robots 13(2):127–141, 2002): interference arises from competition for space between spatially extended robots; group performance improves with system size but **sublinearly**, so **each robot's individual performance decreases as group size increases** — [ISI publication record](https://www.isi.edu/results/publications/13159/mathematical-model-of-foraging-in-a-group-of-robots-effect-of-interference/).
- **Swarm performance vs. density: universal shape.** Hamann, "Towards Swarm Calculus": swarm performance over swarm density is governed by the cooperation-vs-interference dichotomy; at a critical/optimal density performance levels off then **decreases** because added interference outweighs added cooperation; observed performance classes are linear increase, saturation, and increase-then-decrease (with superlinear intervals reported in some robot-swarm experiments); note that most experiments vary N at constant arena area, so density and N are interchangeable — [arXiv:1210.6539](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1210.6539.pdf).
- **Universal Scalability Law applied to robot swarms.** R(N) = cN / (1 + α(N−1) + βN(N−1)), with α = contention (interference for shared space/resources) and β = coherency cost; β > 0 produces a throughput *peak* followed by decline, not mere saturation — [Hamann et al., Guerrilla Performance Analysis for Robot Swarms (Univ. Lübeck record)](https://research.uni-luebeck.de/en/publications/guerrilla-performance-analysis-for-robot-swarms-degrees-of-collab/); [Konstanz open-access copy of Swarm Calculus](https://kops.uni-konstanz.de/server/api/core/bitstreams/0a507123-4cfa-4b39-815f-bb48f0236553/content).
- **Warehouse-scale lifelong MAPF: fewer agents can mean more throughput.** Jiang, Zhang, Veerapaneni & Li, "Scaling Lifelong Multi-Agent Path Finding to More Realistic Settings": on a 140×500 warehouse map (38,586 vertices) with 8,000 agents, WPPL throughput was **5.89 without guidance graphs vs. 31.34 with manual guidance graphs** (≈ 5.3×); on the Game map with 4,000 agents, **disabling 1,500 agents maximized throughput (peak at 2,500 agents)** — direct evidence of operation beyond the density optimum; rotation-constrained (kinematic) action models "more easily lead to congestion" than holonomic grid models — [arXiv:2404.16162](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.16162v1).
- **Guidance graphs / traffic-flow optimization as the congestion-shaping lever.** Guidance Graph Optimization (GGO; CMA-ES and Parameterized Iterative Update) modifies edge costs to induce implicit cooperation and reduce congestion, with large throughput gains in lifelong MAPF — [GGO literature review (themoonlight.io)](https://themoonlight.io/en/review/guidance-graph-optimization-for-lifelong-multi-agent-path-finding); congestion-avoiding path guidance ("Traffic flow optimisation for lifelong MAPF", Chen et al.) reports large overall throughput improvements — [review record](https://liner.com/review/traffic-flow-optimisation-for-lifelong-multiagent-path-finding); recent dense-setting guidance work (Lifelong LaCAM with Local Guidance) sustains high throughput in dense settings — [alphaXiv record](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2605.16855.md); related: [arXiv:2411.16506](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.16506v1).
- **Target congestion in swarms.** Marcolino & Chaimowicz, "Traffic Control for a Swarm of Robots: Avoiding Target Congestion" (IROS 2009): when many robots converge on a common target region, congestion forms; distributed traffic-control rules mitigate it — [VerLab record](https://www.verlab.dcc.ufmg.br/old/publicacoes/publi/marcolino09traffic1).
- **Braess paradox (network-level mechanism).** In user-optimized (selfish-routing) networks, adding a link can increase everyone's cost and removing one can reduce total travel time (Braess 1968); documented cases include the 1990 closure of 42nd Street in NYC reducing area congestion, and Stuttgart traffic improving only after a newly built road was torn up — [Nagurney, The Braess Paradox (UMass)](https://supernet.isenberg.umass.edu/facts/braess.html); generalized-network treatment — [arXiv:1207.3251](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1207.3251); practitioner method: remove links one at a time and compare system travel time — [SSTI, Efficient networks take work](https://ssti.us/2018/01/16/efficient-networks-take-work-traffic-management-and-braess-paradox/).
- **Swarm-size interference at field scale.** The DARPA OFFSET CCAST congestion analysis is motivated by interference growing with swarm size in a fielded 100+-vehicle swarm — [arXiv:2307.16788](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2307.16788).

### Inferences
- The three formalisms agree on shape: throughput T(N) rises ~linearly at low N, bends (α-contention), peaks at N*, and declines (β-coherency / over-density). The MAPF result (peak at 2,500 of 4,000 agents on one map) shows N* can sit at ~60% of a naively chosen fleet size — fleet sizing is itself a congestion-control variable.
- Guidance-graph gains are Braess-consistent: *restricting* individually optimal routes (directed aisles, edge-cost penalties) raised system throughput ~5× at 8,000 agents. For our layout, one-way aisle assignment is the single highest-leverage anti-congestion intervention in this literature, and it specifically eliminates the head-on deadlock class that 1.54-diameter aisles otherwise make inevitable.
- From the USL form, pairwise-interference load grows ~N² in a fixed area (β term), which is why congestion onset sharpens as fleets grow — consistent with Amazon treating congestion *forecasting* (not just avoidance) as necessary at scale.

### Gaps
- Marcolino & Chaimowicz's quantitative interference-vs-N curves (IROS 2009) were not directly retrievable (VerLab pages are index records); the qualitative claim is sourced, the exact curves are not.
- No closed-form "congestion probability vs. N" law for confined warehouses was found; the field expresses scale effects through throughput curves (MAPF) or USL fits (swarm robotics), not a canonical P(congestion | N).
- Hamann's superlinear-interval claim rests on his survey of reported experiments; the underlying primary experiment was not independently verified here.
