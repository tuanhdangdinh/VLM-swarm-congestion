# Decentralized & Reactive Multi-Robot Collision/Deadlock Methods at Intersections and Doorways

Scope: the decentralized line — velocity obstacles/ORCA, buffered Voronoi cells (BVC), control barrier functions (CBFs) — and the recent deadlock-specific literature (symmetric configurations, reciprocal dance/livelock, provable resolution at doorways/intersections, "social mini-games"). Emphasis on the 2–4 agent junction case. All web-verified 2026-09-29; items published after the assistant knowledge cutoff are marked **[post-cutoff-verified]** where relevant.

## Q1. Under what symmetric/dense configurations do ORCA/BVC/CBF methods provably deadlock, and what resolution mechanisms are proposed?

### Takeaway
All three families deadlock on (near-)symmetric configurations: the deadlock set is a force-equilibrium manifold that is measure-zero in theory but is an attractor in practice around bottlenecks; the proposed fixes escalate from random/right-hand perturbation (ORCA, BVC) through geometric characterization + provable perturbation (Grover et al. for CBF-QP) to deliberate exploitation of unstable equilibria (Jankovic) and explicit priority orderings (social mini-games line, Q2).

### Cited Findings
- **ORCA (the baseline being fixed).** van den Berg, Guy, Lin, Manocha, "Reciprocal n-Body Collision Avoidance" (ISRR 2009/2011, Springer STAR vol. 70) is the primary ORCA source; project page with paper, videos, and library links — [ORCA project page, UNC](https://gamma.cs.unc.edu/ORCA/).
- **Reciprocal dance / oscillation.** ORCA's collision-avoidance guarantee assumes perfect symmetry between robots; sensing error in relative velocity leads to a "reciprocal dance" (cyclic side-to-side livelock, same phenomenon as two pedestrians in a corridor). A practical fix in physical ORCA deployments: slightly enlarge one side of the velocity obstacle (asymmetric inflation on the order of 0.001 m/s) to break symmetry — [3-D Reciprocal Collision Avoidance on Physical Quadrotors (arXiv:1411.3794)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1411.3794). RVO itself was originally introduced to remove the velocity-oscillation problem of plain VO when two agents move head-on — [same source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1411.3794).
- **ORCA stuck in symmetric scenes.** In symmetric multi-robot distributions ORCA "usually fails to plan reasonable collision avoidance velocities, resulting in the robot getting stuck"; deadlock is a recognized common failure mode motivating ORCA variants — [Topology-Guided ORCA (arXiv:2407.16771)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.16771); [VR-ORCA (RA-L 2021)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/350339166_VR-ORCA_Variable_Responsibility_Optimal_Reciprocal_Collision_Avoidance).
- **BVC.** Zhou, Wang, Bandyopadhyay, Schwager, "Fast, On-line Collision Avoidance for Dynamic Vehicles Using Buffered Voronoi Cells," IEEE RA-L 2(2):1047–1054, 2017: each robot plans inside its Voronoi cell retracted by safety radius; same guarantee class as RVO without needing velocity sensing/communication. The paper explicitly acknowledges deadlock and proposes **two heuristic solutions** that "perform well in practice to alleviate deadlock" (goal-side heuristic / right-hand detour), i.e., no proof of liveness — [Zhou et al., RA-L 2017 (PDF, Stanford MSL)](https://msl.stanford.edu/papers/zhou_fast_2017.pdf); [Schwager copy](https://web.stanford.edu/~schwager/MyPapers/ZhouEtAlRAL17CollisionAvoidance.pdf).
- **Weighted BVC + right-hand rule.** Pierson et al., "Weighted Buffered Voronoi Cells for Distributed Semi-Cooperative Behavior," ICRA 2020: deadlock in position-swapping games (the canonical antipodal/symmetric case) is avoided "by employing a right-hand rule, consistent with other distributed multi-agent policies" — [Pierson ICRA 2020 (PDF)](https://sites.bu.edu/pierson/files/2021/05/pierson2020icra.pdf).
- **CBF-QP deadlock = force equilibrium.** Grover, Liu, Sycara: deadlock of CBF-QP controllers is "an equilibrium of the closed-loop dynamics" characterized by a **force-equilibrium** on robots when safety constraints are active/tight; analysis via KKT/duality theory and graph enumeration; complexity of deadlock analysis grows ~exponentially with robot count — ["Deadlock Analysis and Resolution in Multi-Robot Systems," arXiv:1911.09146](https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.09146), published at [WAFR 2020 (Springer chapter)](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-030-66723-8_18).
- **Geometry of the CBF deadlock set (2–3 robots).** Grover, Liu, Sycara, "The Before, During, and After of Multi-Robot Deadlock," **IJRR 2023 (WAFR 2020 special issue)**: in deadlock, 2 robots are **collinear** with their goal directions opposed; 3 robots form **symmetric polygons**; the deadlock set sits on the boundary of the safe set and is **non-empty but measure-zero** in state space; they give a provably-correct **decentralized perturbation-and-replan** algorithm that exits deadlock and reaches goals, validated on Khepera-IV robots — [IJRR version](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/02783649221074718); [arXiv:2206.01781](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.01781).
- **CBF liveness from instability.** Jankovic, Santillo, Wang, "Multiagent Systems with CBF-Based Controllers: Collision Avoidance and Liveness From Instability," IEEE TCST 2023 (arXiv 2022): host-only decentralized CBF policies "lack liveness" (gridlock) in Monte-Carlo intersection-like tests; their PCCA scheme (each agent solves the full multi-agent QP with its own information) matches centralized performance; crucially, **passing order is negotiated through exponentially unstable equilibria — no agent ever makes a discrete go/yield decision** — [arXiv:2207.04915](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.04915); [IEEE Xplore](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/10294301/); earlier CDC 2021 version [arXiv:2012.10261](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.10261). Companion review: Jankovic, "Instinctive Negotiation by Autonomous Agents in Dense, Unstructured Traffic," Annual Review of Control, Robotics, and Autonomous Systems 7 (2024): "CBF-based algorithms do not include a decision-making component — the action is continuous, and negotiation happens through instability" — [Annual Reviews](https://www.annualreviews.org/content/journals/10.1146/annurev-control-060923-025701?TRACK=RSS).
- **Safety barrier certificates + perturbation.** The safety-barrier-certificate line (Wang, Ames, Egerstedt, T-RO 2017) is the substrate that Strategic Pseudo-Goal Perturbation (2024) builds on: on detected deadlock, an agent selects a **pseudo-goal within a predefined radius** of its current position, drives there, then resumes its true goal; reduces deadlock instances and improves throughput (arXiv preprint, no formal guarantees stated in abstract) — [arXiv:2407.17766](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.17766). **[post-cutoff-verified]**
- **Priority/scheduling class of resolutions.** A recognized class of deadlock resolution methods uses priority protocols from autonomous-intersection-management: FCFS, auctions, reservations; FCFS is easy but yields long waits under simultaneous arrivals — discussed in the social mini-games literature ([arXiv:2308.10966](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2308.10966)) and in game-theoretic intersection reviews ([arXiv:2311.12341](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2311.12341)).
- **MPC-based deadlock resolution.** "Deadlock Resolution and Recursive Feasibility in MPC-based Multi-robot Trajectory Generation" (arXiv:2202.06071) treats deadlock + recursive feasibility jointly in the BVC/MPC line — [awesomepapers entry](https://awesomepapers.io/robotics/papers/2202.06071) (primary: arXiv:2202.06071). Adaptive CBF-inspired risk measurement for deadlock avoidance appears in [arXiv:2503.09621](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.09621) **[post-cutoff-verified, not read in depth]**.
- **LIVEPOINT** (2025): claims fully decentralized, safe **and** deadlock-free multi-robot control in cluttered environments via minimal velocity perturbations, reporting 100% success in settings (including doorway/intersection benchmarks) where MPC-CBF baselines fail — [arXiv:2503.13098](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.13098). **[post-cutoff-verified at abstract level]**

### Inferences
- The three families fail the same way for the same reason: a purely reactive, symmetric-by-design rule maps a symmetric input configuration to a symmetric response, and the bottleneck geometry (doorway/intersection) keeps re-creating the symmetric input. Every published fix is some flavor of symmetry breaking; they differ only in whether the break is random (perturbation), conventional (right-hand rule), emergent (instability), or explicit (priority/auction).
- The measure-zero result (Grover) is cold comfort in corridors: with matched speeds and mirrored approach angles — exactly our head-on and 4-way scenarios — trajectories are funneled onto the neighborhood of the deadlock manifold, where robots slow to a crawl even if exact equilibrium is never hit. Practical "deadlock" in the field is this neighborhood, not the zero-measure set.
- Right-hand-rule fixes assume lateral free space; they are the first thing to die in a 0.55 m aisle. This is why the aisle-constrained case pushes toward ordering/sequencing (Q2) rather than steering perturbation.

### Gaps
- I could not verify the exact statement of the two BVC deadlock heuristics from the RA-L PDF text itself (the search summary confirms their existence and character); the details (which robot detours, detour direction) should be read from Section V of Zhou et al. 2017 if needed.
- No source found giving a *provable* deadlock characterization for ORCA analogous to Grover's for CBF-QP (ORCA analyses in the literature are empirical).

## Q2. What does the social mini-game / doorway-and-intersection literature (Chandra et al.) establish about minimal deviation and ordering at 2–4 agent junctions?

### Takeaway
Chandra et al. formalize doorways/corridors/intersections as "social mini-games," show deadlock there is fundamentally a product of geometric symmetry, and resolve it by having robots **preemptively, minimally perturb their state onto "liveness sets"** — which amounts to selecting a total ordering (queue) through the bottleneck; game-theoretic variants (GamePlan) compute the ordering by auction and prove collision- and deadlock-freeness of the resulting turn-taking.

### Cited Findings
- **Definition.** Social mini-games = "a non-cooperative, non-communicative, partially observable multi-robot navigation problem in constrained spaces with multiple conflicting agents" — doorways, hallways, corridor intersections — Chandra et al., "Deadlock-free, Safe, and Decentralized Multi-Robot Navigation in Social Mini-Games via Discrete-Time Control Barrier Functions," [arXiv:2308.10966](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.10966); journal version in **Autonomous Robots (2025)** — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10514-025-10194-8). **[journal version post-cutoff-verified]**
- **Deadlock = geometric symmetry.** Deadlocks in social mini-games are "fundamentally a problem of geometric symmetry — when two robots approach a bottleneck at similar speeds and distances, their decentralized controllers often produce identical, conflicting responses" — [arXiv:2308.10966 search-result summary](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.10966).
- **Mechanism: liveness sets + minimally invasive perturbation.** "Barrier certificates allow each robot to preemptively perturb their state in a minimally-invasive fashion onto liveness sets, i.e., states where robots are deadlock-free," inside a discrete-time receding-horizon CBF controller — [arXiv:2308.10966](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.10966). Evaluated on physical F1/10 cars, a Clearpath Jackal, and a Boston Dynamics Spot in doorway, hallway, and corridor-intersection scenarios; metrics: success rate, collision rate, stop time, velocity change, path deviation, time-to-goal, flow rate, vs. decentralized and centralized baselines — [arXiv:2308.10966](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.10966).
- **Game-theoretic CBFs at doorways.** Chandra et al., "Decentralized Multi-Robot Social Navigation in Constrained Environments via Game-Theoretic Control Barrier Functions" (IROS 2023 MAD-Games workshop; also on the UT AMRL GT-MPC-CBF project page) — combines an auction/bid layer with MPC-CBF at doorway scenarios — [workshop PDF](https://iros2023-madgames.f1tenth.org/papers/chandra.pdf); [AMRL project page](https://amrl.cs.utexas.edu/snupi.html).
- **GamePlan (ordering via auction).** Chandra & Manocha, "GamePlan: Game-Theoretic Multi-Agent Planning with Human Drivers at Intersections, Roundabouts, and Merging," IEEE RA-L 2022: each agent "bids" via its observable driving style; **higher priority to more aggressive/impatient agents**; the resulting turn-based ordering is proven game-theoretically optimal (incentive-compatible auction) and **provably prevents collisions and deadlocks** under its assumptions; at 4-way unsignaled intersections it reduces collisions+deadlocks by up to 30% vs prior game-theoretic methods — [arXiv:2109.01896](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.01896); [UMD ISR story](https://isr.umd.edu/news/story/algorithm-helps-autonomous-vehicles-navigate-common-tricky-traffic-situations).
- **Taxonomy/survey.** "Multi-Robot Navigation in Social Mini-Games: Definitions, Taxonomy, and Algorithms" (arXiv 2025) — consolidates the doorway/corridor/intersection benchmark family and classifies deadlock-resolution approaches — [arXiv:2508.13459](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.13459). **[post-cutoff-verified at abstract level]**
- **LLM-mediated ordering.** GameChat (2025): multi-LLM dialogue between robots to negotiate who goes first in constrained environments, claiming safety (CBF) + game-theoretic social optimality (subgame-perfect equilibrium) — [arXiv:2503.12333](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.12333). **[post-cutoff-verified at abstract level]**
- **Deadlock definition used in this line:** "situations where two agents face each other and stop indefinitely because neither can find a path forward without violating safety constraints" — [arXiv:2308.10966](https://arxiv.org/html/2308.10966).

### Inferences
- The load-bearing conceptual move across this literature: at a bottleneck the *only* resolution structure is a **sequence** (a total order of passage). Continuous methods either stumble into an order (Jankovic's instability), guess one (right-hand rule), or compute one (auctions). The perturbation onto "liveness sets" is the continuous shadow of picking a queue position: slow down/offset so that arrival times at the bottleneck are strictly ordered.
- "Minimal deviation" in this line is operationalized as *velocity/time* perturbation more than *spatial* detour — which is exactly the degree of freedom that survives in an aisle where lateral sidestepping is impossible. Their reported metrics (path deviation, velocity change, stop time) treat deviation as a cost to minimize subject to ordering feasibility.
- GamePlan's "aggressive bids win" priority is a social-compatibility heuristic for humans; for a warehouse fleet the same machinery works with any tie-breaking key (battery, load, task priority, downstream congestion), since the proof needs only a strict order, not that particular key.

### Gaps
- The exact theorem statements in arXiv:2308.10966 (e.g., bounds on deviation from the preferred trajectory, or the claim that resolutions correspond to queue orderings) could not be extracted from the abstract-level fetch; the full PDF (Sections on liveness sets) should be consulted for the formal minimal-deviation bound.
- No source found that treats the **4-agent** social mini-game with formal guarantees beyond GamePlan's turn-based auction; most physical experiments in this line are 2–3 robots.

## Q3. Minimum clearance/space for k agents to resolve a mutual block by sequenced retreat (unlabeled motion planning separation bounds)?

### Takeaway
The computational-geometry line gives the cleanest available "how much room do you need" results: for unit-disc robots, pairwise separation **4× radius between like endpoints (start–start, goal–goal) and 3× radius start-to-goal guarantees a solution always exists** and is computable in polynomial time (and these constants are tight); below such separation, unlabeled multi-robot motion planning is PSPACE-hard. Nothing directly gives a "k-robot sequenced-retreat clearance" formula for a cross intersection — that must be derived for our geometry.

### Cited Findings
- **Existence at separation 4.** Adler, de Berg, Halperin, Solovey: in a simple polygon with m unit-disc robots, if all start and target positions are pairwise at least distance **4** apart (radius 1), an unlabeled solution **always exists** and is found in Õ(mn + m²) / O(n log n + mn + m²) time — [Efficient Multi-Robot Motion Planning for Unlabeled Discs in Simple Polygons, WAFR 2014/Springer](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-319-16595-0_1); [ResearchGate record](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/259106239_Efficient_Multi-Robot_Motion_Planning_for_Unlabeled_Discs_in_Simple_Polygons).
- **Optimality variant.** Solovey, Yu, Zamir, Halperin, "Motion Planning for Unlabeled Discs with Optimality Guarantees," RSS 2015 — same separation regime with path-length optimality guarantees — [RSS 2015 PDF](https://www.roboticsproceedings.org/rss11/p11.pdf).
- **Tighter, tight bounds.** SoCG 2022 (Banyassady, de Berg, Bringmann, Buchin, Fernau, Halperin, Kostitsyna, Okamoto, Slot): solution always exists if start–start ≥ 4, target–target ≥ 4, and start–target ≥ **3** (unit radius); the bounds are **proven tight**; if the free space is a single connected component, the bichromatic (start–target) separation requirement can be dropped — [arXiv:2205.07777](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.07777); [SoCG 2022 proceedings](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/LIPIcs.SoCG.2022.12).
- **Hardness without separation.** Unlabeled multi-robot motion planning with arbitrary (dense) placements is PSPACE-hard (Solovey & Halperin) — [arXiv:1408.2260](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1408.2260).
- **Revolving areas.** "Multi-Robot Motion Planning for Unit Discs with Revolving Areas" (2022): existence guarantees when each robot has an empty "revolving area" around its start/goal (a weaker, more local space condition than global separation) — [arXiv:2210.00123](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2210.00123).
- **Follow-up trade-offs.** "Unlabeled Multi-Robot Motion Planning with Improved Separation Trade-offs" continues this line — [arXiv:2603.19502 listing](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.19502). **[post-cutoff item; only the listing verified, treat with care]**
- **Passage direction reservation.** In dense-navigation practice, narrow passages are restricted to single-direction traffic at a time to avoid deadlock and detours — [Dense Multi-Agent Navigation Using Voronoi Cells and Congestion Metric-based Replanning, arXiv:2202.11334](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2202.11334).

### Cited Findings — sequenced retreat / plan-execution guarantees (adjacent)
- RMTRACK (Čap, Gregoire, Frazzoli, IROS 2016): given precomputed collision-free trajectories, robots that are arbitrarily **delayed** (e.g., yielding to humans) can execute them with a control policy that is provably **safe and deadlock-free**, by only re-timing along fixed paths — [arXiv:1603.08582](https://arxiv.org/abs/1603.08582); [IEEE Xplore](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/7759750/); [code](https://github.com/mcapino/rmtrack).
- Deadlock-free **online plan repair** under disturbances: Coskun & O'Kane — [CosOKaVal21 PDF](https://jokane.net/pubs/CosOKaVal21.pdf).

### Inferences
- Translated to our fleet: with robot radius r, the theory's "always solvable" regime needs staging/retreat endpoints spaced ≥ 4r apart (like endpoints) and ≥ 3r from other robots' goals. With our 0.45 m pairwise floor read as an effective 2r ≈ 0.45 m (r ≈ 0.225 m), 4r ≈ 0.90 m and 3r ≈ 0.675 m: retreat waypoints for a 3–4 robot junction unblock should be picked ≥ ~0.9 m apart, roughly one robot-diameter of slack beyond the hard floor. This is a heuristic transfer (the theorems are for unlabeled discs in polygons, not labeled aisle graphs), but it is the only principled clearance number the literature offers.
- The single-connected-component relaxation (SoCG 2022) is good news for a warehouse: aisles form one connected free space, so start–target interference matters less than keeping *retreat* endpoints mutually separated.
- The PSPACE-hardness at low separation is the formal reason a slow deliberative layer is justified: below the separation threshold there is no cheap local rule that always works, so someone must sequence.

### Gaps
- No paper found that states a minimum corridor width or junction pocket size for **k robots to resolve a mutual block by sequenced retreat** specifically (e.g., "a 4-way deadlock needs one free pocket of depth d"). The closest results are the separation bounds above and the revolving-areas condition; a bespoke derivation for our 0.55 m aisle / 0.45 m floor geometry is needed.
- Labeled (as opposed to unlabeled) separation bounds are weaker/less clean; I did not find a labeled analogue of the 4r result with matching tightness.

## Q4. What hybrid schemes combine a discrete sequencer with continuous local avoidance, and how are the layers kept consistent?

### Takeaway
Three consistency recipes recur: (i) fix the geometry, re-time the motion (RMTRACK: robots keep planned paths, the executive only schedules progress — safety and deadlock-freeness proven under delays); (ii) fix the order, let control realize it (auctions/games pick who goes first; MPC-CBF executes subject to that order — GT-CBF, GamePlan, GameChat); (iii) fix the safe set, let the sequencer steer within it (CBF filter under any high-level command). The common invariant: the discrete layer must only emit decisions the continuous safety layer cannot veto into a stall.

### Cited Findings
- **Re-timing along fixed paths.** RMTRACK proves safety + deadlock-freeness when robots track precomputed multi-robot trajectories under arbitrary delaying disturbances, and "scales significantly better with the intensity of disturbances than the naive liveness-preserving approach" — [arXiv:1603.08582](https://arxiv.org/abs/1603.08582).
- **Auction over MPC-CBF.** The game-theoretic CBF line explicitly stacks a bid/priority layer (discrete: who crosses first) on an MPC-CBF tracking layer (continuous: how) at doorways/intersections — [IROS 2023 MAD-Games paper](https://iros2023-madgames.f1tenth.org/papers/chandra.pdf); [GT-MPC-CBF project](https://amrl.cs.utexas.edu/snupi.html); GamePlan supplies the discrete layer's optimality/deadlock-freeness proof — [arXiv:2109.01896](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.01896).
- **Reactive synthesis with deadlock resolution.** "Reactive mission and motion planning with deadlock resolution avoiding dynamic obstacles" (Autonomous Robots 2018, DeCastro et al.): LTL-level mission layer synthesizes discrete responses to local-avoidance deadlocks so the global mission never livelocks — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10514-017-9665-6); [MIT DSpace PDF](https://dspace.mit.edu/bitstream/handle/1721.1/114905/10514_2017_Article_9665.pdf?sequence=1&isAllowed=y).
- **Holistic MAPF execution monitoring.** A 2025 architecture couples MAPF plans with execution monitoring/optimization, in the same layered spirit — [arXiv:2509.10284](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.10284v1). **[post-cutoff-verified at abstract level]**
- **Priority protocols as the discrete layer.** FCFS/auction/reservation protocols from autonomous intersection management are the standard discrete sequencers referenced by the CBF deadlock literature — [arXiv:2308.10966](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2308.10966); [game-theoretic intersection review, arXiv:2311.12341](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2311.12341).
- **Layer-consistency failure mode (negative result).** Decentralized host-only CBF layers under a naive upper layer produce gridlock (lack of liveness) even when each layer is individually correct — the Jankovic Monte-Carlo comparisons — [arXiv:2207.04915](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.04915).

### Inferences
- The RMTRACK principle ("same paths, different schedule") is the strongest consistency guarantee available and maps directly onto an aisle-constrained fleet: if the resolution layer only assigns *order and timing* on a shared path graph — never freehand geometry — the safety layer can always realize it, and deadlock-freeness survives arbitrary stalls (a human steps in, a robot pauses).
- The recurring bug class in hybrid stacks is the continuous safety layer silently vetoing the discrete command (safety filter clamps velocity to zero, so the commanded "GO" never happens and the sequencer's world model diverges). The literature's answer is either proof-level (RMTRACK invariants) or feedback-level (execution monitoring, DeCastro's synthesis reacting to detected deadlock). Our own Phase-3 incident (collision_monitor stop_pub_timeout zeroing cmd_vel after a backup command) is exactly this class.
- For our stack: M3/M5's discrete resolution should be expressed as constraints the local layer already respects (goal poses on the roadmap + ordering/holds), and M5 must watch for "commanded but not moving" as a first-class layer-inconsistency signal, not as robot failure.

### Gaps
- Little published work on keeping a *slow* (sub-Hz) discrete layer consistent with 20–50 Hz local layers specifically; the hybrid papers assume the discrete layer reacts at conflict timescale. The staleness problem (resolution computed for a configuration that has since drifted) is essentially unaddressed — worth stating as a design requirement rather than citing.

## Q5. Is backing out / reversing a first-class maneuver in these frameworks?

### Takeaway
Almost never in the VO/BVC/CBF line — those methods re-steer or stop but rarely command retreat; reversing appears as a first-class primitive only in deadlock-*recovery* work (counter-intuitive moves away from goal), sampling-based recovery, and coordination-graph/plan-repair approaches for corridors. This is a genuine gap our command layer fills deliberately.

### Cited Findings
- **The stalemate and the counter-intuitive move.** When two agents meet in a narrow corridor, reactive controllers on both sides brake to zero — a stalemate; resolution requires one agent to do something "counter-intuitive" relative to its local goal, such as **backing up or moving away from its target** to let the other pass — [LivePoint discussion of corridor deadlock, arXiv:2503.13098](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.13098v1); STL-MPPI-style planners generate **back-and-forward maneuvers** as recovery — [Decentralized Uncertainty-Aware Multi-Agent Collision Avoidance with MPPI, arXiv:2507.20293](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.20293). **[both post-cutoff-verified at abstract level]**
- **Dedicated recovery work.** "Don't Get Stuck: A Deadlock Recovery Approach" (2024) treats recovery from stuck states as its own problem — [ResearchGate record](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/383237868_Don't_Get_Stuck_A_Deadlock_Recovery_Approach).
- **Corridor coordination classics.** Knepper & Rus (2012) applied pedestrian-avoidance principles to deadlock resolution in narrow passageways; Li et al. (2005) used coordination graphs to resolve corridor deadlocks (which involve one robot withdrawing to a bay/junction) — as surveyed in [arXiv:2503.13098](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.13098v1).
- **What VO/CBF do instead.** ORCA restricts to forward half-plane velocity adjustments in practice; CBF-QP methods perturb or exploit instability but the resolution motion is a swerve/slow-down, not a commanded retreat (see Grover's perturbation resolution [arXiv:2206.01781](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.01781) and pseudo-goal perturbation within a radius [arXiv:2407.17766](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.17766) — the pseudo-goal *can* lie behind the robot, but retreat is incidental, not structured).
- **Single-direction passage reservation** is the planning-layer substitute for reversing: prevent opposed entry so no one ever has to back out — [arXiv:2202.11334](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2202.11334).

### Inferences
- No decentralized framework we found issues "robot B: reverse 0.8 m to pocket P, hold, then proceed" as a typed action with guarantees. The closest formal object is RMTRACK-style re-timing (which never needs reversal because paths were jointly planned) and pseudo-goal perturbation (which permits but does not reason about retreat). A centralized sequencer that owns retreat as a first-class primitive — with a designated retreat target and a hold-until-clear condition — is beyond, not behind, the decentralized state of the art.
- Reversal has costs the decentralized literature ignores because it never commands it: rear-facing perception is usually absent (our robots reverse open-loop on odometry), and a reversing robot temporarily *increases* occupancy of the corridor behind it — so the sequencer must clear the retreat lane as part of the same discrete decision, not leave it to local avoidance.

### Gaps
- Found no paper with formal guarantees for *reverse-then-yield* maneuvers under safety constraints (e.g., CBF-safe backup with a rear clearance floor). Our odometry-based backup + hold + yield pipeline appears to have no direct academic counterpart to benchmark against; the nearest anchors are the corridor coordination-graph work (Li et al. 2005) and recovery-behavior papers above.

## Design implications for a centralized slow-cycle reasoner (discrete resolution: who retreats, who crosses, in what order; 0.45 m pairwise floor; aisle-constrained robots)

### Takeaway
The literature converges on: at a bottleneck the resolution *is* an ordering; symmetry must be broken explicitly because our robots cannot sidestep; retreat targets need ~one robot-diameter of slack beyond the hard floor; and the discrete decision must be expressed in terms the local layer cannot veto.

### Inferences (grounded in Q1–Q5 citations)
1. **Emit a total order, not paired advice.** Every successful mechanism (auction, FCFS, liveness-set perturbation, instability) reduces to a strict passage order through the bottleneck (Q1, Q2). For a 3–4 robot junction, M2/M3's output should be a full sequence over the involved robots (cross order + who retreats where), not independent per-pair verdicts — per-pair verdicts can recreate the cyclic symmetry (A yields to B, B to C, C to A) that is exactly the 3-robot symmetric-polygon deadlock set of Grover et al. ([IJRR 2023](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/02783649221074718)).
2. **Symmetry breaking is the reasoner's job, and any strict key suffices.** GamePlan's proof needs only a strict priority order, not its aggressiveness key ([arXiv:2109.01896](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.01896)). Given our known first-slot/robot_1 presentation-order priors in the VLM, the tie-break key should be deterministic and configuration-derived (e.g., which robot has the nearest retreat pocket), with the M3 `swap`/ordering gate as the enforcement point — never left to the model's implicit prior, which is exactly the "identical conflicting responses under symmetry" failure ([arXiv:2308.10966](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.10966)).
3. **Sequenced retreat needs clearance beyond the floor.** Transferring the tight separation bounds (4r like-endpoints, 3r mixed; [SoCG 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.07777)) with 2r ≈ 0.45 m: retreat/staging endpoints should be pairwise ≥ ~0.9 m and ≥ ~0.68 m from another robot's crossing path. Operationally: one retreat pocket per *simultaneously retreating* robot; if only one pocket exists near the junction, the resolution must be strictly sequential (retreat-1 → cross → retreat-2 → …), which the unlabeled-planning existence proofs implicitly perform via free "pivot" space.
4. **Prefer re-timing over re-routing (RMTRACK principle).** Keep resolutions on the existing roadmap: holds, ordered entries, and retreats to named pockets. Deadlock-freeness under stalls then follows RMTRACK-style even when robots are delayed mid-resolution ([arXiv:1603.08582](https://arxiv.org/abs/1603.08582)) — important for a 0.5–2 Hz reasoner that cannot re-decide quickly.
5. **Guard against safety-layer veto.** The documented hybrid failure is the continuous layer nullifying the discrete command (Jankovic's gridlocked host-only policies, [arXiv:2207.04915](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.04915); our own collision_monitor cmd_vel-zeroing incident). Resolution commands must respect the 0.45 m floor *throughout the commanded motion*, including the transient where a retreating robot approaches a third robot behind it; M5 should treat "commanded but stationary > N cycles" as layer inconsistency and re-plan, mirroring DeCastro et al.'s synthesized deadlock responses ([Auton. Robots 2018](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10514-017-9665-6)).
6. **Backing out is our differentiator — structure it.** Since no decentralized framework owns reversal (Q5), the reasoner should own the full retreat contract: retreat target, rear-clearance check against the floor, hold condition, release condition. Passage-direction reservation ([arXiv:2202.11334](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2202.11334)) is the cheap standing rule that prevents most re-occurrence: once a resolution is issued, mark the contested aisle single-direction until the sequence completes.
7. **Expect the near-deadlock funnel, not exact deadlock.** Because the deadlock set is measure-zero but attracting near bottlenecks (Q1), the congestion detector should trigger on the *neighborhood* (sustained low progress + tight pairwise gaps), not on full stops — by the time velocities are exactly zero, robots are typically already inside the mutual-veto region where only sequenced retreat works.

### Gaps
- The k-robot junction clearance rule (implication 3) is our own transfer of unlabeled-disc bounds, not a published result; if it becomes load-bearing (e.g., in scenario design or a paper claim), it should be derived for the labeled, aisle-graph case or validated empirically.
