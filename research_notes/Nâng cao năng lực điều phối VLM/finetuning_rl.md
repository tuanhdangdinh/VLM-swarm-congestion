# Fine-tuning, Distillation, and RL Approaches for VLM Spatial-Planning and Multi-Agent Coordination (2024–2026)

## Key Question 1: What does it take (data, compute) to fine-tune a small open VLM (7–13B) into a competent multi-robot congestion planner — any demonstrated cases?

### Takeaway
No published work demonstrates a 7–13B VLM fine-tuned specifically into a multi-robot *congestion/deadlock* planner end-to-end; the closest demonstrated cases are (a) LoRA/QLoRA fine-tunes of 7–8B models for multi-robot *task decomposition* (DART-LLM on Llama-3.1-8B), (b) VLM-coordinated multi-robot exploration without fine-tuning (COMRES-VLM, Co-NavGPT), and (c) non-LLM imitation-learning MAPF transformers (MAPF-GPT family) showing that as little as a 2M-parameter model trained on expert traces can beat an 85M one. The congestion-planner fine-tune this project would need is a gap in the literature, not a solved problem.

### Cited Findings
- DART-LLM is a QLoRA fine-tuned Llama-3.1-8B specialized for construction robotics supporting multi-robot coordination, task dependencies, spatial reasoning, and action planning — a demonstrated case of an 8B model LoRA-tuned for multi-robot task structure (language-side, not vision-side congestion reasoning) — [HuggingFace: YongdongWang/llama-3.1-8b-lora-qlora-dart-llm](https://huggingface.co/YongdongWang/llama-3.1-8b-lora-qlora-dart-llm/commit/79b4a6a2184a6ddfb5e784f250b0fbb5caa38e1b)
- Vision-Language-Policy (VLP) work builds on Qwen2.5-VL fine-tuned with LoRA so robots interpret language commands and adapt task strategies in real time — evidence that LoRA on Qwen2.5-VL-class models is the standard parameter-efficient path — [arXiv 2509.26324](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.26324v3)
- COMRES-VLM (2025) coordinates multi-robot exploration/search by having a VLM reason over shared occupancy maps, frontier clusters, topological skeletons, and robot states to produce globally consistent waypoint assignments — multi-robot VLM coordination demonstrated via prompting over structured map inputs, not via fine-tuning — [alphaXiv overview 2512.19178](https://alphaxiv.org/overview/2512.19178v1)
- Co-NavGPT: multi-robot cooperative visual semantic navigation using an LLM as global planner assigning frontier goals to multiple robots — prompting-based, no fine-tuning — [arXiv 2310.07937](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.07937v3)
- MAPF-GPT (AAAI 2025): a pure imitation-learning (decoder-only transformer, non-LLM) MAPF solver trained on expert demonstrations, scalable at inference — [arXiv 2409.00134](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2409.00134); [AAAI version](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/34477)
- MAPF-GPT-DDG (ICML 2025): fine-tunes the pre-trained MAPF-GPT with centralized expert data via a "delta-data generation" mechanism (active fine-tuning on states where the learner diverges from the expert); a 2M-parameter fine-tuned model matches the earlier 85M-parameter model at far lower training cost, surpasses all learning-based MAPF solvers on solution quality, and runs instances up to 1M agents — [arXiv 2506.23793](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.23793v1); [ICML 2025 page](https://icml.cc/virtual/2025/49317)

### Inferences
- Data requirements for the demonstrated neighbors are modest at the task-structure level (QLoRA instruction datasets for DART-LLM-style tuning), but the heavy lifting in MAPF-GPT-class results comes from large volumes of solver-generated state-action pairs — cheap to generate with LaCAM/CBS on CPU. Good news for an academic lab: the expensive part is GPU-hours, not data acquisition.
- Compute feasibility on a single L4 (24 GB): QLoRA on a 7–8B VLM (Qwen2.5-VL-7B, Cosmos-Reason2-8B) fits in 24 GB with 4-bit base weights and is the only realistic local training option; full fine-tuning and GRPO-style RL with multiple rollouts generally need A100/H100-class memory (Cosmos-Reason2's card lists H100/A100 as tested hardware). A 2B model (Cosmos-Reason2-2B) would allow LoRA and even modest RLVR locally.
- For the warehouse congestion use case specifically, nothing published does "image of congested scene → strategy choice" fine-tuning; the nearest template is SFT on structured scene text (robot states, as COMRES-VLM uses) + solver-labeled outcomes — a novel but low-risk contribution.

### Gaps
- No paper found that fine-tunes any VLM (any size) on multi-robot congestion/deadlock *resolution strategy selection* from overhead imagery — the exact task of this project appears unpublished.
- No published GPU-hour or dataset-size figures for the DART-LLM/VLP LoRA fine-tunes were surfaced in search results; exact budgets would require the full papers.

## Key Question 2: RLVR for planning — which 2025–2026 works show spatial/planning gains, and do they transfer to images (not just text mazes)?

### Takeaway
A post-R1 wave of 2025–2026 RLVR work shows genuine spatial/planning gains on *image* inputs, not just text mazes: VPRL (GRPO over visual-state rollouts) beats SFT text reasoning by ~27% on visual navigation tasks, Ariadne shows RLVR expands the capability boundary beyond the base policy and transfers zero-shot from synthetic mazes to real map/navigation benchmarks (+16% MapBench, +24% ReasonMap), and Spatial-SSRL derives verifiable rewards directly from RGB/RGB-D images. The open issue is reward design for continuous spatial quantities (addressed by "Smooth Operator"-style dense rewards). All of it is single-agent.

### Cited Findings
- AlphaMaze (2025): two-stage SFT (tokenized maze representations) + GRPO with crafted rewards teaches standard LLMs step-by-step maze navigation and elicits chain-of-thought — the canonical text-maze RLVR baseline — [arXiv 2502.14669](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.14669)
- Visual Planning / VPRL (ICLR 2026 oral): GRPO post-training of large *vision* models to plan purely in the visual modality (sequences of images, no text mediation); substantially improves FrozenLake, Maze, and MiniBehavior navigation and surpasses SFT textual reasoning by 27% average exact-match — [arXiv 2505.11409](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.11409v3); [ICLR 2026 oral](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2026/oral/10006696)
- Ariadne ("Does RLVR Extend Reasoning Boundaries?", 2025): RLVR on VLMs extends the spatial-reasoning boundary beyond the effective support of the base policy — succeeds on instances where the base model fails even with large pass@k budgets — and transfers zero-shot from synthetic maze training to out-of-domain benchmarks: average +16% on MapBench and +24% on ReasonMap (subway transfer), plus validation on real-world navigation — [arXiv 2511.00710](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.00710)
- Spatial-SSRL (CVPR 2026): self-supervised RLVR deriving verifiable signals directly from ordinary RGB/RGB-D images via five pretext tasks (shuffled patch reordering, flip recognition, crop inpainting, regional depth ordering, relative 3D position prediction) — verifiable spatial rewards without human labels — [CVPR 2026 poster](https://cvpr.thecvf.com/virtual/2026/poster/36488)
- "Smooth Operator" (2026): identifies that standard relative-ranking RLVR (GRPO) treats near-miss continuous spatial predictions the same as total failures (reward sparsity, advantage collapse) and proposes Smooth Numerical Reward Activation + Absolute-Preserving GRPO to create a dense reward continuum for 3D spatial tasks — [arXiv 2601.07695](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.07695)
- Cautionary result — "Beyond Semantics" (2025): intermediate reasoning tokens produced by trace-trained models can improve performance even when the traces are semantically meaningless, warning against reading "reasoning" into trace-trained planners — [arXiv 2505.13775](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.13775)

### Inferences
- Transfer to images is now demonstrated (VPRL, Ariadne, Spatial-SSRL), but all of these are *single-agent* navigation/spatial tasks; no RLVR work surfaced that rewards multi-agent conflict resolution from images. Combining an RLVR recipe with a MAPF simulator as the verifier (conflict-free plan = reward) is an obvious and apparently unclaimed niche.
- Compute feasibility: GRPO samples groups of rollouts per prompt (typically 8–16), so RLVR on a 7–8B VLM is marginal-to-infeasible on one L4; realistic options are RLVR on a 2–3B VLM locally or renting A100 hours for short runs. SFT-first (AlphaMaze-style stage 1) on L4, then a short cloud RLVR stage, matches the published two-stage recipes.

### Gaps
- No 2025–2026 RLVR paper found that targets *multi-agent* spatial coordination (all are single-agent spatial QA or navigation); this appears to be a genuine literature gap, not a search failure.
- Exact model sizes/GPU budgets for VPRL and Ariadne training were not in the retrieved summaries.

## Key Question 3: Solver-trace distillation (teach the VLM/LLM from MAPF/priority-planning oracles) — demonstrated results?

### Takeaway
Solver-trace distillation is well demonstrated for *non-VLM transformers* (Searchformer on A* traces, Stream of Search, MAPF-GPT/DDG on expert MAPF traces) with strong results — 93.7% optimal Sokoban for Searchformer, state-of-the-art learnable MAPF for MAPF-GPT-DDG — and for *language-only* small models (SmallPlan distills GPT-4o-level path planning into SLMs via SFT+RL). Distilling MAPF oracle traces into a *vision*-language model remains undemonstrated.

### Cited Findings
- Searchformer (2024): encoder-decoder transformer trained to predict A* *search dynamics* (tokenized add/remove events of the search tree), then fine-tuned via expert iteration; optimally solves unseen Sokoban 93.7% of the time using up to 26.8% fewer search steps than A* — [arXiv 2402.14083](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.14083)
- Stream of Search (2024): trains an LM on linearized search trajectories from symbolic planners *including mistakes and backtracking*, then lets it self-improve; training on the search process (not just optimal solutions) is the key ingredient — [arXiv 2404.03683](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.03683v1)
- MAPF-GPT (AAAI 2025): imitation learning at scale from centralized expert MAPF data; MAPF-GPT-DDG (ICML 2025) adds delta-data generation — collecting new expert labels specifically on states where the student's policy diverges — yielding a 2M-param model matching an 85M-param one and surpassing all learning-based MAPF solvers; scales to 1M agents — [arXiv 2409.00134](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2409.00134); [arXiv 2506.23793](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.23793v1)
- SmallPlan (2025, MBZUAI/VinUni): LLM-guided distillation into Small Language Models for sequential path planning over scene graphs, trained with interleaved simulation-powered SFT and RL; fine-tuned SLMs "perform competitively with GPT-4o on sequential path planning" while being edge-deployable, and the RL phase makes them cost-aware (travel distance, number of trials) — [arXiv 2505.00831](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.00831)
- Distilling on-device language models for robot planning with minimal human intervention (CoRL 2025 proceedings): teacher-generated data replaces human annotation for planner distillation — [PMLR v305, Ravichandran et al.](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v305/ravichandran25a.html)
- LLM-NAR (2025): couples LLMs with pre-trained graph neural algorithmic reasoners to guide MAPF, rather than distilling the solver into the LLM weights — [arXiv 2508.17971](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.17971v1)

### Inferences
- The consistent lesson across Searchformer / SoS / MAPF-GPT-DDG: distilling the *search process or on-policy corrections* beats distilling only clean optimal solutions, and model-size requirements collapse when data is targeted (2M params sufficing for MAPF). For this project, a CBS/LaCAM oracle labeling congestion scenes with the strategy a solver-grade plan implies (yield/reroute/swap) is cheap to produce and is exactly the kind of verifiable supervision these recipes use.
- No work distills solver traces into a VLM that consumes *camera images*; every demonstrated pipeline uses tokenized grids, scene graphs, or state text. Practical implication: distill into the text/state channel (the M1 SceneState JSON) rather than the image channel — that is where the evidence says the win is.

### Gaps
- No demonstrated MAPF-oracle-to-VLM (image-input) distillation found anywhere in 2024–2026 search results.
- Beyond-Semantics-style analyses have not been run on MAPF-trace-trained models specifically, so whether such models "understand" conflicts or pattern-match is open.

## Key Question 4: Cosmos Reason 2 and Gemini Robotics-ER 1.5/2.0 — documented strengths/limits for multi-agent spatial ordering and temporal prediction

### Takeaway
Both model lines document strong *spatio-temporal* and *embodied* reasoning, but neither documents multi-agent/multi-robot coordination as an evaluated capability. Cosmos-Reason2 (2B/8B/32B, post-trained from Qwen3-VL with SFT+RL on physical-AI data) reports large deltas over its base on robotics/AV benchmarks (e.g., AV collision detection 72.06% vs 37.67%) — consistent with the project's measured ER-2 conflict-prediction advantage being a post-training effect; Gemini Robotics-ER 1.5's public claims center on pointing, timestamp-accurate video causality, and a tunable thinking budget, with SOTA asserted across ~15 academic benchmarks (ERQA, Point-Bench) but few public numbers.

### Cited Findings
- Cosmos-Reason1 (2025) training recipe: four stages — vision pre-training, general SFT, Physical AI SFT (1.82M understanding QA pairs + 1.94M long chain-of-thought reasoning traces), and Physical AI RL post-training; physical common-sense ontology spans space, time, fundamental physics; benchmarks: 604 physical-common-sense questions/426 videos plus six embodied-reasoning benchmarks (610 questions/600 videos) built from RoboVQA, BridgeDataV2, AgiBot, RoboFail, HoloAssist, AV data; "Physical AI SFT and reinforcement learning bring significant improvements" — [arXiv 2503.15558](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.15558v1); [NVIDIA research page](https://research.nvidia.com/labs/cosmos-lab/cosmos-reason1)
- Cosmos-Reason2 comes in 2B, 8B, and 32B; post-trained from Qwen3-VL-Instruct via SFT + RL on embodied-reasoning datasets (video, sensor signals, physical-reasoning tasks); features improved spatio-temporal understanding and *timestamp precision*, 2D/3D point localization and bounding boxes with reasoning, 256K context — [HuggingFace nvidia/Cosmos-Reason2-32B](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/Cosmos-Reason2-32B); [NVIDIA build catalog (NIM API available)](https://build.nvidia.com/nvidia/cosmos-reason2-8b/modelcard)
- Cosmos-Reason2-32B vs Qwen3-VL base deltas (model card): General 75.85% vs 73.07%; Robotics 60.60% vs 55.06%; Self-Driving 70.15% vs 48.08%; Smart Spaces 77.79% vs 47.55%; VideoPhy2 43.98% vs 36.57%; AV Collision detection 72.06% vs 37.67% — the collision-detection delta is the closest documented analogue to multi-agent conflict prediction — [HuggingFace nvidia/Cosmos-Reason2-32B](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/Cosmos-Reason2-32B)
- Cosmos-Reason2 documented limitations: performance degrades with fast camera movements, overlapping human-object interactions, low lighting with high motion blur; tested on H100/A100 BF16; NVIDIA Open Model License (commercial use permitted) — [HuggingFace nvidia/Cosmos-Reason2-32B](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/Cosmos-Reason2-32B)
- Gemini Robotics-ER 1.5 is evaluated on 15 academic benchmarks including ERQA and Point-Bench (pointing, image QA, video QA) and claims state of the art for embodied reasoning with "visuo-spatial-temporal understanding" — [arXiv 2510.03342 (Gemini Robotics 1.5 tech report)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.03342); [Google developers blog](https://developers.googleblog.com/en/building-the-next-generation-of-physical-agents-with-gemini-robotics-er-15/)
- ER 1.5 documented capabilities: semantically precise 2D pointing grounded in affordance reasoning; video understanding with cause-effect and *timestamp-level* task-sequence identification; long-horizon orchestration and progress/success detection; tunable "thinking budget" trading latency vs accuracy (performance scales with thinking tokens; exact scores not disclosed in the blog); multi-agent/multi-object coordination capabilities are *not* explicitly detailed — [Google developers blog](https://developers.googleblog.com/en/building-the-next-generation-of-physical-agents-with-gemini-robotics-er-15/)
- ER 1.5 training/recipe disclosure is thin in public materials: the tech report abstract highlights Motion Transfer for multi-embodiment learning and interleaved natural-language reasoning, without post-training data specifics — [arXiv 2510.03342](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.03342)

### Inferences
- The project's measured result (ER-2 16/20 vs gpt-4o 9/20 on conflict prediction) is *consistent with but not predicted by* vendor documentation: neither Google nor NVIDIA evaluates multi-robot conflict/ordering, so the measurement is novel evidence with no vendor benchmark to reconcile against. Cosmos-Reason2's AV-collision delta is the nearest public datapoint supporting the idea that physical-AI post-training specifically improves "will these trajectories conflict" judgments.
- Cosmos-Reason2-8B (open weights, Qwen3-VL base, NIM API available) is the most project-compatible of these: API-first usable via build.nvidia.com, and the 2B/8B open weights are the only embodied-reasoning models that could plausibly be QLoRA-tuned on an L4 — the natural candidate if the project ever moves training-side.
- The blog's thinking-budget framing (no temperature control mentioned) matches this project's prior observation that the ER API exposes no temperature control.

### Gaps
- No "Gemini Robotics-ER 2.0" technical report or model card was found as of 2026-10-06; public documentation covers the 1.5 generation (the project's "ER-2" naming may correspond to a successor preview API whose per-benchmark documentation is not public).
- Exact ERQA/Point-Bench scores for ER 1.5 are in the full tech-report PDF and were not retrievable from the abstract or blog.
- Cosmos-Reason2 8B/2B per-benchmark tables (vs the 32B table above) were not captured; the NIM 8B model-card page did not render content on fetch.

## Key Question 5: Is prompting+verification (no training) still competitive with fine-tuning on 2025–2026 multi-robot benchmarks?

### Takeaway
On hard MAPF-style benchmarks, out-of-the-box LLM prompting (even with agentic/feedback workflows) fails beyond trivial maps, while trained solver-trace models (MAPF-GPT-DDG) dominate — pure prompting is not competitive with task-specific training for *full joint planning*. However, the competitive 2025–2026 pattern for practical systems is hybrid: prompting a strong frozen VLM for *semantic/strategy decisions* while verification/grounding/solvers handle geometry (COMRES-VLM, LLM-NAR — and this project's M3 gate), which sidesteps exactly the failure mode the MAPF-benchmark papers document.

### Cited Findings
- "Solving Multi-Agent Path Finding as an LLM Benchmark: How, How Good and Why" (TMLR 2025, Chen et al.): LLMs with an agentic workflow succeed at single-agent planning and MAPF in empty-room maps without obstacles, but *fail to plan on harder room maps and maze maps* of the standard MAPF benchmark; the authors take the position that directly solving MAPF with LLMs has not been successful, attributing failure to the combined complexity of coordination and long-horizon reasoning — [TMLR 2025](https://mlanthology.org/tmlr/2025/chen2025tmlr-solving)
- MAPF-GPT-DDG (trained, non-prompted) surpasses all existing learning-based MAPF solvers and scales to 1M agents — the trained alternative strictly dominates LLM prompting on the same problem class — [arXiv 2506.23793](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.23793v1)
- LLM-NAR (2025): rather than better prompts or fine-tuning, pairs the LLM with a pre-trained graph neural algorithmic reasoner to inform MAPF decisions — a verification/grounding hybrid — [arXiv 2508.17971](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.17971v1)
- VPRL cuts the other way for spatial *navigation*: GRPO post-training beats SFT and textual-reasoning prompting by 27% exact-match on visual navigation — where RL training is applied, it wins over frozen prompting on those single-agent tasks — [arXiv 2505.11409](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.11409v3)
- COMRES-VLM achieves multi-robot exploration coordination with a *frozen* VLM by feeding it heavily structured inputs (frontier clusters, topological skeletons, occupancy maps) — system-level results without any training, via input engineering + structure — [alphaXiv 2512.19178](https://alphaxiv.org/overview/2512.19178v1)
- SmallPlan shows fine-tuned small models reach GPT-4o-level sequential path planning — a frontier model prompted zero-shot is matchable by a cheap distilled model once the task distribution is fixed — [arXiv 2505.00831](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.00831)

### Inferences
- The literature splits cleanly by task granularity: for *joint trajectory-level* multi-robot planning, prompting is demonstrably not competitive (TMLR 2025) and training wins; for *strategy-level* semantic decisions over structured state (this project's Layer 2: congestion type + strategy choice), prompting+verification remains the published state of practice in 2025–2026, with no fine-tuned baseline yet shown to beat it. The project's architecture (frozen VLM + M3 RAG grounding + M4 safety) matches the hybrid pattern the field converged on.
- Training-side upgrade ladder suggested by the evidence, in feasibility order for a single-L4, API-first lab: (1) keep prompting+verification and add solver-verified few-shot traces (zero training); (2) QLoRA SFT of a 2B–8B open model (Cosmos-Reason2-2B/8B, Qwen2.5-VL-7B) on CBS/LaCAM-labeled congestion scenes — fits an L4, data generatable offline on CPU; (3) short cloud RLVR stage with the simulator as verifier — not L4-feasible, rentable; (4) full fine-tune or anything 32B — out of budget.

### Gaps
- No head-to-head 2025–2026 study compares a fine-tuned VLM vs prompting+verification on a *multi-robot congestion/deadlock* benchmark; the comparison exists only for grid MAPF (where both pure approaches are dominated by specialized trained solvers).
- The TMLR paper's exact model list and per-map success-rate tables were not retrievable from the abstract page; the full PDF is needed for precise numbers.
