Researched 2026-10-05. Scope: jamming, phase transitions, fundamental diagrams — canonical anchors plus 2018–2026 robot/AGV applications, for the related-work chapter of a warehouse multi-robot congestion thesis whose scenes are small-N structured conflicts.

## KQ1 — Fundamental diagram and jamming transitions: canonical anchors

### Takeaway
Each sub-field has a clean 1–2 cite anchor set: Lighthill–Whitham(–Richards) for the macroscopic flow–density relation and kinematic shock waves; Nagel–Schreckenberg for spontaneous (phantom) jam formation as a density-driven transition; Helbing–Molnár for the social-force model and Helbing–Farkas–Vicsek (Nature 2000) for faster-is-slower and exit clogging; Liu–Nagel and O'Hern et al. for the granular jamming phase diagram; Zuriguel et al. for the clogging transition at bottlenecks. Named phenomena matter more than derivations: fundamental diagram, phantom jam / stop-and-go waves, faster-is-slower, arching/clogging, point J.

### Cited Findings

**Traffic flow theory**
- Lighthill, M.J. & Whitham, G.B. (1955), "On kinematic waves II: A theory of traffic flow on long crowded roads", *Proc. R. Soc. Lond. A* 229, 317–345 — the LWR model: traffic as a compressible fluid obeying conservation of vehicles plus an assumed flow–density relation; names **kinematic (shock) waves** that propagate backwards through jams. Companion anchor: Richards, P.I. (1956), "Shock waves on the highway", *Oper. Res.* 4, 42–51. — **VERIFIED (canonical)**; the LWR framing is restated in the modern CA-traffic literature, e.g. [Density waves and jamming transition in cellular automaton models for traffic flow (arXiv:cond-mat/9808152)](https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/9808152).
- The empirical flow-vs-density curve itself ("fundamental diagram": flow rises with density to a capacity maximum at a critical density, then falls to zero at jam density) predates LWR: Greenshields, B.D. (1935), "A study of traffic capacity", *Highway Research Board Proceedings* 14, 448–477 — **VERIFIED (canonical)**. The shape description (rise to max, decay to jam density) is confirmed in the robot single-file literature — [arXiv:2403.08683](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.08683).
- Nagel, K. & Schreckenberg, M. (1992), "A cellular automaton model for freeway traffic", *J. Phys. I France* 2, 2221–2229 — minimal stochastic CA in which **spontaneous jams ("phantom jams") emerge above a critical density with no external cause**; the canonical microscopic jamming-transition cite for traffic. — **VERIFIED (canonical)**; the jamming-transition-as-function-of-density-and-noise phenomenology is confirmed in [arXiv:cond-mat/9808152](https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/9808152) (density waves move upstream at a density-independent velocity).
- Optional survey anchor: Chowdhury, D., Santen, L. & Schadschneider, A. (2000), "Statistical physics of vehicular traffic and some related systems", *Phys. Rep.* 329, 199–329 — **VERIFIED (canonical)**; one cite covering CA traffic, ant trails, and pedestrian analogies.

**Pedestrian dynamics**
- Helbing, D. & Molnár, P. (1995), "Social force model for pedestrian dynamics", *Phys. Rev. E* 51, 4282–4286 — pedestrians as self-driven particles under social forces; the standard microscopic crowd model. — **VERIFIED (canonical)**.
- Helbing, D., Farkas, I. & Vicsek, T. (2000), "Simulating dynamical features of escape panic", *Nature* 407, 487–490 — names the **"faster-is-slower" effect**: above an optimal desired speed, friction and **intermittent arching/clogging at the exit** make total egress time increase; escape occurs in irregular bursts because "small groups of pedestrians temporarily block the way out". — **VERIFIED (web)**: phenomenon and mechanism confirmed via [the Collective Dynamics retrospective "Revisiting the paper 'Simulating dynamical features of escape panic'"](https://collective-dynamics.eu/index.php/cod/article/view/A168) and [Beyond the "faster is slower" effect (arXiv:1706.06140)](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1706.06140).
- Empirical pedestrian fundamental diagram: Seyfried, A., Steffen, B., Klingsch, W. & Boltes, M. (2005), "The fundamental diagram of pedestrian movement revisited", *J. Stat. Mech.* P10002 — single-file pedestrian experiments; linear relation between velocity and inverse density; single-file and 2D fundamental diagrams coincide for 1 m⁻² < ρ < 5 m⁻². The benchmark empirical FD for crowds. — **VERIFIED (web)**: [arXiv:physics/0506170](https://arxiv.org/abs/physics/0506170).
- Experimental confirmation of faster-is-slower across systems (sheep, grains, vibrated silos — useful if a reviewer asks whether FIS is real outside simulation): Pastor, J.M. et al. (2015), "Experimental proof of faster-is-slower in systems of frightened, stressed or cooperative agents", *Phys. Rev. E* 92, 062817 — **unverified** (from memory; not fetched this session).

**Granular / active-matter jamming**
- Liu, A.J. & Nagel, S.R. (1998), "Jamming is not just cool any more", *Nature* 396, 21–22 — proposes the **jamming phase diagram** unifying glasses, granular media and foams in temperature–load–density space; names "jamming" as a phase transition into a rigid, disordered state. — **VERIFIED (canonical)**.
- O'Hern, C.S., Silbert, L.E., Liu, A.J. & Nagel, S.R. (2003), "Jamming at zero temperature and zero applied stress: The epitome of disorder", *Phys. Rev. E* 68, 011306 — defines **point J**, the critical packing fraction at which a granular system rigidifies; the technical anchor for jamming-as-phase-transition. — **VERIFIED (canonical)**.
- Zuriguel, I. et al. (2014), "Clogging transition of many-particle systems flowing through bottlenecks", *Sci. Rep.* 4, 7324 — unifies **clogging** across sheep herds, pedestrian crowds, grains and colloids: the distribution of time lapses between consecutive passages through a constriction has a **power-law tail; the clogged phase is defined by divergence of the mean time lapse (exponent ≤ 2)**. Clogs are caused by **arches** of a few bodies spanning the constriction. — **VERIFIED (web)**: [UB repository record](https://diposit.ub.edu/dspace/handle/2445/126027); mechanism restated in [Souzy et al., Transition from clogging to continuous flow in constricted particle suspensions (arXiv:2004.04413)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.04413) (stable arches at small outlet-to-particle ratio; intermittent regime with power-law exponent < 2 for 2.43 < D/d < 5.26).
- Active-matter option (only if the thesis wants the self-propelled-particle framing): Cates, M.E. & Tailleur, J. (2015), "Motility-induced phase separation", *Annu. Rev. Condens. Matter Phys.* 6, 219–244 — self-propelled particles spontaneously separate into dense (jammed) and dilute phases purely because they slow down where crowded (**MIPS**). — **VERIFIED (canonical)**; applied to real robot swarms in [Non-Uniform Robot Densities in Vibration Driven Swarms Using Phase Separation Theory (arXiv:1902.10662)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.10662).

### Inferences
- A clean citation scheme for the thesis: FD + shock waves → Lighthill-Whitham 1955 (+ Richards 1956, + Greenshields 1935 if the diagram's empirical origin is wanted); phantom jams → Nagel-Schreckenberg 1992; crowd model → Helbing-Molnár 1995; faster-is-slower + exit clogging → Helbing et al. 2000; jamming phase diagram → Liu-Nagel 1998 (+ O'Hern 2003); bottleneck clogging transition → Zuriguel et al. 2014. Six to eight cites cover the entire physics backdrop.
- "Clogging" (Zuriguel) is the physics term closest to warehouse *bottleneck* congestion; "jamming" (Liu-Nagel) is bulk density-driven rigidification; "phantom jam" (NaSch) is flow instability without an obstacle. Using these three words precisely maps directly onto a bottleneck/deadlock/anomaly taxonomy.

- Exact page ranges for the canonical cites were not re-fetched; verify in BibTeX pass.
- Pastor et al. 2015 (experimental FIS) not confirmed this session.

## KQ2 — Applications to robot fleets, AGVs, and warehouses

### Takeaway
There is a small but direct literature: robot swarms have experimentally reproduced the fundamental diagram and a two-stage jamming transition (Alonso-Llanes/Garcimartín/Zuriguel 2024); swarm-robotics scaling studies find a two-phase (near-optimal vs collapsed) performance transition with system size (Kuckling et al., ICRA 2024); the MAPF community explicitly imports traffic-flow ideas to fight congestion at 10k-agent scale (Chen et al., AAAI 2024; GGO, IJCAI 2024); and DeepFleet (Amazon, 2025) is the industrial endpoint — fleet foundation models whose headline applications are congestion forecasting and routing around jams.

### Cited Findings
- **Robot fundamental diagram, measured**: Alonso-Llanes, L., Garcimartín, A. & Zuriguel, I. (2024), "Single file motion of robot swarms", [arXiv:2403.08683](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.08683) — wheeled robots in single file **reproduce the fundamental diagram with a free-flow → congested transition as density increases**; stop-and-go waves in the congested regime; above a second density threshold, **average jam duration and number of robots per jam diverge**, marking a transition from intermittent congestion to total congestion. (Note the authors: this is the Pamplona granular-clogging group applying their methodology to robots — the explicit granular→robot bridge.) — **VERIFIED (web)**. Journal venue of record unverified (arXiv listing only).
- **Two-phase performance in swarm scaling**: Kuckling, J., Luckey, R., Avrutin, V., Vardy, A., Reina, A. & Hamann, H., "Do We Run Large-scale Multi-Robot Systems on the Edge? More Evidence for Two-Phase Performance in System Size Scaling", ICRA 2024, [arXiv:2310.11843](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.11843) — simulation + queueing-theory and population models: **at critical system sizes, performance separates into two phases, nearly-optimal and minimal**; systems running near optimum may be in a transient state approaching breakdown. — **VERIFIED (web)**.
- **Interference/density regulation in swarms**: Mayya, S., Pierpaoli, P. & Egerstedt, M., "Voluntary Retreat for Decentralized Interference Reduction in Robot Swarms", [arXiv:1812.02193](https://arxiv.org/abs/1812.02193) (ICRA 2019 per memory — venue **unverified**) — in densely packed swarms, spatial interference (competition for physical space) degrades task throughput; a decentralized stay-or-retreat rule using only binary encounter information restores performance; validated on real robots. — **VERIFIED (web)** for content.
- **Ant/robot excavation clogging**: Aguilar, J. et al. incl. Goodisman, M.A.D. & Goldman, D.I. (2018), "Collective clog control: Optimizing traffic flow in confined biological and robophysical excavation", *Science* 361, 672–677, DOI 10.1126/science.aan3891 — fire ants, autonomous robots and CA models in narrow tunnels: **individual idleness and selective retreating robustly optimize digging throughput — idleness reduces the frequency of flow-stopping clogs, retreating reduces the dissolution time of clusters that do form**. — **VERIFIED (web)**: [publisher record via VIVO](https://vivo.colorado.edu/display/pubid_241856), [PDF at Goldman lab](https://crablab.gatech.edu/pages/publications/pdf/science-2018.pdf).
- **Ant traffic contrast (why evolved collectives avoid the jammed branch)**: John, A., Schadschneider, A., Chowdhury, D. & Nishinari, K. (2009), "Trafficlike collective movement of ants on trails: Absence of a jammed phase", [arXiv:0903.2717](https://arxiv.org/abs/0903.2717) (*Phys. Rev. Lett.* 102, 108001 per memory — volume **unverified**) — ant trails show **no decreasing (jammed) branch of the fundamental diagram**; velocity stays roughly constant as density rises. Reinforced experimentally by Poissonnier, L.-A., Motsch, S., Gautrais, J., Buhl, J. & Dussutour, A. (2019), "Experimental investigation of ant traffic under crowded conditions", *eLife* 8:e48945 — [eLife PDF](https://cdn.elifesciences.org/articles/48945/elife-48945-v1.pdf). — **VERIFIED (web)** for both papers' existence and claim.
- **MAPF imports traffic-flow theory**: Chen, Z., Harabor, D., Li, J. & Stuckey, P.J. (2024), "Traffic Flow Optimisation for Lifelong Multi-Agent Path Finding", *Proc. AAAI* 38(18), 20674–20682 — existing MAPF methods "plan free-flow optimal paths, which creates congestion"; guiding agents along congestion-avoiding paths yields large throughput gains while coordinating **>10,000 agents**. — **VERIFIED (web)**: [AAAI page](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/30054), [arXiv:2308.11234](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.11234).
- **Guidance Graph Optimization**: "Guidance Graph Optimization for Lifelong Multi-Agent Path Finding", [arXiv:2402.01446](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.01446) (IJCAI 2024 — [proceedings PDF](https://ijcai.org/proceedings/2024/0035.pdf)) — learns edge weights that inflate the cost of frequently used edges so later paths avoid congested lanes; a directly warehouse-shaped congestion-shaping method. Author list **unverified** this session (Zhang/Jiang/Bhatt/Nikolaidis/Li per memory). — **VERIFIED (web)** for paper and mechanism.
- **DeepFleet and neighbors**: "DeepFleet: Multi-Agent Foundation Models for Mobile Robots", [arXiv:2508.08574](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.08574v2) — Amazon Robotics foundation models trained on movement data from hundreds of thousands of warehouse robots; four architectures (robot-centric decision transformer, robot-floor cross-attention, image-floor convolutional, graph-floor); stated downstream applications: **congestion forecasting, adaptive routing, proactive rescheduling**; Amazon claims ~10% fleet-efficiency improvement — [Amazon Science blog](https://www.amazon.science/blog/amazon-builds-first-foundation-model-for-multirobot-coordination). — **VERIFIED (web)**.
- **AGV/warehouse operations-research flow studies**: queueing-network models of robotic mobile fulfillment systems (RMFS) show **strong increase of blocking effects between vehicles as fleet size grows** and evaluate zone assignment by congestion — [TUM thesis/report](https://mediatum.ub.tum.de/doc/1453020/document.pdf), [Erasmus ERS-2017-001-LIS report](https://repub.eur.nl/pub/95392/ERS-2017-001-LIS.pdf), [least-congested zone assignment study](https://iima.ac.in/publication/robot-storage-zone-assignment-strategies-mobile-fulfillment-systems). Simulation studies report **diminishing then negative returns of fleet size: throughput collapse ranges up to ~85% reduction from vehicle congestion**, and a bistable regime where identical arrival rates yield either near-maximum performance or near-zero collapse — [Oxford thesis (ORA)](https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid:a8f635cf-32b1-4b4a-9601-b5048afa45ee/files/sth83m063x), [Birmingham hybrid order-picking congestion study](https://research.birmingham.ac.uk/en/publications/analysing-the-effects-of-congestion-on-hybrid-order-picking-syste/). — **VERIFIED (web)** at the claim level; specific numbers come from search synthesis of these pages and should be pulled from the PDFs before quoting. The canonical Kiva-system cite for the warehouse setting is Wurman, P.R., D'Andrea, R. & Mountz, M. (2008), "Coordinating hundreds of cooperative, autonomous vehicles in warehouses", *AI Magazine* 29(1), 9–20 — **VERIFIED (canonical)**.

### Inferences
- The Alonso-Llanes/Garcimartín/Zuriguel robot paper is the single best related-work cite for "fundamental diagrams measured on actual robots", and its authorship makes the granular-physics lineage explicit — one sentence can connect the thesis's warehouse robots to the clogging literature through it.

## KQ3 — Deadlock vs congestion as distinct regimes

### Takeaway
Yes, the literature formalizes the distinction, but in two separate traditions that the thesis can bridge: (a) discrete-resource AGV/manufacturing theory defines deadlock as circular wait and liveness loss (structural stoppage — qualitatively distinct from slowdown), with livelock as a third category; (b) continuous-control robotics (Grover/Liu/Sycara) defines deadlock as a force-equilibrium of the closed-loop dynamics — robots stand still at a stable equilibrium short of their goals. Congestion/slowdown, by contrast, lives in the flow-physics vocabulary (reduced velocity branch of the FD, intermittent clogging). No single paper maps cleanly onto a deadlock/bottleneck/anomaly triple; the mapping has to be assembled.

### Cited Findings
- Grover, J.S., Liu, C. & Sycara, K., "Deadlock Analysis and Resolution for Multi-Robot Systems", WAFR (14th International Workshop on the Algorithmic Foundations of Robotics), [arXiv:1911.09146](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1911.09146) — deadlock under CBF-based reactive controllers is "an equilibrium of system dynamics" causing standstill before the goal; **characterized as force-equilibrium on the robots; the deadlock set is non-empty, bounded, and lies on the boundary of the safety set**; analysis complexity grows ~exponentially with robot count; provably correct decentralized resolution demonstrated for **two and three robots** (Khepera-IV hardware). — **VERIFIED (web)**: [CMU RI record](https://www.ri.cmu.edu/publications/deadlock-analysis-and-resolution-for-multi-robot-systems). Follow-up: "The Before, During, and After of Multi-Robot Deadlock" ([DeepAI record](https://deepai.org/publication/the-before-during-and-after-of-multi-robot-deadlock)) — venue/year **unverified**.
- AGV resource-theoretic tradition: deadlock = circular wait on shared traffic resources (each vehicle blocked by another in the group, none can proceed); **livelock** = vehicle keeps moving/retrying but never reaches its goal; "**traffic liveness**" = every vehicle retains the ability to complete its assignment, compromised by deadlock and livelock formation — [Reveliotis-group paper (Georgia Tech, Med-19)](https://www2.isye.gatech.edu/~spyros/publications/Med-19.pdf), [A-DRIVE (arXiv:2204.04910)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2204.04910) (deadlock at four-way intersections, merges, single-track lanes), [MARL deadlock handling for AMRs (arXiv:2511.07071)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.07071). Deadlocks "occupy shared traffic resources without making progress, potentially causing severe congestion" — i.e., the AGV literature treats deadlock as a *cause* of congestion, not a point on the same continuum. — **VERIFIED (web)** at claim level.
- Flow-physics side of the distinction: in clogging language, **congested flow is intermittent (power-law bursts, finite mean passage time) while the clogged state is an absorbing/permanent arrest (divergent mean time lapse)** — exactly a slowdown-vs-structural-stoppage dichotomy with a quantitative order parameter — [Zuriguel et al. framing via UB record](https://diposit.ub.edu/dspace/handle/2445/126027) and [arXiv:2004.04413](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.04413). The robot single-file study adds the intermediate regime explicitly: free flow → congested intermittent flow → totally congested, with the last transition marked by diverging jam duration — [arXiv:2403.08683](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.08683). — **VERIFIED (web)**.

### Inferences
- A defensible mapping for the thesis taxonomy: **deadlock** ↔ circular-wait / force-equilibrium / clogged-absorbing state (structural stoppage; needs external or rule-based intervention — cite Grover et al. + AGV liveness literature + Zuriguel's divergent-mean criterion); **bottleneck** ↔ intermittent clogging at a constriction (finite but heavy-tailed resolution times — cite Zuriguel 2014, Helbing 2000 arching); **anomaly** has no flow-physics counterpart (it is a fault/outlier category) — honest to say so rather than force a citation.
- The two deadlock formalisms (resource circular-wait vs. dynamical equilibrium) correspond to the thesis's two layers: M5's task/zone registry view (resource) and M4's Nav2/velocity view (dynamical). That correspondence is citable structure for the related-work section.

### Gaps
- No paper found that explicitly unifies the flow-physics (clogging) and computer-science (circular wait) definitions of deadlock in one framework; nearest is the AGV literature's informal "deadlock causes congestion".
- Vis, I.F.A. (2006) EJOR survey of AGV design/control and Fragapane et al. (2021) EJOR AMR survey are standard umbrella cites here but were **not verified** this session.

## KQ4 — Metrics transferable to small-N scored cells

### Takeaway
The flow literature's measurable quantities port surprisingly well to small-N scored cells: (1) time-lapse/burst statistics and their divergence (Zuriguel) justify *time-to-resolution* with a principled censoring rule for "never resolves"; (2) cluster/clog *dissolution time* (Aguilar) is literally time-to-resolution for a 3–5 agent clog; (3) throughput under guidance (Chen et al.) and restricted flow through a constriction (clogging papers) justify *restricted throughput*; (4) delay-vs-free-flow is the oldest traffic metric of all (free-flow speed is the FD's zero-density intercept) and appears in evacuation work as egress time vs. optimal-speed egress time.

### Cited Findings
- **Time-lapse distribution / divergence criterion**: the clogging transition is *defined* by the divergence of the mean time lapse between consecutive passages (power-law tail exponent ≤ 2) — [Zuriguel et al. via UB record](https://diposit.ub.edu/dspace/handle/2445/126027); [arXiv:2004.04413](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.04413). Gives a citable rationale for treating "unresolved after timeout" as a distinct outcome class rather than a large time value. — **VERIFIED (web)**.
- **Jam duration and jam size**: average jam duration and number of robots per jam, with divergence marking total congestion — [arXiv:2403.08683](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.08683). Direct small-N analogs: duration of the conflict episode, number of robots drawn in. — **VERIFIED (web)**.
- **Clog frequency and cluster dissolution time**: Aguilar et al. 2018 scores excavation strategies by (a) frequency of flow-stopping clogs and (b) dissolution time of clusters that do form — [Science 2018 PDF](https://crablab.gatech.edu/pages/publications/pdf/science-2018.pdf). This is a two-part cost (incidence × time-to-resolution) directly adoptable for oracle cost tables. — **VERIFIED (web)**.
- **Egress/escape time vs. speed** (faster-is-slower curve): total evacuation time as a function of desired speed has a minimum at an optimal speed — [Collective Dynamics retrospective](https://collective-dynamics.eu/index.php/cod/article/view/A168). Analog: resolution time vs. commanded aggressiveness; justifies that "push harder" strategies can score worse. — **VERIFIED (web)**.
- **Throughput as the lifelong objective**: lifelong MAPF scores by overall throughput (goals reached per unit time), and congestion-avoiding guidance is evaluated purely on throughput gains — [Chen et al., AAAI 2024](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/30054); [GGO, arXiv:2402.01446](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.01446). — **VERIFIED (web)**.
- **Fleet-level efficiency delta**: DeepFleet reports a ~10% travel-efficiency improvement as its headline metric — [Amazon Science blog](https://www.amazon.science/blog/amazon-builds-first-foundation-model-for-multirobot-coordination). — **VERIFIED (web)**.
- **Delay vs. free-flow baseline**: free-flow speed/travel time is the FD's low-density limit (Greenshields 1935; LWR 1955 — **VERIFIED (canonical)**); delay = actual minus free-flow travel time is the standard highway-engineering congestion measure (Highway Capacity Manual, Transportation Research Board — **unverified edition/section**, cite generically or verify).

### Inferences
- A defensible metric suite for small-N scored cells, each with a flow-physics citation: **time-to-resolution** (↔ clog dissolution time, Aguilar 2018; jam duration, arXiv:2403.08683), **resolved-vs-unresolved as separate outcome** (↔ divergent-mean clogging criterion, Zuriguel 2014), **per-robot delay vs. solo free-flow traversal of the same cell** (↔ delay vs. free-flow, traffic engineering), **throughput through the contested resource during the episode** (↔ restricted bottleneck flow; lifelong-MAPF throughput), **number of robots entangled** (↔ jam size).
- The free-flow baseline is cheap to obtain in the thesis's setting (run each cell with one robot) and makes the delay metric exactly the flow-theoretic quantity, not just an analogy.

### Gaps
- "Time-to-resolution" as a named metric was not found verbatim in the flow literature; nearest named quantities are clog/cluster dissolution time and jam duration.
- HCM delay-metric citation not verified to a specific edition.

## KQ5 — Small-N conflicts as microscopic building blocks of macro congestion

### Takeaway
No paper states the scale-bridging thesis in exactly those words, but three verified lines of evidence assemble it: (1) clogs/jams are *caused* by arches — transient structures of a handful of bodies (Zuriguel; Helbing's "small groups temporarily block the way out"); (2) deadlock theory is explicitly built up from 2–3 robot cases because complexity explodes exponentially beyond that (Grover et al.); (3) macro-scale collapse emerges from pairwise interference as system size crosses a threshold (Kuckling et al.; RMFS blocking curves). Together these license the claim that resolving small-N structured conflicts attacks congestion at its generative unit.

### Cited Findings
- Clogs at constrictions form when **a few particles arrive simultaneously and form an arch** spanning the outlet; whether flow is continuous or clogged is set by the stability of these few-body arches — [arXiv:2004.04413](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.04413) (stable arches vs continuous flow by D/d ratio); same arch mechanism across grains, sheep, pedestrians in [Zuriguel et al. 2014 via UB record](https://diposit.ub.edu/dspace/handle/2445/126027). — **VERIFIED (web)**.
- In escape dynamics, outflow stops because "**small groups of pedestrians temporarily block the way out**"; the macro quantity (egress time) is controlled by the statistics of these micro blocking events — [Collective Dynamics retrospective on Helbing et al. 2000](https://collective-dynamics.eu/index.php/cod/article/view/A168). — **VERIFIED (web)**.
- Deadlock analysis is tractable and exact at N = 2, 3 (duality + graph enumeration; hardware demos with two and three robots), with complexity increasing ~exponentially in N — [arXiv:1911.09146](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1911.09146). — **VERIFIED (web)**.
- Macro performance collapse appears as a sharp two-phase transition in system size, driven by local interference accumulating — [arXiv:2310.11843](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.11843); queueing models of RMFS show blocking effects between vehicles growing strongly with fleet size — [TUM document](https://mediatum.ub.tum.de/doc/1453020/document.pdf). — **VERIFIED (web)**.
- In confined excavation, system throughput is optimized by changing the behavior of individuals in the few-agent clog (idleness, selective retreat), i.e., micro-conflict policy determines macro flow — [Aguilar et al., Science 2018](https://crablab.gatech.edu/pages/publications/pdf/science-2018.pdf). — **VERIFIED (web)**.
- Ant systems show macro jamming can be *absent* when individuals adopt conflict-level conventions (no overtaking, priority rules), again locating the control point at the micro interaction — [arXiv:0903.2717](https://arxiv.org/abs/0903.2717); [Poissonnier et al. 2019, eLife](https://cdn.elifesciences.org/articles/48945/elife-48945-v1.pdf). — **VERIFIED (web)**.

### Inferences
- Suggested citable argument chain for the thesis: macro congestion transitions exist in robot collectives (Kuckling 2024; Alonso-Llanes 2024) → the generative events of those transitions are few-body structures (arches: Zuriguel 2014; blocking groups: Helbing 2000) → few-body conflicts are exactly where formal analysis and resolution are tractable (Grover 2020/21) and where behavioral intervention provably restores flow (Aguilar 2018; ant conventions) → therefore studying structured 2–5 robot conflict cells is studying congestion at its unit of formation, not a toy reduction.
- This also pre-empts the obvious reviewer objection ("your N is too small to say anything about congestion"): the physics literature's own causal unit is small-N.

### Gaps
- No single paper found that *quantitatively* decomposes a macro fundamental diagram into statistics of identified small-N conflict motifs (e.g., jam-seed census). The argument above is assembled, not quoted — the thesis should present it as a synthesis, with the chain of citations.
- Did not verify the exact robot count used in Aguilar et al.'s robophysical trials (memory says ~4 robots; check the Science PDF before quoting a number).
