## Q1. Offline / replay evaluation harnesses for embodied VLMs and LLM agents, and the offline-to-closed-loop gap

### Takeaway
The strongest methodological result is from autonomous driving: **pseudo-simulation** (NAVSIM v2, CoRL 2025) shows that an offline bench predicts closed-loop rankings much better if it adds a small number of *pre-rendered perturbed follow-up states* per logged scene. On 83 planners it reaches r = 0.89 with closed-loop nuPlan, against r = 0.83 for the best purely open-loop metric. Earlier nuPlan work found open-loop and closed-loop scores could even be negatively correlated. For LLM agents, 2026 work replaces naive log replay with doubly-robust "replay up to the divergence point" estimators and learned world models. Embodied VLM benchmarks such as EmbodiedBench run in closed loop inside their own simulators. They are not replay harnesses, so they offer no ready-made way to re-score our 187 logged items.

### Cited Findings
- **Pseudo-simulation for autonomous driving** (Cao et al., CoRL 2025, PMLR v305): this is an offline bench on real logs. Before evaluation it generates synthetic observations of plausible future states with 3D Gaussian Splatting, then scores the planner in two stages: the logged state first, then about 12 perturbed states, weighted by proximity to the planner's stage-1 trajectory. It correlates with nuPlan closed-loop scores at R² = 0.8 / r = 0.89 over 83 planners, against R² = 0.7 / r = 0.83 for the best open-loop baseline. It needs about 13 planner inferences per scenario instead of 80 for an 8 s closed-loop rollout at 10 Hz. **[search-snippet, consistent across arXiv HTML and PMLR listing]** — [arXiv:2506.04218](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.04218); [PMLR v305 cao25a](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v305/cao25a.html); code: [autonomousvision/navsim](https://github.com/autonomousvision/navsim) (1.1k stars, last push 2025-10-27). *Applicability:* this is the template for our bench. For each of the 187 logged decision items, pre-generate a few perturbed "what would the scene be after strategy X" states and score the VLM on those, instead of only on the logged state.
- **"Parting with Misconceptions about Learning-based Vehicle Motion Planning"** (Dauner et al., CoRL 2023) reported a misalignment, and in places a *negative* correlation, between open-loop and closed-loop nuPlan scores. This is the canonical warning that offline accuracy can rank systems wrongly. **[search-snippet]** — [arXiv:2306.07962](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.07962). *Applicability:* cite it to justify validating the text-only replay bench against a few closed-loop episodes before trusting its rankings.
- **"Closing the Loop: Motion Prediction Models beyond Open-Loop Benchmarks"** (May 2025): better open-loop accuracy does not always give better closed-loop driving. **[search-snippet]** — [arXiv:2505.05638](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.05638). *Applicability:* a second, more recent citation for the same gap.
- **Counterfactual evaluation of agents in non-resettable environments** (NeurIPS 2026 listing): the candidate agent runs live only until its first irreversible action (the "commit point"). The rest of the episode is then estimated from historical logs with doubly-robust corrections, and the authors bound the bias. **[search-snippet; NeurIPS virtual page only, arXiv ID not pinned]** — [NeurIPS 2026 poster page](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2026/168256). *Applicability:* this matches our setting, where a strategy dispatch is the irreversible action. Logged outcomes can be reused only for items where the new VLM picks the *same* strategy as the logged one; any other pick needs a model or a re-run.
- **ADWM: Autoregressive Diffusion World Models for Off-Policy Evaluation of LLM Agents** (arXiv Jun 2026): trains a world model on pre-collected trajectories to score new agent policies, and reports a mean rank correlation of 0.82 with true performance across benchmarks. **[search-snippet]** — [arXiv:2606.05558](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.05558). *Applicability:* this conceptually supports the learned-surrogate route. With 187 items, a learned world model is not realistic; a 2D kinematic simulator (Q2) is the practical "world model".
- **Off-policy evaluation from logged deployment data** (Ozolcer & Bae, NeurIPS 2025 workshop): self-normalized importance sampling and doubly-robust estimators on the logs of a deployed LLM agent. The policy with the higher average value on the logs harmed specific subgroups. **[search-snippet; primary URL not fetched — locate before citing]**. *Applicability:* IS/DR estimators need logged action probabilities. Our logs hold one T=0 choice per item, so classic OPE is mostly inapplicable without logged propensities.
- **Practitioner caveat on "shadow replay"**: naive replay penalizes a model that *would have changed* the trajectory, because once the candidate diverges there is nothing to score it against. **[blog, informal]** — [tianpan.co, 2026-05-22](https://tianpan.co/blog/2026-05-22-shadow-replay-punishes-models-that-change-the-conversation). *Applicability:* this is exactly the bias of scoring a new VLM's strategy on items whose outcome was produced by a different strategy.
- **EmbodiedBench** (ICML 2025 Oral): 1,128 tasks in four closed-loop environments. EB-ALFRED and EB-Habitat test high-level planning; EB-Navigation and EB-Manipulation test low-level actions. The best model, GPT-4o, averaged 28.9%. Removing vision cut low-level performance by 40–70% but barely affected high-level tasks. **[search-snippet; abstract-level]** — [arXiv:2502.09560](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.09560); repo [EmbodiedBench/EmbodiedBench](https://github.com/EmbodiedBench/EmbodiedBench) (358 stars, last push 2026-05-30). Sources disagree on the number of models evaluated (13 on arXiv vs 24 on the SlidesLive talk page). *Applicability:* the vision-ablation finding is directly relevant. It predicts that adding images to a *high-level strategy-selection* bench may move scores little, so test this rather than assume it.
- **VLABench** (OpenMOSS): a large-scale benchmark for VLAs, embodied agents and VLMs, focused on manipulation. **[repo verified]** — [OpenMOSS/VLABench](https://github.com/OpenMOSS/VLABench) (482 stars, last push 2025-11-11, about a year stale). *Applicability:* low. It covers manipulation only and is cited only as a "we considered it" entry.
- **VIKI-Bench** (arXiv 2506.09049): a hierarchical benchmark for VLM-driven embodied multi-agent cooperation with multi-view visual input. It does not include a top-down deadlock task. **[search-snippet]** — [arXiv:2506.09049](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.09049). *Applicability:* related work for positioning only.

### Inferences
- Our 187-item text-only bench is an *open-loop* bench in the nuPlan sense. Its numbers are valid for "does the VLM pick a feasible / oracle-agreeing strategy given this state". They are not valid for "does the system resolve congestion faster". Report it under that name and cite 2306.07962 plus 2506.04218 for the caveat.
- The cheapest upgrade borrows pseudo-simulation's two-stage design. Stage 1 is the logged item as it is today. Stage 2 runs each candidate strategy forward in a fast 2D kinematic sim (Q2) to produce the *follow-up* state, then asks the VLM again, or scores the outcome directly. That turns 187 single-step items into short counterfactual rollouts without Isaac.
- To estimate the offline-to-closed-loop gap, run a small calibration set of the same scenarios in Isaac and in the 2D sim, then report a rank correlation between them, as NAVSIM did with 83 planners. Even 5–10 configurations give a defensible "bench validity" number.

### Gaps
- No embodied-robotics (non-driving) paper was found that measures offline-vs-closed-loop correlation for a *VLM high-level planner*. The driving literature is the only quantified evidence.
- The arXiv ID and authors for the NeurIPS 2026 counterfactual-evaluation paper and for the Ozolcer & Bae workshop paper were not pinned down.

## Q2. Lightweight multi-robot simulators that run faster than realtime

### Takeaway
Three tiers apply to us.
- **ROS2/Nav2-preserving 2D sims:** Flatland (avidbots, now ROS 2 Humble through Rolling) and Stage via `stage_ros2` (has a jazzy branch). Both keep the real Nav2 stack and can run faster than wall clock under `/clock`, so the M4/M5 code runs unmodified.
- **Pure-Python kinematic sims:** IR-SIM (diff-drive, ORCA/RVO behaviours, matplotlib top-down render, LLM-generated YAML scenarios, very active) and VMAS (vectorized PyTorch). They are orders of magnitude faster but drop Nav2, so strategies must be re-implemented as simple controllers.
- **Grid MAPF suites:** POGEMA and LoRR Start-Kit. Fast and scalable, but discrete and throughput-oriented.

GPU physics engines (MJX, Genesis, ManiSkill, Isaac Lab) are overkill for 2–6 diff-drive robots and still need a GPU.

### Cited Findings

**ROS2 / Nav2-compatible**
- **Flatland (avidbots/flatland)**: "a lightweight 2.5D robot simulator for ROS 1 and ROS 2". Its README says it can "Run faster than real time; connected ROS nodes may encounter timing issues if they cannot keep up" and "Allows headless execution, with RViz based visualization if needed". The dev container supports **Humble, Jazzy, Kilted, Lyrical, Rolling**, and there are branches such as `ros2`, `issue-108-ros2-jazzy-flatland-viz-updates` and `diff-drive-plugin-tf-broadcast-optional`. **[verified — README on `ros2` branch via GitHub API]** — [github.com/avidbots/flatland (ros2 branch)](https://github.com/avidbots/flatland/tree/ros2). Activity: 123 stars, last push 2026-10-05. *Applicability:* this is the best drop-in replacement for Isaac when the Nav2 stack must stay in the loop. It has diff-drive, 2D lidar and a Jazzy target. It has no camera, so a top-down image must be synthesized from poses (Q3).
- **OpenRobOps/sim-flatland**: "Lightweight 2D ROS 2 / Nav2 simulated robot", pushed 2026-10-21, 3 stars. **[repo listing verified]** — [OpenRobOps/sim-flatland](https://github.com/OpenRobOps/sim-flatland). *Applicability:* a small but recent Flatland+Nav2 wiring example worth copying for launch files. Low maturity.
- **ros2_flatland_rl_tutorial**: a ROS 2 Flatland RL tutorial with 182 stars, last push 2025-07-01. **[repo listing verified]** — [FilipeAlmeidaFEUP/ros2_flatland_rl_tutorial](https://github.com/FilipeAlmeidaFEUP/ros2_flatland_rl_tutorial). *Applicability:* a reference for driving Flatland episodically from Python.
- **Stage + stage_ros2 (tuw-robotics)**: a ROS2 bridge supporting multiple robots with one or several TF trees, with branches `humble`, `jazzy`, `jazzy-devel` and `lyrical-devel`. The authors recommend their modified Stage fork; upstream `rtv/Stage` was last pushed in 2021. **[verified — README + branch list]** — [tuw-robotics/stage_ros2](https://github.com/tuw-robotics/stage_ros2) (last push 2026-07-22); [rtv/Stage](https://github.com/rtv/Stage). *Applicability:* a mature multi-robot 2D sim with namespaced robots that fits our `robot_N` namespacing. Its speed-up behaviour under Nav2 was not checked.
- **Arena-Rosnav**: the current docs describe "A modular ROS 2 (Jazzy) platform for researching and benchmarking autonomous robot navigation" with Nav2 as a classical planner and the simulators "Gazebo, Isaac Sim, MuJoCo, flatsim" (flatsim: "2D, no renderer"). Multi-robot and faster-than-realtime support are not explicitly documented. **[verified — docs.arena-rosnav.org]** — [docs.arena-rosnav.org](https://docs.arena-rosnav.org/); repo [Arena-Rosnav/arena-rosnav](https://github.com/Arena-Rosnav/arena-rosnav) (131 stars, last push 2025-10-24); Arena 5.0 RSS 2025 demo paper (adds Isaac integration and a social-nav benchmark) — [RSS 2025 p092](https://roboticsproceedings.org/rss21/p092.html). *Applicability:* this is the only maintained framework that already puts Nav2 + Jazzy + Isaac + a 2D backend under one scenario/task generator. It suits a two-fidelity design (2D for sweeps, Isaac for confirmation). It is heavyweight to adopt and is social-nav (human crowd) focused, not robot-robot congestion.
- **Gazebo (gz-sim) headless**: by default Gazebo simulates as fast as it can, and the real-time factor is sim time divided by wall time. A user who set the physics real-time update rate to 0 reported an RTF of about 16, but noted that sensor data in RViz did not scale by the same factor. **[verified — gz docs + Discourse thread]** — [Gazebo Sim pause/run docs](https://gazebosim.org/api/sim/9/pause_run_simulation.html); [Discourse: faster-than-realtime](https://discourse.openrobotics.org/t/gazebo-faster-than-realtime-operation/48592); Nav2's official TB4 Gazebo sim: [ros-navigation/nav2_minimal_turtlebot_simulation](https://github.com/ros-navigation/nav2_minimal_turtlebot_simulation) (last push 2026-09-21). *Applicability:* it runs on CPU with no GPU and has a top-down camera sensor, so it could even render the overhead ArUco frame. It costs more per robot than Flatland, and Nav2 nodes become the bottleneck at high RTF.
- **Isaac Sim RTF context**: an NVIDIA forum report gives RTF ≈ 0.14 with three Nova Carters (0.19 for one robot). **[forum, informal]** — [NVIDIA forum thread](https://forums.developer.nvidia.com/t/how-can-we-get-simulation-time-close-to-real-time/307676). *Applicability:* this backs our own experience that Isaac runs *slower* than realtime per episode, so any 2D sim at RTF ≥ 1 is a several-fold to 100× gain before counting GPU cost.
- **FastSwarmSim**: a multi-UAV ROS 2 simulator with lock-step time, reported at up to about 100× realtime for one vehicle with lidar disabled. **[Discourse announcement]** — [Open Robotics Discourse](https://discourse.openrobotics.org/t/announcing-fastswarmsim-lightweight-multi-uav-ros-2-simulation-with-lock-step-time/57578). *Applicability:* aerial only. Its *lock-step clock* design is the pattern to copy so Nav2 does not fall behind a fast sim (the Flatland README warns of exactly that).

**Pure Python, no ROS required**
- **IR-SIM** (Han et al., arXiv:2606.08729, Jun 2026): "A Lightweight Skill-Native Simulator for Navigation, Learning, and Benchmarking". Scenarios are YAML files covering kinematics (`diff`, `omni`, `omni_angular`, `acker`), geometric collision checking, lidar, behaviours and a matplotlib renderer. The abstract claims it "runs up to several hundred times faster on the evaluated CPU platform" (baseline and hardware not named in the abstract). It ships **agent skills that let LLMs compose and modify the YAML scenarios**, cutting LLM scenario-construction time by more than 50%. README behaviours include multi-robot **RVO/ORCA** collision avoidance, a social-force model, dynamic random obstacles, grid maps and video export. **[verified — arXiv abstract + README via GitHub API]** — [arXiv:2606.08729](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.08729); [hanruihua/ir-sim](https://github.com/hanruihua/ir-sim) (1.2k stars, last push **2026-11-11**); [docs](https://ir-sim.readthedocs.io/en/). ROS 2 integration was **not found** in the paper's comparison table or the README. *Applicability:* this is the best fit for the cheap counterfactual-rollout tier in Q1. It is diff-drive, multi-robot, renders a top-down image natively, and its LLM-driven YAML generation matches the "more diverse congestion scenes" need. Strategies (yield, reroute, backup) would need small Python controllers in place of Nav2.
- **VMAS** (proroklab): a vectorized 2D physics engine in PyTorch with multi-robot scenarios. One secondary summary reports 30,000 parallel simulations in under 10 s and more than 100× faster than MPE. Agents are holonomic by default; diff-drive dynamics are enforced by transforming actions. Rendering is optional (`vmas[render]`). **[search-snippet + repo verified]** — [arXiv:2207.03530](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.03530); [proroklab/VectorizedMultiAgentSimulator](https://github.com/proroklab/VectorizedMultiAgentSimulator) (629 stars, last push 2026-05-19). *Applicability:* useful only for massive sweeps or RL baselines. Its batch-parallelism is wasted on a VLM-in-the-loop bench, where the VLM call dominates the cost.
- **PyRoboSim**: "2D mobile robot simulator for behavior prototyping with optional ROS 2 interface". **[repo listing verified]** — [sea-bass/pyrobosim](https://github.com/sea-bass/pyrobosim) (395 stars, last push 2026-11-02). *Applicability:* a task/world-semantics sim (rooms, objects). It could host strategy-level logic with ROS 2 hooks, but multi-robot congestion physics is not its focus (not verified).
- **Robotarium Python simulator**: unicycle (diff-drive) dynamics with single-integrator and unicycle **barrier certificates** for collision avoidance, plus plotting. **[verified — README]** — [robotarium/robotarium_python_simulator](https://github.com/robotarium/robotarium_python_simulator) (last push 2026-10-16). *Applicability:* a tiny-dependency diff-drive sim. Its barrier certificates are a ready-made "safety layer" analogue to M4. Code that passes the simulator can be submitted to real Robotarium robots for free.
- **CrowdNav / gym-collision-avoidance**: last pushes 2022-08 and 2023-04 respectively, so effectively unmaintained. **[repo metadata verified]** — [vita-epfl/CrowdNav](https://github.com/vita-epfl/CrowdNav); [mit-acl/gym-collision-avoidance](https://github.com/mit-acl/gym-collision-avoidance). *Applicability:* avoid as runtime dependencies. The scenario semantics are covered in prior notes.

**Grid / MAPF**

- **LoRR Start-Kit + PlanViz**: the League of Robot Runners competition kit (C++ with Python bindings, Python ≥ 3.11, Docker evaluation env) plus **PlanViz**, a Python visualizer for planner output. **[verified — README]** — [MAPF-Competition/Start-Kit](https://github.com/MAPF-Competition/Start-Kit) (last push 2026-07-15); [MAPF-Competition/PlanViz](https://github.com/MAPF-Competition/PlanViz). *Applicability:* flow-based at large N (as prior notes say). Only PlanViz's rendering is reusable.
- **RWARE (Multi-Robot Warehouse)**: a MARL grid warehouse environment, last pushed 2024-09-15. **[repo metadata verified]** — [semitable/robotic-warehouse](https://github.com/semitable/robotic-warehouse). *Applicability:* a warehouse look-alike, but a discrete grid MARL env with no recent maintenance. **GPU physics engines (for completeness)**
- **MuJoCo / MJX**: very active (google-deepmind/mujoco last push 2026-11-11). ### Inferences
- A recommended two-tier design follows from the sources above.
  1. **Tier A, IR-SIM.** Re-implement 3–4 strategies as Python controllers. Generate many congestion scenes from YAML, LLM-assisted via IR-SIM skills. Run counterfactual rollouts per logged item at CPU cost.
  2. **Tier B, Flatland with Nav2 Jazzy on `/clock`.** Spot-check that Tier A outcomes survive the real Nav2 stack.
  3. **Isaac.** Reserve it for the final confirmatory set.

  This mirrors Arena-Rosnav's multi-backend philosophy without adopting its whole framework.
- Speed estimates per tier remain unmeasured for our 2–6-robot scenes. The only quantified figures are IR-SIM's "several hundred ×" (unspecified baseline), Gazebo RTF ≈ 16 (one user) and Isaac RTF < 1. Benchmark one head-on scene in each candidate before committing.
- Nav2 is the speed ceiling in Tier B, not the sim. Lock-step clocking (FastSwarmSim pattern) or a modest RTF of 2–5× is realistic; 100× is not.

### Gaps
- No published RTF numbers were found for Flatland + Nav2 multi-robot, or for stage_ros2 under Jazzy.
- Whether Flatland's ROS 2 diff-drive plugin is fully Jazzy-released (apt) or source-only was not confirmed. The branch names suggest active Jazzy work.
- IR-SIM's exact speed table and any ROS 2 bridge were not extracted from the paper body; only the abstract and README were read.
- Arena-Rosnav's "flatsim" backend repo was not located under the Arena-Rosnav org listing, which shows only an old `flatland` fork last pushed 2024-02.

## Q3. Synthesizing top-down scene images from recorded robot states

### Takeaway
No turnkey "robot-state log → overhead VLM image" tool exists for indoor multi-robot logs. In driving, the standard practice is BEV rasterization of agent polygons on map layers (trajdata, motion-prediction rasterizers). For robots, the practical options are IR-SIM's matplotlib renderer, PlanViz for grid plans, or a ~100-line in-house OpenCV renderer driven by our existing map YAML and `RobotState` records. Pseudo-simulation's 3DGS approach would be photoreal but needs camera frames, which we never persisted.

### Cited Findings
- **trajdata** (NVlabs): "A unified interface to many trajectory forecasting datasets" with a Map API. Last push 2026-10-14. **[repo verified; rasterization details not confirmed in README excerpt]** — [NVlabs/trajdata](https://github.com/NVlabs/trajdata). *Applicability:* driving-dataset oriented. Borrow its raster conventions (agent-centric crop, per-layer channels), not the library.
- BEV rasterization practice: render map polygons plus each actor's past polygons onto an RGB raster at a fixed resolution ([arXiv:2004.06247](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.06247)), or encode each map element as a binary mask channel and add per-timestep agent channels ([arXiv:2104.08368](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.08368)). **[search-snippet]** *Applicability:* this specifies what a synthetic overhead frame should contain: walls/racks, robot footprints with heading, velocity arrows, goals, and motion history.
- **IR-SIM renderer**: a matplotlib-based top-down renderer with GIF/video export, driven by the same YAML world. **[verified — README]** — [hanruihua/ir-sim](https://github.com/hanruihua/ir-sim). *Applicability:* if recorded episodes are re-expressed as IR-SIM worlds, every replayed or counterfactual state gets a free top-down image.
- **PlanViz** (LoRR): a Python visualizer for grid MAPF plans. **[verified — linked from Start-Kit README]** — [MAPF-Competition/PlanViz](https://github.com/MAPF-Competition/PlanViz). *Applicability:* grid-only, so it suits only a POGEMA/grid side study.
- **MetaDrive** includes a lightweight top-down renderer for driving and is actively maintained (last push 2026-10-16). **[repo metadata verified]** — [metadriverse/metadrive](https://github.com/metadriverse/metadrive). *Applicability:* driving-specific, so use it only as a design reference.

### Inferences
- The lowest-risk path is to reuse M1's own `annotation.py` drawing code, which already draws status-colored boxes, velocity arrows and goals, on a blank map raster produced from the scenario map via the homography. That gives synthetic frames stylistically aligned with what the VLM saw live, minus the camera texture.
- Project memory (gpt-4o refusals) recorded that the *real annotated camera frame* triggered refusals (about 25% on replay) while synthetic frames drew 0/240. A synthetic renderer therefore also removes a known confound, but it introduces a sim-to-real image gap that must be disclosed. A small paired check on any live episodes that still exist would quantify that gap.
- Garg et al. (arXiv:2404.06413, covered in prior notes) already used top-view rendered images for VLM deadlock resolution, which gives precedent for synthetic overhead images as VLM input.

### Gaps
- No tool was found that converts ROS 2 bag pose/odometry streams into overhead images for VLM evaluation. This appears to be custom work everywhere.
- No study was found that measures VLM decision agreement between real overhead camera frames and synthetic renders of the same state for robotics. That would be a novel measurement for this project.
